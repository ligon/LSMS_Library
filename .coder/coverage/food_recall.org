#+TITLE: food_recall.csv -- the recall window and visit count of every food module
#+DATE: [2026-09-09]

* What this is

One row per =(country, wave)= cell that has a =food_acquired= module: *how long
a recall the food question asks for, and how many times it is asked.*  67 rows,
matching the =sane= cells of =latest.csv=.

It exists because =food_expenditures= returns a number whose reference period is
recorded nowhere (GH #851).  Most of the corpus turns out to be *7 days, asked
once* -- but GhanaLSS delivers 30 days of coverage, Burkina Faso 2014 delivers
28, and Guatemala and Panama 15, and nothing in the API distinguishes them.

*This is a documentation artifact, not a derived one.*  It is not regenerated by
=make=; it is corrected in place as questionnaires get read, and its git history
is the journal.

* Columns

| column                  | meaning                                                          |
|-------------------------+------------------------------------------------------------------|
| =recall_verbatim=       | the window as the source states it; empty when nothing states it |
| =recall_days=           | the length of ONE ask.  Blank unless determinate                 |
| =n_visits=              | how many times the food module is ASKED (see below)              |
| =total_exposure_days=   | =recall_days x n_visits=, only where both are known               |
| =n_items_delivered=     | distinct =j= delivered in that wave -- the LIST-LENGTH axis       |
| =basis=                 | the evidence rung (see below)                                    |
| =evidence=              | =path:line=, or a Documentation path + page                      |
| =within_wave_variation= | the axis the window varies on inside one wave, if any            |

** =n_visits= counts ASKS, not interviewer visits

GLSS7 fields *seven* interviewer visits but asks the food module at *six* of
them -- visit 1 is intake.  =recall_days x n_visits= is arithmetic only on asks:
6 x 5 = 30 days of coverage, where 7 x 5 = 35 is the fieldwork cycle.  Where the
two differ the interviewer-visit count is in =notes=.  A single-visit survey is
=n_visits= 1, never blank.

** =basis= -- the evidence rung, borrowed from =capability.py=

Deliberately the same ladder as the coverage verdicts, for the same reason: a
weak source must not be laundered into a fact.

| rung             | what it means                                                  |
|------------------+----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =questionnaire=  | the instrument states it, quoted (or transcribed into a script) |
| =variable-label= | a Stata variable label states it                                |
| =config-comment= | a maintainer asserted it in repo config or prose               |
| =filename=       | inferred from a source filename (=conso7jours=) -- a GUESS      |
| =not-recorded=   | nobody has looked                                              |

Current distribution: *not-recorded 33 · config-comment 23 · questionnaire 8 ·
variable-label 3 · filename 0.*

Only 8 of 34 stated windows are questionnaire-grade.  Twenty-three are a
maintainer's assertion -- including GhanaLSS 1998-99 and 2012-13, whose "five
days apart" is an analogy to GLSS7 rather than a sourced quote.

*Rung corrections applied 2026-09-09*, recorded in =notes=: Guatemala 2000 from
=questionnaire= to =variable-label= (its verbatim is a Stata label, not an
instrument quote); Panama 1997/2003/2008 from =questionnaire= to
=config-comment= (evidence is a wave-script structural comment).

* =n_items_delivered= -- the axis that may matter more than recall

Computed, not recorded: =groupby('t').j.nunique()= on the delivered table.  54
of 67 cells; the other 13 (Benin, Cambodia, CotedIvoire, EthiopiaRHS,
Guinea-Bissau, Panama, Togo) need a cold build.

Beegle et al. (2012) find that *item-list length costs ~21% of reported food
consumption against recall length's ~7%* -- collapsing 58 items into 11
categories moved the number three times as far as going from a 7-day to a
14-day recall.  The corpus's list lengths move at least that much, and they move
*within* a country across its own panel:

| country      | delivered distinct items, by wave                     |
|--------------+-------------------------------------------------------|
| Ethiopia     | 25, 26, 55, 73, 74                                    |
| GhanaLSS     | 58, 58, 117, 112, 114, 114, *179*                     |
| Burkina Faso | 55, 130, 152                                          |
| Uganda       | 61, 63, 70, 71, 77, 77, *117*, 116                    |
| Nigeria      | 90, 90, 96, 104, 116, 116, 110, 110                   |

Ethiopia's list roughly triples across its own five ESS waves.  On Beegle's
evidence that alone could move measured food consumption by more than any recall
difference in this table -- *and it would read as growth*.  Every one of those
cells grades =sane=.

*Two caveats before anyone leans on the number.*  (1) =j= is the HARMONISED
Preferred Label, so the count reflects this library's =harmonize_food=
vocabulary as much as the instrument; Ethiopia's 26 -> 55 in one step could be
the ESS adding items or us harmonising later waves more finely, and separating
those is real work nobody has done.  (2) It is *delivered* distinct items -- the
union over households of what was actually reported -- which approximates the
printed list from below, never above.

* The rules that make it worth trusting

1. *Blank beats plausible.*  Never interpolate from a sibling wave.  GhanaLSS
   2005-06 has eleven interviewer visits and known ask counts (10 purchased / 9
   produced) and *every numeric column blank*, because its spacing is
   unestablished and the only tempting number descends from boilerplate that
   belongs to GLSS3.  That row is what this file is for.
2. *Every non-empty =recall_verbatim= carries an =evidence= citation.*
3. *No =#= comments in the CSV* -- same trap as =blessed.csv=, whose loader
   reads it without a =comment== arg.
4. *A cell with within-wave variation gets no single number.*  GhanaLSS 1991-92
   is 2 days x 7 asks rural and 3 x 10 urban; filling either would be the error
   the row exists to flag.

* Within-wave variation -- 5 cells, 3 countries, only 2 of them GhanaLSS

| axis           | cells                                                  |
|----------------+--------------------------------------------------------|
| =by-s=         | GhanaLSS 2005-06, Nigeria 2018Q3 + 2019Q1              |
| =stratum=      | GhanaLSS 1991-92 (urban 3-day/10 asks, rural 2-day/7)  |
| =two-periods=  | Burkina Faso 2021-22 (7 days AND 30 days, unmarked)    |

*The axis means the DELIVERED table mixes windows, not that the instrument
offered several.*  Guatemala 2000 and Panama 1997/2003/2008 were initially
tagged =by-column= and were wrong: their instruments do mix an actual 15-day
recall with usual-month estimates, but both maintainers RESOLVED it -- Panama's
wave script says "We use ONLY s11a10a so the non-purchased quantity shares the
15-day recall basis of purchased s11a6a" -- so the shipped window is
single-valued at 15 days.  Reclassified 2026-09-09, with the source-level
variation moved to =notes=.  The promotion script refuses a cell that carries
both an axis and a number, which is how the error surfaced.

Nigeria W4's own config states it: the purchase block records the most recent
purchase within 30 days while produced/in-kind are 7-day consumption, so
"Quantity summed across =s= is therefore not a 7-day consumption total for W4."
*Within-wave variation is the common case, not a GhanaLSS quirk* -- which is why
no carrier keyed on =(wave, table)= alone can express this file.

* The work queue

33 of 67 cells (49.3%) are =not-recorded=, covering ~3.1M of 11.4M rows:
Uganda 8, EthiopiaRHS 5, Malawi 5, GhanaSPS 3, Mali/Niger/Senegal 2 each, and
one each for Benin, Burkina Faso, Cambodia, CotedIvoire, Guinea-Bissau, and
GhanaLSS 2005-06.

Per =capability.py='s logic this is a *bounded human step per series* -- one RA,
one questionnaire -- not a per-cell research project.  Filling it is what would
let any of the machinery debated in GH #851 have something to carry.
