The Ghana Living Standards Survey (Ghana LSS) is one of two major surveys we have for Ghana, the other being the Ghana Social Panel Survey (GhanaSPS).

rounds span 1987--2017.  Each round is designed as an independent
nationally representative cross-section; there is no rotating panel
or refreshment sample mechanism --- /from GLSS3 onward/.

* Sampling Design

** Overview

Two-stage stratified cluster sample.  Enumeration Areas (EAs) from
the national census serve as the PSU.

*The take of households is fixed per /workload/ and per /stratum/, NOT
per EA.*  A second PPS stage (on the ratio of listed households to
1984-census households) allocates workloads among the selected EAs, so
an EA receives 0, 1, 2 or 3 workloads.  GLSS3: 15 households per urban
workload, 10 per rural workload, 407 workloads across 366 EAs that
received at least one.  Main report Appendix 1 (=1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3report.pdf=,
printed p.109) states it verbatim for GLSS1's 16-household workloads:

#+begin_quote
an EA, for example, which received two workloads provided 32 households for the sample.
#+end_quote

/Corrected 2026-08-22./  This subsection previously read "with a fixed
take of households per EA", and that imprecision was load-bearing: it
led a reader to infer that a =clust= code /bundles/ EAs (from GLSS1/2's
32 = 2x16) and to write that inference into a config comment.  *That
inference is refuted*: =clust= /is/ the EA -- one EA, two workloads.
The decisive check is that take-15 clusters are 100 per cent urban
while take-20 clusters are 100 per cent rural, which no bundling story
produces.  See =slurm_logs/ghana_audit/FINDINGS_glss3_selfweighting.org=
(PR #715) for the full reconciliation, which matches GSS's own design
table cell-for-cell.

*** GLSS7 sample design, from the Main Report

- Two-stage stratified.  First stage: *1,000 EAs* (PSUs) from the 2010
  PHC frame, allocated to the 10 regions by stratified systematic PPS;
  *438 urban and 562 rural*.  Second stage: a full household listing in

  achieved and served here).

  the young.*  The allocation is driven by a PER-DOMAIN precision
  target, not by population share:

  the proportion of persons under the poverty line accurately at 5%

  Measured on the served data: mean weight runs *0.23 (Upper West) to

*** GLSS7 weights: the structure, documented AND verified

The Main Report's "Computation of Weights" gives the formula, and it
names two DIFFERENT household counts per EA:

- =M_hi=  = number of *2010 Population Census* households in EA /i/ of stratum /h/
- =M_hi*= = number of households *listed* in EA /i/ of stratum /h/ before fieldwork

First stage is PPS on the CENSUS size; the second stage takes =b= = 15
households from the LISTED households.  So the expansion factor carries
a =M_hi* / M_hi= term -- six years of differential EA growth between the
2010 frame and the 2016-17 listing.  The report is explicit that this is
not a self-weighting design:

#+begin_quote
The GLSS7 was not a self-weighting sample design because disproportionately
larger samples from regions with smaller populations were drawn.
#+end_quote

*Verified against the weights this library serves* (1,000 clusters):

| EAs wholly urban or wholly rural | 20-strata design | *0 of 1,000* clusters mix |
| =a11= = 41 urban EAs, Western | report, verbatim | *41* |
| =a12= = 57 rural EAs, Western | report, verbatim | *57* |
| 438 urban / 562 rural EAs | report /narrative/ (p.285) | 439 / 561 -- matches the report's own *tables* |

*** The stale-frame problem, and what it costs (NOT discussed in the report)

=M_hi*= is observed only for EAs that were *selected on =M_hi=*, and
=M_hi= is the 2010 census count.  An EA that grew faster than its
stratum since 2010 was therefore selected with too low a probability at
the first stage.  Three consequences, only the first of which the
weights repair:

1. *Bias: repaired.*  The =M*/M= term in the expansion factor is exactly
   the Horvitz-Thompson correction for the realised selection
   probability, so totals and means stay design-unbiased.
2. *Efficiency: not repaired, and the cost is large.*  Under-selected
   fast-growing EAs carry correspondingly large weights, and variable
   weights cost precision.  Kish =deff_w = 1 + CV^2(w)=, measured:

   | weight variation | deff_w | effective n |
   |------------------+--------+-------------|
   | all households (total) |  1.835 | 54% of nominal |
   | *within stratum only*  | *1.409* | *~71%* |

   The between-stratum part is a deliberate trade -- it buys the
   per-domain poverty precision the allocation was designed for.  The
   *within*-stratum part is frame drift and buys nothing: roughly *29%
   of the effective sample* spent on the frame being six years old.
   Implied growth ratios run x0.56 (p10) to x1.75 (p90), one EA at
   *x10.2* of its stratum median; the residual is mildly right-skewed
   (+0.13).
3. *Coverage: NOT repairable by weighting.*  An EA created after the
   2010 PHC has =M_hi = 0=, hence first-stage selection probability
   zero, and cannot enter the sample at all.  No weight fixes a zero
   probability.  This falls precisely on the newest peri-urban
   settlement -- the fastest-growing part of the population -- and is a
   coverage gap, not an efficiency loss.  Consistent with the measured
   pattern that *Greater Accra's RURAL stratum is the most dispersed*
   (within-stratum sd 0.836).

Also note the observed =M*/M= distribution is *not* the population
distribution of EA growth: EAs enter the sample with probability
proportional to their *2010* size, so the growth we can see is
size-biased on the stale measure.  If growth is concentrated in EAs that
were small in 2010, the 0.483 figure understates true dispersion --
plausible but *unverified here*, since =M= and =M*= are not in the
served data (only their ratio, up to a stratum constant, via the
weight).

*The Main Report does not discuss any of this.*  Its only frame

identification, not the EA list.  There is no mention of new EAs, frame
ageing, or the precision cost.  Treat the above as this repository's
analysis, not as a claim sourced to GSS.

** Weights

GLSS1 and GLSS2 are *self-weighting* (equal probability of selection;
no weight variable in the data).  GLSS3 (1991-92) has a =weight=
variable in =POV_GH.DTA= but it is constant 1.0 (self-weighting).
GLSS4 (1998-99) =POV_GH.DTA= has probability weights normalised
to mean ~1 (sum ~ n), which are a different type from the expansion
weights in later rounds.

GLSS5 (2005-06) onward provides *expansion weights* in the cover
page or location file: =weight= in =sec0.dta= (GLSS5), =weight= in
=g6loc_edt.dta= (GLSS6), =WTA_S= in =g7sec0.dta= (GLSS7).  These
sum to the national population.

=panel_weight= equals =weight= for all waves.

/Corrected 2026-08-24./  This used to read "Since every round is an
independent cross-section, there is no panel weight."  *The premise is false
for GLSS1 -> GLSS2* (see /Survey Program/ above and /Tensions/ s1): those two
rounds are a rotating panel, half retained.  The mirroring is therefore
a *house convention*, not a consequence of independent sampling --- and for
GLSS2 it is thin, because the retained half is not distinguished from the

=PANELC.DAT= (shipped in both =1987-88/Data/= and =1988-89/Data/=) carries the

*CAVEAT -- the carve-out is a CPI deflator, not a sampling weight.*

routinely truncated out of short quotations, and someone will otherwise
read it as contradicting the constant =weight=.  It resolves (Appendix
1, "Re-weighting", printed pp.113--114) to a *monthly national CPI
deflator to March 1992 prices*, keyed on interview date -- e.g. the
September 1991 factor is 17925.4/17066.4 = 1.0503, identical for every
household interviewed that month.  It is orthogonal to =weight= and
must *never* be wired as one.  =POV_GH.DTA= carries =month= and =year=,
so a user who wants it can reconstruct it.

*** SUPERSEDED (2026-08-22): "only expansion weights, to avoid mixing"

The decision recorded here until 2026-08-22 read, verbatim:

#+begin_quote
The =sample= feature only includes expansion weights (GLSS5--GLSS7)
to avoid mixing weight types.  GLSS1--GLSS4 have =weight= and
=panel_weight= as NaN.
#+end_quote

*The hazard it guarded against is now handled library-wide, so the
workaround is no longer needed.*  =Country._finalize_result= rescales
every wave's =weight= and =panel_weight= to within-wave mean 1 at API
time, dividing by that wave's own mean over non-null values (see
=lsms_library/country.py::_normalise_sample_weights= and =CLAUDE.md=
§"=sample()= and Cluster Identity").  A normalised column is divided by
1.0000 and is unchanged; an expansion column becomes normalised.  The
two types are therefore indistinguishable at the API and cannot be
mixed by a user summing across waves.  Raw values remain in the cached
parquet.

*Cost of the superseded workaround: four waves of usable weights* --
GLSS1, GLSS2, GLSS3 and GLSS4 all have =weight= and =panel_weight=
NaN today, and =null_read_audit= reports exactly that ("GhanaLSS/sample/weight
and panel_weight are empty in four of six waves", =CLAUDE.md=).  GLSS3's
constant 1.0 and GLSS4's genuine probability weights *can now be wired*
without creating a mixed-scale table.

*This entry does not wire them.*  PR #714 (draft) owns GLSS3 + GLSS4;
GLSS1/GLSS2 have no weight variable in the data at all, so for those two
the NaN is the honest answer and stays.

*Normalisation makes the two /scales/ comparable; it does not make the
two /designs/ the same.*  GLSS3 was deliberately amended to /restore/ a
self-weighting sample by a published procedure and GLSS4 was not --
Poverty Profile Appendix 4 (=1991-92/Documentation/pdf/PovProf.pdf=,
printed p.57):

#+begin_quote
The latter [restoring a self-weighting sample] was done for GLSS 3 following a procedure devised by Scott and Amenuvegbe (1991).

The same procedure though was not applied for GLSS 4.
#+end_quote

That documented design change is why GLSS3 ships a constant weight and
GLSS4 a real distribution (min 0.127 / median 0.85 / max 6.02), and the
contrast is a survey fact that must survive the rescaling, not be read
as an artefact of it.

*Direction of bias, by construction.*  Under GLSS4's rule every locality the

have grown to urban status since the 1984 census" -- not an error.  Any
GhanaLSS urbanisation series that crosses 1998-99 is measuring that
definition, not only the country.

** Oversamples and sub-samples

*This is the part a reader pooling GLSS rounds needs before averaging
anything.*  In this country, as in most of the corpus, specialization lives
*inside* a wave rather than as a whole wave.

*** GLSS1 -> GLSS2: a rotating panel with a half-retained sub-sample

The GLSS3 main report's Appendix 1 describes the GLSS1/GLSS2 design.
=1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3report.pdf=, "Sample design for GLSS1 and GLSS2",
printed page 110:

#+begin_quote
The original idea was that a rotating panel design would be used, with half of the sample being retained each year for re-interview and the other half being replaced. This design would provide a representative sample of households each year, but it also had the advantage of continuity from one year to the next, enabling more precise estimates to be made of the changes occurring in the socio-economic situation of households. Half the workloads from GLSS1 were therefore retained for GLSS2, and attempts were made to re-interview the same households. The other half of the GLSS2 sample was taken from the 100 EAs in replicate 6 of the master sample.
#+end_quote

And the master-sample construction, same appendix, printed page 109:

#+begin_quote
The Ghana Statistical Service (GSS) had created a master sample of enumeration areas (EAs), which serves as the sampling frame used for selecting households for each annual round of the GLSS. To prepare this master sample, the 12,969 EAs from the 1984 Population Census were first placed in order, by region within each location (rural/semi-urban/urban), in turn within each ecological zone (coastal/forest/savannah). A total of 800 EAs were then systematically selected from this list with probability proportional to size (PPS), and these selected EAs were then assigned systematically to eight separate replicates of 100 EAs. Each replicate thus provides a representative PPS sample of EAs. For GLSS1 two replicates (1 and 5) were used.
#+end_quote

So *GLSS2 is documented as two sub-samples in one wave*: a retained panel half
(re-interview attempts on GLSS1 workloads) and a fresh half drawn from
replicate 6.  This is precisely the "specialization inside a wave" shape.
It also *contradicts* the existing =Survey Program= text -- see /Tensions/
below.

Corroborating, from GLSS1's own DDI overview (=1987-88/Documentation/GLSS
1987-88 Report.pdf=, "Sampling Procedure", printed "- 2 -"; decoded, see the
transcription caveat above):

#+begin_quote
The resulting list is 3200 households and 800 replacement households in something less than 200 sampling areas (specifically 178 in 1987-88 and 170 in 1988-89). Each group of 16, 32 or 48 households within a sampling area is referred to as a cluster in the GLSS data sets and in this document.
#+end_quote

That is one design document describing both rounds' realised sampling areas --
consistent with a single master sample worked across two years, not two
independent draws.

*** GLSS3: the design breaks with GLSS1/GLSS2

Same appendix, printed page 110:

#+begin_quote
For GLSS3 it was initially expected that the same sample design would be followed, with replicate 6 being retained and replicate 1 being replaced by replicate 2. A listing exercise was therefore carried out for replicate 2 in July/August 1989. However, following discussions between the GSS and the World Bank, it was decided that GLSS3 would differ substantially from GLSS1 and GLSS2, by giving much fuller attention to household expenditure and consumption, and with less attention to some of the topics covered previously. In order to obtain high quality data on household consumption and expenditure it was also decided that a larger and more widespread sample was required.
#+end_quote

(The sentence ends there; a superscript footnote marker "15" follows
="required"= in the source and has been dropped per the transcription note.)

The rotation was therefore abandoned at GLSS3.  This is the documentary basis
for "no cross-wave linkage exists for GLSS3 onward" in =Panel Linkage= below --
and it is a stronger basis than the ID-availability argument recorded there.

** Universe vs. what the weights represent

The GLSS documentation is unusually explicit that these are different things,
and says so in two rounds.

GLSS5, =2005-06/Documentation/ddi-documentation-english-5.pdf=, "Weighting",
PDF page 6:

#+begin_quote
The GLSS 5 is not a self-weighting sample design because disproportionately larger samples from regions with smaller populations were drawn. Therefore each sample household did not have the same chance of selection into the sample. Hence, weights were computed to reflect the different probabilities of selection in order to obtain the true contribution of each selected EA in the sample based on the first and second stage probabilities of selection.

The household weight variable is called WEIGHT which is attached to each section data

A grossing up factor (637.89) was used for the agriculture sections instead of the weight variable.
#+end_quote

GLSS6, =2012-13/Documentation/REPORTS/GLSS6_Main Report.pdf=, "9. Computation
of Weights", printed page 207:

#+begin_quote
The GLSS6 is not a self-weighting sample design because disproportionately larger samples from regions with smaller populations were drawn. Therefore, each sample household did not have the same chance of selection into the survey sample. Hence, weights were computed to reflect the different probabilities of selection in order to obtain the true contribution of each selected EA in the sample based on the first and second stage probabilities of selection.
#+end_quote

GLSS6 quantifies the resulting disproportionality, same appendix, printed page 206:

#+begin_quote
The overall raising factor for the survey is 368. This means that, on average, the GLSS6 conducted interviews with 1 in 368 of the population of all ages per cluster. However, interviewing rates ranged from as low as 1 in 97 in Upper West Region and 1 in 158 in Upper East Region to as high as 1 in 650 in the Greater Accra Region and 1 in 703 in Ashanti Region (Table A7).
#+end_quote

Consequences a user must not miss:

- The universe is national in both rounds, but the *sample* is heavily tilted
  toward the small northern regions -- by a factor of roughly 7 between Upper
  West and Ashanti in GLSS6.  Unweighted GLSS5/GLSS6 statistics are *not*
  national statistics.
- *GLSS5's agriculture sections do not use =WEIGHT=.*  GSS states a flat
  grossing-up factor of 637.89 for them instead.  Anyone weighting GLSS5
  agriculture data with the household =weight= variable is using a weight GSS
  did not intend for those sections.  This is not currently reflected anywhere
  in =Sampling Design= above.
- For GLSS1 and GLSS2 the documentation asserts the opposite regime.  From the
  GLSS3 report, Appendix 1, printed page 109:

  #+begin_quote
  The total sample therefore consisted of 3200 households, and the sample design provided a self-weighting sample, since each household in Ghana had an equal probability of being selected.
  #+end_quote

  This corroborates the existing =Weights= subsection's "GLSS1 and GLSS2 are
  self-weighting" with a documentary citation, which it previously lacked.

Data on expenditures is somewhat unusual, in two different ways.

First, for waves before 2016--17, the survey instrument elicits the amount spent in purchasing different items, but does /not/ elicit quantities purchased.  (A separate price survey was conducted, and could presumably be used to obtain quantities.)  A separate module elicits quantities of home produced food consumed, as well as the hypothetical price at which home produced goods could be sold.

Second, starting in the 1991--92 wave, rather than eliciting expenditures over a single recall period, repeated visits are made over the course of about a month, with expenditures and consumption from own production collected at every visit.  The number of visits, their spacing and the recall length each differ by wave -- seven visits five days apart in GLSS4, GLSS6 and GLSS7, eleven in GLSS5 -- so they are tabulated per wave below rather than summarised here.  In =food_acquired= we keep the =visit= level and sum over it in the derived tables (patterns at the visit level are worth examining, and are now sliceable across =s=; see below).

NB: Differences in elicitation across waves may complicate comparisons across those waves.  In particular, the two rounds in the 1980s ask for purchases (12A) "since my last visit" and only once, and ask for own production (12B) over NO recall window at all -- months eaten in the last twelve, times per unit, and the value of one eating occasion, from which the served value is /derived/ (see "12B is a value per eating occasion" below) -- while subsequent rounds elicit both across several visits. **** GLSS3 is stratified: 8 rural visits, 11 urban -- and two recall windows

The one wave whose recall window is not constant within the wave.
=1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3Intman.pdf= section 1.9:

There will be 8 interviewer visits at 2-day intervals to each rural household
and 11 interviewer visits at 3-day intervals to each urban household in a
cycle.  Thus a workload for the 16 days in a rural cluster is 10 households,

Both modules are asked at every visit after the first (p.42: "Part H is to be
administered on every visit except the first"), and p.43 states the count
directly -- responses are solicited at all visits except the first, "in other
words for urban it will be 10 times and 7 times for rural areas".  So:

- *urban*: 10 asks x 3 days = *30 days* of coverage;
- *rural*:  7 asks x 2 days = *14 days*.

urban-only.

***** The literature: Scott and Amenuvegbe (1990), commissioned FOR the GLSS

There is a controlled experiment on exactly this question, run in Ghana
*because of* this survey, and it anticipates the mechanism:

#+begin_quote
Scott, Chris and Ben Amenuvegbe (1990).  /Effect of Recall Duration on
Reporting of Household Expenditures: An Experimental Study in Ghana./
Social Dimensions of Adjustment in Sub-Saharan Africa Working Paper No. 6.
Washington, D.C.: World Bank.  World Bank report no. 8905.
http://documents.worldbank.org/curated/en/598051468774854613
#+end_quote

The same experiment appears again as, almost certainly, its published version:

#+begin_quote
Scott, Chris and Ben Amenuvegbe (1991).  "Recall Loss and Recall Duration: An
Experimental Study in Ghana."  /Inter-Stat/ 4(1): 31--55.
#+end_quote

/Probable, not verified./  Same authors, same experiment, same headline result;
the working paper closes by saying "the authors plan a fuller analysis of this
effect for later publication", and the journal version runs 25 pages against the
working paper's 19.  The experiment was funded by a UK Overseas Development
Administration grant and =Inter-Stat= was an ODA-linked statistics bulletin, so
it is the natural outlet -- but that last link is unconfirmed and a serials
catalogue would settle it.  *Both are in circulation as the citable form*: the
UN /Household Sample Surveys/ handbook cites the 1990 working paper, Beegle et
al. (2012) cite the 1991 /Inter-Stat/ version.

A sample-size difference between the two citations is NOT evidence of revision:
the working paper reports both numbers -- 144 households selected, 135
surviving absence and refusal "for analysis".  Beegle quotes the former, the
executive summary the latter.

(Neither is to be confused with Scott and Amenuvegbe /1991/ in the /Journal of
Official Statistics/ 7(3): 367--373, the self-weighting sampling paper cited at
the top of this file.  Three papers, two years, same two authors.)

*Why it exists*, in the authors' words: the GLSS design "called for a 2-week
recall period for each household, a period chosen essentially for logistic
motives rather than in response to any evidence of recall efficiency", and the
Social Dimensions of Adjustment project was about to propagate that design
across Africa.  It is a standalone experiment -- 135 households, each
interviewed *11 times* at varying intervals, ODA-funded, sponsored by the Ghana
Statistical Service -- NOT an experiment embedded in the GLSS itself.

*What they found*, over 13 frequently purchased items:

- reported daily expenditure falls consistently as the recall period lengthens
  from 1 day to 1 week, then *little or no further fall* from 1 to 2 weeks; a
  1-year recall was erratic.  The curve is steep early and flat later;
- after re-weighting away weekly/monthly purchase cycles, with start-up bias
  eliminated and recall bounded, the loss averages *2.9% for every day added*;
- the decline is *larger for items purchased more frequently*;
- so a weekly recall underestimates frequent purchases by *~20%* -- and that is
  in what they call "an abnormally well controlled field situation".

*Their mechanism, which is the part that matters here.*  They favour the
reading that the 1-day figure is the most accurate and that this is recall
lapse rather than a boundary artefact.  Their hypothesis is that beyond a
threshold recall length respondents *switch from factual to normative
reporting* -- "what I usually buy" rather than "what I bought" -- and normative
reporting overlooks exceptional (typically high) purchases, biasing downward.

*This is what our own measurement observes directly.*  On GLSS4, within
household and across ask number, reported spend is essentially invariant to the
realised interval: doubling the gap from 5 to 10 days changes reported spend by
+1.2% where proportionality predicts +100% (elasticity 0.018; item counts
identical at 18.8).  That is normative reporting, measured -- a household
reporting "what I buy between visits", not "what I bought in N days".  Their
"larger decline for frequently purchased items" likewise matches the first-ask
excess being mostly extensive-margin here (1.42x more items, GLSS3 rural).

*The consequence for aggregation.*  If the reported amount does not scale with
the elapsed interval, then dividing by measured days does not normalise -- it
injects fieldwork tempo into the estimate.  Bin GLSS4 households by measured
exposure and total spend is flat while the implied daily rate spans 2.7x,
entirely from the denominator.  The defensible quantity is the per-ask amount
at the *nominal protocol* interval, not the realised one.

*The question is not settled in the literature.*  The UN /Household Sample
Surveys in Developing and Transition Countries/ (ch. 24) cites this study and
immediately notes the dispute: Mahalanobis and Sen (1954) on the Indian
National Sample Survey found a one-MONTH reference period less biased than a
one-week one for certain foods -- the opposite sign -- and Deaton (1997) holds
that nobody has separated recall decay from boundary telescoping.  Our GLSS3
contrast is evidence on precisely that open question: at *identical* interval
lengths the first ask still carries 24-40% more, and the step is 1.84x for the
unaided rural stratum against 1.17x for the diary-assisted urban one.

/Corrected 2026-09-11./  This paragraph used to single out GLSS5 as "the
weakest first-ask step (1.02x)" on the belief that its own-production module

GLSS3 supplies one discriminating measurement.  There the first interval is

** Trap 1: a bare =get_categorical_mapping= returns an EMPTY dict, silently

column ships 100% null while the build reports success.

** Trap 3 (RESOLVED 2026-08-21): GLSS1/GLSS2 cluster region is now MEASURED

*Kept as history.*  For the record of what the inference was and what it cost,
because the shape of the mistake recurs.

*** What it was

GLSS1 and GLSS2 have *no cluster-location variable in the microdata* -- a sweep
of all 170 dictionaries finds none; the only near-hit, =Y00A::LOC=, is 99.8%
constant at 1 and is a fieldwork flag.

So both waves read =cluster_features.Region= and =household_roster.Birthplace=
from the *same* column, =Y01A.DAT:REGION= -- the person's *region of birth*.
=mapping.py::cluster_features= then *inferred* the cluster's region as the modal
birth region among members under 12 (young children being least likely to have
migrated), with =Age: AGEY= pulled into =myvars= purely to feed that filter.

The inference was strong on its own terms: 0 ties, the mode an outright
majority in all 346 clusters, median modal share 91.5%, minimum 55%, off a
median of 31 children per cluster.  It was *not* a coin flip.

It was still a guess about geography derived from a migration-sensitive
question, and it was *wrong for five clusters* -- every one naming an
*adjacent* region, which is exactly the border-migration failure an under-12
filter cannot dodge:

| wave    |    v | name                | correct     | inferred |
|---------+------+---------------------+-------------+----------|
| 1987-88 | 1080 | Adwamasek/Wiwasi    | Ashanti     | Central  |
| 1987-88 | 1107 | Ahantamwa(Tipokrom) | Brong Ahafo | Ashanti  |
| 1987-88 | 1168 | Tato Bator          | Brong Ahafo | Volta    |
| 1987-88 | 1188 | Damanko             | Volta       | Northern |
| 1988-89 | 2334 | Huni Ofori          | Central     | Eastern  |

*** What replaced it

*Appendix I of =GHA_1988_GLSS_Basicinfo_EN.pdf=* -- "Complete List of GLSS
Clusters" -- is the survey's own cluster list, and the BID carries a real text
layer (no OCR).  It is *authoritative* for GLSS1/GLSS2 cluster attributes
(decision, 2026-08-21).  Transcribed to =_/appendix_i_clusters.org=; the ten
clusters the document itself marks "should be excluded" are in
=_/appendix_i_extra_workloads.org= (none of which appear in the shipped data --
checked).

It matches *176 of 176* and *170 of 170* shipped clusters on
=CLUST = 1000 + yr1= / =2000 + yr2=, and additionally supplies
=Ecological_zone=, which these waves previously lacked.

*** The lesson worth keeping

The inference was reasonable, well-documented, measured, and *nobody looked for
a better source for a year*.  The better source was a text-layer PDF sitting in
=Documentation/= the whole time.  Before building an approximation, read the
documentation the survey shipped with itself.

After this change =Y01A.DAT:REGION= feeds =household_roster.Birthplace=
*only*, which is what it actually is.

** Trap 8: =convert_categoricals=True= turns the CODE you decode on into a LABEL (2026-09-05, GH #782)

The most expensive instance of Trap 1's family so far: *149,047 rows shipped
the survey's own raw labels as =j= for years*, and every guard we own said the
build succeeded.

=2005-06/_/food_acquired.py= read =sec8h.dta= (own production) with
=convert_categoricals=True=, which returns =foodcd= as *decoded label text*
(='beef'=, ='maize-flour/dough'=), then decoded it against
=labelsd['Code_8h']=, which is keyed by the *numeric code*.  Match rate:
*0 of 424,052*.  And =Series.replace()= passes an unmatched value through
*unchanged*, so instead of an error, or even a NaN, every produced row emitted
the survey's label as though it were a Preferred Label.

The purchase block was fine and *must not be "fixed"*: =sec9b.dta= is read with
=convert_categoricals=False=, so =freqcd= stays numeric and matches its
=Code_9b= map 1,436,956/1,436,956 = *100%*.  The two blocks differ only in that
one flag.

*Why the file must be read TWICE.*  Both reads are load-bearing, and this is
not gold-plating:
- =convert_categoricals=True= is *required* for =s8hq1= (the consumed-own-produce
  filter is ='yes'=/='no'=, not =1=/=2=) and for =s8hq13= (the native unit as
  text -- 2005-06 has no =units= table, so nothing else decodes it);
- =j= must come from a =convert_categoricals=False= read, or it is a label.
The second read costs 0.3 s; the frames are asserted row-aligned.

*Decode on the CODE axis, not the label axis.*  The label axis is the obvious
fix and is the wrong one.  =harmonize_food='s =Label_8h= column transcribes the
survey's own text, and for codes 91/92 it had been transcribed with the
spelling *corrected* -- =alcoholic beverages= / =non-alcoholic beverages=,
where the =.dta= says =alchoholic beverages= / =non-alchoholic beverages= (sic).
A =Label_8h= decode therefore covers only *96.8%*, and is one well-meant typo
fix away from silently matching nothing again.  =Code_8h= agrees with the data's
codes for all 63 items; =Code_9b= with =freqcd= for all 164.  The =Label_*=
columns are *documentation of the source spelling* -- =Label_9b= matches its
source 164/164 -- so the two 8h cells were corrected to the survey's spelling,
misspelling and all.  *Do not "fix" them again.*

*=.map()= is necessary but NOT sufficient.*  Swapping =.replace()= for =.map()=
turns an unmatched code into NaN, which =_drop_nonfood= (=isinstance(x, str)=)
would then discard *silently* -- the same disease with a different face.  The
script asserts coverage explicitly (=_assert_decoded=).

*What this cost, and why nothing caught it.*  Every guard the library owns
checks *shape*.  =_assert_built_required_columns= checks =j= is present; the
grain audit checks the index is unique; the null-read guard checks a column is
not all-NaN.  =j= was present, unique and fully populated -- with the wrong
vocabulary.  CLAUDE.md already names the only check that discriminates: /"a
plausible-looking distribution therefore proves nothing; the discriminating
check is a subset assertion against the canonical vocabulary."/  There was
none on =j=.  There is now: =tests/test_ghanalss_food_label_canonical.py=.

Note the =community_prices= section below says, in its own words, that the
overlap with =food_acquired().j= /"was checked against the =harmonize_food=
tables, not a built =food_acquired="/.  That is exactly the gap: the config was
right and the built table did not match it.

** Trap 9: a duplicate index in =food_acquired= silently REWRITES =Price= table-wide (2026-09-05, GH #782/#323)

*This one is armed and waiting.*  It did not fire in the shipped data, but only
because =2005-06/_/food_acquired.py= now keeps its index unique on purpose.  Any
future duplicate in GhanaLSS =food_acquired= re-arms it.

=food_acquired= is in =_ADDITIVE_MEASURE_COLUMNS=, so when its index is
non-unique =Country._normalize_dataframe_index= does *not* collapse with
=.first()= -- it SUMs =Quantity= / =Expenditure= and then, at
=country.py:5522=, re-derives

: df['Price'] = df['Expenditure'] / df['Quantity'].where(df['Quantity'] != 0)

The grain audit lists =Price= as =reconciled= on the grounds that it is
"re-derived from the summed totals", finds the residual lossless, and correctly
emits *no* =GrainCollapseWarning=.  Nothing here is a bug in core --- for a
survey where =Expenditure = Quantity x Price=, the reconciliation is exactly
right.

*GhanaLSS is not such a survey.*  =Expenditure= is structurally =NaN= for
=s='produced'= (the instrument records a farmgate =Price= and a =Quantity=, and
never a produced value), and the re-derivation is applied to the *whole frame*,
not merely to the duplicated groups.  Measured on the delivered table:

| rows with a Price     | block does not fire | block fires |
|-----------------------+---------------------+-------------|
| =s='produced'=        |   883,789 (95.2%)   |  44,112 (4.8%) |
| whole table           |   883,789           |   4,375,274 |

So a single duplicate anywhere in the table would *destroy 839,677 recorded
farmgate prices* and *fabricate* a =Price= on ~4.33M purchased rows, where
=u='Value'= makes =Quantity == Expenditure= and the "price" is therefore 1.0 ---
the "Kwacha per Kwacha" quantity =CLAUDE.md= warns about.  Silently, and with the
audit correctly reporting nothing, because the audit's premise is that the
re-derivation is information-preserving.

Fixing 2005-06's decode (Trap 8) *created* 24 such duplicates in 12 groups --- two
farmgate prices for one harmonised commodity, e.g. =mutton= and =goat= both
becoming =Goat=.  They are resolved in the wave script (=Quantity= sums, =Price=
becomes the quantity-weighted mean) rather than left to core, which is what
=CLAUDE.md= prescribes: core does not aggregate, and a reducer applied
downstream "only puts a signature on the corpse".

*Corrects the commit message of =b93f9c2c=*, which described core's behaviour as
=groupby().first()= discarding one price.  That was measured to be wrong: the
mechanism is the additive re-derivation above, and it is considerably worse than
=.first()= would have been.

** One food vocabulary, not seven: the cross-wave drift (2026-09-05, GH #782)

Each wave's =harmonize_food= carries its *own* =Preferred Label= column and
nothing reconciled them.  Seven commodities therefore shipped under two
spellings depending on the wave -- =Egg=/=Eggs=, =Tea bag=/=Tea bags=,
=Condiment=/=Condiments= and four =Other X=/=Other Xs=.

*This is invisible to any per-wave check.*  All seven waves are internally
clean (0 collisions each, measured); the defect exists *only in the assembled
cross-wave frame*.  Any guard that reads one wave at a time will pass forever.
That is why the guard reads all seven tables at once, and why it is
config-only: reading org tables costs milliseconds, so it need not be gated on
a warm cache.

*The country-level =_/categorical_mapping.org= =#+name: harmonize_food= is the
authority* (it was =_/food_items.org= =#+name: food_label= until GH #783 moved it),
and not merely by preference: =_/nutrition.py:187= resolves an unmatched =j=
through a =by_label= fallback keyed on *that* table's Preferred Label, so a
wave spelling it differently gets no FCT code and drops out of =nutrition=.
The table already knew about all seven -- it carries one column per wave
recording each wave's own label -- so every canonical form was read off it.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:
- =Condiment= and =Egg= follow the country table *against majority wave usage*
  (five waves said =Eggs=).  Popularity is not authority here; =by_label= is.
- =Tea= is emitted by *neither* 2012-13 nor 2016-17, but the country table lists
  =Tea bags= / =Tea bag= in those columns against Preferred =Tea= (FCT 12_008).
  A recorded decision, not a guess.

The =Aggregate Label= axis had the *same* six drifts and is user-visible
through =labels='Aggregate'=; it was fixed in the same pass, along with an
encoding artefact (2012-13 code 262's Aggregate read =Cooked Rice and Stew=
followed by two mojibake =Ê= characters, a twin of code 254's clean label).

*Why a rename here is provably safe.*  For the six singular/plural pairs, both
variants share the collision key, so the measured "0 internal collisions per
wave" is a proof that no wave holds both -- a rename cannot merge two distinct
foods.  =Tea= needed checking by hand, because its key differs: 2012-13 carries
only =Tea bags=, 2016-17 only =Tea bag=, neither has a separate =Tea=.

** =harmonize_price_item= and =harmonize_food= are ONE vocabulary (2026-09-05, GH #782)

A price item flagged =Food: yes= asserts it sits on that wave's
=harmonize_food= axis -- which is the whole reason =community_prices.j= can
join =food_acquired.j=.  Nothing enforced it, and a one-sided edit drifts
silently; renaming the food axis above immediately broke four waves' price
items.

*Derivation was considered and rejected*, with a reason rather than a shrug:
the price item =Code= (=ln=, 644 values in 2016-17) is a *different code space*
from =Code_9b=/=Code_8h=, and the survey ships no crosswalk.  The only link IS
the Preferred Label string, so "deriving" would mean adding a foreign-key
column -- exactly as much config as the column it replaces, and one more thing
to drift.  Derivation is also wrong-shaped: *400 of 2016-17's 643 price items
are non-foods* (charcoal, refuse disposal, public-toilet fees) that
=harmonize_food= must never gain.  The =Food= column already carries the
author's signed statement (=yes= on the axis / =own= a food the axis does not
name / =no= non-food), so the invariant to enforce is a *subset over
=Food == 'yes'=*, and the guard asserts exactly that.

That check immediately found a *pre-existing* violation unrelated to the
rename: 2012-13 price item 103 ='cigarette (555)'= was flagged =Food: yes= with
Preferred Label =Cigarrette= -- a double-r spelling that appears nowhere on
2012-13's food axis, nor anywhere else in the corpus (the other three price
waves spell it =Cigarette=).  2012-13's =harmonize_food= has no separate
cigarette label at all: source code 163 ='cigarrette, cigar'= resolves to
=Tobacco=.  The price item is now =Tobacco=, which is what that wave's own axis
calls the commodity.  Re-flagging it =Food: own= was rejected as a *false
statement*: =own= means the axis does not name the food, and this axis does.


** The =SU= -> =Rural= fold: the decision, and what the survey does and does not say (2026-08-21; evidence verified and corrected 2026-08-22)

Not a trap.  A *decision*, recorded here because the reasoning that was first
published for it turned out to be false, and the next person to look at this
will otherwise reconstruct the false version.

The 2026-08-21 version of this section said the survey "offers no evidence
either way" and carried the settlement-population definition below as an
unverified second-hand quote.  Both statements were wrong: the definition is
real and is verbatim in *four* files this repository ships, and it *is*
evidence bearing on the fold -- see /What the survey says/.  Corrected
2026-08-22 (GH #690).  The decision itself is unchanged and is not in
question here.

*What was decided.*  BID Appendix I classifies every GLSS1/GLSS2 cluster three
ways -- =U=, =R=, =SU= (semi-urban).  =SU= is *52 of 263* Appendix clusters,
20%.  Canonical =Rural= stays the binary ={Urban, Rural}=; =SU= is delivered as
*=Rural=*.  @ligon's call, 2026-08-21, on GH #690.  An earlier iteration of
that PR added a canonical =Semi-urban= value to =lsms_library/data_info.yml=;
*it was withdrawn*, and the file is byte-identical to =development= again.

*What is NOT true, and must not be re-cited.*  The fold was originally argued
from GLSS2's own 7-way =rural= table in
=1988-89/_/categorical_mapping.org=, whose =Classification= column puts
=Small Town -> Rural=:

- that =Classification= column is *editorial*.  It is *not on the
  questionnaire*; someone added it (GH #692).
- the table is not a settlement ladder for the household at all.  It decodes
  =Y01C:NRCPL= -- a *non-resident child's* place of residence, GLSS2 Household
  Questionnaire Section 1 Part C, column 13 (GH #692, PR #696).

So the 7-way =rural= table does *not* "already answer the folding question for
the middle of its ladder".  Do not cite it for the fold.  (The survey *does*
have something to say -- just not there.  See the next heading.)

*What the survey says.*  GLSS1's own sample design defines the three tiers by
*settlement population*.  Verbatim, from the joint Basic Information Document
covering both rounds, §3.1 "Sample Design",
=1987-88/Documentation/technical document/GHA_1987_GLSS_Basicinfo_EN.pdf=,
PDF page 13, printed page 11:

#+begin_quote
A final concern was that all three of the country's ecological zones (coastal, forest and savannah), and each of urban, semi-urban and rural areas (population greater than 5000, 1500 to 5000, and less than 1500, respectively) form the same proportion in the sample as they do in the national population.
#+end_quote

The same sentence appears, word for word, in three further files this
repository ships -- so the numbers are not resting on one transcription:

| File                                                                      | Where                                        | Text layer     |
|---------------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------|
| =1987-88/Documentation/technical document/GHA_1987_GLSS_Basicinfo_EN.pdf= | §3.1, PDF p. 13 / printed p. 11              | plain          |
| =1988-89/Documentation/technical document/GHA_1988_GLSS_Basicinfo_EN.pdf= | §3.1, same text                              | plain          |
| =1988-89/Documentation/ddi-documentation-english-4.pdf=                   | "Sampling" -> "Sampling Procedure", PDF p. 7 | plain          |
| =1987-88/Documentation/GLSS 1987-88 Report.pdf=                           | "Sampling Procedure"                         | CID -- decoded |

So =SU= is a real, *defined* tier -- and the fold is being made across a
boundary the survey drew itself.

*Band boundaries.*  The BID's "1500 to 5000" is inclusive-ambiguous at both
ends.  The GLSS3 main report sharpens it, =1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3report.pdf=,
note to Table 7.3, PDF p. 75 / printed p. 57 (repeated under Table 7.9, printed
p. 61):

#+begin_quote
Note:  Small rural localities are those with a 1984 population of less than 1500.  Semi-urban localities are those with a 1984 population of at least 1500 but less than 5000.
#+end_quote

Read together: rural < 1500 <= semi-urban < 5000 <= urban.  Note the one
inconsistency, quoted rather than reconciled: the BID says urban is "greater
than 5000", GLSS3's wording implies urban is *at least* 5000.  The population
is the *1984 Census* population of the locality, not anything measured by the
survey.

*What this does and does not settle.*  It is a *size* band, and no
GLSS1/GLSS2 document says in words "semi-urban is rural".  What it does
establish is narrower and still useful: *the survey's own urban threshold is
5,000, and the whole semi-urban band sits below it.*  So =SU= is not
ambiguous relative to the survey's definition of urban -- it is, by that
definition, not urban.  That is an argument about the fold's *direction*, and
a far better one than the withdrawn =Classification= column.  It is not a
statement that GSS intended =SU= to be reported as rural.

/Corrected 2026-09-02 (GH #705)./  "The survey's own urban threshold" above
means *GLSS1/GLSS2's* -- and GLSS3's and GLSS6's -- not the programme's:
GLSS4 set its urban threshold at *1,500* and folded the same 1,501--5,000
band onto *urban*.  See /The urban cutoff MOVES across rounds/ under
/Sampling Design/ for the round-by-round table and what it does to the
delivered =Rural= column.

Three further pieces of evidence point the same way.  None is a GLSS1/GLSS2
sentence saying "semi-urban is rural"; take them for exactly what they are.

- *GLSS1's own report never uses a semi-urban category, and its three
  locality buckets exhaust the sample.*  =1987-88/Documentation/reports/GHA_1987_GLSS_Firstyear_Report_EN.pdf=
  (GSS, August 1989) reports throughout by LOCALITY = ACCRA / OTHER URBAN /
  RURAL / COUNTRY (Table 1, printed p. 6; Figure 3, printed p. 8), and its
  Method section, printed p. ix, describes the very design quoted above as
  "Stratification criteria were urban/rural and ecological zones."  Table 1's
  sample sizes are Accra 1,393, Other Urban 3,956, Rural 10,143 -- summing to
  *15,492*, which is exactly the BID's GLSS1 individual count.  The
  semi-urban clusters are therefore inside one of those three, and the report
  does not say which.
- *The arithmetic says which.*  This is *inference from published totals*, not
  a quote.  Urban = 65 clusters (BID Table 1a, below) against 5,349 urban
  persons is 82 persons per cluster, and rural+SU = 111 clusters against
  10,143 persons is 91 -- both near the overall 15,492/176 = 88.  Counting SU
  as urban instead gives 100 clusters at 53 persons and 76 clusters at 133, a
  2.5x spread that a design of 16/32/48-household workloads cannot produce.
- *GSS itself performs this exact collapse one round later.*  In the GLSS3
  main report, Tables 7.3 (printed p. 57) and 7.9 (printed p. 61) nest
  "Semi-urban" and "Small rural" as the two sub-rows of a *Rural* total,
  opposite Urban (Accra, Other urban); the prose reads "Within rural areas,
  incomes were rather higher in semi-urban areas than in small rural areas"
  (printed p. 61); and printed p. 85 lists "Accra/other urban/rural" and
  "Accra/other urban/semi-urban/small rural" as alternative breakdowns of one
  locality axis -- i.e. rural = semi-urban + small rural.  Same statistical
  office, same 1984-Census thresholds, *different round*: GLSS3's sample is
  not GLSS1/GLSS2's and its locality variable is not Appendix I's =UrbRur=.

*What was searched, so the negative is falsifiable.*  Searched for any
statement assigning =SU= to a side of a two-way split: the 1987 BID, the 1988
BID, the GLSS1 DDI (decoded end to end -- the glyph map is partial, 48 of 84
cids, but it resolves every letter and digit in the terms searched), the GLSS2
DDI, the GLSS3 main report, and the GLSS1 First Year Report (its text layer
plus rendered pages ix, x, xi, 6, 8).  In GLSS1/GLSS2 documentation every
*classification* keeps three tiers: the definition sentence, BID Table 1a
(printed p. 13), the =IMPRENT= regression dummies "rural, semi-urban, coastal,
savannah" (printed p. 22), and the master-sample ordering "by region within
each location (rural/semi-urban/urban)" quoted under /Oversamples and
sub-samples/ above.

One phrase in that corpus *is* two-way, and it is not a classification -- flagged
so the next reader does not mistake it for one.  The GLSS1 DDI's Overview says a
"price questionnaire was used in both urban and rural areas", and the BID says
supervisors completed community questionnaires "for almost all rural, most
semi-urban clusters and one urban cluster" (PDF p. 10 / printed p. 8).  Both describe *which
clusters got which instrument*; the BID's version, which is the more specific of
the two, names semi-urban separately.  Fieldwork coverage, not a settlement
taxonomy.

*Not* searched, and so
establishing nothing: the bodies of the image-only GLSS1/GLSS2 documents
beyond the pages named (the First Year Report's text layer is *front matter
only* -- 9 of 115 pages carry any text; the Preliminary Report has 93
characters, i.e. none), and the questionnaires, which are image-only.

/Provenance of the quote./  The GH #601 sweep

/How the CID file was decoded, 2026-08-22, in case it is needed again./  The
PDF embeds one subset font (=1E1ba2Arial=, 88 glyphs, =Identity-H=, no
=cmap=, no =post=, no =ToUnicode=), so =pdfminer= emits =(cid:NN)= per glyph
*but preserves real spaces and newlines*.  That makes it a plain
monoalphabetic substitution with word boundaries intact.  Solve it by sliding
long known cribs over the stream and keeping placements consistent with a
partial bijection; a 60-character crib pins a placement uniquely.  The
sentence under test was *excluded from the crib set* so that finding it is
evidence and not assumption.  Two checks that the decode is not echoing its
cribs: the digit =5= is pinned independently by "August 5, 2008" on the cover
and by the table-of-contents page sequence 14/15/16; and the decoded GLSS1
DDI reads "The methodology that was *used* reflects the purpose of the
survey", where the BID and the GLSS2 DDI both read "that was *chosen*" -- a
divergence a crib-echo could not produce.

*Corroboration of the wiring (not of the direction).*  BID Table 1a, titled as
printed "Survey Coverage / Number of Clusters in Household, Community, and
Price Surveys by Region" -- but its rows are *localities*, not regions, and
the column totals are corroborated by the prose beneath it (102 and 71

| Round             | Urban | Semi-Urban | Rural | Total |
|-------------------+-------+------------+-------+-------|
| Year 1 (1987-88)  |    65 |         35 |    76 |   176 |
| Year 2 (1988-89)  |    56 |         31 |    83 |   170 |

The delivered fold's cold counts (below: 111 Rural / 65 Urban and 114 Rural /
56 Urban) reconcile with this *exactly* -- 76+35 = 111, 83+31 = 114, Urban
unchanged in both rounds.  That is a check on the Appendix I classification

1a itself keeps the three tiers.

*What the fold costs, stated plainly.*  The U/R/SU distinction *is lost* in the
delivered =Rural= column.  It is not recoverable from =sample()= or from

*Where the wiring lives, and why it is shaped that way.*  =urbrur_abbrev= in
=_/appendix_i_clusters.org=, read explicitly by
=ghanalss.py::appendix_i_cluster_attributes= with two asserts (non-empty dict;
no unmapped abbreviation).  Deliberately *not* a =Code=-keyed
=categorical_mapping.org= table: it is never auto-applied, cannot collide with

=rural= categorical table (measured: it makes 1987-88 and 2005-06 inherit a

** Cluster counts, and a vocabulary trap in the documentation

Post-fix cluster counts are exactly the distinct =CLUST= values in the source:
*176* (1987-88) and *170* (1988-89).

Do *not* read these as an exact match to the DDI's realised figures of 178 and
170 "sampling areas".  The same DDI sentence defines the terms apart: "Each
group of 16, 32 or 48 households within a sampling area is referred to as a
cluster in the GLSS data sets".  A sampling area may hold more than one
cluster.  Observed households per cluster (1987-88: min 10, median 16, max 48;
1988-89: min 14, median 16, max 47) match that design.  Treat the agreement as
corroboration, not identity.

** GLSS1/GLSS2 =.DAT= files are COMMA-SEPARATED WITH A HEADER ROW, not fixed-width

The =.DCT= dictionaries look like Stata =infile= dictionaries and give
=start-column / width / type / name= for a fixed-width layout.  *The shipped
=.DAT= does not use that layout.*  =Y09A.DAT= line 1 is
=CID,CLUST,YRTITLND,HID,ACRFRM,...= and every row is comma-delimited.  Reading
one with =pd.read_fwf(colspecs=...)= built from the =.DCT= "succeeds" and
returns garbage -- shredded comma-strings spread across the wrong columns, with
the numeric tail silently all-NaN and =nunique=0=.  Use the =.DCT= only as the
authoritative *variable list*.

*** RESOLVED in the library reader (2026-08-22, GH #704)

=get_dataframe= now reads a =.DAT= through its sibling =.DCT= and *detects*
the layout rather than assuming one, so callers no longer need the
=pd.read_csv(..., na_values=['.'])= workaround this section used to
prescribe.  See the =.DAT=/=.DCT= block comment in =lsms_library/local_tools.py=
for the detection rule.  Two things it fixes that were live here:

1. *=.= is Stata's ASCII missing marker, and nothing was honouring it.*  A
   single =.= in a numeric column silently demoted the whole column to
   =str=.  Across the corpus's 531 =.DCT=-paired =.DAT= files, 449 gained
   correctly-typed columns.
2. *Worse than a =str= column: a MIXED =int=/=str= object column.*  Pandas
   infers dtypes per chunk, so a bare =read_csv= on a large =.DAT= with a
   =.= in it assigned =int64= to some chunks and =object= to others -- and
   the same code then appeared *twice*, once as =318= and once as ='318'=.
   Measured on =1987-88/Data/Y12A.DAT=: =FOODCD= has *61* real food codes
   and one =.=, and the old reader reported *123* distinct values
   (61 ints + 61 strings + the dot).  =FOODBLV= 2 -> 5, =MFOODBLY= 12 -> 25.
   This is the =food_acquired= item code =j= for both GLSS1 and GLSS2.  A
   groupby on such a column silently splits every key in two.  *This one
   was not previously recorded anywhere.*

*** Why this belongs with Traps 1-4

Same signature as the categorical-decode traps above: *a thing that exists,
looks authoritative, and silently does nothing (or does the wrong thing)
while the build reports success.*  Trap 1 was a lookup dict that came back
empty; Trap 4 was a fallback table nobody had ever exercised; the
=#+name:= trap was an Org keyword attaching to the wrong element.  Here it
is a *data dictionary that is wrong about its own file's layout* -- and,
underneath it, a missing-value marker that no reader was reading.  In every
case the failure is silent and looks exactly like success.

The general rule these keep re-teaching: *when a declaration and the data
disagree, the declaration is not automatically the loser -- but neither is
it automatically the winner.  Test one against the other and fail loudly
when they cannot be reconciled.*

* GLSS3 and GLSS4 ship at =.dta= format 105, and their missing doubles are 2^333

*This is a reader fact, not a data defect, and the library already handles it.*
Recorded here because it looks exactly like corruption if you meet it cold, and
one sweep (PR #704) already did.

Every =.DTA= in =1991-92/= (155 files) and =1998-99/= (156 files) carries the
=.dta= format stamp *105* -- Stata 5.  In format 105 and earlier, Stata's
system-missing (=.=) for a *=double=* is *2^333* = =1.7498005798264095e+100=
(IEEE-754 =0x54C0000000000000=).  Stata 6 (format 108) changed it to 2^1023 =
=8.98846567431158e+307=, which is the code everyone recognises.  Measured in
these two waves: *8,680,566 cells* across *1,873 =double= columns* in *126
files*.

- =pandas.io.stata.StataReader= recodes it (the =_format_version <= 105= branch
  of =_do_convert_missing=), so =get_dataframe()= returns =NaN= and the
  delivered tables are correct.  Verified: 0 delivered cells carry the value.
- *=pyreadstat= does not.*  Any script that reaches for =pyreadstat.read_dta=
  directly on a GLSS3/GLSS4 file gets =1.7498e100= where the answer is
  "missing", and it will pass every =notna()= / =isna()= check you write.
- *The pandas fix is in pandas 3.0 only* (PR #59325).  =pyproject.toml= still
  says =pandas >=2.2=.  On pandas 2.2.x, =food_acquired.Price= for 1991-92
  (=S8H.DTA:s8hq14=, 9.8 % sentinel) has a mean of *1.71e99* instead of *342.6*,
  and =household_roster.MonthsAway= (=S1.DTA:s1q22=) is sentinel for *50.6 %* of
  the 20,403 roster rows.

Do *not* "fix" this with a sentinel list: the value is a legitimate,
type-and-format-specific missing code that the supported reader already
understands, and a second copy of that rule here would diverge from it.  The
discriminator is *format stamp <= 105 AND storage type =double=*, never the
survey year.  GLSS1/GLSS2 (=COMM.DAT= etc.) carry *none* of it; GLSS5/6/7 are
format 113+ and carry none either.

Full audit: =slurm_logs/sentinel_audit/FINDINGS_1.7498e100.org=.

** The grain is (t, v, j, u, obs) -- three vendors per item, in every wave

Every form records up to THREE observations per item: the 1987 form's "1ST /
2ND / 3RD OBSERVATION" (KG + PRICE), BID §2.3 "Prices from up to three
vendors are collected", GLSS3/4/5/6 "1ST / 2ND / 3RD OBSERVATION" (KG or
KG/LITRE + PRICE; DESCRIPTION + 1ST/2ND/3RD PRICE for non-foods), GLSS7's
a/b/c PRICE / QTY / UOM.  Malawi's and Nigeria's =community_prices= are
=(t, v, j, u)=; Nigeria =.first()=-s same-cell duplicates.  Here the three
observations are kept as rows under =obs= (core does not aggregate), so
GhanaLSS departs from the precedent by one index level.  =obs= 1-3 is the
form's slot; larger values occur only where the source holds more than one
record for a (cluster, item, unit) and are enumerated deterministically
(interview month, then description/brand, then slot).  A fourth cause is the
=j= axis itself: where several price items fold onto one label (Nescafe tin
+ sachet -> =Coffee=; the five chocolate drinks -> =Chocolate Drink=, 5 x 9
rows = =obs= 45 in 1998-99) the rows stack under one (v, j, u) and
=Description= tells the items apart.  Share of rows at =obs= <= 3: 1987-88
98%, 1988-89 97%, 1991-92 90%, 1998-99 88%, 2012-13 87%, 2016-17 81%.

An observation slot with no reported price is not a row (an empty slot is
not a reported price).  Slots dropped, per wave: 1987-88 1,262 of 17,844
(7.1%); 1988-89 2,265 of 14,907 (15.2%); 1991-92 and 1998-99 none (the files
are already one row per observation); 2012-13 191,223 of 479,466 (39.9% --
second and third vendors are often blank, and so are non-food items a
market does not sell); 2016-17 31,458 of 252,366 (12.5%).

- 1988-89: two clusters were priced twice -- 2305 in June AND September
  1989 (a genuine repeat visit), 2310 twice in one month (25 of its 48 item
  pairs byte-identical).  Cluster 2726's rows carry =YRINT=88, MOINT=3=: a
  first-year price carried into the second-year set (BID p.17: "price data
  are available from the first year for 43 additional clusters" -- only this
  one materialises in the shipped file).
- 1991-92 / 1998-99: the files are already long (one row per observation)
  and carry no slot; typically three rows per (clust, item), but 546 / 744
  (clust, item) pairs carry six and 8 / 108 carry nine.
- 2016-17: several brand lines per item and the two mis-keyed clusters below.

** What is and is not in the file, per wave

- *1987-88 / 1988-89.*  =PRICE.DAT= is comma-delimited with a header (Trap
  above; the reader handles it).  Per (CLUST, ITEMNO): =QUANn=/=PRICEn= for
  n=1..3, =MOINT=/=YRINT= (the price interview, "may not correspond to the
  date of the household interview", BID §6.2), and =PRICEnU= (=PRICEn/QUANn),
  =DEFL= (Appendix E CPI) and =PRICE= (mean of PRICEnU / DEFL) -- CALCULATED
  fields, verified (99.1% / 99.4% of rows reproduce them) and NOT stored.
  1987-88 has =TYPRES= (BID's 7-way location type); 1988-89 does not.  BID
  §2.3: "Weighing scales were used to determine the exact weight of food
  items", and the file confirms the form's bases item by item: eggs =QUAN=1=
  (priced each), the evaporated-milk tin =0.17=, the four drugs 3-4 tablets,
  akpeteshie =0.25= (the "1 quarter" bottle), every DESCRIPTION item =1=.
  1988-89 carries one extra code 48 (QUAN median 0.5, PRICE1 120) on no form
  we hold: kept as "Unidentified price item 48".
- *1991-92 / 1998-99.*  =G3PRICE.DTA= / =G4PRICE.DTA= ship ONLY the per-unit
  value (=p= / =price= = PRICE/KG -- values like 500/7 = 71.4286 give it
  away); the weighed KG is not distributed and the files carry no labels
  (GLSS3/4 Stata 5 files, as noted above).  =NumberOfUnits= is therefore the
  form's stated basis per item (=Basis= in =harmonize_price_item=).  =time=
  in G3PRICE is the survey month (251 of 256 clusters have exactly one
  value), not a visit counter.
- *2012-13.*  Foods: =s1stkg=/=s1stpx= etc. -- the KG/LITRE figure and the
  price per observation.  Whether a figure is kg or litres is NOT in the file;
  the =Unit= column of the 2012-13 table records a per-item judgement
  (liquids in litres).  Non-foods: the reader's free-text basis =s2desc=
  (3,437 distinct strings: "20PCS", "20 PIECES", "20 PICES" ...) -> =u= is
  =Other Unit= and the text goes to =Description=.  The =.dta= value labels
  (=fcode= 1-102, =nfcode= 103-157) are the coding of the shipped file; the
  PRINTED GLSS6 form lists 89 + 47 items under a different numbering (its 006
  is "Bread - sugar bread" where the file's 6 is; its 016 "Chicken" is the
  file's 16; but 017 "Game birds" is nowhere in the file and the file's 17 is
  crustaceans).  Decode from the labels, never from the printed form.
  Prices are stamped per EA but were collected at 41 market numbers (323
  region/district/market keys for 1,015 EAs; up to 20 EAs share one key, and
  EAs sharing a key carry identical prices -- =s1stpx= has one distinct value
  within 18,854 of the 32,000 non-null (key, item) cells).  The cover's
  =remarks= say why: "KOKOLIGU HAS NO MARKET AND THEY USE THAT OF NANDOM".
  Kept native; a consumer who wants market-grain prices dedups on
  (region, district, market).
- *2016-17.*  =ln= is the item code (644 values), =bname= the item,
  =itname= the brand line (1,207 values), =price{a,b,c}= / =quantity{a,b,c}= /
  =unit{a,b,c}= / =unito{a,b,c}=.  The unit codes carry the file's OWN value
  labels: the =unit_9b= list, except code 72 = "Service" (=unit_9b= says
  Visit) and an extra 75 = "Visit".  Decode from the price file's labels.
  *=quantity{a,b,c}= is NOT always a count of =unit{a,b,c}= (2026-09-08).*
  The form is =PRICE / QTY / UOM= per observation with QTY entered by the
  enumerator, and for CONTAINER units the enumerator often entered the
  container's *content weight in grams* instead of a count.  Decisive case:
  =Millet=, =u='Bowl'=, clusters 70735 / 70789 / 70791 -- =Price = 4.00=,
  =NumberOfUnits = 4000=, against a =food_acquired= unit value for
  Millet/Bowl in Northern of *exactly 4.00 GHS*.  =Price= is the price of one
  bowl; the 4000 is the bowl's weight.  So the column contract's
  "=NumberOfUnits= = the quantity, in =u=, that =Price= refers to" is *false
  for these rows*, and =Price / NumberOfUnits= returns ~1/1000 of the true
  per-unit price (a log gap of +8.3, a ratio of 4,050).  Affects *5,351 of
  220,908 rows (2.4%)*; top units Yard 1,729, Piece 801, Inches 304, Single
  294, Watts 262, Feet 254, Capsule 240.  Nothing is wrong in
  =community_prices= -- it stores the reported fields, which is its contract
  -- so the handling belongs in the consumer.  The constant-free screen used
  by =slurm_logs/price_sources/GhanaLSS/analysis.py= is: keep an observation
  when =u= is a metric mass/volume unit (QTY is then unambiguously a quantity
  of =u=) or when =NumberOfUnits == 1= (the two readings coincide).  It is
  not lossless -- a genuine crate of 24 eggs goes with it -- and recovering
  those rows by reading =Price= as the per-unit price needs a row-by-row
  judgement (QTY = 2 on a Bowl is ambiguous).
  *A weaker form of the same disease affects BOTH instruments in this wave*:
  metric-prefix miscoding.  Beer at =u='Liter'= priced 0.0080 GHS/L in the
  price file against 15.15 GHS/L in =food_acquired= (Eastern); Chicken
  (thigh) at =u='Gram'= carrying a =food_acquired= unit value of 5.00 GHS
  *per gram* (Ashanti); Oil (palm) at =u='Milliliter'= priced 5.33 GHS per ml
  in the price file against 0.0125 in =food_acquired= (cluster 70014).  37%
  of matched 2016-17 unit-value-vs-community-price cells have a log gap above
  =log 1.5= as a result.  Not fixable in config; it is enumerator error in
  the source.
  ALL 398 price =clust= values are in =g7sec0.dta='s 1,000 EAs, but the price
  file's =region=/=district= contradict the household cover for 11
  (clust, region) pairs -- clusters 70002 and 70909 hold a second EA's rows
  under region 5 (cover: 1 and 10), and 70195/70210/70211/70247/70327/
  70747/70906/70908 are stamped with a wrong region for their single set of
  rows.  =v= is =clust= regardless (the cover is authoritative for region);
  the duplicated content shows up as =obs= 4-6.  Phase 3b may want to drop
  70002 and 70909.

** =j=, =u= and the shared axes

No wave's price code scheme is its consumption (=Code_9b=) scheme -- 0 of 644
GLSS7 codes and 5 of 102 GLSS6 codes are label-identical -- so =j= is decoded
by NAME through a per-wave =harmonize_price_item= table (=Code | Label |
Preferred Label | Food | Unit | Basis | Note=) appended to each wave's
=categorical_mapping.org=, onto that wave's =harmonize_food= Preferred Label
where the axis names the item (=Food=yes=), the later waves' spelling where a
food is not on the wave's axis (=Food=own=: 1987-88 has no Bread / Pepper /
Juice / Guinea corn / specific-oil labels; 1998-99 no Cocoyam leaves / Tomato
paste / Snail), and an own label for non-foods -- made unique per code (=Orange [ln 771]= is
a toilet roll, =Apple [ln 812]= a wrist watch, =Sony [ln 640]= a corded
phone, =Sony [ln 669]= a radio) wherever a non-food name is also a food
label, recurs across blocks, or is an "OTHER (SPECIFY)" line: 80 such codes
in 2016-17, none elsewhere.  Where the axis folds two
price items together (Goat (fresh) + Fresh mutton -> =Goat=; Onion +
Shallots -> =Onion=; palm wine -> =Wine=, pito -> =Beer=) both are kept and
=Description= tells them apart.  The overlap with =food_acquired().j= was
checked against the =harmonize_food= tables, not a built =food_acquired=
(the corpus's largest table; not built on this slice).

=u= is the =_/unit_labels.org= Preferred Label.  Labels the price surveys
need that the axis lacks: =Tablet=, =Capsule=, =Bottle= (the 1980s/1990s
drug bases) -- reported, not added (=unit_labels.org= is not in this task's
ownership).  While there: =unit_labels.org= maps "Margarin tin" to
=Margarin tin= and "margarin tin" to =Margarine Tin= -- two Preferred Labels
for one spelling pair.

** What =food_acquired.Price= on produced rows MEASURES (2026-09-08)

Section 8H's price column -- =s8hq14= (1991-92, 2005-06), =s8hq10= (1998-99,
2012-13), =s8hq{i}p= (2016-17), all of which land in
=food_acquired.Price= on =s='produced'= rows -- is worded identically across
GLSS3--GLSS7:

#+begin_quote
For how much could you sell one unit of......now?
#+end_quote
(GLSS3, =1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3QPartB.pdf=, Section 8H col. 14; col. 13
is =ENTER UNIT THAT THE RESPONDENT CHOOSES / SEE LIST OF UNIT CODES=.)

#+begin_quote
For how much would you sell one unit of ..............now?
USE MOST OCCURING UNIT OF MEASURE
#+end_quote
(GLSS6, =2012-13/Documentation/QUESTIONNAIRES/GLSS6 Part B Questionnaire.pdf=;
GLSS7, =2016-17/Documentation/GLSS7_MODULE B _14_10_16.docx=, Section 8H Q9.)

It is therefore a *seller-side* valuation by wording.  *Measured, it is not
one.*  In 2016-17 -- the only wave where GhanaLSS also has a purchase unit
value, because every earlier wave elicits purchases by VALUE -- the median
log gap between the household's purchase unit value and its own-consumption
valuation, over *322 matched (item, unit, geography) cells*, is *exactly
0.0000*, and *31.7% of cells agree to the last digit*.  Households answer
with the local retail price they know.  *Anyone valuing own-consumption in
GhanaLSS at =food_acquired.Price= and calling it a producer or farmgate price
is mis-labelling a purchase price.*

Two further properties of that column, both measured on 2012-13
=s='produced'=, =u='Kg'=: it is *very coarse* -- essentially three values,
0.5, 1.0 and 2.0 GHS/kg, respondents rounding to coin denominations -- and it
is *not* on the same lot size as =community_prices=, which weighs a retail
portion: 0.11 kg for pepper and 2.85 kg for yam, so the surveyed per-kg price
of a perishable is a small-portion price and a staple's is a bulk price.  The
market/own-valuation ratio in that wave consequently runs from 0.59 (yam) to
12.0 (pepper) *by lot size and perishability, not by price level*.

The unit is also the one *the respondent chooses*, which is why the =u= axes
of the two instruments barely meet before 2016-17: shared units cover 7.0% of
1991-92's produced rows, 3.5% of 1998-99's and 11.2% of 2012-13's, against
91.5% in 2016-17.  The price survey weighs (BID §2.3 /"Weighing scales were
used to determine the exact weight of food items"/; the GLSS3 price form's
column is =KG=); Section 8H does not.  *That is a property of the two
instruments, not a mapping defect, and no crosswalk table fixes it.*
