* Overview

Niger has four waves in the LSMS Library: two pre-EHCVM national
surveys (2011-12, 2014-15) and two EHCVM waves (2018-19, 2021-22).

* Sampling Design

EHCVM convention (2018-19 and 2021-22): =grappe= is the cluster
identifier (=v=); each grappe is visited in exactly one =vague=, so
household identifier is =(grappe, menage)= without a vague level.
See CLAUDE.md "EHCVM countries" for the cross-country pattern.

Pre-EHCVM waves (2011-12, 2014-15) follow their own conventions;
see the wave-level =data_info.yml= files for index definitions.

* Sampling universe, exclusions and sub-samples

What each Niger wave's *own documentation* says its sample represents.  This
records claims, not data: no =.dta= file was opened for it, and nothing here is
reverse-engineered from the microdata.  Where a wave has no statement, that is
recorded as a gap together with what was searched.

Read this alongside =Sampling Design= above, which is about *identifiers*
(=grappe= / =menage= / =vague=); this section is about *what population the
sample claims to stand for*.  Evidence gathered 2026-07-21 for GH #601; the
full cross-country sweep, with the search record for every wave, is
=slurm_logs/POPULATION_STATEMENTS_2026-07-21.org= (GH #603 will decide how the
library represents any of this -- nothing here proposes a schema).

** 2011-12 -- ECVM/A 2011 (WB catalog 2050)

There is *no DDI =UNIVERSE= field* for this wave, in the shipped DDI report or

statement is the "target population" sentence inside the Sampling Procedure,

panel-specific tracking rule bounds who could enter the 2014 sample at all

** Oversamples and sub-populations

*No oversample, booster sample, or special sub-population sample is documented
for any Niger wave* in the sources listed above.  Niger has nothing of the
Serbia/Montenegro MOP-Roma or Azerbaijan three-sub-population shape.

Four structural facts do have to be known before pooling, none of which is an
oversample:

1. *Stratification is not oversampling, and nothing says which it is.*  The
   2011-12 / 2014-15 design uses 26 explicit strata built from Niamey, other
   urban, agricultural, agro-pastoral and pastoral zones.  Pastoral and
   agro-pastoral zones are named strata; *no source consulted states whether
   the allocation across them was proportionate or disproportionate*, and that
   was not checked against the data.  Recorded as unknown.
2. *2014-15 is a whole-wave panel*, not a fresh draw: it re-interviews the 2011
   households under the tracking rule quoted above.  Split-offs are carried
   (the wave's household key is =GRAPPE + MENAGE + EXTENSION=, with
   =EXTENSION= 0 = non-mover, 1 = whole household moved, 2 = split-off
   individual in a new household).
3. *2018-19 and 2021-22 each pack two seasonal visits into one library wave.*
   For 2018-19 this is quoted verbatim above (each EA split in half, visited
   Oct-Dec 2018 and Apr-Jul 2019).
4. *2021-22 mixes panel households with replacements inside a single wave.*
   The sweep records, from BID section 4 and the catalog Sampling Procedure --
   *as summary, not as a verbatim quote, so treat it as secondary* -- that the
   same clusters are revisited, the 2018/19 households re-interviewed where
   found, and the cluster topped back up to 12 households where fewer are
   found; final sample 6,622 households (2,511 urban, 4,111 rural), visit 1 =
   3,303 HH (17 Nov 2021 - 13 Feb 2022), visit 2 = 3,319 HH (3 Jun - 31 Aug
   2022).  So a 2021-22 row is either a panel household or a top-up
   replacement, and the documentation gives no flag distinguishing the two.

** Membership rule (adjacent evidence, deliberately not claimed as Niger's universe)

The =Household Presence / MonthsSpent= section below records that the EHCVM
instrument establishes household membership through two binary six-month
filters (=s01q12=, =s01q13=) rather than a continuous months-present variable.
That is the *operational* membership rule standing behind the phrase "de jure
households".

The WAEMU-common EHCVM-2 enumerator manual states that rule and one further
scope exclusion explicitly.  *That manual is shipped in Burkina Faso's wave
directory, not Niger's* --
=lsms_library/countries/Burkina_Faso/2021-22/Documentation/manuelagent_ehcvm2_version_v2.pdf=,
printed p. 4 (PDF p. 7).  It is regional instrument boilerplate (its worked
examples name Dosso and Niamey, i.e. Niger), which is exactly why it is
recorded here as *adjacent evidence* and not as a Niger universe statement:

#+begin_quote
Attention : le personnel diplomatique ne fait pas partie du champ de l'enquête. En effet la résidence d'un diplomate est considérée comme extraterritorial, c'est-à-dire que juridiquement hors du pays concerné. Cependant les autres personnes travaillant dans ces organisations font partie du champ de l'enquête, tout comme les étrangers n'ayant pas de statut de personnel diplomatique.
#+end_quote

Translation (ours, not source text): "Note: diplomatic personnel are not part
of the scope of the survey.  A diplomat's residence is considered

other people working in these organisations are part of the survey's scope, as

#+begin_quote
Membre du ménage. Un membre du ménage est une personne résidant habituellement dans le ménage. ** Pooling caution

The declared universe *changes across this country's own series*, so pooling
all four Niger waves pools more than one declared population:

- 2011-12 and 2014-15 :: national minus certain Arlit (Agadez) strata, minus
  collective housing -- a documented, quantified geographic-plus-population
  subtraction.
- 2018-19 :: "all de jure households" minus institutional populations, with no
  geographic subtraction stated -- and the statement is NADA boilerplate.
- 2021-22 :: no declared universe at all.

Nothing in the documentation reconciles these, and nothing here reconciles them
either.

* Household Presence / MonthsSpent

The EHCVM questionnaire (used in the 2018-19 and 2021-22 waves) does
NOT ask "How many months did [NAME] live here in the past 12 months?"
(the continuous MonthsSpent/MonthsAway variable available in Uganda,
Ethiopia, Tanzania, Malawi, and many other LSMS countries).  Instead,
EHCVM uses two binary eligibility filters:

- =s01q12= :: "Has [NAME] lived continuously in this household for at
  least 6 months?"
- =s01q13= :: "Does [NAME] intend to stay at least 6 months?"

These determine membership eligibility but do not give a continuous
duration measure.  Downstream analysts who need a presence filter for
=household_characteristics= should use these binary variables directly
rather than expecting a =MonthsSpent= column in =household_roster()=.

The pre-EHCVM waves DO have a continuous months-of-presence variable:

- 2011-12 :: =ms01q19= in the household roster file records the
  number of months the individual spent in the household over the
  past 12 months.  See the wave-level =data_info.yml= for details.
- 2014-15 :: =MS01Q19= in the household roster file records the
  number of months the individual spent in the household over the
  past 12 months.  See the wave-level =data_info.yml= for details.

* The =u= sentinel: a quantity recorded with no unit (GH #842 / #847)

Niger's enumerators sometimes recorded an amount and left the unit
blank.  =u= is a DECLARED index level in four Niger tables, so a null
there is not a harmless gap -- it is a *deferred silent deletion*.

** Why a NaN =u= is worse than a wrong =u=

The row is served, so no build-time guard fires: the null-read audit
watches for an all-null declared COLUMN, and the grain audit watches a
collapse that has already happened.  Neither looks at a partly-null
declared INDEX LEVEL.  The row then vanishes in whichever =groupby=
runs first -- =_finalize_result='s canonical de-dup, =Feature()=
assembly, or the consumer's own aggregation -- because pandas
=groupby(dropna=True)= drops NaN keys.  Nothing counts the loss, and
it is attributed to the consumer's code.  That is #847's framework
half; this section is Niger's country half.

*THE COUNTS ARE NOT WHAT THE ISSUE SAID, AND THE REASON IS WORTH
KNOWING.*  #842 measured 441 NaN-=u= rows in =crop_production= by
reading the delivered table.  That is only the rows that SURVIVED.
Measured in the wave parquets instead, 5,663 rows carried a NaN =u=
(2011-12 3,150; 2014-15 1,079; 2018-19 443; 2021-22 991 -- a further
34 already carried the =Manquant= label, 11 + 23 in the two ECVMA
waves); the difference is whether the wave's index happens to be
unique.
2018-19's =(t, i, plot, crop, u)= index IS unique, so no collapse runs
and its 441 nulls were served.  The other three waves' indexes are
non-unique -- deliberately so, one (plot, crop) can carry several
reported harvest lines -- so the collapse ran and DELETED every
NaN-keyed row before anyone saw it.  Whether a defect is visible was
decided by an accident of index uniqueness.

** The sentinel

Every missing unit is now filled with =Unknown= at build time, by one
helper (=niger.py=, =fill_missing_u= / =U_NA=) shared by all four
tables.  =Unknown= is the CORPUS-canonical spelling, not a Niger
invention: =lsms_library/data_info.yml= defines it as the non-ordinal
sentinel for don't-know / refused / unmappable, and Uganda's
=crop_production= already writes it onto this same =u= level.  Niger
used to write the French =Manquant=; that spelling is retired.

The relabel runs through =_/categorical_mapping.org='s =#+name: u=
table, which maps =Manquant= / =manquant= / =produit absent= /
=Produit absent= onto =Unknown=.  That table is read on BOTH paths --
at build time by =local_tools.get_categorical_mapping= and at read
time by =Country._apply_categorical_mappings= -- so Niger emits
=Unknown= directly AND a pre-#842 parquet still holding =Manquant= is
relabelled on the way out.  The two alternatives were measured and
both fail: a =spellings:= block on =u= in the canonical
=data_info.yml= is read as a CLOSED vocabulary by
=diagnostics._check_declared_spellings= and would fail every real unit
corpus-wide, and a row in the global =categorical_mapping/u.org=
cannot win because =_row_union_categorical= keeps the COUNTRY row on a
key collision.

** =community_prices= is the one table that still DROPS instead of filling

Measured on the raw CS07 grids: 795 price-bearing observations in
2011-12 (218 in passage 1, 577 in passage 2) and 0 in 2014-15 carry a
usable price with no unit value at all -- and *0 of the 795 carry a
usable =Quantity= either*, so there is no weight to fall back on.
=community_prices.Price= is
defined as the price for =Quantity= units of =u=, so such a row has no
basis to be a price OF anything -- unlike a crop line, where the
reported harvest Quantity is still a fact worth serving with an
=Unknown= unit.  =_finish_community_prices= therefore keeps dropping
them, and the drop is now COUNTED in the code comment rather than
being invisible.  Revisiting it is a data decision about what a
community price is, not a sentinel decision.

The asymmetry with =crop_production= is therefore not arbitrary: a
crop line keeps a REPORTED harvest quantity that stays meaningful with
an unknown unit, while these 795 rows have neither a unit nor a
weight.

One conflation is recorded here because it is real and lives only in
this table: in the CS07 unit list the missing-unit marker and the
product-absent marker are the same kind of answer (=manquant= /
=produit absent=), and both now resolve to =Unknown=.  Everywhere else
in Niger =Unknown= means only "amount reported, unit not recorded".
=niger.py='s =_COMMUNITY_MISSING_UNITS= keys on that label and MUST
move with it -- leaving it spelled =Manquant= would have silently
stopped dropping the product-absent rows.

** Consumer rule

=u == 'Unknown'= is NOT a unit.  Exclude it before any per-unit price,
quantity or conversion, and REPORT how many rows you excluded.
Keeping it silently yields a price per nothing; dropping it silently
is the bug the sentinel exists to make visible.  Note this interacts
with the harvest-unit / sold-unit mismatch recorded under "Prices"
below: a =crop_production= row whose harvest unit is =Unknown= can
never be matched to its sold unit, so it drops out of any
=Value_sold / Quantity_sold= analysis on that ground too.

Only the four =u= tables change data.  No other country's cache hash

** FIXED: the 2011-12 hours formula divided by 52 where it should divide by 12

=Niger/2011-12/_/people_last7days.py= built =farm_hrs= / =SB_hrs= /
=wage_hrs= as =(month * day * hour) / 52= per job.  The three inputs, read
from the Stata variable labels of =ecvmaind_p1p2.dta= in *both* the French
and the English-labelled copy (=ecvmaind_p1p2_en.dta=) and confirmed against
questionnaire =ECVMA_Quest_MEN_P1_V10_ENG.pdf= section 4 part B, are:

| var                | question | meaning                                        |
|--------------------+----------+------------------------------------------------|
| =ms04q29= / =q55=  | 4.29     | months this job was done in the last 12 months |
| =ms04q30= / =q56=  | 4.30     | hours per *day* usually devoted to this job    |
| =ms04q31= / =q57=  | 4.31     | days per *WEEK* usually devoted to this job    |

So =d * h= is *already* usual hours per week.  Dividing =m * d * h= by 52
treats =d= as days per MONTH and delivers =(months/52) x usual weekly hours=
-- 0.23x the intended figure for a full-year job.  The correct annual-average
week imputes the exact calendar 52/12 weeks per month (2011-12 does not ask
weeks-per-month; 2014-15's =MS04Q26= does):

: annual hours = m * (52/12) * d * h  ->  weekly average = / 52  ==  (m*d*h)/12

*This is a deliberate departure from World Bank parity.*  =NER_ECVMA1.do:1413=
has =gen av_hours1 = (month_job1 * hour_job1 * day_job1) / 52 // (week average
of hours)= -- the same error, with a comment asserting the dimension it does
not have.  =NER_ECVMA2.do:1454= (2014-15) is =(month * week * hour * day)/52=
and is coherent.  Parity with the WB panel is not the same as being right.
