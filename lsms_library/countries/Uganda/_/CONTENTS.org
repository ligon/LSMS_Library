* Overview

The Uganda National Panel Survey (UNPS) is conducted by the Uganda
Bureau of Statistics (UBOS) with support from the World Bank
Living Standards Measurement Study (LSMS).  It builds on the
Uganda National Household Survey (UNHS) 2005/06 baseline.

- World Bank catalog :: https://microdata.worldbank.org/index.php/catalog/1001

* Sampling Design
** Overview

Two-stage stratified cluster sample.  Enumeration Areas (EAs) are
the primary sampling unit (PSU), drawn from the 783 EAs visited in
the UNHS 2005/06.  The UNPS selected 322 EAs covering 3,123
households.

The UNPS uses two visits per year (six months apart) to capture
both agricultural seasons.

*** Why 2018-19 / 2019-20 =v= is a =DISTRICT/PARISH= composite (GH #323)

Those two waves have no usable parish /code/ — =parish_code= carries
only 19 distinct values for 751 parishes, and the YAML has flagged it
BROKEN for a while — so =v= was set to the free-text parish /name/.

*Parish names are not unique in Uganda.*  20 of 2018-19's 751 names and
23 of 2019-20's 793 recur across districts /as coded/; =CENTRAL= alone
appears in TEN (HOIMA, KAGADI, KALUNGU, KIRYANDONGO, KYOTERA, MITOOMA,
…).  Keying on the bare name fused genuinely different parishes into a
single "cluster", whose =Region= / =District= / GPS were then resolved
by an undeclared =groupby().first()= — i.e. arbitrarily.

Qualifying the name by its district makes =v= an actual cluster key:
751 → 781 clusters (2018-19) and 793 → 824 (2019-20).  Both =sample()=
and =cluster_features()= build =v= from the same pair via =mapping.v()=
in each wave's =_/=, so the two cannot drift apart —
=_join_v_from_sample= hands one key to every other Uganda table.

**Read this before quoting "clusters spanning more than one district:
20 → 0 and 23 → 0".**  That number is *zero by construction* under a key
that contains the district.  It is a check that the key was applied, not
evidence that the key is right, and it structurally cannot see the
opposite error — *over-splitting*, where one real parish becomes two
clusters because a household coded the wrong district.  The audit that
can see it uses the survey's own administrative path as an independent
witness: group GSEC1 by =(county, subcounty, parish)= and count distinct
=v=.  Results:

- *2018-19: 0* — all 20 of its splits are genuinely distinct parishes.
- *2019-20: 3*, now resolved by a three-entry =_V_ALIASES= table in
  =2019-20/_/mapping.py=:

  | county   | subcounty | parish     | district as coded    | hh    |
  |----------+-----------+------------+----------------------+-------|
  | BUSIKI   | NAMUTUMBA | NAWANSAGWA | NAMTUMBA / NAMUTUMBA | 1/10  |
  | KASSANDA | KITUMBI   | KIJUNA     | MUBENDE / KASSANDA   | 1/9   |
  | OMORO    | BOBI      | PALWO      | GULU / OMORO         | 2/8   |

  =NAMTUMBA= is a misspelling (one household in the whole wave).
  Kassanda District was carved out of Mubende in 2019 and Omoro out of
  Gulu in 2016, and a straggler household still codes the /parent/.  In
  all three the two groups agree on =region= as well, so this is one
  place recorded two ways.  Four households would otherwise sit in a 1–2
  household phantom cluster instead of the 10–11 household cluster they
  belong to: 827 → *824* clusters.  The alias is keyed on the
  =(district, parish)= /pair/ — Mubende and Gulu are still real,
  populated districts here (29 and 37 households), so a bare district
  rename would be wrong.  =District= is declared row-valued in that
  wave's =cluster_features= and resolved through the same table, so the
  merge does not trade three over-splits for three =<NA>= districts.

  Two further district misspellings in 2019-20 (=PALISA= for =PALLISA=,
  =RUKINGIRI= for =RUKUNGIRI=) and one in 2018-19 (=PALISA=) are
  deliberately *not* aliased: the correctly-spelled district holds no
  household in the affected parish, so they split nothing.  Cosmetic
  label warts, not grain defects.

Deliberately *not* keyed any finer.  Adding =subcounty_name= splits 10
further groups (2018-19) and 25 (2019-20), but the ones examined are
spelling artefacts of a single subcounty — =NYENGA= / =NYENGA DIVISION=
(centroids 0.0 km apart), =LUBAGA= / =RUBAGA DIVISION= (5.3 km),
="KAGADI  TOWN COUNCIL"= / ="KAGADI TOWN COUNCIL"= (a doubled space) —
so it would fragment real parishes on data-entry noise.  (32 of
2019-20's 824 clusters still span more than one county/subcounty
/spelling/ for this reason.  One of them, =KAKUMIRO/KIKWAYA=, looks like
a genuine within-district parish-name collision rather than a spelling
artefact; it is untouched here and unquantified across the other waves.)

Note the source misspells "district" as =distirct_name= in 2018-19.

*** Hybrid =v= in 2009-10 (movers and split-offs)

The 2009-10 =comm= column is labeled /Current EA Code as in 2005/06
Data/: it is the /original 2005-06/ sampling-frame EA, not a new
2009-10 code.  That convention makes sense for panel continuity
(every /original/ 2005-06 household keeps its EA across waves) but
leaves two groups of 2009-10 households without a usable =comm=:

| =hh_status= | count | why =comm= is blank                                     |
|-------------+-------+---------------------------------------------------------|
| 2 (mover)   |   192 | Left the original EA; no 2005-06 code applies.          |
| 3 (split-off) | 368 | Did not exist in 2005-06; no 2005-06 code applies.     |
| 1 (orig. non-mover, anomaly) | 5 | Isolated data-entry gap (HHIDs =3163001601..1608=). |
| Total       |   565 | 19% of 2 975 interviewed households.                    |

These 565 households are not a data bug — by survey design they are
/outside the 2005-06 sampling frame/ that =comm= encodes.  They do,
however, carry modified GPS coordinates from =UNPS_Geovars_0910.dta=
(=lat_mod=/=lon_mod=, jittered ±2 km urban / ±5 km rural, with a
=source= flag distinguishing current-location from origin-location
fixes), so a within-wave spatial identifier can still be built.

To avoid silent data loss in downstream tables (the old behaviour
dropped these 565 households in =roster_to_characteristics='s
=groupby=), =sample()='s =v= column in 2009-10 is populated by a
hybrid rule:

- =v = comm= (7-digit numeric string) when =comm= is non-blank —
  2 410 households, 317 distinct panel EAs.  Matches CSECTION1's
  =comm= index exactly, so community-questionnaire joins work as
  they always did.
- =v = "@{lat:+.2f},{lon:+.2f}"= (a /synthetic/ label prefixed with
  =@=, binned to a 0.05° grid ≈ 5.5 km) when =comm= is blank but
  coordinates are available — 541 households, ~339 distinct bins.
  These labels do /not/ match any CSECTION1 =comm=, by design: the
  community questionnaire was not administered at the movers' or
  split-offs' 2009-10 locations, so a non-match is the correct
  outcome of a join.
- =v = <NA>= when neither =comm= nor coordinates are available —
  24 households, the refreshment / geo-missing tail that is also
  reported in the =UNPS_Geovars_0910= docs as /Invalid: 24/.

Implementation: in =data_info.yml= the =sample:= block uses the
=dfs:=/=derived:=/=drop:= pattern to merge GSEC1 and the geovars
file, then dispatches to the =coalesce_coord_bin= transformer in
=lsms_library/transformations.py=.  The 0.05° grid resolution and
=@= prefix are documented defaults on the transformer and can be
overridden in the YAML if another wave or country needs different
values.

=cluster_features= now does the same thing (GH #323).  It previously did
NOT: =sample= built the synthetic labels but =cluster_features= kept the
raw blank =comm=, so all 565 out-of-frame households were deleted
outright by the =(t, v)= =groupby(dropna=True)= and *339 =(t, v)= pairs
present in =sample()= had no =cluster_features()= row at all*.  Its YAML
block now declares =v= as a /myvar/ with =final_index: [t, i]= — the
=derived:= dispatcher runs after =set_index(final_index)=, so =v= has to
still be a column when the transformer sees it — and invokes the SAME
=coalesce_coord_bin= transformer on the same =lat_mod=/=lon_mod= with the
same defaults.  That makes the labels identical to =sample='s by
construction rather than by coincidence, which is the point: the two
=v='s MUST agree or =_join_v_from_sample= hands the rest of the library a
key that matches nothing.  Orphan count is now 0 for 2009-10.

The 24 households with neither a =comm= nor a GPS fix keep =v = <NA>= and
are dropped — with a warning naming the count.  Nothing can locate them,
and a fabricated cluster would be worse than a missing one.

Why only 2009-10 gets the hybrid treatment: 2010-11 and 2011-12
also use =comm= from GSEC1, but their raw surveys populated it for
every household (movers and split-offs got re-coded with their
original-sample EA), so no fallback is needed.  From 2013-14 onward,
=v= is a parish identifier — every household has one — and the
issue disappears by construction.

*** HH-level =Region= on =sample()=

Since =v= can be a synthetic =@lat,lon= label (§above) that has no
entry in =cluster_features()=, consumers that needed =Region= used to
pull it from =cluster_features()= and silently lose those households
— the cluster-level table is keyed on real EA codes and cannot answer
for synthetic ones.  =sample()= now exposes =Region= directly as a
household-level column for every Uganda wave (it reads the same
=region= field that =cluster_features()= does, with the same Kampala
→ Central fold in 2009-10 and 2010-11 where applicable).

Practical consequence: =Country('Uganda').sample()[['v','Region']]=
is complete for every household; joining =Region= from =sample()=
— rather than =cluster_features()= — avoids dropping the 565
movers/split-offs from any =(i, t, m)=-keyed regression where =m=
is derived from Region.  Recommended pattern:

#+begin_src python
uga = ll.Country('Uganda')
d = uga.household_characteristics()
# Region = market at HH level, available for all HH including movers
d = d.join(uga.sample()[['Region']])
d = d.rename(columns={'Region': 'm'}).set_index('m', append=True)
#+end_src

Continue to use =cluster_features()[['Region']]= when you want the
cluster-level view (e.g., summarising EAs); use =sample()[['Region']]=
when you want every surveyed household covered.

** Rotating panel and refreshment sample

Beginning in 2013-14, one-third of the original sample is refreshed
each wave.  Households participate for three consecutive waves, then
rotate out.  This creates two sub-populations within each wave:

- Continuing panel :: Households tracked from a prior wave.  These
  receive a /longitudinal/ (panel) weight.
- Refreshment sample :: Newly drawn households entering the sample.
  These receive a /cross-sectional/ weight but no panel weight (or
  a zero/NaN panel weight).

The =rotate= variable in GSEC1 distinguishes the two groups (=rotate=1=
for continuing panel, =rotate=0= for refreshment) in waves that
have it (2013-14, 2015-16).

*** Weight construction (from BID)

The panel weight construction follows an 8-step procedure
(BID Section 6, available at catalog 1001):

1. Begin with 2005 UNHS base weights (=hmult=: inverse probability
   of selection with EA-level non-response correction).
2. Incorporate probability of sub-selection from UNHS into UNPS
   (varies by stratum: 100% for Kampala, 36--43% for others).
3. Incorporate probability of selection into tracking (for split-offs).
4. Fair-share correction for composition changes (factor of 1/2 for
   households receiving new members from other households).
5. Pool the component weights.
6. Derive attrition-adjusted weights via logistic model on individual
   covariates (gender, age, marital status, education, etc.).
7. Trim extreme weights.
8. Post-stratify to known population totals.

The cross-sectional weight (=wgt_X=, =h_xwgt_W5=) is a positive
weight for all sample and non-sample members of interviewed UNPS
households, including refreshment sample households that have no
panel history.  The panel weight is zero or NaN for non-sample
individuals and refreshment households.

For cross-sectional analysis within a single wave, use =weight=.
For longitudinal/panel analysis across waves, use =panel_weight=
(and restrict to households where it is non-null and positive).

*Note (2026-09-09).*  EPAR (Evans School, UW, TR#335) rescales survey
weights to World Bank rural/urban population totals per wave, and
weights its median-price ladders with that raked weight, so EPAR's
published price/value LEVELS are not comparable to ours (within-wave
mean-one) -- only ratios and shares are.  See
=slurm_logs/2026-09-09_epar_curation/EPAR_PROJECT.org=.

** Sampling universe, exclusions and sub-samples

Evidence gathered 2026-07-21 under GH #601.  Every quote below was
re-verified against the cited PDF at the cited page.

Transcription conventions, so a later reader can tell quote from edit: runs
of whitespace introduced by PDF justification are collapsed to single spaces;
the source's typographic double quotes (around "wave", "tracking", "core",
"target tracking") are transcribed as ASCII straight quotes; the =fi=/=ff=
ligatures in the 2015-16 DDI are transcribed as ordinary ASCII letters; an
en-dash in the 2019-20 BID is transcribed as "-".  *No words were changed,
added, or reordered*, and nothing is elided except where marked with =...=.

*** There is no declared universe for any Uganda wave

No document for any of the eight waves carries a DDI =Universe= or "target
population" field.  Searched, per wave: every local Basic Information
Document and DDI PDF under =countries/Uganda/{wave}/Documentation/=,
converted in full with pdfminer and grepped for =universe=, =target
population=, =coverage=, =scope=, =excluded=, =exclusion=, =nomadic=,
=institutional=, =sampling frame=; plus the World Bank catalog
study-description pages for ids 1001, 2166, 2059, 2663, 3460, 3795 and 3902.
=universe= returns *zero hits* in all of them.

What exists instead, in every wave, is a *coverage claim* plus
sampling-procedure prose:

- a DDI =Geographic Coverage= field reading =National= (2009-10, 2018-19) or
  =National coverage= (2010-11) or =National Coverage.= (2015-16); and
- one recurring sentence about a "nationally representative sample of
  households".

Two things follow, and both matter when pooling Uganda with other countries.

1. *This is not the World Bank NADA boilerplate.*  The sentence that appears
   verbatim in 15 waves of the #601 corpus -- "The survey covered all de jure
   households excluding prisons, hospitals, military barracks, and school
   dormitories." -- does *not* appear anywhere in Uganda's documentation.
   Uganda's recurring sentence is UBOS/LSMS-ISA text specific to the UNPS.
   So it attests a real survey-programme claim, not a metadata template.

2. *But it is copy-forward within the UNPS series.*  The same "carried out
   over a twelve-month period ... on a nationally representative sample of
   households" sentence, and the same paragraph of sampling procedure around
   it, is reprinted essentially unchanged from 2009-10 through 2019-20 --
   and the 2010-11 BID says so outright: "Refer to the UNPS 2009/10 Basic
   Information Document for detailed information on the UNPS survey design"
   (=UNPS_2010-11_Basic-Info-2014.pdf=, sec. 1.2, PDF p. 3).  There are
   therefore effectively *two* independent coverage statements here -- one
   for the UNHS 2005/06 baseline and one for the UNPS series -- not eight.
   Treating agreement across waves as corroboration would be double-counting.

*Gap, recorded as a gap.*  No Uganda document found states an
institutional-population exclusion (prisons, hospitals, barracks, school
dormitories) or a nomadic / homeless / collective-quarters exclusion.  That
is *not* evidence that no such exclusion applies -- it is evidence that the
question is unanswered in the documents this repo holds.  See the search
list at the head of this subsection for exactly what was looked at.

*** Exclusions

**** IDP camps -- the one geographic/population exclusion, and it reverses

The UNHS 2005/06 baseline *included* the internally-displaced population, by
adding a sampling stage for it (=UNHS.2005-06.Interviewer.Manual.FINAL.pdf=,
"SURVEY DESIGN", PDF p. 4):

#+begin_quote
However, in some districts of Northern Uganda where large populations have
been displaced by the LRA war and are living in Internally Displaced People
(IDP) Camps, a three-stage sampling design will be adopted, with the
selection of the IDP as the first stage unit, the respective 'blocks' within
the camp as the second stage selection and the households within the blocks
as the third stage units of selection.
#+end_quote

The UNPS, which subsamples the UNHS 2005/06 frame, then *drops* those extra
EAs.  The wording drifts across waves; each is quoted as printed:

| Wave    | Verbatim                                                                                                                          | Source                              |
|---------+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------|
| 2009-10 | Since IDP camps are now mostly unoccupied, the extra EAs in IDP camps are not a part of the UNPS subsample.                        | DDI 1001, Sampling, PDF p. 17       |
| 2010-11 | -- no exclusion statement; see gap note below                                                                                     | --                                  |
| 2011-12 | Since IDP (internally displaced people) camps are now mostly unoccupied, the extra EAs in IDP camps are not a part of the UNPS subsample. | BID sec. 1.2, PDF p. 6       |
| 2013-14 | Since IDP camps are now mostly unoccupied, the extra EAs in IDP camps are not a part of the UNPS subsample.                       | BID sec. 1.2, PDF p. 6              |
| 2015-16 | Since most IDP (Internally Displaced People) camps in the Northern region are currently unoccupied, the EAs that constituted IDP camps were not part of the UNPS sample. | DDI 3460, Sampling, PDF p. 4 |
| 2018-19 | (same sentence as 2015-16)                                                                                                        | catalog 3795, Sampling Procedure    |
| 2019-20 | Since most IDP (Internally Displaced People) camps in the Northern region are currently unoccupied, the EAs that constituted IDP camps were not part of the UNPS sample. | BID sec. 1.2, PDF p. 7 |

Read plainly: from 2009-10 onward the UNPS frame is the UNHS 2005/06 frame
*minus* its IDP-camp EAs.  The documents justify this by the camps being
"mostly unoccupied" -- they do not claim the formerly-displaced population is
picked up elsewhere in the frame, and they do not quantify what was dropped.
Anyone using UNPS Northern-region estimates for the post-conflict period
should carry that carve-out explicitly.

defined as the last time that household was interviewed - in this case Wave

4(2013/14); and/or wave 3 (2011/12); and/or, Wave 2 (2010/11). If they were
last interviewed in Wave 0 or Wave 1, they were excluded from the survey at
this time.
#+end_quote

Wave 0 is the UNHS 2005/06 baseline and Wave 1 the UNPS 2009/10.  This is a
panel-following rule rather than a statement about the target population, but
it *bounds who can appear in 2019-20*: a household last seen in 2005/06 or
2009/10 is out of scope for Wave 8 by design, not by attrition.

**** Persons excluded from tracking

Same source, sec. 4.1 (PDF p. 32), describing the rule in force through
Wave 7:

#+begin_quote
Split-offs could originate from both original and shifted households, though
the only household members eligible for target tracking as split-offs were
the household head and individuals related to them such as spouse, biological
children, parents of the head or spouse, etc. (codes 1-7 of Section 2
Question 4 in the household questionnaire). Servants, other relatives and
non-relatives (codes 8-96) were not tracked.
#+end_quote

and, for Wave 8 (PDF p. 33):

#+begin_quote
In those households, the only individuals marked for tracking are the
previous wave's household head, spouse, and children over age 15. Other
household members were not tracked beyond their known location from the
previous wave - they were only included in interviews if they still lived
with one of these "core" members.
#+end_quote

Consequence for =household_roster= / =household_characteristics=: servants,
non-relatives and distant relatives who leave a UNPS household cannot form an
in-sample split-off household.  Relationship categories are therefore *not*
followed symmetrically over the panel.

*** Oversamples and sub-samples inside a wave

Uganda's specialisation lives *inside* waves, not as whole waves.  The
documented sub-samples, in descending order of how much they should change
what a reader does with the data:

**** 1. Ten districts oversampled by the UNHS 2005/06, deflated in the UNPS

=2011-12= BID sec. 1.2 (PDF p. 6) and =2013-14= BID sec. 1.2 (PDF p. 6),
identical wording:

#+begin_quote
Within each stratum, the UNPS EAs were selected from the UNHS 2005/06 EAs
with equal probability, and with implicit stratification by urban/rural and
district (in this order), except for the rural portions of the ten districts
that were oversampled by the UNHS 2005/06. In these districts, the
probabilities were deflated, to bring them back to the levels originally
intended.
#+end_quote

Restated more vaguely from 2015-16 onward (=2015-16= DDI Sampling, PDF p. 4;
=2019-20= BID sec. 1.2, PDF pp. 6-7): "However, the probabilities of selection for the
rural portions of ten districts that had been oversampled by the UNHS 2005/06
were adjusted accordingly."

*Gap.*  *The ten districts are never named* in any document searched (all
eight waves' BIDs/DDIs plus the 2005/06 interviewer manual, grepped for
=oversampl=).  So the oversample is attested but not reconstructible from
what the repo holds; it is absorbed into the weights and cannot be identified
in the data from documentation alone.

**** 2. The 20% "target tracking" sub-sample (waves 1-4)

Two households per EA -- 20% of the sample -- were designated for tracking
movers, and *only those* were followed outside their original EA.
=2019-20= BID sec. 4.1 (PDF p. 32):

#+begin_quote
Prior to the UNPS 2009/10 field work, 20% of households (two per EA) were
randomly selected for purposes of tracking individuals that had moved from
original locations since UNHS 2005/06. These were the only households tracked
even if they had moved beyond their original EA/parish, they maintained the
"target tracking" status during Wave 2 (2010/11), Wave 3 (2011/12) & Wave 4
(2013/14).
#+end_quote

Same fact in the =2011-12= and =2013-14= BIDs, sec. 1.2 (both PDF p. 6):
"Prior to the start
of the 2009/10 field work, 2 UNPS households were also randomly selected in
each EA for the purposes of tracking baseline individuals that moved away
from original locations since the UNHS 2005/06."

And the asymmetry between movers and split-offs (=2019-20= BID sec. 4.1,
PDF p. 32):

#+begin_quote
In prior waves, movers were tracked and interviewed if they moved somewhere
else within Uganda, even if they were not selected as part of the target
tracking sample. ... In prior waves, split-offs were only tracked and
interviewed if they moved to somewhere else within Uganda and were previously
selected as part of the target tracking sample.
#+end_quote

*This is the sharpest sub-population fact in the Uganda record.*  Through
Wave 4 the two groups enter on different terms: a whole household that
shifted was followed regardless, but a split-off was eligible for interview
only if its origin household was one of the two per EA in the target-tracking
sample -- i.e. split-offs enter through a 20% sub-frame.  The documents do
not quantify the resulting under-representation, and this section does not
either.  It bears directly on the 565 movers and split-offs documented above
under "Hybrid =v= in 2009-10 (movers and split-offs)": those households are
outside the 2005-06 sampling frame, and the split-offs among them (368 in
2009-10) reached the sample through that sub-frame.  Do not read split-offs
in waves 1-4 as a representative sample of new household formation.

**** 3. The one-third rotating refresh (2013-14 onward)

=2015-16= DDI, "SAMPLE REFRESH" (PDF p. 4):

#+begin_quote
Starting with the UNPS 2013/14 (Wave 4) fieldwork, one third of the initial
UNPS sample was refreshed with the intention to balance the advantages and
shortcomings of panel surveys. Each new household will be visited for three
consecutive waves, while baseline households will have a longer history of
five or six years, given the start time of the sample refresh. This same
sample was used for the UNPS 2015/16 (Wave 5) Once a steady state is reached,
each household will be visited for three consecutive years, and at any given
time one third of the households will be new, one third will be visited for
the second time, and one third for the third (and last) time. The total
sample will never be too different from a representative cross-section of the
country, yet two-thirds of it will be a panel with a background of a year or
two.
#+end_quote

(Sentence-boundary punctuation after "(Wave 5)" is missing in the source;
quoted as printed.)  New households were drawn from a *different frame*:
"New households were identified using the updated sample frames developed by
the UBOS in 2013 as part of the preparations for the 2014 Uganda Population
and Housing Census." (same page).  The =2013-14= BID sec. 1.2 anticipates
this with the 2012 census: "parts of the sample will start to be replaced by
new households extracted from the updated sample frames developed by the UBOS
as part of the 2012 Uganda Population and Housing Census."  The refresh
therefore mixes two sampling frames within a wave.

*TENSION with "** Rotating panel and refreshment sample" above.*  That
section states, without qualification, "Beginning in 2013-14, one-third of
the original sample is refreshed each wave."  The documentation does not say
that for 2015-16.  It says the refresh happened at Wave 4 and that "This same
sample was used for the UNPS 2015/16 (Wave 5)", and it puts the each-wave
one-third rotation in the future conditional -- "Once a steady state is
reached".  So *each wave* is the design intent; whether it was achieved
between W4 and W5 is contradicted by the DDI's own next sentence.  Neither
claim should be deleted; the existing text should be read as the design and
this quote as what the DDI reports actually happened.

*Bears on the open TODO "Missing panel weight for 2019-20".*  That LOGBOOK
entry reads "Appears to be panel-only (no refreshment sample). Confirm with
UBOS documentation."  The 2019-20 BID does *not* support "no refreshment
sample" -- sec. 4.1 (PDF p. 33) treats the refresh as operative: "The one-third of the
original sample households that have been rotated out as part of the panel
refresh are no longer tracked or interviewed at all." and "The new one-third
of the sample rotated in as part of the panel refresh is not tracked beyond
their location indicated during the listing exercise in late 2013."  But that
second sentence dates the listing to *late 2013*, i.e. the Wave 4 refresh, so
this passage is plausibly carried forward from an earlier BID rather than
describing Wave 8 fieldwork.  Recorded as evidence bearing on the TODO, *not*
as resolving it -- the identical-=wgt= observation in the data is not
explained by anything found in the documentation.

**** 4. The agriculture sub-sample (all UNPS waves)

The Agriculture Questionnaire is not administered to the whole wave.
=2013-14= BID sec. 1.2 (PDF p. 7) and =2019-20= BID sec. 1.2 (PDF p. 7), in
the instrument list:

#+begin_quote
Agriculture Questionnaire, (administered to the subset of UNPS households
engaged in agricultural activities)
#+end_quote

Any table sourced from the agriculture instrument (=crop_production=,
=plot_features=, =plot_inputs=, =plot_labor=, =livestock=) is therefore

*** Universe versus what the weights represent

The 2013-14 BID is unusually candid that the UNPS is trying to be two things
at once, and that the sample is a compromise between them
(=2013-14/Documentation/Basic Information Document.pdf=, sec. 4 "UNPS 2013/14
Panel Weights", PDF p. 16):

#+begin_quote
The UNPS has two broad analytic goals. The first is to track the same sample
of people from 2005/06 to 2013/14 to see how their lives have changed. The
first goal is met by revisiting the same cases in each wave. Two-thirds of
the original panel households (3200 household) were followed up in 2013/14
wave. The second is to provide a cross-sectional snapshot of the Ugandan
household population. The second goal, however, necessitates the
incorporation new households and individuals in each wave to account for the
changing population. The UNPS addresses this conflict by allowing for the
inclusion of a sub-sample of split-off households in the wave and refreshing
a third of the sample.
#+end_quote

and, on which weight answers which question (same document, sec. 4, PDF
p. 22):

#+begin_quote
For cross-sectional estimates of population dynamics based only on the UNPS
2013.14 data, the data users must use the variable wgt_X as part of GSEC1.dta
of the UNPS 2013/14 package for household weight. This variable includes
sampling weights for original as well as split-off and refreshed households
and is generated as a result of the procedures detailed above. ... The
variable wgt part of GSEC1.dta of the UNPS 2013/14 package for panel
households was computed only for the interviewed both in 2011/2012 and
2013/14.
#+end_quote

This corroborates the =weight= / =panel_weight= guidance in "** Sampling
Weights" above from the primary source: =wgt_X= represents the Ugandan
household population cross-sectionally; =wgt= represents only the
2011/12-and-2013/14 panel.  They are answers to different questions about
different populations, and the 1145 NaN panel weights recorded in the weights
table are that by construction, not missingness.

Note also the 2015-16 DDI's own hedge, quoted in full above: "The total
sample will never be too different from a representative cross-section of the
country" -- a claim of approximate, not exact, cross-sectional
representativeness, made about a design that had not yet reached its steady
state.

*Numeric discrepancy in the primary sources, recorded not resolved.*  The
size of the UNPS panel is given as *3,123* households in the 2009-10 DDI, the
2011-12 BID, the 2013-14 BID, the 2015-16 DDI and the 2019-20 BID -- and as
*3,200* in the 2010-11 DDI ("Out of the 7,400 households interviewed during
the UNHS 2005/06, 3,200 households were selected for the UNPS"; abstract:
"The survey covered 3,200 households") and in the 2013-14 BID passage quoted
just above ("Two-thirds of the original panel households (3200 household)").
The "** Overview" section above uses 3,123, which is the majority reading;
the 3,200 figure is flagged here so a reader who meets it in the source
documents knows it is a documentation inconsistency, not a second sample.

The =u= table also retains the per-wave variant columns (=2005-06=,

* =people_last7days= counts MEMBERS, not visitors

The question ("how many people were present in the last 7 days?") is
asked separately for household /members/ and for /visitors/.  Through
2013-14 the two live in parallel COLUMNS and the library maps
=Men/Women/Boys/Girls= onto the member block, ignoring the visitor block
(confirmed against the Stata variable labels):

| Wave    | File    | Member columns (used) | Visitor columns (ignored) |
|---------+---------+-----------------------+---------------------------|
| 2005-06 | GSEC14  | =h14q1--4=            | =h14q5--8=                |
| 2009-10 | GSEC15A | =h15a1--4=            | =h15a5--8=                |
| 2013-14 | GSEC15  | =T6FQ01a--d=          | =T6FQ01e--h=              |

From 2018-19 the questionnaire went LONG: the same distinction moved out
of the column suffix and into a ROW category.  =GSEC15A= carries exactly
two rows per household keyed by =CEA01= ∈ {=Household members=,
=Visitors=}, and =CEA01A--D= are the counts /for the selected category/.
The extraction was never updated — =CEA01= sat commented out in the YAML

That is a coin flip, and it lost: 1,636 of 3,242 households (50.5%) in

2.77 → 4.53 (2019-20), measured at source; 4.60 / 4.55 through the API
after the panel-id walk.

Members-only is not a judgement call — it is what the three earlier
waves above already do.  If you want "people fed" = members + visitors,
that is a DIFFERENT table and should be declared as one.

* =cluster_features= is a PROJECTION, and it says what it could not resolve

Every Uganda wave extracts =cluster_features= from GSEC1, the household
COVER PAGE, so the frame is one row per HOUSEHOLD while the table is
declared at =(t, v)= — a 4--10× inflation.  The reduction to cluster
grain is intended (GH #161); until GH #323 it was *undeclared*, so it
fell through to the framework's =groupby().first()=.  That reducer skips
NA per column, so where the households of a cluster disagree it does not
even return one of their rows — it assembles a composite from the first
non-null value of each column independently, a "household" that appears
nowhere in the survey.

The projection is now performed explicitly by the country-level hook
=uganda.cluster_features()=, which hands it to =reduce_to_agreed=
(=lsms_library/build_transforms.py=).  Its contract is /lossless or
loud/:

- households that AGREE collapse to the agreed row, silently;
- a household that reports nothing where another reports a value is an
  ABSENCE, not a contradiction, so the observed value survives.  This is
  most of the apparent damage: 2005-06 lost 2,551 rows to the Site-1
  audit but has ZERO clusters whose households actually disagree on
  =Region= / =Rural= / =District=;
- households that genuinely DISAGREE get =<NA>= in the offending column
  plus a =GrainConflictWarning= naming the clusters.

*Consumers will now see =<NA>= where they previously saw a value*, and in
the two =comm= panel waves it is a third of a column, not a handful of
cells.  Measured on the returned table:

| Wave    | =District= | =Region= | =Rural= | =Latitude= / =Longitude= |
|---------+------------+----------+---------+--------------------------|
| 2010-11 | 27.8%      | 5.0%     | 4.4%    | 41.2%                    |
| 2011-12 | 33.0%      | 7.8%     | 0.0%    | 49.8%                    |

That is the point: the old value was an arbitrary pick, or a composite,
among disagreeing households.  Two causes of disagreement remain, and
neither is a bug we can configure away:

1. /Panel dispersal/ — =comm= (2005-06 … 2011-12) is the *2005-06 EA of
   origin*, which the panel carries forward when it tracks a mover.  A
   household that moved district still reports its origin EA, so its
   =District= / =Region= legitimately differ from its EA-mates'.  93
   clusters in 2010-11, 106 in 2011-12.
2. /Sub-cluster variation/ — =v= is a PARISH from 2013-14 on, and a
   parish contains several enumeration areas, some urban and some rural,
   each with its own geovar fix.  =Rural= and the GPS are therefore not
   parish attributes at all: 65 / 122 / 122 clusters conflict on =Rural=
   in 2015-16 / 2018-19 / 2019-20 (70 in 2013-14).

For a per-household answer use =sample()=, which carries exact
household-level =Rural= and =Region= for every household.

*GPS is not averaged.*  Where the within-cluster coordinates vary at all
they vary by a MEDIAN of 4.6--42.9 km and by up to 584 km (measured, all
waves) — that is a broken cluster key, not a scatter about a centroid,
and averaging would smear two places into one and hide the evidence.
Same argument that retired core's =.mean()= on 2026-07-13.  An analyst
who wants a centroid computes one.

Four 2010-11 clusters disappear entirely: their households agree on
NOTHING (=Region=, =Rural=, =District= and both coordinates all
conflict), so every cell is blanked and the framework's
=dropna(how='all')= safety net removes the now-empty row.  42 households
therefore reference a =v= with no =cluster_features= row.  That is the
honest outcome — =comm= is not identifying a place for them — and what
used to fill the gap was a fabricated composite.

* Harvest condition on =crop_production= (GH #323 / #637)

** The defect

The UNPS post-harvest module (AGSEC5A season A / AGSEC5B season B) asks
question 6 as a single compound question: *how much did you harvest, in
what unit, and in what condition/state*.  A household that took some of a
crop off a plot fresh and the rest after drying it answers TWICE.  Both
answers are separate source rows (2009-16) or separate parallel column
slots (2018-20).

=crop_production= was keyed =(t, i, plot, j, u, season)=, with no level
for the condition, so those answers landed on the same index tuple and the
de-duplication block at the end of =uganda.crop_production_for_wave= summed
them.  Dry weight was added to fresh weight.  A measured example from
2011-12:

#+begin_example
HH 1033000506  plot -1-6  Coffee  Kilogram (KG)
   240 kg  "Dry after additional drying - in pods or shell/husks"  month 6
   100 kg  "Fresh/raw harvested - in pods or shell/husks"          month 6
   -> summed to 340 kg
#+end_example

340 is not a quantity of anything.  Across the panel this affected *3 654
index groups*.

The quietest instances were 2009-10 and 2010-11: those waves have no
harvest-month column, so *every* one of their collisions looked like an
exact duplicate of an identical record, while in fact fresh and dry
weights were being added together.

** Fix: =condition= is an index level

The grain is now =(t, i, plot, j, u, condition, season)=.  Labels come
from the =harvest_conditions= table in =_/categorical_mapping.org=, via
=uganda._harvest_condition_map()=; the canonical cross-country vocabulary
is declared in =lsms_library/data_info.yml= under
=Columns > crop_production > condition=.

*Quantities in different conditions are not commensurable.*  Do not sum
across this level without a drying / shelling conversion.  =transformations.harvest_kg=
groups by =(t, i, plot, j)= and therefore still sums across conditions —
that is a *pre-existing* property of that transform, unchanged by this
work, and it remains the caller's problem (its own docstring already warns
that its unit coverage is a lower bound).

*** =KgFactor=: the reported kg rate is now carried

=a5aq6d= / =a5bq6d= ("6d. Record the conversion factor into kg") are carried
into =crop_production= as the *=KgFactor*= column — kilograms per ONE unit of
the row's =u=, declared =optional: true= in =data_scheme.yml=.  A reported 0 is
stored NaN ("not recorded", not "weighs nothing").  This is what keeps 2018-19
season A usable despite having no unit: its kilograms are =Quantity *
KgFactor=.  It is also *non-circular*, unlike the =Expenditure/Quantity=
inference in =transformations._get_kg_factors=.

*What the screened 6d outliers are (measured 2026-09-09, against the
hypothesis that they are total harvests in kg written into the factor box).*
=harvest_kg_factors= rejects 79 of the 14 149 reported 2018-19 factors as
implausible (> 250 kg/unit; 57 in 5A, 22 in 5B).  Of the 79: 12 have
=KgFactor == Quantity= (the kg total written in both boxes; 11 of 12 plausible
as a total); 20 have =KgFactor / Quantity= within 5% of a modal container
weight {10, 12, 15, 20, 50, 90, 100, 120} — but non-screened rows hit that test
15.2% of the time by chance, so only ~8 of the 20 are excess; 13 are calendar
years; 34 fit nothing, and they include the largest values (Sorghum 147 000 on
qty 147, Sim Sim 260 000 on a Kg row of qty 67 — neither a factor nor a
plausible total).  Read as totals, the defensible rows (=== Quantity= or
container ratio, and at or below the crop's p95 plot harvest in other waves)
are *19 rows, 9 440 kg = 0.16%* of the wave's kg; the loosest reading (every
screened row at or below p95) is 38 rows, 17 680 kg = 0.30%.

*The mechanism is confined to the tail.*  The same confusion would be
invisible below the screen (a 120 kg total on qty 2 looks like a 120 kg/unit
factor), so it was sized where the unit is known: on 5B rows with a
weight-bearing label and qty > 1 (4 532), 6d is within 10% of the label weight
on 70.0%; among the other 30.0%, the share with =6d / qty= within 0.5–1.5x the
label weight is 15.4% — *exactly the shuffled-Quantity control* (15.4%, sd
0.7%).  Those "neither" rows are a different plausible per-unit weight (median
0.83, p10–p90 0.4–1.4 of nominal — the sack as weighed, not as labelled), not
totals.  On 5A (unit unknown, qty > 1, sub-threshold; 6 151 rows) 75.1% of
factors are themselves a modal container weight; among the rest, =6d / qty= is
a modal weight 18.7% vs 15.9% under shuffling — an excess of ~42 rows, 0.68% of
5A.  So 6d is a per-unit factor for the body of both sections, and the
total-in-the-factor-box reading explains roughly 20–40 of the 79 screened rows
and almost nothing else.

*Decision: no =reported_total= layer in core.*  A rule that reads an outlier
as a total needs a container weight to check against, and 5A has no unit; the
generic container test fires at 15% by chance; the kilograms at stake are
0.16–0.30% of the wave.  The screened rows keep their raw =KgFactor= on
=crop_production= (verified: all 57 5A =none= rows still carry 6d), so an
analyst who wants the total reading can apply it.  The one narrow rule that
needs no heuristic — =KgFactor == Quantity and > KG_FACTOR_MAX=, read as
=kg = Quantity= (12 rows, 6 720 kg) — is *not* adopted either; it is recorded
here in case the count grows when the other six waves' factors are wired
(#849).

*The 5.90M kg for 2018-19 against 0.6–0.94M in every other wave is coverage,
not scale*: 99.4% of 2018-19 rows resolve to kg (the reported factor is on
every row) versus 15.7–29.8% elsewhere, where kg exists only where the unit

Added 2026-07-21 after the PR #649 review demonstrated the gap by mutation.

** There are TWO builders for a country table, and they can disagree

The =assets= row above (1.00x, despite =_/assets.py= using the identical
idiom) is not luck.  Chasing it turned up the thing that actually matters,
and it is bigger than =earnings=:

*A country-level =_/{table}.py= is NOT what =Country(...).{table}()= runs.*
=country.py='s =load_from_waves= loops the waves calling =run_make_target(...,
wave=w)= and *concatenates the wave parquets*; the country script is reached
only through =country_fallback = run_make_target(method_name, wave=None)=,
which fires *only when no wave produced anything*.  Verified by planting a
=raise= at the top of =_/earnings.py=: =Country('Uganda').earnings()= returned
22 447 rows without tripping it.  Under =LSMS_BUILD_BACKEND=make= the same
call raises the sentinel -- so the backend switch changes *which builder runs*,
not merely how it is scheduled.

*The Makefile reaches the country scripts by prerequisite, so this is not a
corner case.*  =income= has no wave-level scripts, so it always falls through
to make -- and the =income.parquet= target lists =enterprise_income.parquet=
and =earnings.parquet= as prerequisites.  Building =income= therefore rewrites
both through their country scripts.  Measured, before the fix in this entry:

| =Country('Uganda').enterprise_income()=, cache without it | *13 935* rows |
| the same call, after anything built =income=              | *78 040* rows |

*Same API call, two answers.*  Which builder *runs* is decided by config --
the Makefile prerequisite graph and which wave-level targets exist -- not by
cache state.

*What makes the wrong answer PERMANENT is the hash rule, and it is worth
understanding because the yml config can never detect it.*  =to_parquet=
called from a country script does not stamp =lsms_cache_hash=.  Measured on
a live cache right after an =income()= build had rewritten =earnings= through
its script:

: earnings          lsms_cache_hash=NO     <- written by the country script
: enterprise_income lsms_cache_hash=yes
: income            lsms_cache_hash=yes
: assets            lsms_cache_hash=yes
: sample            lsms_cache_hash=yes
: household_roster  lsms_cache_hash=yes

=cache_freshness= grades a hashless parquet =legacy=, and =legacy= means
*trust once, then stamp* -- its docstring says so explicitly
("trust-once-then-stamp migration of legacy / *script-written* parquets").
Measured:

: before API call: stamped = NO  | rows = 22447
: API returned    : (22447, 1)          <- served as-is, no rebuild
: after  API call : stamped = yes | rows = 22447

The hash it gets stamped with is computed from the *yml/config inputs*
(=data_info.yml=, =data_scheme.yml=, =_/Makefile=, the =*.org= build inputs,
the wave/country modules, the DVC sidecar md5s).  None of those changed --
only the *builder* did.  So the content-hash mechanism is *structurally
unable* to notice, and the artifact grades =fresh= from then on.
=Country._evict_hashless_wave_caches= exists for exactly this hazard but
globs =*/_/{table}.parquet= -- *wave* parquets only.  There is no
country-level twin for =var/{table}.parquet=.

That is how GH #772 reached the reporter as 129 160 rows: the
=var/earnings.parquet= on that machine had been written by the Makefile
chain, served hashless, and stamped =fresh= on the next read.  It is also why =assets= reads clean -- nothing had triggered
=_/assets.py= recently, so what is served is the wave concatenation
(=Value=, =(t, i, j)=), not the script's output (=assets=, =(i, t, m)=).
Those two disagree in *column name and index*, so whichever wrote last wins.

The bare =!make= tag desugars to ={materialize: make}=
(=yaml_utils._construct_make=), identical to what =earnings= declares, so
none of this is a config difference between the tables.

*Not fixed here, because the fix is a design decision, not a patch*: which
builder is authoritative?  Filed as *GH #788*.  Fixing the two Uganda
scripts (this entry) makes the two builders *agree* for =earnings= and
=enterprise_income= -- both now deliver 22 447 and 13 935 by either path,
verified -- but that is convergence by hand, one table at a time, and
=assets= still disagrees with its own script.

*** Mechanism: =a5aq8= / =a5bq8= were DIVIDED BY 100 in this release (2026-09-08, #829)

Investigated against the raw =.dta=, both UNPS questionnaires and the World
Bank catalog.  Three hypotheses were on the table -- (H1) the value was
*recorded* in hundreds; (H2) the sale QUANTITY is on a different unit basis
(quintals) and only looks like a value defect; (H3) the wave script wires the
wrong variable.  *H2 and H3 are dead.  H1 is right in its arithmetic and wrong
in its mechanism: nothing asked for hundreds -- a division by exactly 100 was
applied to the two columns somewhere in data preparation.*

**** The decisive evidence

1. *Storage type.*  =a5aq8= / =a5bq8= are Stata =double= in 2015-16 and
   =int32= in *every* other wave (2010-11, 2011-12, 2013-14, 2018-19,
   2019-20).  A currency answer key is not a double.

2. *=a5aq8= x 100 is an exact integer in 100.00% of rows* -- 3 898 season-A
   and 3 008 season-B sale rows, no exceptions.  And the *roundness ladder*
   of the reconstructed value reproduces its neighbours':

   | fraction of positive values divisible by | 2013-14 raw | 2015-16 raw | 2015-16 x100 |
   |------------------------------------------+-------------+-------------+--------------|
   | 1 (i.e. integral)                        |      1.0000 |      0.9949 |       1.0000 |
   | 100                                      |      0.9988 |      0.6692 |       0.9949 |
   | 1 000                                    |      0.9561 |      0.1481 |       0.9510 |
   | 10 000                                   |      0.6701 |      0.0049 |       0.6690 |

   The raw 2015-16 column is the 2013-14 column shifted *two decades* to the
   right, not a different quantity.  Its non-integer share (0.51% season A,
   0.30% season B) is exactly the share of neighbouring waves' values that
   are NOT multiples of 100 (0.53% / 0.22% in 2011-12, 0.12% / 0.44% in
   2013-14) -- i.e. precisely the rows a divide-by-100 would leave fractional.
   The smallest values are =0.02=, =0.09=, =0.15=, =0.52= UShs, which no
   enumerator typed.

3. *Sold-unit == Kg rows only, where no conversion factor enters at all*:

   | median UGX/kg, u(sold)=Kg | 2010-11 | 2011-12 | 2013-14 | 2015-16 | 2019-20 |
   |---------------------------+---------+---------+---------+---------+---------|
   | season A                  |     700 |    1000 |    1000 |  *10*   |    1333 |
   | season B                  |    1000 |    1000 |    1000 |  *10*   |    1300 |

   and per crop, pooling seasons, with the x100 reconstruction beside it:

   | median UGX/kg | 2010-11 | 2011-12 | 2013-14 | 2015-16 | 2019-20 | 2015-16 x100 |
   |---------------+---------+---------+---------+---------+---------+--------------|
   | Maize         |     300 |     500 |     500 |       5 |     700 |          500 |
   | Beans         |     700 |    1000 |    1000 |      12 |    1400 |         1200 |
   | Cassava       |    1000 |    1000 |    1000 |      12 |    1500 |         1200 |

   | Finger millet |     500 |     700 |     600 |       6 |     800 |          597 |
   | Coffee        |    1000 |    1000 |     640 |      15 |      -- |         1500 |

**** It is not a mixture

The owner's stated worry was that only *some* rows are mis-scaled.  They are
not.  Of 2 268 sale rows whose sold unit is Kg, *99.25% imply a price below
100 UGX/kg* and only *13 rows (0.57%)* reach 130 UGX/kg -- 2013-14's *1st
percentile*.  The whole distribution moved: the p25 / p50 / p75 ratios of
2013-14 to 2015-16 are 100.0 / 100.0 / 100.0 exactly (p10 and p90 are 125 and
120, which is ordinary year-to-year price drift, not a second population).
Wave totals: =sum(a5aq8)= is 1 369 573 070 UShs over 6 490 sale rows in
2013-14 against *16 619 683 over 6 906 rows* in 2015-16.

**** H2 (unit basis) is dead

The sold unit =a5?q7c= disagrees with the harvest unit =a5?q6c= on *4.4%* of
2015-16 sale rows -- LESS than 2013-14's *13.5%*.  And the table above is
computed on rows where the *sold* unit is literally Kg (code 1), so no
conversion factor of any kind is in the arithmetic.  A quintal/kg confusion
also has the wrong sign (it would make the implied price 100x too LARGE).

**** H3 (mis-wired variable) is dead

- The variable *label is byte-identical* across waves: =A5AQ8:What was the
  value?= in 2013-14 and 2015-16 alike; the World Bank catalog's own variable
  record (study 3460, file F15, =V320=) carries the same label with
  =min 0 / max 80000=, i.e. NADA's summary was computed from this same file.
- Value still scales with quantity: the slope of =log(a5aq8)= on
  =log(qty_sold x kg-factor)= is *+0.744* in 2015-16 against *+0.713* in
  2013-14 and *+0.744* in 2011-12.  Shape unchanged; only level moved.
- *The defect is field-specific.*  Of the 432 variables carried by the same
  name in both waves across 16 AGSEC files, *=a5aq8= and =a5bq8= are the ONLY
  two whose median moves by ~100x* (93.6x and 100.0x).  38 of those 432

  only two that go =int32 -> double=*; the only other two that become

  transport?"), a currency field in the same record, is still =int32= at a
  normal level (median 1 000 UShs against 3 500 in 2013-14).

kgs), 50.0 for Sack (50 kgs), 20.0 for Tin (Debe) (20 lts) and 15.0 for
Plastic Basin (15 lts).  Cell detail is genuinely mixed -- =Sack (100 kgs)= vs
=Tin (Debe)= (n=61) has median q7d *100.0*, favouring =u= on 83.6% of rows,
while =Sack (100 kgs)= vs =Plastic Basin= (n=22) favours =Unit_sold= on 54.5%.

*So: =Unit_sold= is the right COLUMN and the wrong RULE.*  On the 96.1% of
rows where the two labels agree nothing is ambiguous.  On the 3.9% where they
disagree, the row's unit reporting is unreliable and the disagreement does not
say which of the two answers is sound -- =Unit_sold= is the closer denominator
*less than half the time*.  Treat those rows as AMBIGUOUS: exclude them, or
resolve them with a reported per-row sold kg factor, and *report how many you
excluded*.  The =data_info.yml= and =data_scheme.yml= notes said "a price per
ONE =Unit_sold= -- NEVER per =u=" when this entry was first written; that
instruction was wrong on the majority of exactly the rows it governed and has
been replaced.

Carrying the column is still strictly better than not: before it, a sale
reported in kilograms was stamped with a =Sack (100 kgs)= label and there was
*no signal at all* that anything was wrong.

*** De-duplication: a disagreeing sold label is blanked, never guessed

The de-duplication block at the end of =crop_production_for_wave= SUMS
=Quantity_sold= across rows that share the whole declared index.  A sum taken
across two *different* sold units is in no unit, so the label columns are
collapsed by =build_transforms.reduce_to_agreed(on_conflict='na')= -- the
same "lossless or loud" reducer Uganda's =cluster_features= hook uses: the
agreed value is kept, a conflicted cell is set NA with a

*Nothing is dropped*; only the label is withheld.  Corpus-wide this is *11 of
130 606 groups* -- 2009-10 6 =Unit_sold=; 2011-12 1; 2013-14 2 =Unit_sold= +
1 =Condition_sold=; 2015-16 1 =Condition_sold=.  Those groups are a

(2026-09-10, from the red-team of this change, item 4).  Measured on the
pre-collapse frames, *433 index groups already carry more than one source
row* (445 rows collapsed away); 422 of them agree on both sold labels and 11

=Value_sold=, and three carry a *different* =harvest_month= (6 vs 9, 6 vs 5).
They are two genuinely distinct harvest+sale records that happen to share
=(t, i, plot, j, u, condition, season)= -- so by the CLAUDE.md rule
("duplicates on a declared index mean the IDENTIFIER IS BROKEN or a LEVEL IS
MISSING -- fix the index; do not declare a reducer") the right long-run fix
is a *within-(plot, crop, unit, condition) record ordinal*, not a reducer.

*This is PRE-EXISTING, and #824 only made 11 of the 433 visible.*  On
=development= those same 433 groups already have their =Quantity= and

(H25209-04-01 already reports month 6 for a pair whose second record says 9).

Two properties of that reducer worth knowing before you rely on it.  *Under

no grain report and nothing stamps it into the wave parquet, so a warm read

survive the cache" lesson, and it is a property of =reduce_to_agreed= itself
-- Uganda's =cluster_features= hook has the same gap -- not of this change.

** TODO Uganda is the ONE country pytest deletes the cache of, and two tests rely on the cache

Found 2026-08-20 while trying to verify this PR's refreshed
=tests/fixtures/uganda_baseline.json= entries.  Not a data defect -- a
*test-harness* idiosyncrasy that is specific to Uganda by name, and that
makes a regression net silently vacuous.  Recorded here because the next
person to verify a Uganda table cold will hit it in the same order.

*1. The repo-root =conftest.py= purges Uganda's parquet cache at every
pytest session start.*  =_purge_country_caches("Uganda")= runs
unconditionally (unless =--no-purge=), calling =Country("Uganda").clear_cache()=
plus deleting in-tree =Uganda/var/*.parquet=.  No other country is named.
The rationale in the comment is sound (v0.7.1 shipped extraction-time
changes that stale parquets would mask), but the consequence is not
stated there:

*2. =tests/test_uganda_invariance.py= only READS parquets -- so in a
clean isolated =LSMS_DATA_DIR= it skips all 53 of its entries.*  Its
=uganda_root= fixture skips with "No Uganda parquets built (requires data
access)" because the session-start purge just emptied the directory and
nothing in that module rebuilds it.  Whether it checks anything therefore
depends on whether some *other* module, running *earlier in the same
session*, happens to build the table it pins -- i.e. on alphabetical test
ordering.  Measured: the module alone is *53 skipped*; run after
=test_uganda_crop_condition.py= it checks a handful and skips the rest.

*Consequence, stated plainly*: =test_uganda_invariance.py= is NOT a
reliable net for a change to a Uganda parquet's shape or content.  To
verify a baseline entry, build the table explicitly and compare
fingerprints outside pytest.  That is how this PR's three refreshed
entries (=var/cluster_features=, =var/sample=, =var/people_last7days=)
were checked -- shape, columns, index names, dtypes and =content_hash=
all matched.

*3. =tests/test_uganda_tables.py= DELETES generated parquets.*  Its
=_backup_and_remove= / =_restore_or_cleanup= pair backs a parquet up,
removes it, and on restore -- if there was no pre-existing file to
restore -- =unlink()=s whatever the test run generated.  In a fresh
=LSMS_DATA_DIR= that means a session leaves *fewer* parquets than it
built, which is the second way a later reader finds an empty cache.

Fixing any of this is out of scope for a config PR; it wants its own
change with its own before/after.  Do not "fix" it by re-adding a
build step to the invariance module without measuring what that costs.

** WAITING No =RecallWindow=, and the window is ITEM-SPECIFIC -- a real gap

*Do not read the absent column as "Uganda uses one window".*  An earlier draft
of this section said exactly that and it is *false*.  The evidence is in the

: h15cq9  How much came from gifts\in-kind sources in the past [RECALL]? VALUE

=[RECALL]= is a *CAPI fill*, exactly like =[ITEM]= beside it: the recall period
*varies by item* and the label is the un-substituted template.  2011-12 carries
the identical labels.  So Uganda's non-food module is the same shape as Togo's
section 9 -- amounts reported over item-specific windows -- and this table sums
them.

and =nonfood_items.org= has only =Code / Preferred Label / Aggregate Label=.

Value purchased=), so those waves say even less.

Recovering it means reading the questionnaire item list per wave and adding a
recall column to =nonfood_items.org=.  =RecallWindow= is declared
=optional: true= in =lsms_library/data_info.yml= precisely so that can be done
later without a migration.  *Out of scope for GH #817, which was about shape.*
Same gap, same class, as Nigeria's =sect8= / =sect11= pooling.
