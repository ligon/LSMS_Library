#+TITLE: Recall windows: measure the interval where the survey recorded it, declare it where it did not
#+AUTHOR: Sue
#+DATE: [2026-09-09]

* Status

DESIGN, not implemented.  Written for GH #851 after the GhanaLSS visit-offset
work (commits =e55299142=, =87c1c252d=).  Awaiting red-team.

* The problem

=food_expenditures= returns a number whose reference period is nowhere
recorded, and the period is not constant:

- across *waves*: GhanaLSS 2016-17 is six 5-day recalls = 30 days; 1991-92 urban
  is ten 3-day recalls = 30 days but 1991-92 *rural* is seven 2-day = 14 days;
- across *countries*: a 7-day-recall country's figure is a seventh of a 30-day
  one, with nothing marking either;
- across *food and non-food*: Togo's non-food module alone splits into 7-day,
  30-day, 3-month, 6-month and 12-month sub-modules;
- within a *single wave and table*: GLSS3's window depends on the household's
  stratum, and GLSS5's differs between =s='purchased'= (ten asks) and
  =s='produced'= (nine).

Everything downstream silently mixes them.

* The governing principle

From @ligon, 2026-09-09, and it is already how =visit= behaves:

#+begin_quote
Preserve all detail in =food_acquired=; aggregate (at least by default) in
derived tables.
#+end_quote

=visit= is kept on =food_acquired= (D1) and summed away by =food_expenditures= /
=food_quantities= / =food_prices=, and summed (not =first()=-ed) by =Feature()=
via =_ADDITIVE_MEASURE_COLUMNS=.  The recall window should follow the same rule:
carry the finest thing the survey recorded, aggregate downstream.

* The key insight (@ligon)

Do not build a table of recall rules.  *Record each visit and its date*, then
read the interval off the data row by row.  The window stops being a fact we
assert about a wave and becomes a measurement: how many days elapsed, and how
many visits happened, for this household.

Corollaries:
- GLSS3's urban/rural split needs no stratum lookup.  A rural household's dates
  say the gaps were 2 days and there were 8 of them.
- A missed visit stops being a confound.  We do not care *why* a gap was 10
  days instead of 5, only that it was.

* Two regimes, and the partition is checkable

| regime | when                            | window comes from        | example                        |
|--------+---------------------------------+--------------------------+--------------------------------|
| *R1*   | repeated visits, dates published | MEASURED from the dates  | GhanaLSS 1991-92, 1998-99      |
| *R2*   | single visit, stated recall      | DECLARED, from the instrument | Togo nonfood (7/30/90/180/365 days) |

The test is mechanical: does the table have more than one visit per household?

*R1 and R2 do not compete; they partition.*  Togo's =RecallWindow= column stays
exactly right for Togo.  It is regime two, and its window genuinely is a
property of the question ("DEPENSES DES 7 DERNIERS JOURS"), not of the
fieldwork -- there is no interval to measure.

** The awkward third case: protocol known, dates not published

| wave    | cover file    | rows      | per-visit dates?                       |
|---------+---------------+-----------+----------------------------------------|
| 1991-92 | =S0B.DTA=     | 1/HH      | YES -- =vd/vm/vy 1..11=, wide          |
| 1998-99 | =SEC0B.DTA=   | ~6/HH     | YES -- long, =visitcd= + =s0day/month/year= |
| 2005-06 | =sec0.dta=    | 1/HH      | no -- one =ddate/mdate/ydate=          |
| 2012-13 | =SEC0.dta=    | 1/HH      | no -- one =ddate/mdate/ydate=          |
| 2016-17 | =g7sec0.dta=  | 1/HH      | no -- one =ddate/mdate/ydate=          |

Measured 2026-09-09.  The two OLDEST waves publish per-visit dates; the three
newest do not.  So R1 applies where we least need it, and the current wave --
the one that started this -- cannot be measured from its published extract even
though its protocol is documented ("every sixth day i.e. after every five days
... seven (7) times", GLSS7 Main Report).

*This is why measurement cannot simply replace declaration.*  The design is
=measured WHERE AVAILABLE, declared otherwise, and the provenance recorded=.

* Proposal

** P1.  Per-visit dates land on =interview_date=, keyed by calendar visit

The canonical index is *already* =(t, v, i, visit)= (=data_info.yml:21=), and
=local_tools.melt_visit_intervals= already melts per-visit timestamps onto a
=visit= level -- built for EHCVM.  No new machinery; wire GhanaLSS 1991-92
(wide) and 1998-99 (long) through it.

** P2.  =visit= is the join key, and it must be the SAME clock

Today's renumbering made =food_acquired.visit= the calendar visit.
=interview_date.visit= must agree.  *This is not free* -- see O1.

** P3.  Exposure is DERIVED at API time, not stored

Rather than stamping a window column on 5.3M rows:

: days(i, k) = date(i, visit k) - date(i, visit k-1)
: exposure(i, s) = sum of days(i, k) over the visits k present for that (i, s)
: rate = sum(Expenditure) / exposure          # ratio of sums

One source of truth (the dates), no duplication, no cache-hash cost if the join
lives in =_finalize_result= (already in =_build_registry._EXCLUDED_CALLABLES=,
which is how the population record costs nothing).

*The estimator is a ratio of sums, NOT a mean of ratios.*  With unequal gaps
=mean(expenditure_k / days_k)= treats a 10-day observation as one data point
equal to a 5-day one.  Only =sum(expenditure) / sum(days)= is the household's
daily rate over its actual exposure.  They coincide only under equal spacing,
which real fieldwork does not deliver.

** P4.  =RecallWindow= extends to R2 tables, with a machine-readable length

Keep the instrument's verbatim string (provenance), and add a canonical
vocabulary =window label -> days= in =data_info.yml=.  This is exactly the =u=
pattern: raw label -> Preferred Label -> kg factor.  Today the conversion dict
lives in a *comment* in =Togo/_/data_scheme.yml=, so nothing can use it.

** P5.  =recall.yml=, a =population.yml= sibling, records PROVENANCE not value

Per (wave, table): the regime, and where the window came from --
=measured-from-dates= / =declared-from-instrument= / =declared-from-protocol= /
=not-recorded= -- with =source_file=, =locator=, =confidence=.  A =.yml= sibling
moves no cache hash (measured for =population.yml=).

Its job is *not* to carry the number.  It is to make "nobody has looked"
distinguishable from "there is no window", which is the failure that let the
Albania unevidenced-absence claim ship.  GLSS7 becomes a visible
=declared-from-protocol= rather than a silent assumption.

** P6.  Warn, never fence

Pooling rows with different windows is the same disease as pooling different
populations, and #603 settled the policy: warn, name the classes, drop nothing.
=PopulationHeterogeneityWarning= is the model.  The warning must also fire in
=Feature()=, where =visit= is dropped and summed across countries.

** P7.  The API is a kwarg, in the existing idiom

: food_expenditures(period=None)      # today's behaviour, unchanged
: food_expenditures(period='day')     # sum / exposure
: food_expenditures(period='annual')  # x 365.25

alongside =units==, =labels==, =basis==, =currency==.  =period=None= stays the
default, so nothing moves for existing callers.  Without a consumer the
machine-readable length is documentation, which is precisely how
=RecallWindow='s conversion dict ended up unusable.

* Open problems.  These are the design's weak points, stated so they get attacked

** O1.  GLSS4's =visitcd= origin is inconsistent -- BLOCKING

Measured on =SEC0B.DTA=, per household =(min, max)= of =visitcd=:

| (2, 7) | 4,052 households |
| (1, 6) | 1,879            |
| (1, 5) | 23               |
| (2, 6) | 17               |
| (1, 7) | 14               |
| (3, 7) | 12               |

Both main groups have six visits, but ~68% are numbered 2..7 (calendar) and
~31% are numbered 1..6 (an ordinal of recorded visits).  So =visitcd= is *not*
a drop-in join key for =food_acquired.visit=.  A normalisation rule is needed
and none is yet justified.  "max == 7 implies calendar" is a guess, not a rule.

** O2.  Corrupt date sequences

GLSS4 measured gaps include negative values: -360 (x17, almost certainly a
98->99 year typo), then a scatter of -329, -238, -207, -179, -171, -148...
Roughly 1-2% of sequences.  A measured-interval implementation needs a
plausibility screen AND a defined fallback for households whose dates are
unusable.  Naive differencing will produce negative exposures.

** O3.  GLSS5's interval is unknown

GLSS5 fields eleven visits (its =s9bq10= is "amount spent by eleventh visit"),
but the spacing is not established.  The tempting inference -- 3 days, from the
GLSS7 Manual's Table 2 -- is from boilerplate that has already been shown to
descend from GLSS3-urban.  *Do not put that number in a config file without
GLSS5's own manual.*

** O4.  Exposure is per =(i, s)=, not per =i=

GLSS5 asks purchases at ten visits and own production at nine.  A single
household exposure would be wrong for one of them.

** O5.  The denominator must come from the VISIT table, not the food rows

If a household has no =food_acquired= row at visit 5 for item j, that is "bought
none", not "not visited".  Counting exposure from food rows would shrink the
denominator for households that bought little, inflating their rate.  Exposure
must be counted from =interview_date= (visits that happened), independently of
whether any item was recorded.

** O6.  Does R1 even help the waves we care about?

GLSS5/6/7 -- the three largest and most used GhanaLSS waves -- are R2 by
publication accident.  If the answer for them is a declared protocol anyway, is
the R1 machinery worth building for two old waves plus EHCVM?  *Counter*: GLSS3
is the only place we can VALIDATE a declared protocol against measured dates
(median gap exactly 3.0 urban / 2.0 rural), which is direct evidence about how
far protocol declarations can be trusted elsewhere.  That may be the strongest
argument for R1 and it should be tested, not assumed.

** O7.  Is a GLSS7 CAPI paradata / tracking file hiding somewhere?

Not yet checked.  =g7sec0.dta= is one row per household, but GLSS7 was CAPI and
the manual describes a per-visit "summary of the survey result" page.  If those
timestamps were published in some other file, GLSS7 moves from R2 to R1 and O6
mostly dissolves.  *Check before building the fallback.*

* What this does NOT propose

- Annualising anything by default.  Reported values stay reported; =period== is
  opt-in.
- Removing =RecallWindow=.  It is right for R2.
- A per-wave table of recall rules.  That is the thing this design exists to
  avoid.
