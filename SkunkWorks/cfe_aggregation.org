#+title: Cfe Aggregation

* Notes on CFE Aggregation of Foods
** Some notation.  
Let \(X_0\) be a \(J_0\times N_0\) data matrix, with \( J_0\) the number of distinct food items observed in the data, and \(N_0\) the number of households for which at least some food expenditures are observed. 

Let \(A\) be a \(J\times J_0 \)matrix of ones and zeros, with \(J\leq J_0\), and let \(X_A := AX_0 \).
Thus \(A\) /aggregates/ expenditures for each household.

Let \(M_A = \one(X_A=0)\), and let \(D_{\underline{J}}\) be a linear operator such that \(D_{\underline{J}}X_A\) drops all columns of \(X_A\) for which the column sum of \(M_A\) is less than \(\underline{J}\). 
** CFE Demands
Let \(Y_A := \log X_A\).  Residualize \(Y_A\) by projecting on a set of household-time characteristics \(z\) and a complete set of good-time fixed effects, obtaining residuals \(R\).  Extract a single factor from the matrix \(R = \beta_Aw_A^\T + \varepsilon\) via SVD or principal components (see algorithm in =CFEDemands=); the vector \(\beta_A\) are the \(J_A\) Frisch elasticities associated with the different goods.  The sign of \(w\) is chosen such that the elements of (\beta_A\) are mostly positive, and we normalize using something like \(\var(w) = 1\).

** Criteria for choosing \(A\)
These notes concern the optimal choice of \(A\).

There are several criteria we might want ot consider in choosing \(A\), alone or together.
- Missingness  of Households :: Other things equal, we would prefer that \(A\) be chosen so that  \(D_{\underline{J}}X_A\) has as many columns as possible (we don't want to drop households without a good reason). 
- Missingness of Cross-Products :: Let \(c_A\) be the number of elements in \(M_A^\T M_A\) that take values greater than some \(\underline{c}\) (say 30, for heuristic reasons, but this should be a dial).  We'd like this number to be larger rather than smaller, other things equal.  This criterion comes from a need to estimate second moments of \(X_A\) while also preserving a finer aggregation of goods.
- Semantic similarity :: Loosely defined (but see https://github.com/ligon/LSMS_Library/issues/226 for some ideas).  If \(A\) aggregates two goods it's desirable for the labels to be semantically similar, so that the aggregated good is readily interpretable as a familiar type of food.  So don't aggregate milk and fish, for example; do aggregate grouper and talapia.  Consider computing semantic similarity scores via =gensim= or =word2vec= (or training these models on our corpus of food labels).
- \(\beta\)-similarity :: In the CFE demand system, it only makes sense to aggregate two goods if their income elasticities are similar.  Within the CFE system, this means that we should only consider aggregating good \(i\) and \(j\) if the estimated Frisch elasticities \(\beta_i\) and \(\beta_j\) are "close" using some statistical measure of distance.
- Precise estimates of \(w\) :: Our ultimate goal is really to obtain the best estimates of the vector \(w\).  What the best statistical criterion is an open question.

** Proposed Approach
To formalize the choice of the aggregation matrix \(A\), the problem can be framed as constrained hierarchical clustering over the \(J_0\) goods, evaluated against a clear statistical criterion for \(\hat{w}\).

**Statistical Precision of \(w\) as the Primary Objective**

In the single-factor model \(R_A = \beta_A w_A^\top + \varepsilon\), household \(i\)'s latent factor \(w_i\) is estimated by projecting its observed residual vector \(R_{i, A}\) onto \(\beta_A\). If household \(i\) consumes a subset of goods \(\mathcal{J}_i \subseteq \{1, \dots, J\}\), generalized least squares yields:

\[\hat{w}_i = \left(\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_i} \frac{\beta_{j, A}^2}{\sigma_{j, A}^2}\right)^{-1} \sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_i} \frac{\beta_{j, A}}{\sigma_{j, A}^2} R_{ij, A}\]

Assuming roughly homoskedastic residual variances \(\sigma_j^2 \approx \sigma^2\), the estimation variance is:

\[\text{Var}(\hat{w}_i \mid \mathcal{J}_i) \approx \frac{\sigma^2}{\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_i} \beta_{j, A}^2}\]

This exposes the fundamental trade-off of aggregation:

- The Benefit ::  Aggregating goods converts zero expenditures to non-zeros, expanding the active index set \(\mathcal{J}_i\) and reducing households dropped by \(D_{\underline{J}}\).
- The Cost :: Combining distinct goods averages out their Frisch elasticities. Since \(\left(\sum_{k} \beta_k\right)^2 \leq K \sum_k \beta_k^2\) by Jensen's inequality, aggregating goods with disparate \(\beta\) compresses the denominator \(\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_i} \beta_j^2\), degrading factor precision.

A natural target criterion to minimize over valid matrices \(A\) is the mean asymptotic variance:
\[\mathcal{L}(A) = \frac{1}{N_A} \sum_{i=1}^{N_A} \left(\sum_{j \in \mathcal{J}_i} \beta_{j, A}^2\right)^{-1}\]

*** Formulating the Agglomerative Distance Metric**

Direct combinatorial search over binary partition matrices \(A \in \{0, 1\}^{J \times J_0}\) is NP-hard. An effective alternative is constrained agglomerative clustering using a composite pairwise distance between food items \(j\) and \(k\):

\[d(j, k) = \lambda_1 d_{\text{sem}}(j, k) + \lambda_2 \frac{\vert{}\hat{\beta}_j - \hat{\beta}_k\vert{}}{\text{SE}(\hat{\beta}_j - \hat{\beta}_k)} + \lambda_3 d_{\text{missing}}(j, k)\]

- \(d_{\text{sem}}(j, k)\) :: Cosine distance (\(1 - \cos(\mathbf{v}_j, \mathbf{v}_k)\)) computed from food label embeddings (Word2Vec, fastText, or a sentence-transformer fine-tuned on agricultural/food dictionaries).
- \(\beta\)-Distance :: Studentized distance between provisional elasticities. Set \(d(j, k) = \infty\) if \(\text{sign}(\hat{\beta}_j) \neq \text{sign}(\hat{\beta}_k)\) to avoid combining inferior goods with luxuries.
- \(d_{\text{missing}}(j, k)\) :: Sparsity complementarity, measured via Jaccard distance on consumption indicator vectors \(\mathbf{m}_j, \mathbf{m}_k \in \{0, 1\}^{N_0}\):
\[d_{\text{missing}}(j, k) = 1 - \frac{\vert{}\text{supp}(\mathbf{m}_j) \cap \text{supp}(\mathbf{m}_k)\vert{}}{\vert{}\text{supp}(\mathbf{m}_j) \cup \text{supp}(\mathbf{m}_k)\vert{}}\]

This prioritizes merging items with complementary zero patterns to quickly expand household coverage.

*** Operational Algorithm
- Step 1 (Pilot Estimation) :: Estimate preliminary \(\hat{\beta}^{(0)}\) on the subset of high-frequency items or broad baseline groups.
- Step 2 (Constrained Agglomeration) :: Apply hierarchical clustering with hard constraints (e.g., \(d_{\text{sem}} \leq \tau_{\text{sem}}\), ensuring grouper and tilapia can merge, but fish and milk cannot).
- Step 3 (Stopping Rule) :: Evaluate the agglomeration tree sequentially. Halt merges at the dimension \(J^*\) that minimizes \(\mathcal{L}(A)\) while satisfying the second-moment support threshold \(c_A \geq \underline{c}\).
