#+title: CFE Aggregation

* Notes on CFE Aggregation of Foods

We want to aggregate goods within a country to improve estimates of
\(w=-\log\lambda\), where \(\lambda\) is marginal utility of expenditure.
Summing goods with similar Frisch elasticities can preserve their relation
to welfare while rescuing sparse goods and the households that depend on
them. Choosing an aggregation requires assessing both approximation error
and statistical precision, together with household coverage.

** Notation and support

Let \(X_0\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq0}^{J_0\times N_0}\) contain expenditures
on \(J_0\) distinct foods. Columns index household-period observations
\(c=(i,t,m)\), with \(m\) denoting a market; for a single cross-section,
\(N_0\) is simply the number of households. Every element is nonnegative.
Within the survey's expenditure module, adopt the convention that
unreported item expenditures are zero. Such entries can conceal missing
responses or underreporting; the data need not identify these separately.
The expenditure basis, recall treatment, and coverage of the questionnaire
are fixed before choosing an aggregation.

Let \(A\in\{0,1\}^{J\times J_0}\), with \(J\leq J_0\), assign each
food to exactly one nonempty group:
\[
  \sum_{g=1}^J A_{gj}=1,\qquad \sum_{j=1}^{J_0} A_{gj}\geq1.
\]
Then \(X_A=AX_0\) aggregates expenditures. Excluding a good from estimation
is a separate selection decision, not an unrecorded deletion from \(A\).

Define the zero mask \(M_A=\mathbf{1}\{X_A=0\}\) and the positive mask
\(O_A=1-M_A\). Nonnegativity implies that a group's positive support is
exactly the union of its constituents' supports:
\[
  O_{A,gc}=\mathbf{1}\{\exists j:A_{gj}=1,\ X_{0,jc}>0\}.
\]
Thus a group can have adequate support even though its individual foods
would be discarded. This can keep households in estimation. Aggregation
does not increase a household's number of positive entries; its benefit
comes from retaining usable groups and improving their joint support.

A rule requiring at least \(\underline J\) positive groups retains columns
for which
\[
  \sum_g O_{A,gc}\geq\underline J,
  \qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
  \sum_g M_{A,gc}\leq J-\underline J.
\]
Once chosen, the retained columns can be selected by right multiplication
\(X_A S_A\), where \(S_A\) is an \(N_0\times N_A\) selection matrix.
The data-dependent rule choosing \(S_A\) is not itself a fixed linear
operator. The current =prepare_data= instead uses a threshold expressed
as a fraction of the number of goods, together with other filters.

For the selected households, the matrix of paired positive-observation
counts is
\[
  C_A=(O_A S_A)(O_A S_A)^\top.
\]
It has dimension \(J\times J\). Estimation through the current complete
covariance matrix requires every retained pair to meet a minimum count
\(C_{A,gh}\geq\underline c\), with a configurable threshold such as 30.
A large number of supported pairs alone does not ensure this. Counts are
necessary numerical safeguards, not guarantees of identification or
precision.

** CFE demands and aggregation

Take logarithms only of positive expenditures. After accounting for
household characteristics and good-market-period effects, the CFE model
has a single-factor representation
\[
  R_{A,gc}\simeq\beta_{A,g}w_c+\varepsilon_{A,gc}.
\]
Whether this remains an adequate approximation after aggregation is part
of the question. The target \(w\) is common to the candidate partitions;
their estimates \(\hat w_A\) can differ. The \(\beta\)'s are Frisch
elasticities. Choose the factor's sign so that most loadings are positive,
and impose a common scale before comparing estimates across partitions.
For example, common goods kept as singletons could anchor that scale.
Normalizing each fitted factor on its own retained sample is insufficient
to define a common latent target.

*** Zeros and incomplete log data

A zero in the expenditure matrix supplies no finite log observation.
Thus the zero mask also describes unavailable entries in the log data,
without identifying why each entry is unavailable. The
[[file:/home/ligon/Projects/CovariateRiskSharing/Text/risk-sharing.org::2349][risk-sharing application]] likewise includes zeros for unreported food
items. Aggregation should not require a classification of these entries
into true zeros and missing responses that the survey cannot supply.

There is also an economic reason for zeros. The
[[file:/home/ligon/Research/VESDemand/Text/estimating_imue.org::451][household-production argument in estimating_imue.org]], also used in
[[file:/home/ligon/Projects/CovariateRiskSharing/Text/risk-sharing.org::298][the risk-sharing paper]], permits corner solutions for purchased inputs
while preserving CFE demands. If food inputs \(j\) produce a composite
\(r\), input expenditures take the form
\[
  x_{rj}=\eta_{rj}(p)x_r,\qquad
  x_r=b_r(p,z)\exp(\beta_r w),\qquad \eta_{rj}(p)\geq0.
\]
An unused input has \(\eta_{rj}=0\). Summing a fixed subset \(G\) gives
\[
  \sum_{j\in G}x_{rj}
  =b_r(p,z)\left(\sum_{j\in G}\eta_{rj}(p)\right)\exp(\beta_r w).
\]
Whenever this sum is positive, its elasticity is exactly \(\beta_r\),
including when some constituent expenditures are zero. Substitutable
inputs to the same composite therefore supply a structural case for
aggregation with complementary supports.

For estimation, the manuscript treats unavailable expenditure observations
as [[file:/home/ligon/Research/VESDemand/Text/estimating_imue.org::151][missing at random conditional on shadow prices, observed characteristics,
and welfare]]. This permits zero patterns to vary with welfare. It is a
restriction on the observation process, not a claim that every reported
zero is a verified nonpurchase. Aggregate estimation still needs suitable
conditional error moments; increased positive support alone does not
establish them. Unreported positive amounts belong in sensitivity analysis
of measurement and selection, without requiring an observable second mask.

*** Summing averages the elasticities

Hold prices, demographics, and item disturbances fixed and write
\(x_j(w)=a_j\exp(\beta_j w)\), allowing \(a_j\geq0\). For a group
\(G\) with positive total expenditure, define
\(h_G(w)=\log\sum_{j\in G}x_j(w)\) and within-group expenditure shares
\(s_j(w)=x_j(w)/\sum_{k\in G}x_k(w)\). Then
\[
  h_G'(w)=\sum_{j\in G}s_j(w)\beta_j,\qquad
  h_G''(w)=\sum_{j\in G}s_j(w)(\beta_j-h_G'(w))^2.
\]
The aggregate loading is an expenditure-share-weighted mean of the
constituent elasticities; its curvature is their weighted variance.
If all constituents have elasticity \(\beta_G\), then
\(h_G(w)=\beta_Gw+\log\sum_j a_j\) exactly. Similar elasticities give
small curvature, supporting aggregation as an approximation.

More precisely, let \(\Delta_G=\max_{j\in G}\beta_j-\min_{j\in G}\beta_j\).
The deviation from the tangent at \(w_0\) satisfies
\[
  0\leq h_G(w)-h_G(w_0)-h_G'(w_0)(w-w_0)
  \leq \Delta_G^2(w-w_0)^2/8.
\]
This suggests specifying a tolerable error in log expenditure over a
commonly normalized welfare range. It does not directly bound the error
of the estimated \(w\). Shares generally vary with welfare, demographics,
and prices, so one fitted aggregate coefficient need not equal a simple
average of the fitted constituent coefficients.

The identities include zero-share constituents when the coefficients
\(a_j\) are fixed, as in the household-production argument. They do not
cover arbitrary reporting or participation changes with \(w\) that alter
those coefficients. Even equal elasticities do not ensure that demographic
controls retain their original form:
\(\log\sum_j\exp(\alpha_{jtm}+\gamma_j'z_c)\) need not be linear in
\(z_c\). These are qualifications to the fitted aggregate specification.

*** Noise and precision

The starting assumption for estimating the fine-good elasticities is
\(\operatorname{Cov}(\varepsilon_c)=\sigma_0^2 I\), conditional on
welfare and controls. Under this assumption, a first-order expansion of
the aggregate's log error gives
\[
  e_{Gc}\simeq\sum_{j\in G}s^0_{jc}\varepsilon_{jc},\qquad
  \operatorname{Var}(e_{Gc})\simeq\sigma_0^2\sum_{j\in G}(s^0_{jc})^2,
\]
where \(s^0\) denotes shares at the systematic expenditures about which
the expansion is taken. Aggregation can therefore induce unequal error
variances across groups and households even when fine-good errors are
homoskedastic. Retaining homoskedasticity after aggregation is a further
restriction to assess.

For example, two equal-share goods with the same elasticity provide the
same first-order information about \(w\) when summed as when used
separately, conditional on known loadings and nuisance terms: the sum
retains the elasticity and halves the error variance. With very unequal
shares, the sum instead places most weight on one noisy observation and
can lose substantial precision even with identical elasticities.

For a conditional benchmark, suppose the nuisance terms and loadings are
known, and let \(\mathcal J_c\) be the observed groups for household-period
\(c\). With diagonal error covariance, GLS gives
\[
  \hat w_{A,c}=
  \left(\sum_{g\in\mathcal J_c}\frac{\beta_{A,g}^2}{\sigma_{A,gc}^2}\right)^{-1}
  \sum_{g\in\mathcal J_c}\frac{\beta_{A,g}R_{A,gc}}{\sigma_{A,gc}^2},
  \qquad
  V_{A,c}=\left(\sum_{g\in\mathcal J_c}
    \frac{\beta_{A,g}^2}{\sigma_{A,gc}^2}\right)^{-1}.
\]
Under common variance within that partition this reduces to
\(V_{A,c}=\sigma_A^2/\sum_{g\in\mathcal J_c}\beta_{A,g}^2\).
The noise variance cannot be canceled when comparing partitions. With a
general covariance \(\Sigma_{A,c}\), conditional GLS information is
\(\beta_{A,\mathcal J_c}'\Sigma_{A,c}^{-1}\beta_{A,\mathcal J_c}\).

This benchmark is not the complete uncertainty calculation for
=Regression.get_w=, which uses a joint, unweighted least-squares fit with
market-period intercepts. Existing =w_var= and =w_cov= provide conditional
diagnostics under their stated assumptions. Uncertainty in loadings,
controls, and partition selection also matters. Similarity within groups
should preserve sufficient variation between groups to distinguish
welfare from the market intercept in the current estimator.

** Criteria for choosing \(A\)

We want accurate welfare estimates for a declared population. A useful
conceptual loss on a fixed evaluation set \(\mathcal H\), with a common
factor normalization and fixed population weights \(\omega_c\), is
\[
  \mathcal R(A;\mathcal H)=
  \frac{\sum_{c\in\mathcal H}\omega_c
    \operatorname{E}[(\hat w_{A,c}-w_c)^2]}
       {\sum_{c\in\mathcal H}\omega_c}.
\]
The evaluation set must be scoreable for all compared candidates; report
coverage and performance outside it separately. The \(\omega_c\)'s are
population weights, distinct from both welfare \(w_c\) and expenditure
shares. Observed data do not reveal true \(w\), so the loss needs simulation
and empirical validation. Fitted standard errors alone omit approximation
bias and uncertainty from choosing the partition.

Alongside error, report coverage over the original population:
\[
  q(A)=\frac{\sum_{c=1}^{N_0}\omega_c
    \mathbf{1}\{\hat w_{A,c}\text{ is available}\}}
    {\sum_{c=1}^{N_0}\omega_c}.
\]
A scalar objective combining accuracy and coverage needs an explicit
cost for failing to estimate a household's welfare. Until that choice is
made, report a frontier of coverage and validated error. Averaging only
over each partition's surviving households can reward exclusion.

Admissible partitions should also satisfy the following conditions.

- Economic approximation :: Limit within-group beta dispersion on an
  economically meaningful scale, allowing for estimation uncertainty.
  Failure to reject equality is not evidence of equivalence. For estimates
  from a joint fit, use
  \(\operatorname{Var}(\hat\beta_j-\hat\beta_k)=V_{jj}+V_{kk}-2V_{jk}\).
  A sparse item with unidentified beta cannot pass this test by default;
  its assignment requires a disclosed assumption and validation.
- Interpretability :: Use food categories and curator restrictions to
  define coherent groups. Grouper and tilapia are plausible candidates;
  milk and fish are not. Embeddings can suggest merges, but their proximity
  does not establish demand similarity. Apply constraints to whole groups
  so successive pairwise merges do not produce an incoherent result.
- Estimability :: Check paired support after sample selection, market
  coverage, and variation in the fitted loadings. Unexpected negative
  loadings deserve diagnosis; positive Frisch elasticities do not by
  themselves distinguish luxuries from necessities.

Complementary purchase patterns can improve support. Jaccard distance is
large for complementary supports and small for overlapping supports, so
minimizing it would favor the latter. Evaluate the actual improvement in
usable support and household retention when proposing a merge. Geographic
or questionnaire differences can also create complementary patterns.
[[https://github.com/ligon/LSMS_Library/issues/226][Issue 226]] records earlier proposals for semantics and beta similarity;
its non-rejection heuristic remains a proposal to assess.

** The related preparation question

Aggregation and sample preparation interact. The reviewed =prepare_data=
first removes households below a fraction-of-goods threshold, then removes
goods with insufficient covariance support, and repeats on the surviving
data. Its intended early marginal-support filter has no assignment, and
deleted households cannot return when the number of retained goods falls.
In a reproduced example, this retains 40 households and four goods;
removing unsupported goods first retains 60 households with those same
four goods. These preparation defects are now repaired on CFEDemands'
=feature/prepare-data-scoring= branch, which also provides =score_w= and
exclusion diagnostics. The two filters use the same inclusive count boundary.

The covariance-support helper also uses a greedy deletion rule whose
ties depend on column order. A reproduced example retains two goods even
though a valid three-good set exists; reversing column order finds the
three-good set. Neither retained-good count nor the current heuristic
alone defines the best welfare-estimation sample. The
[[file:cfe_aggregation_review.org::*The related preparation problem][review's preparation section]] gives the examples and runnable checks.

We should distinguish observations needed to estimate common parameters
from observations sufficient to estimate a household's welfare given
those parameters. Once loadings and relevant nuisance terms are fitted,
some households excluded from parameter estimation may still support
useful scores. Under the maintained homoskedastic model, their conditional
information depends on \(\sum_{g\in\mathcal J_c}\beta_g^2/\sigma^2\),
not simply the fraction of all goods observed. Scoring additional households
requires the estimated controls and market effects as well as beta.

A separate scoring step would keep these quantities fixed. Passing the
broader sample through =Regression= again instead refits them. Improvements
to preparation should therefore be evaluated at a fixed partition before
crediting aggregation with all gains in household coverage.

** Proposed approach and remaining choices

Implementation divides into three parts. Their interfaces can be specified
before settling the statistical objective.

*** Preparation and scoring in CFEDemands

Repair the ineffective support filter and reconsider household eligibility
against the retained goods. Preserve the original observations and report
the reason for each exclusion. Make the selection policy explicit so that
alternative policies can be compared at a fixed aggregation.

Add an interface for estimating \(w\) on additional households using an
existing fit. It must retain the fitted good set, demographic specification
and coefficients, centering transformations, market-period effects, beta,
and normalization. Recomputing centering on the new sample would change
the comparison even if beta were fixed. Initially, score observations
only where the required fitted goods, controls, and market-period effects
are available; return reasons when they are not. New markets or periods
require a separate treatment of their nuisance parameters.

Checks should establish that scoring the fitting sample reproduces its
point estimates, that new households leave fitted parameters unchanged,
and that scoring a household alone or alongside other new households gives
the same result. Conditional uncertainty should use the existing variance
machinery where applicable, with its conditioning assumptions explicit.
This interface serves both household recovery and validation.

*** Practical semantic constraints in LSMS Library

Start with a country-specific table assigning fine items to interpretable
food families, with explicit exceptions for incompatible preparation or
preservation forms and goods required to remain separate. Existing curated
Aggregate labels and questionnaire categories can seed this table where
available. They are proposals for admissibility, not evidence that their
members have equal elasticities or that the existing partition is optimal.

Where the table is incomplete, a language model can draft assignments from
labels and survey documentation, recording the reason and uncertainty for
each proposal. Review ambiguous local names and composite foods, then store
the accepted map as a versioned input. Unresolved items should remain
explicit rather than being merged into a catch-all category. No embedding
model needs to be trained on the country's food labels to begin this work.

The output is a rule for whether a proposed /whole group/ is admissible,
plus interpretable labels. The statistical search can split a food family;
cross-family merges require an explicit exception. This avoids a chain of
pairwise similarities gradually combining incompatible foods. Alternative
semantic maps can later be compared as sensitivity exercises.

*** Statistical evaluation and search

An evaluator of supplied partitions would establish what a search should
optimize. It should compare fine labels and existing curated groups under
explicit preparation rules, then report retention, paired support,
residual variances, conditional uncertainty, and validation results on a
common factor scale. Use the scoring interface to distinguish fitting
coverage from the population for which welfare can be estimated.

Constrained agglomeration is a practical candidate-generation method.
Estimate pilot loadings on adequately supported goods, propose economically
coherent merges, then refit and evaluate candidate partitions. The pilot
does not identify the loadings of excluded sparse goods, and broad-group
loadings do not identify their constituents. Score cuts along the tree;
there is no reason to assume that error changes monotonically or that the
first worsening identifies the best cut. A single greedy tree explores
only part of the partition space.

Validation should include simulations with known welfare, varying beta
dispersion, expenditure shares, noise, and zero patterns. Include both
substitution among inputs to a common composite and unreported positive
expenditures, respecting the maintained conditional observation assumptions
in the baseline and varying them in sensitivity exercises. On real data,
select partitions on training households and evaluate on held-out
households, keeping a household's panel observations together. Prediction
of reserved goods should exclude those goods from the household's factor
score. Resampling intended to capture partition selection uncertainty must
repeat selection and fitting.

The [[file:aggregation/evaluation.org::*Uganda observation patterns][Uganda observation-pattern experiment]] now uses complete household food
masks from the delivered 2019-20 data, preserving sparse goods and their
joint support. Median households report ten positive purchased foods, or
fifteen with total recorded acquisition, against 114 delivered food labels.
The revised preparation and frozen scoring recover most of the household
coverage lost by the original selector in this experiment. Existing curated
groups add little further scoring coverage, although they improve known-welfare
precision under a substitution mechanism. They worsen it when variable
numbers of independently positive components are simply summed. Both cases
use equal within-group betas; the interpretation of zeros and amounts still
matters. These are conditional simulation results, not an empirical ranking
of Uganda partitions.

Adding nonfood expenditures to the estimation sample is not presumed to
improve precision. In prior Uganda fits, their inclusion produced highly
implausible estimates. A working explanation is that different survey
instruments generate markedly different expenditure-error variances,
violating the maintained homoskedasticity assumption. These reported
results have not been reproduced in this review. Keep food aggregation
as the initial scope. Predicting held-out nonfood expenditures with a
food-based factor is a separate diagnostic, whose value depends on the
measurement process and relation to welfare. Better prediction alone does
not establish more accurate MUEs.

*** Dependencies and remaining choices

Preparation fixes and the scoring interface now support the
[[file:aggregation/evaluation.org][supplied-partition evaluator and initial simulation checks]]. Semantic curation
can proceed independently and supply additional admissible groups. It does
not block statistical work on supplied partitions. Choosing an optimal
fitting sample is itself a statistical problem; exposing selection policies
in =prepare_data= does not settle it. Evaluate supplied partitions before
adding a search over them.

Choices still to settle include the reference population and its weights,
the common factor normalization, an acceptable approximation tolerance,
and the treatment of unscored households. These determine the statistical
objective before a particular clustering algorithm can be preferred.
