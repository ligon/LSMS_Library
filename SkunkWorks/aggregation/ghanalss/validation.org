* Published partition

The country =Aggregate Label= column combines four narrowly defined food
groups. The 233 labels delivered across seven waves map to 228 groups;
the 179 labels delivered in 2016-17 map to 175. Tobacco labels remain
singletons and are excluded from the CFE experiments. Every unselected
food retains its exact Preferred name. This is a conservative first
partition, not an estimate of an optimal aggregation.

* Evaluation design

The main experiment uses 2016-17 purchased amounts summed over the six
five-day recalls, Region markets, and the existing fourteen sex-age counts
plus log household size. The 14,009 sampled households include nineteen
with missing characteristics. They remain in the coverage denominator.
All have known Region and positive sample weights. Zero and unavailable
amounts both supply no positive log-expenditure observation. Level amounts
are summed before taking logs; no new own-production valuation is used.

A fixed household split within Region by Rural strata assigns 8,397
households to training, 2,793 to validation and 2,819 to the final test
(seed 20260916). Every candidate uses the same split. The production CFE
branch =feature/prepare-data-scoring= supplies preparation, beta estimation,
and frozen scoring, with =min_obs=30=, =min_prop_items=0.1= and =alltm=False=.
No alternate estimator or selector is introduced.
Survey weights enter coverage, normalization and prediction diagnostics;
the CFE point estimator retains its existing unweighted fit.

Salt stays a singleton and fixes the loading scale at one. The score
origin is the survey-weighted mean among complete-control, Salt-observed
training households. The fixed comparison population consists of
complete-control, Salt-observed evaluation households. Score coverage uses
the entire evaluation sample, including households outside that population.

Onion and Egg are reserved prediction targets, excluded from every fit
and scoring vector. Weighted regressions of their positive log amounts on
the controls, market indicators and frozen scores use training households
only. Validation uses the same positive-target population for each map;
missing or unidentified predictions invalidate a complete-population
comparison. Thus these errors concern positive-food prediction, not error
against an observed true welfare measure.

For uncertainty in loadings, sixty joint draws resample training PSUs
within Region by Rural strata, repeating preparation and beta estimation.
There is no nested beta bootstrap. Cubed sugar, macaroni and yellow gari
are not identified in the full fine fit because of the all-pairs covariance
requirement. Separate diagnostic fits place each of these and its candidate
partner beside the same nine dense reference foods, including Salt. Those
fits use the existing estimator and the same joint resampling draws.
Their comparability relies on a common factor across the shared foods;
the smaller fits are a diagnostic, not a replacement production estimator.

The paired prediction intervals resample all 949 validation PSUs, including
PSUs with no positive target observations, within strata (1,000 draws,
seed 20260918). They condition on the fitted training models. They do not
include training-fit uncertainty, selection uncertainty or household
dependence across training and validation within a PSU. They should not
be read as unconditional confidence intervals for the chosen partition.

* Selection evidence

The fine model's central ninety percent training score interval is
[-0.45368, 0.36268] in Salt units; its origin is zero. For a deterministic
sum of log-linear demands, the tangent approximation error over this
interval is at most \((\max_j\beta_j-\min_j\beta_j)^2 R^2/8\), with
\(R=0.45368\). The table plugs
the empirical 95th percentile of each joint bootstrap span into this
formula. It is a curvature diagnostic conditional on the model and fixed
range, not a bound on estimation error in welfare or a simultaneous
confidence statement. It does not bound distortions due to reporting,
composition or unequal aggregate error variances.

The initial analysis tolerance was 0.10 log expenditure, recorded before
fitting in the [[file:../../../.coder/ledger/ghanalss-aggregate-validation.md][ledger]]. Passing that loose screen is not sufficient for
inclusion. These four groups also have narrow semantic scope and no
material deterioration in the reserved-food validation. One fish draw
does not retain both members; its uncertainty summary uses the other
59 draws and does not treat the missing coefficient as zero.

The point probe loadings for cubed and granulated sugar are 1.983 and
2.043; those for macaroni and spaghetti are 0.859 and 0.798. The full fine
loadings for bottled and canned malt drinks are 1.470 and 1.728, and for
Kapla and Tilapia 1.217 and 1.566. Similarity is a practical judgment with
sampling uncertainty, not a conclusion from failing to reject equality.

Several plausible alternatives are retained as singletons:

- Imported/local rice and white/yellow gari have appreciably different
  point loadings. Rice improves Onion prediction slightly but worsens Egg
  prediction; neither group increases scoring coverage.
- Bread types show a similar prediction tradeoff and wider loading spans.
- Broad smoked fish reduces fitting coverage. Chicken cuts have an
  unsupported breast coefficient and substantial differences between the
  identified cuts.
- Pork cuts, sausages and named spirits remain unsupported even when
  pooled, so their near-identical scores are not evidence of equivalence.
- Crab/shrimp, tinned milk forms, coconut forms and processed fish have
  incomplete constituent loading evidence. Their small prediction changes
  do not identify the missing loadings. They remain candidates for further
  work, not accepted merges.
- Onion/shallot touches a reserved target and was not tested in this
  experiment. Powdered pepper lacks sufficient semantic documentation.

* Real-data results

In the =validation_selected_*= and =test_selected= CSVs, the evaluator's
=draft= row denotes the fixed four-group selection above. The =draft= row
in the earlier =validation_purchased= directory denotes the original
five-group proposal; those are different experiments.

The combined map retains 86 fitted goods in the purchased experiment,
including its four new aggregates. It also admits Pawpaw and Cerelac,
which fail the full fine preparation: changing the covariance support
can affect goods outside the proposed groups. Both fits use 7,713
training households, with a negligible change in their weighted coverage.
Both score 2,769 validation households: weighted coverage is 99.271%.

On total recorded acquisition both maps retain 88 fitted goods and score
2,771 validation households (99.302% weighted coverage). Fitting coverage
falls from 95.947% to 95.933%, or two training households. That small loss
is reported rather than hidden by comparing only common survivors.

The group support gains from the inventory do not translate into large
gains in scored-household coverage. Frozen scoring already reaches almost
all households here. Nor are fitted aggregate betas mechanically averages
of the estimated fine betas: changing positive reporting patterns,
composition, covariance support and the normalization fit can move them
outside the fine estimates' range. For example, the combined fit gives
malt drinks a loading of 1.870. The share-weighted derivative identity
does not assert an identity between these separately fitted estimators.

The four group memberships were frozen before examining the final test.
Both maps score 2,802 of its 2,819 households (99.473% weighted coverage).
Onion MSE rises from 0.345748 to 0.346913, a change of 0.001166 or 0.34%;
the conditional paired interval is [0.000223, 0.001975]. Egg MSE falls
from 0.741421 to 0.740324, a change of -0.001097 or -0.15%, with interval
[-0.003885, 0.001835]. There are 1,631 and 631 positive target observations,
respectively, on the fixed comparison population; resampling covers all
960 test PSUs. The small Onion deterioration is not hidden by the favorable
validation results. These checks support a limited practical partition,
not a claim that it improves welfare precision. The fine Preferred output
remains available and is still the default.

* Empirical-mask sensitivity

The simulation adapter retains all 14,009 Ghana household positivity masks
and their survey weights and markets, replacing identifiers with row numbers.
It reuses =survey_simulation.survey_inputs= and the CFE data-generating
process, with seeds 11, 12 and 13, a Salt loading of 0.6 and fine-good log
error standard deviation 0.2. Synthetic welfare and a complete numeric
control are independent of the masks. Thus the nineteen households with
missing real controls are present with synthetic controls; this is a
conditional sensitivity experiment, not a simulation of that missingness.

The =masked= regime combines homoskedastic fine-good errors with the actual
reporting pattern. The =substitutes= regime divides each member amount by
the number of positive members in its group, holding the no-error total
fixed when constituent loadings agree. The =unequal= regime uses that same
substitution mechanism with constituent loadings spanning 0.5--1.5 times
their group loading. Each replicate refits preparation, beta and welfare
for both maps, on a common anchor-observed comparison population.

The saved [[file:simulation_selected/simulation.csv][simulation results]] contain known-welfare errors and scoring
coverage for each mechanism and seed. These runs cannot establish how
survey reporting depends on true welfare or how instrument errors behave.

All eighteen fits succeed. Weighted scoring coverage is identical between
maps in every replicate, averaging 99.331%. In the masked regime the
selected map's core MSE rises by 0.23--2.15% across the three seeds. In the
substitutes regime it changes by -1.64% to +0.53%, and with unequal
constituent loadings by -1.56% to +0.19%. These are small sensitivity runs,
not precise Monte Carlo estimates. Even equal constituent loadings do not
guarantee a precision gain when reporting counts and error variances change.

* Publication and verification

The country table preserves all 205 original rows and their wave/FCT
cells, and appends 47 exact delivered labels with blank crosswalk cells.
Its 252 rows contain 242 distinct Preferred labels and 237 Aggregate
labels; nine Preferred names are historical country entries absent from
the delivered universe. Duplicate Preferred rows agree on Aggregate.
No new FCT assignment is inferred from a documentary crosswalk.

The nutrition reader now recognizes survey wave columns explicitly and
ignores Aggregate/display columns. Some appended exact names are spelling
aliases of the historical nutritional vocabulary. The spelling test still
checks the historical crosswalk; separate tests check the delivered axis
and the complete, unambiguous Aggregate assignment.

API verification checks =food_acquired= as a row-preserving rename and
=food_expenditures= as a sum of levels, on both purchased and total bases
for all seven waves, retaining missing cluster keys in the comparison.
Default/Preferred output is checked separately. No household records,
scores, identifiers, masks or fitted models are committed.

All 164 targeted tests pass: 61 isolated aggregation tests, 90 existing
configuration/label tests and 13 consolidated Ghana Aggregate tests. The
latter make nine public API calls and check every wave within each result;
there are no skips or errors. A separate comparison with the prepublication
inventory confirms identical Preferred vocabularies in every wave and all
752 item-by-wave expenditure totals on each basis. Maximum relative-scaled
differences are 2.54e-16 for purchases and 2.77e-16 for total recorded value.
All 739 nutritional wave rows and 195 existing Preferred-to-FCT lookups
match the prepublication table exactly. The API and skill-catalog audit
also passes.

The thirteen API tests emit 64 existing audit warnings, including missing
early Price/strata, quantity extremes, missing modern units and early
cluster keys. These data qualifications are retained; the checks do not
repair or suppress them. Missing cluster keys survive expenditure sums.

* Reproduction

Use the LSMS branch =feature/ghanalss-aggregate-curation= with the sibling
CFE branch =feature/prepare-data-scoring=. From the LSMS root:

#+begin_example
export OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS=1 OMP_NUM_THREADS=1 MKL_NUM_THREADS=1
export LSMS_BUILD_WORKERS=1 PYTHONPATH="$PWD/../CFEDemands:$PWD"

# Original candidates and the combined first draft.
.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation \
  --output SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/validation_purchased

# Additional candidates; Onion is reserved, so its group is excluded.
.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation \
  --additional SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/additional_candidates.yml \
  --names fine fish_fresh_frozen_named crab_shrimp bread_types named_spirits \
    tinned_milk_forms coconut_forms named_processed_fish \
  --output SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/validation_additional

.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation \
  --bootstrap-only 60 \
  --output SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/bootstrap_purchased

.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation \
  --probe-members 'Sugar (cubed)' 'Sugar (granulated)' Macaroni Spaghetti \
    'Cassava (white, flour)' 'Cassava (yellow, flour)' --probe-draws 60 \
  --output SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/probes_purchased

for basis in purchased total; do
  .venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation \
    --basis "$basis" --names fine draft \
    --specification SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/selected_groups.yml \
    --output "SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/validation_selected_$basis"
done

.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation \
  --names fine draft --evaluation test \
  --specification SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/selected_groups.yml \
  --output SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/test_selected

.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.ghana_validation --simulate \
  --specification SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/selected_groups.yml \
  --output SkunkWorks/aggregation/ghanalss/simulation_selected

# Idempotent publication: preserves the original crosswalk cells.
.venv/bin/python -m SkunkWorks.aggregation.publish_ghana

.venv/bin/python -m pytest --confcutdir=SkunkWorks/aggregation \
  SkunkWorks/aggregation -q
.venv/bin/python -m pytest --no-purge -q \
  tests/test_ghanalss_aggregate.py tests/test_ghanalss_food_label_canonical.py \
  tests/test_ghanalss_nutrition.py
#+end_example

The saved CSVs report all attempted candidates and missing constituent
coefficients. The first candidate run predates the paired-difference
reporting and reference-quantile fields; its point predictions use full-rank
calibrations and are unaffected by the later identification guard.
The later combined and final-test runs use those guards and the complete
PSU resampling population. The scripts reuse existing Country extraction,
CFE fitting/scoring, and Org editing machinery; implementation choices and
verification are recorded in the ledger.
