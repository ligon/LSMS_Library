#+title: Evaluating supplied food aggregations
#+author: Sue
#+date: 2026-09-16

* What is implemented

We can now compare supplied food partitions through the revised CFE fitting
and scoring pipeline. The [[file:evaluation.py][evaluator]] records fitting coverage, scoring
coverage, paired support, residual variances, loadings, and error when true
welfare is supplied. Semantic curation can provide candidate maps as it
develops. It needn't precede these statistical checks.

This is an experimental interface in =SkunkWorks=. It depends on CFEDemands
with =Regression.score_w=, implemented on =feature/prepare-data-scoring=
at commit =3cdfae9=. It reuses that estimator and the tested CFE data
generator; the [[file:../../.coder/ledger/cfe-aggregation-evaluation.md][ledger]] records the remaining assumptions.

* The comparison

The caller declares the fine expenditure universe, an exhaustive membership
map for each candidate, population weights, training and evaluation
households, and two reference sets. All observations of a household stay
on the same side of the split. Amounts are nonnegative; zeros and unreported
entries contribute zero to group totals within the declared questionnaire
universe. Summation precedes logging.

One reference set fixes the origin using training households. An unmerged
good anchors the units. For declared anchor loading \(b_a>0\), report
\[
  \widetilde w_{A,c}=
  \frac{\widehat\beta_{A,a}}{b_a}
  \left(\widehat w_{A,c}-\overline{\widehat w}_{A,R}\right),
\]
where the bar uses the declared population weights on the same training
reference cells \(R\) for every candidate. This fixes a convention; it
doesn't eliminate uncertainty in the fitted anchor loading. Held-out
outcomes and simulation truth never choose the transformation.

The second set fixes the evaluation population for comparable MSE. A
candidate that cannot score every positive-weight cell in that set gets
no comparable MSE. The evaluator doesn't silently take the intersection
of survivors. Coverage uses the entire declared evaluation population;
error outside the comparison set describes each candidate's own scored
subset and therefore has a different interpretation.

Identification is checked market by market using =Regression.w_cov().V22=.
A market containing only single-good fitting households cannot separate
its intercept from their welfare, even when loadings are well determined
elsewhere. Such scores are unavailable. They cannot contribute to the
normalization reference either.

Returned conditional fitting variances remain in the model's raw units.
They omit loading and partition-selection uncertainty and are not intervals
for the normalized held-out scores. The point-error experiment includes
parameter-estimation error by refitting each simulated sample.

* First reproducible experiment

[[file:simulation.py][The simulation]] uses =cfe.dgp.expenditures= with 240 households, two periods,
one market, one characteristic, and ten foods. Six foods have a common
loading of 1.2 in the baseline; four other loadings are 0.6, 0.4, 1.1, and
2.0. Log measurement errors have standard deviation 0.2. Sixty percent
of households form the training sample. The first good anchors the scale
at its true loading of 0.6.
Fitting uses =min_obs=30=, =min_prop_items=0.1=, and =alltm=True=.

In the sparse cases, one quarter of households report only foods from the
six-item pool. The common comparison and normalization sets are declared
using the other households. With =substitutes=, each cell reports exactly
one randomly chosen pool member. The =unequal= case changes those six
loadings to span 0.2--3.0. With =unreported=, initially positive pool
expenditures are independently unreported with probabilities depending on
true welfare, independently of expenditure errors conditional on welfare.
The =complete= case has no zeros.

The table gives means across seeds 11, 12, and 13. Each run fits the fine
partition, the six-food pool, and a broader pool that also absorbs the two
high-loading common foods. Every candidate scores the common comparison
set in these runs. Full results, including the broader pools, are in
[[file:example_results.csv][example_results.csv]].

#+name: tab:initial-partition-comparison
#+caption: Mean coverage and common-population MSE across three seeds.
| Mechanism    | Fine coverage | Pool coverage | Fine common MSE | Pool common MSE |
|--------------+---------------+---------------+-----------------+-----------------|
| Complete     |         1.000 |         1.000 |         0.00586 |         0.00737 |
| Substitutes  |         0.750 |         1.000 |         0.00941 |         0.00817 |
| Unequal beta |         0.750 |         1.000 |         0.01041 |         0.01166 |
| Unreported   |         0.764 |         0.918 |         0.00936 |         0.01440 |

These are checks of the comparison, not a precision study. Three seeds
don't establish a ranking. They do show that the implementation can expose
both coverage gains and error costs: rescuing sparse foods helps in the
equal-loading substitution example, while gains in coverage coexist with
greater common-population error under unequal loadings or underreporting.
The simulation gives no empirical recommendation for Uganda.

* Uganda observation patterns

The ten-food experiment understates the sparsity of Uganda food data. The
new [[file:survey_simulation.py][survey simulation]] keeps complete observed household masks, including
which foods occur together, and generates amounts with known welfare on
that support. It also shows that sparse item coverage needn't imply equally
low household scoring coverage after the preparation changes.

** What is observed

These profiles use =Country('Uganda').food_expenditures= with Preferred
labels. The denominator includes every household in =sample()=, including
households absent from the food table. Purchases and total recorded
acquisition are separate expenditure bases; the latter adds recorded values
of own production and in-kind receipts without imputing additional values.
Two tobacco labels are excluded. Full profiles are in
[[file:uganda_mask_profiles.csv][uganda_mask_profiles.csv]].

Joint support is still thinner. In 2019-20 the median distinct food pair
is jointly positive for only five households with purchases, or ten with
total acquisition, across the /full/ sample. Only 21.7% and 32.9% of
pairs respectively reach thirty observations even before the training split.
Marginal coverage alone misses that constraint on covariance estimation.

The counts depend on the universe being counted. The 2019-20 raw module
has 126 item codes per household, mapping to 124 distinct Preferred labels.
Of these, 116 appear in the delivered total-expenditure table, including
the two tobacco labels. We use its remaining 114 foods for both bases;
112 have any positive purchases. The 2010-11 delivered table has 70 labels
before removing tobacco. Thus the recollection of roughly 70 or 126 items
is consistent with the changing survey instrument, but questionnaire codes,
Preferred labels, and delivered positive foods are different counts.

The template is the library's delivered observation pattern. It inherits
the existing extraction rules, including Uganda's removal of records with
unknown unit codes; it isn't a reconstruction of every raw questionnaire
response. Zero and unavailable expenditures have the same treatment here.
The profile records API audit warning classes without household identifiers.

** Simulation and normalization

We use 2019-20, whose food module has a documented seven-day recall window.
Markets come from household =sample()['Region']=, avoiding the known loss
of movers through cluster joins. Three of its 3,098 sample households have
missing Region, contrary to an older completeness statement in
=Uganda/_/CONTENTS.org=. They are reported and excluded before simulation;
all 3,095 remaining households have positive cross-sectional weights. The
41 households without positive total food expenditure, or 84 without
positive purchases, remain in the original profile denominators.

Each replicate preserves every usable household's complete Boolean mask,
Region, and survey weight. We generate one synthetic welfare draw and one
complete numeric characteristic per row using =cfe.dgp.expenditures=, with
unit prices and independent log expenditure shocks of standard deviation
0.2. These draws are independent of the observed mask. Neither actual
expenditure amounts nor observed household characteristics enter the
simulation. Consequently it doesn't reproduce losses from missing controls
or possible dependence between welfare and observation in the real survey.

The supplied partition uses existing Aggregate labels, except that
=Cassava (flour)= remains a singleton because two codes assign its Preferred
label to different groups. This leaves 72 groups from 114 foods. The map
is a trial input, not a certification of economically admissible composites.
Group loadings are a fixed permutation of 72 evenly spaced values from
0.4 to 2; Salt remains a singleton with loading 0.6. This construction
doesn't use estimated loadings or evaluation outcomes to choose groups.

Sixty percent of households train the model. The reference and common
comparison populations are declared before fitting: households reporting
Salt, on the training and evaluation sides respectively. The same weighted
reference and Salt loading fix the origin and scale for both partitions.
The common comparison covers, on average, 86.8% of evaluation weight for
purchases and 89.5% for total acquisition. Error there says nothing directly
about the remaining households. Scoring coverage includes them all.

The three amount mechanisms share exactly the same masks and random draws:

- Masked :: Each positive fine amount follows the original CFE DGP, with
  equal loadings inside each curated group and homoskedastic fine-good log
  errors. Summing introduces variation from the number of positive members.
- Substitutes :: Divide each positive amount by the number of positive
  members of its group in that household. With equal loadings and no
  expenditure noise, this holds the group total fixed whenever at least one
  member is present. It adds an allocation term to fine-good log expenditure;
  those fine-good regression errors needn't remain homoskedastic or independent.
- Unequal :: Keep that substitution mechanism, but spread loadings within
  each multi-food group from 0.5 to 1.5 times its group loading. Singleton
  loadings stay unchanged.

These mechanisms express different assumptions about amounts behind the
same observation pattern. The mask alone can't choose among them.

** Coverage and error

We ran seeds 11, 12, and 13 with =min_obs=30=, =min_prop_items=0.1=, and
=alltm=True=. All 36 candidate fits completed and scored their fixed common
comparison populations. The [[file:uganda_mask_results.csv][replicate results]] retain counts, warnings,
coverage, and errors. The table below compares coverage on the /same training
population/: the original preparation function at CFEDemands =e4fc38f=,
the revised fitting selector, and frozen scoring of all training households
using the revised fit. We execute the historical function from git rather
than recreate its algorithm. Only that first column uses the old selector.

#+name: tab:uganda-preparation-coverage
#+caption: Mean survey-weighted training coverage (%); three household splits.
| Basis     | Partition | Original fit | Revised fit | Revised frozen scoring |
|-----------+-----------+--------------+-------------+------------------------|
| Purchased | Fine      |         63.8 |        88.2 |                   96.6 |
| Purchased | Curated   |         77.6 |        89.2 |                   96.7 |
| Total     | Fine      |         82.0 |        92.0 |                   97.7 |
| Total     | Curated   |         88.0 |        92.8 |                   97.7 |

Coverage is identical across the three amount mechanisms because their
supports agree. The original purchases fit loses many households before
the sparse goods are removed. Reconsidering them against the smaller
retained universe recovers much of that loss. After the revisions,
aggregation adds about one percentage point of fitting coverage and almost
no scoring coverage with this particular grouping. Held-out scoring
coverage averages 96.0% for fine purchases and 96.1% for curated purchases;
both total-acquisition partitions score 97.5%. These are conditional on the
declared known-Region, positive-weight population and complete synthetic
controls, not estimates of real-world welfare accuracy or complete-data
coverage for every downstream fit.

#+name: tab:uganda-mask-error
#+caption: Mean squared error on the fixed Salt-observed evaluation population; three seeds.
| Basis     | Amount mechanism | Fine MSE | Curated MSE |
|-----------+------------------+----------+-------------|
| Purchased | Masked           |  0.00598 |     0.00665 |
| Purchased | Substitutes      |  0.00713 |     0.00547 |
| Purchased | Unequal          |  0.00849 |     0.00811 |
| Total     | Masked           |  0.00231 |     0.00348 |
| Total     | Substitutes      |  0.00449 |     0.00207 |
| Total     | Unequal          |  0.00552 |     0.00401 |

Under equal-loading substitution, the curated partition reduces common
MSE by about 23% for purchases and 54% for total acquisition. In the masked
experiment it raises MSE by about 11% and 51%. Equal betas alone don't make
the two experiments equivalent: variable numbers of positive components
change group totals and their errors. Nor does moderate loading dispersion
necessarily eliminate an aggregation benefit, as the unequal case shows.
Three seeds establish neither a reliable ranking nor an optimal partition.

Run from LSMS with access to the Uganda microdata. The LSMS environment
supplies the Country dependencies; =PYTHONPATH= selects the sibling CFE
feature branch. Experiment outputs contain only aggregate support profiles
and simulation summaries. Country may populate its normal local data cache;
no household-level experiment outputs are committed.

* Next statistical work

Use more replications and broader variations in beta dispersion, expenditure
shares, and dependence between observation and welfare to study the
coverage/error frontier. The observed-mask experiment now supplies realistic
support; its synthetic controls and amount mechanisms remain assumptions.
Empirical validation needs a declared country sample and reserved outcomes;
true-welfare MSE is unavailable there. Candidate selection belongs inside
training data, with a further untouched sample for assessing the selected
procedure. An anchor basket, alternative preparation policies, and a loss
for unscored households remain explicit choices. None prevents using this
evaluator now.
