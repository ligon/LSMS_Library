* Arithmetic correction versus physical assumptions
The old step 2 took a median of Expenditure where the definition required
Expenditure/Quantity. Its ratio therefore had units of kg per transaction,
then was multiplied by quantity again. That is a dimensional arithmetic defect.

To separate it from the item-axis design, a *labelled, unshipped in-memory
counterfactual* changes exactly that one expression in =e07e2a93f= and nothing
else. It loses *280185.922520 kg*, Energy 527469544.708529, A 83160295.414035,
and B12 662508.316752. Moving from that counterfactual to the actual
=60f98ab1d= item-axis/support code restores *20920.767476 kg*, Energy
23731471.738485 and B12 48637.275419, while A falls another 1987284.648465.
The two sum to the shipped commit's delta. This isolates the denominator's
numerical effect; it does not validate a common-price assumption as a weighing
method. Neither counterfactual is proposed as a new policy.

*Source-provenance correction [2026-10-14 release-draft review].* The first
version of this report called row 4003 a "New source contradiction, left
unresolved" and said "Those claims cannot both describe that numeric record."
That conclusion omitted a specific derivation note in the SAME row. The
stronger claim of competing unsupported intended values is withdrawn; the
complete recorded provenance supports a specific override of a generic
starting assumption. This correction changes no numerical replay result.

=nutrition.org='s =fct= row 4003,
=SWEETPOTATO,YELLOW,W/SKIN,FRESH,RAW=, records =vit_a_rae_mcg= *225 per 100 g*,
which correctly becomes 2250/kg in the CSV and served FCT. Its =fct_source=
is =HarvestPlus Uganda=, =fct_source_code= is =11507=, and
=fct_source_descr= reads:
#+begin_quote
Based on USDA #11507 Sweet potato,unprepared; vitamin A and carotenoids assumed = 0.
#+end_quote
But the adjacent =water_source_descr= also records the carotenoid derivation:
#+begin_quote
Applied water content of 67.4 g based on an average of 3 yellow flesh varieties reported by Kidmose et al., 2007 (Salyboro,199062.1 and Nyathi Odiewo);betacarotene content also derived from the mean of the same 3 varieties.
#+end_quote
The retention-factor source/code/description fields are empty. The numeric
record is retinol =0=, alpha-carotene =10=, beta-carotene =2697=,
beta-cryptoxanthin =0=, vitamin A RAE =225= and vitamin A IU =4503=, per
100 g. Using the dietary-carotenoid RAE factors stated by the
[[https://ods.od.nih.gov/factsheets/VitaminA-HealthProfessional/][NIH Office of Dietary Supplements]] (read 2026-10-14),
=0 + 2697/12 + 10/24 + 0/24 = 225.1666667=, which rounds to the recorded
whole-microgram =225=. The nearby WHITE sweet-potato row 3003 carries the
same generic zero note, a West 1988 water adjustment, and zero carotenoids
and RAE. The yellow row's specific note therefore supplies a coherent
explanation for its nonzero value; it is not evidence that zero should
replace 225.

The interpretation is a recorded specific yellow-variety carotenoid override
to the generic USDA starting assumptions. It is an inference from the full
record, not independent verification of Kidmose et al. (2007): that paper's
measurements, variety selection and preparation basis have not been checked
in this investigation. Any follow-up should clarify the general note's scope
and confirm the cited derivation/source version before proposing a numerical
change. The same fields and numbers are present at =8ec0bab4e=, =v0.14.0=
and this investigation's integrated parent. Thus this clarification is
neither a #850 nor a #967 numerical change.
