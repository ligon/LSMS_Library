* The one-paragraph answer

GhanaSPS is *not* the poor relation its non-LSMS provenance suggests.  Its
Section 4 is a *plot-level* agricultural module in *all three waves*, with a
plot identifier that joins 100 per cent to the already-wired =sample()=
household set.  All five corpus agriculture features have real source data in
all three waves.  =plot_features=, =crop_production=, =plot_labor= and
=livestock= are genuinely constructible panel-wide; =plot_inputs= is
constructible but *asymmetric* --- the wave that has input quantities for
chemicals (2009-10) has none for seed, and the two waves that have seed
quantities (2013-14, 2017-18) attribute chemical quantities to plots only
through a decodable but *inferred* index.  Nothing here warrants a closing
=absent_verdicts= row; every cell is =todo=.

The single most consequential finding is the one the brief asked for: *the
unit of observation is the plot in every wave*, and it does not change.  What
changes is the *shape* of the questionnaire around it --- wide-per-plot in
2009-10, long-per-plot in 2013-14 and 2017-18 --- and the presence of a
*second season* in 2009-10 that the later waves dropped.

** Not previously recorded --- new, and each cost real measurement

*** 1. =Crop_encrypted/= is Boxcryptor-encrypted AND unobtainable

=2017-18/Data/Crop_encrypted/= holds =cropprice_W3.dta.bc=,
=croppriceother_W3.dta.bc=, =unitcon_W3nonpii.dta.bc= and =FolderKey.bch=.
=.bc= / =.bch= is the Boxcryptor file/folder-key convention, and there is no
passphrase anywhere in this repository.  Independently of the encryption,
=get_data_file()= returns *=None=* for all four --- =permissions()= reports
={'wb_api': 'read', 'ligonresearch_s3': 'read'}=, so the refusal is not a
permissions gate; the blob simply does not come back through the sanctioned
path.

Consequence, and it is scoped: *wave 3 has no usable crop price table and no
usable unit-conversion table.*  That does not block =crop_production= (the
harvest module carries its own reported quantity and native unit), but it does
mean W3 crop *valuation* and any kg conversion would have to come from
somewhere else.

*** 2. Plot size converts to hectares exactly, and 2009-10 hands you the factors

=2009-10/Data/S4AII.dta= ships a producer-computed =area_ha= ("Land size in
hectares"), non-null for *5,553 of 5,698 plots (97.5 per cent)*.  Dividing it
by the reported native size recovers the conversion constants to float
precision (standard deviation ~1e-8 within each unit):

| native unit | hectares per unit | plots |
|-------------+-------------------+-------|
| Acre        |          0.404694 |  4,538 |
| Pole        |          0.409551 |    717 |
| Rope        |          0.236342 |    238 |
| Plot        |          0.102388 |     60 |

W2 and W3 ship *no* =area_ha= --- but they report the same native units
(=totalsize= non-null 100.0 per cent in W2, 99.6 per cent in W3; units
Acres / Poles / Ropes-or-"Robes" / Plots).  So the canonical
=plot_features.Area= in hectares is reachable in all three waves *using wave
1's own factors*.  Note W3 spells "Rope" as *"Robes"* and splits the acre
answer across =plotsizeacres= (yes/no) and =plotunit=; the naive
=plotsizeunit= column omits acres entirely (4,791 acre plots are only visible
in =plotunit=).  Reading =plotsizeunit= alone in W3 loses 89 per cent of the
plots' units.

*** 3. 2009-10 is a TWO-SEASON wave; 2013-14 and 2017-18 are not

This is the largest cross-wave asymmetry and it is invisible from filenames.

- =S4AV1= / =S4AV2= are the *major* and *minor* season crop-harvest blocks.
- =S4AVI1= / =S4AVI2= are the major and minor season chemical blocks.
- =S4AVIII1= / =S4AVIII2= are the major and minor season seed blocks.
- =S4AIX1..4= are four *stages* of major-season labour; =S4AIX5..8= the same
  four stages for the minor season.

Fill rates corroborate the split: =S4AV1= crop-1 harvest quantity is non-null
on 4,825 plots, =S4AV2= on *1,412*; major-season labour fills 2,516 plots,
minor-season 544.  Major-season start months cluster February--June, minor
August--September.

*** 4. A producer LABEL ERROR in =S4AV2=, settled by the questionnaire (C4)

Every quantity/value variable in =S4AV2= carries a variable label saying
*"in the last major season"* --- e.g. =s4v_a122i= reads "A122.1 Quantity of
the crop harvested in the last major season-crop 1".  *That label is wrong.*
=Documentation/Household_questionnaire_Part_A.pdf= reads, verbatim at A120:

#+begin_quote
CROPS LAST MINOR SEASON: CROP (HARVESTS) 1 --- ASK ABOUT CROP HARVESTS IN ORDER
OF MOST IMPORTANT TO LEAST IMPORTANT ... A122. What is the quantity harvested
in the last minor season?
#+end_quote

Anyone wiring =crop_production= for wave 1 off the =.dta= labels alone will
stamp both blocks =major= and silently double-count the season.  The
questionnaire, and the parallel =S4AVI2= label ("In the past *minor* season,
have you used chemical 1 on your plot"), both settle it.

*** 5. The four wave-1 labour blocks are stages, named in the questionnaire

Also from Part A, verbatim section headers:

| file    | questions | stage                                                                                          |
|---------+-----------+------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------|
| S4AIX1  | A290--A298 | land preparation (clearing/weeding before planting, seeding/nursery, planting, transplanting) |
| S4AIX2  | A299--A307 | field management (weeding after planting, fertilizer and pesticide application, irrigation)   |
| S4AIX3  | A308--A316 | harvesting                                                                                    |
| S4AIX4  | A317--A325 | post-harvest activities (including preservation for storage)                                  |
| S4AIX5--8 | A327--A362 | the same four stages, minor season                                                          |

Nothing in the =.dta= says this; the variable labels are identical across the
four files.  Without the questionnaire the four blocks are indistinguishable

*** 6. Wave 2's chemical-to-plot key is an UNRESOLVED TEMPLATE STRING

=2013-14/04l_chemquestions.dta= records chemical quantity at the household
level and allocates it to plots via =chemplots1..30= plus
=chemperplot1..10_chemquant=.  The *values* of =chemplots1= are the literal
survey-software placeholders:

: '%pplotnamearray[1]', '%pplotnamearray[2]', ... '%pplotnamearray[9]'

i.e. the plot's *ordinal position in the household's plot array*, never
substituted with a plot name.  Recovering =plotid= from that requires assuming
array position k is the k-th plot in the household's roster.  That assumption
is probably right and is definitely *not recorded anywhere*.

Wave 3 does not have this problem: =2017-18/04l_chemquestions.chemplots= is a
plain space-separated plot list (=''=, ='1'=, ='1 2'=, ='1 2 3 4'=, ...).

In 2013-14, *10,884 of 14,860 rows have =quantity= == 0* and the =animal=

=Giunea fowl=, and so on, plus =CAT=, =DOG=, =DUCK=, =Turkey=, and one row
literally spelled ='50'=.  The seven core species (=cattle=, =sheep=, =goats=,
=pigs=, =rabbits=, =chickens=, =draught animals=) are lower-case and clean; the

*** 8. Every wave-1 money variable is split cedis / pesewas

=S4AIV=, =S4AV1/2=, =S4AVI1/2=, =S4AVIII1/2=, =S4BI=, =S3AI= all record value
as a *pair* --- ".1 (cedis)" and ".2 (pessewas)" (the misspelling is the
producer's).  Any value column is =cedis + pesewas/100=.  Reading only the
=i= member silently truncates.  W2 and W3 record a single amount.

*** 9. =04o_cropsalesstoresservices= is not crop sales

Despite the name it is *agricultural services* --- tractor/plough hire and
fertiliser/chemical application services (=ploughtractor_serviced*=,
=fertilizers_chem_serviced*=).  234 rows in W2, 9,872 in W3.  Crop sales are
in =04o_cropsalesstoresquestions=.

*** 10. The pp/ph skill's GhanaSPS row does not describe these waves

=.claude/skills/add-feature/pp-ph/SKILL.md= lists GhanaSPS as
"Some waves | Planting/harvest questionnaires".  *No wave of GhanaSPS has a
post-planting and a post-harvest round.*  I searched every wave's
=04h_agsection= / =S4AII= for a round / visit / season / wave variable and
found none; each wave is a single visit collecting the season by recall.

The skill is not useless here --- wave 1's major/minor season split is
*exactly* the same duplicate-index hazard one level in (two blocks about the
same plot needing distinct index values) --- but a reader who takes the row
literally will go looking for a second round that does not exist.  I have not
edited the skill; flagging it for the owner.

* The grain question, answered plainly

*The unit of observation is the plot in every wave, and it does not differ
between waves.*  What differs:

1. *Layout.*  W1 is wide (one row per plot, crop/chemical/seed detail spread
   across numbered variable families, capped at 5 harvest records and 4 seeds
   per plot-season).  W2 and W3 are long (a separate file at plot-crop and
   plot-stage grain).  A W1 build needs a melt; W2/W3 do not.
2. *Season.*  W1 carries an explicit major/minor season level.  W2 and W3 ask
   about "last season" only.
3. *Stage naming.*  W1's labour stages are four fixed operations known only
   from the questionnaire; W2 has 7 stages in a =stagenum= / =stage= column;
   W3 has =stageid= / =stagename=.
4. *Crop-sales grain.*  Plot-crop in W1 (=S4BI= carries =plotno=) and W3
   (=04o= carries =plotid=), but *household-crop in W2* --- W2's =04o= has no
   =plotid= at all.
5. *Livestock roster shape.*  W2 asks all 7 species of everyone (73 per cent
   zero rows) with a free-text tail; W1 and W3 list only what is owned, with
   clean labelled categories.

*Is =plotid= a panel identifier?*  *W1 -> W2 yes; W2 -> W3 no.*  This was
filed as undetermined in the first draft of this note and has since been
*tested*.

Among the 2,455 households present in both W1 and W2, *4,916 of W1's 5,082
plots (96.7 per cent) reappear as the same =(household, plot number)= in W2's
=04h_preroster=* --- which is explicitly a carry-forward list (=knowplot=,
=stillowner=).  The numbering is carried across W1 -> W2 by construction.

W3 ships no such carry-forward file, so the 71.3 per cent positional overlap
between W2 and W3 (2,851 of W2's 3,996 plots in the 2,181 common households)
proves nothing on its own --- it is consistent with continuity *and* with
coincidence, because a household with three plots in each wave overlaps on
three plot numbers whatever the numbering means.

So it was tested against a *permutation null*: compare attributes that ought to
be time-invariant for a given piece of land, under the true =(i, plotid)=
pairing, against the same comparison with =plotid= permuted *within household*
in W3 (20 draws).  If the numbering carries information, the true pairing must
beat the null.

| attribute (matched W2/W3 plots)  |  true | null (permuted)  |    lift |
|----------------------------------+-------+------------------+---------|
| plot area within 10 per cent     | 15.2% | 14.1 +/- 0.4     | +1.1 pt |
| soil colour identical            | 34.9% | 34.6 +/- 0.3     | +0.3 pt |
| distance from home within 20%    | 22.0% | 21.8 +/- 0.3     | +0.2 pt |

Restricting to *multi-plot* households --- 561 of the 2,181 common households
have exactly one plot in each wave, for which permuting is a no-op and the null
is therefore inflated --- barely moves it: area 15.9 per cent true against 14.4
per cent null, a lift of *+1.5 points*.

*That is a negative result, not an inconclusive one.*  If =plotid= tracked the
same land, area agreement would sit far above the null; it sits on top of it.
The reading is that *=plotid= in W2 and W3 is a within-interview enumeration
order, not a persistent plot identifier*.  Do not join plot-level GhanaSPS data
across those two waves on =(i, plotid)=.

Two honest caveats on the test.  =plotlocation_region= was included and is
useless here --- it is populated for only 66 of the 2,851 matched plots, so its
0.0 per cent agreement is an artefact of emptiness and is *not* evidence.  And
the low *absolute* agreement rates partly reflect genuine recall noise (farmers
re-estimating plot size four years apart); that is exactly why the comparison
is against a null rather than against a threshold --- the null absorbs the
noise, and the lift is what carries the signal.

** =crop_production= --- CONSTRUCTIBLE, all three waves; sales column asymmetric

Canonical grain is =(t, i, plot, j, u, condition, season)= (Uganda) or
=(t, i, plot, crop)= (Nigeria).  GhanaSPS gives:

| wave    | source                | grain                              | rows with a real record |
|---------+-----------------------+------------------------------------+-------------------------|
| 2009-10 | =S4AV1= + =S4AV2=         | plot x season x crop-slot (melt)   | ~4,825 major / 1,412 minor plots, <= 5 crops each |
| 2013-14 | =04n_harvestquestions=  | plot-crop                          | 8,229 (10 dup tuples)   |
| 2017-18 | =04n_harvestquestions=  | plot-crop                          | 7,391 after dropping 1,926 skips |

Columns available: =Quantity= (=harvestquantity= / =s4v_a81i=), =u=
(=harvestunit= / =s4v_a81ii=, a ~45-code native unit list in all waves),
harvest month (W2/W3 =harvestmonths=; W1 season months in =S4AIX1/5=), damage
quantity, and expected-still-to-harvest quantity.

=Quantity_sold= / =Value_sold=:

- *W1*: =S4BI= is plot-crop (=plotno= present, 27,576 of 27,591 non-null) ---
  4,752 real sale records with total revenue in cedis+pesewas.
- *W2*: =04o_cropsalesstoresquestions= has *no =plotid=* --- household-crop
  only.  Worse, sales are split across *nine buyer channels*, each with its own
  quantity, unit and price (=samecomm=, =othercomm=, =traders=, =contractors=,
  =orgs=, =agg=, =outgrow=, =coop=, =other=).  Summing them is a
  transformation, and the units can differ between channels.  I would emit NaN
  for W2, exactly as Nigeria does for its W3--W5.
- *W3*: same nine-channel structure but *with* =plotid=, key unique on
  =(FPrimary, plotid, cropcode)= with 0 duplicates.

*No =condition= level exists* in any wave.  GhanaSPS instead records the
*part* of the crop harvested (=s4bi_b63ii= / =s4v_a80ii=: Leaves / Branches /
Bark / Sap / Stem / Roots-tuber / Fruit-Seeds-Nuts / Bulb) --- a different
distinction, and one the canonical schema has no home for.  Do not force-fit
it into =condition=.

*Risks*: (a) the =S4AV2= major/minor label error, which is a silent
double-count if missed; (b) W1's 5-harvest-record cap per plot-season truncates
plots with more crops (=A78= lists up to 10 crops grown); (c) crop
vocabularies drift --- 42 crop names in W2, 41 in W3, 31 shared, and the
differences are mostly spelling (=papaya= + =pawpaw= in W2 vs =pawpaw/papaya=
in W3; =groundnut/peanut= vs =groundnut/ pea nut=) so a =harmonize_crop= table
is needed but is not hard; (d) crop *codes* are not stable (W3 introduces
=-666= for "other").

** =plot_labor= --- CONSTRUCTIBLE, all three waves, at a RICHER grain than the corpus has

| wave    | source        | grain                                                    | rows   |
|---------+---------------+----------------------------------------------------------+--------|
| 2009-10 | =S4AIX1..8=     | plot x season(2) x stage(4) x source(casual/permanent/family) x group(men/women/children) | 8 files x ~5,700 |
| 2013-14 | =04m_aglabourquestions= | plot x stage(7) x source(self/family/communal/hired/other) | 32,851 |
| 2017-18 | =04m_aglabourquestions= | plot x stage x source                                | 20,663 |

Person-days are directly reported: W1 gives days *and* average hours/day *and*
number of workers separately for each of nine cells; W2/W3 give
=familymendays= / =familywomendays= / =communal*days= / =hired*days= /
=other*days= plus =personaldays= for the respondent's own labour.  Uganda's
=PersonDays= convention (row-sum of the man/woman/child day cells of *one*
question) transfers directly.

=Wage=: W2 has =hiredavgpayman= / =hiredavgpaywoman= / =hiredavepaychild= plus
=hiredpayunit= (per day / week / month / plot / acre / pole / rope) in the
*plot*-level =04m_aglabour=; W3 folds the same block into the stage-level file
and *drops the child rate*.  W1 has hired-labour payment in the =S4AIX=
blocks.  Note the wage is a *rate with a unit*, not a total cash amount ---
Uganda's and Nigeria's =Wage= is cash paid.  These are not the same quantity.

*The honest complication*: GhanaSPS's natural extra index level is =stage=,
which does not exist in the corpus vocabulary (Uganda and Nigeria carry
=season= instead).  =CLAUDE.md= already records that =plot_labor= is
deliberately *not* registered in =index_info= because per-country index names
diverge; adding =stage= (and, for W1 only, =season=) widens that divergence.
That is a design decision for the owner, not a data problem.

*Risks*: (a) the four W1 stage files are indistinguishable from their variable
labels --- get the stage assignment wrong and the rows are mislabelled but
still present, which is the quiet kind of error; (b) W1 has no =stage= or
=season= column to read, so both levels must be *assigned by the script* from
which file the row came; (c) W3's =04m= has 640 null-key rows to drop.

** =livestock= --- CONSTRUCTIBLE, all three waves; wave 2 needs real cleaning

Canonical grain =(t, i, animal)= with =HeadCount= / =HeadAcquired= /
=HeadSold= / =Value=.

| column       | 2009-10                        | 2013-14                | 2017-18                |
|--------------+--------------------------------+------------------------+------------------------|
| =HeadCount=    | =s3ai_1=                         | =quantity=               | =quantity=               |
| =HeadAcquired= | *not asked*                    | *not asked*            | *not asked*            |
| =HeadSold=     | *not asked*                    | *not asked*            | =quantitysold=           |
| =Value=        | =s3ai_3i/ii= (cedis+pesewas)     | =currentvalue=           | =currentvalue=           |

*=Value= is a herd total, not a per-head price, in ALL THREE waves.*  This was
the biggest open item in an earlier draft of this note and it is now *settled
by C4 on all three instruments*, which agree verbatim:

- W1, =S4AI= variable label :: "Q3.1 What is the current value of these
  animals if you sold *all of them* (cedis)".
- W2, =ISSER_Yale_Part A_WAVE II.docx= :: "What is the current value of these
  animals, *if you sold all of them*?  Gh Cedis and pesewas".
- W3, =HH_Questionnaire_23_10.docx= :: "What is the current value of the
  [animal type], *if you sold all of them*?  Indicate amount as a decimal
  value (in Ghana cedis and pesewas).  For example, enter 2.50 for 2 Ghana
  cedis and 50 pesewas."

Uganda's and Nigeria's =Value= is the reported value of *one* head ("if you
sold one today").  These are *not the same quantity*, and mapping GhanaSPS's
column onto the canonical =Value= without saying so would put a herd total in
a per-head column.  Either divide by =HeadCount= (a transformation, so not in
core) or declare a distinct column.  Note also that W3 states the amount as a
*decimal cedis* value, so no cedis/pesewas recombination is needed there.

GhanaSPS also carries per-species *expenses* (feed bought, feed home-produced,
veterinary, shelter, labour, water, other) and *revenue* in all three waves ---
richer than the canonical =livestock= schema, and with no home in it.

*Risks*: (a) W2's 10,884 zero rows and 69-spelling free-text tail; (b) the
herd-total-vs-per-head mismatch above --- now a *known* mismatch to declare
around, not an unknown; (c) W1's cedis/pesewas pair; (d) species vocabulary
differs --- W1 has "Drought Animal" and "Other Farm Animals", W3 has "Fish" and
"Draught animal (donkey, horse, bullock)", W2 has lower-case free text; a
=harmonize_species= mapping is needed per wave.

This is the one feature I would not call constructible without qualification.
The canonical convention (Uganda, Nigeria) is that *every row carries a

GhanaSPS meets that unevenly:

* Ranked recommendation

Effort estimates are relative and assume a competent agent with this note in
hand.  All five need =materialize: make= --- none of them is expressible in
=data_info.yml=, because every one requires either a melt (W1) or a
concatenation of per-wave files with different column names.

*If only one gets dispatched, dispatch =plot_features=.*  The list below is
ordered by effort, and on effort alone =livestock= is the easier start.  On
*leverage* the answer is different, and leverage is what matters when the
question is what to build first:

1. =plot_features= is the keystone.  Three of the other four features key on
   the plot identifier it defines.  Wiring it first sets that convention once,
   deliberately; wiring anything else first leaves the convention to be
   invented ad hoc by whoever gets to =crop_production=.
2. It carries =Area=, the *only* column the canonical schema marks *required*
   anywhere in the agriculture set, and that column is provably reachable in
   all three waves (the wave-1 hectare factors above).  High confidence, high
   value.
3. Its risks are mechanical and checkable --- read the right unit column, apply
   the right factor, map a stable tenure vocabulary.  None of them is a
   judgement call about what a number means.
4. It is now also the natural home for the =plotid=-is-not-a-panel-id finding.
   That fact has to be written down in the first plot-keyed feature that lands,
   or the second one to land will quietly join on it.

** 1. =livestock= --- do this first

- Effort :: *small*.  One source file per wave, household-animal grain,
  key already unique in all three.
- Why first :: it is the only one of the five with no plot key to get wrong, it
  exercises the =(t, i, animal)= index that is already registered in
  =index_info=, and it produces a table three other countries can be compared
  against immediately.
- Specific risk :: W2's free-text tail and 73 per cent zero rows.  The
  =Value= question that used to sit here is *settled*: all three instruments
  ask for the herd total ("if you sold all of them"), so the column must be
  declared as a herd value or divided by =HeadCount= in a transformation ---
  it must not be quietly aligned to Uganda's per-head =Value=.

** 2. =plot_features= --- highest value per unit of effort

- Effort :: *small-to-medium*.  Two files per wave (size/soil from
  =04h_agsection= or =S4AII=+=S4AIII=, tenure from =04i_landtenure= or =S4AIV=),
  a 1:1 merge on a key that is already unique.
- Why second :: it establishes the =plot_id= that the other three plot features
  key on, and =Area= --- the only *required* column --- is reachable in all
  three waves with exact factors.  Roughly 15,750 plot-rows panel-wide.
- Specific risk :: *the W3 unit column, and it is silent.*  Read =plotunit=,
  not =plotsizeunit=.  =plotsizeunit= omits acres entirely, so 4,791 of 5,366
  W3 plots (*89.3 per cent*) have a null unit under the naive reading --- and
  therefore a null =Area=, the required column.  Note what does *not* save you:
  the Site B null-read guard fires on a required column that is *100 per cent*
  null in a wave, and this lands at 89.3 per cent.  It will not fire.  Nor will
  =_assert_built_required_columns=, which checks presence, not content.  This
  failure ships green.
- Second risk :: do not declare =Irrigated= from =othersource= without deciding
  that "non-rain water source" is what the canonical column means.
- Third risk :: =plot_id= does not identify the same land across W2 and W3
  (permutation test above).  Build per-wave and *say so* in
  =data_scheme.yml= --- the =t= level already scopes every row correctly, so
  nothing needs to be prevented, but the limitation must be written down where
  the next person will read it.

** 3. =crop_production= --- the substantive prize, but W1 is real work

- Effort :: *medium* for W2/W3 (already long-format, one file each);
  *medium-to-large* for W1 (melt 5 crop slots x 2 seasons out of a wide file,
  and get the season assignment right against a label that lies).
- Why third :: it is what most users mean by "agriculture data", and W2/W3
  alone give ~15,600 plot-crop harvest records.  Consider shipping W2+W3
  first and adding W1 as a second PR --- the wave asymmetry is honest and the
  =t= slice makes it visible.
- Specific risk :: the =S4AV2= label error (silent season double-count); the
  =Quantity_sold= asymmetry (plot-crop in W1/W3, household-crop in W2, and
  nine buyer channels in W2/W3 that must not be summed in core).

** 4. =plot_labor= --- constructible but needs an index decision first

- Effort :: *medium* for W2/W3; *large* for W1 (eight files, four
  script-assigned stages, two script-assigned seasons).
- Why fourth :: the data is excellent --- 53,500 plot-stage rows in W2+W3
  alone --- but the natural index carries a =stage= level the corpus does not
  have, and that is a decision the owner should take before an agent bakes it
  into a script.
- Specific risk :: W1's stage assignment comes from the questionnaire, not the
  data.  A wrong assignment is invisible in every automated check.

** 5. =plot_inputs= --- wire it last, or scope it down

- Effort :: *large*, and the effort does not buy a uniform table.
- Why last :: the coverage is genuinely ragged (chemicals-with-quantity in W1
  only at plot level; seed-with-quantity in W2/W3 only; organic never), and the
  W2 plot attribution rests on an unrecorded ordering assumption.
- Recommendation :: either (a) build only the rows that carry a real reported
  quantity and accept that W1 has no seed rows and W2/W3 have inferred
  chemical-plot links, or (b) defer until someone reads the W2 instrument and
  confirms the =%pplotnamearray= ordering.  Do *not* introduce flag-only rows
  to fill the organic gap.
