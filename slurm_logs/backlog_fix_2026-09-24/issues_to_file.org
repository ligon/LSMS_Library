* Malawi crop_production: the defensive collapse in assemble_crop_production COMPOSES a row and silently drops 27 sales / 373,600 MWK

** Symptom

The grain-guard groupby at the end of
=lsms_library/countries/Malawi/_/malawi.py::assemble_crop_production=
(=_grain = ['t', 'i', 'plot_id', 'crop', 'u', 'condition']=; reducers
=Quantity: 'sum'=, =crop_variety: 'first'=, =Quantity_sold: 'first'=,
=Value_sold: 'first'=, =_branch: 'first'=) fires when a household grew
two same-label VARIETIES of one crop -- distinct =_crop_code= values that
=harmonize_crop= maps to one Preferred Label -- on one plot, in one unit
and one shelled/unshelled state.  The known pairs: Citrus 1005 / 1010 /
1011, Groundnut 11 / 12 / 16, Maize 1 / 3 (local vs. hybrid / composite).
The two rows are merged into one: harvest =Quantity= is SUMMED across the
varieties, =crop_variety= is the first row's, and where BOTH varieties
were sold =Quantity_sold= / =Value_sold= keep the FIRST variety's sale and
drop the other.  The served row is a composite that exists nowhere in the
survey: the plot's whole harvest against one variety's sale.

Measured on the cold build of =fix/malawi-derivations= (2026-09-25 review;
=collapse_loss.log=), identical on the GH #833 bundle:

| wave    | plot-crop groups collapsed | groups with >= 2 sold rows | sales dropped by first() | MWK dropped | of which carried a registry key |
|---------+----------------------------+----------------------------+--------------------------+-------------+---------------------------------|
| 2010-11 |                        127 |                          6 |                        6 |      28,900 |                               2 |
| 2013-14 |                         54 |                          2 |                        3 |      18,200 |                               3 |
| 2016-17 |                          7 |                          1 |                        1 |      50,000 |                               1 |
| 2019-20 |                         98 |                         16 |                       17 |     276,500 |                               5 |
| total   |                        286 |                         25 |                       27 |     373,600 |                              11 |

Nothing counts this.  It is in neither =attrs['sale_suppressed']= nor
=attrs['sale_basis_mismatch']=, no =SaleAttachmentWarning= fires, and the
sale-basis ladder's per-key tallies (=attrs['sale_basis_ladder']=) count
SALES attached -- so the ladder's delivered ROWS per key are below its
attached sales (wildcard 947 / 341 / 449 / 1,041 rows vs 949 / 357 / 450 /
1,046 sales) and the 2026-09-24 accounting attributed the whole shortfall
to the GH #869 unmapped-crop drop, which explains only 13 (all 2013-14).
The remaining 2 / 3 / 1 / 5 are this collapse.

** Why it is a bug and not a guard

The comment above the groupby says it "survives only as a guard against a
genuinely repeated line at that grain, which the groupby above has already
consolidated".  That is true of =_consolidate_harvest= (keyed on
=_crop_code=, so it never merges varieties) but false of this collapse,
which keys on =crop= AFTER =_crop_code= has been dropped.  Two varieties
are two rows at the *source's* grain and one row at the *declared* grain;
the collapse resolves that by composing, which is the pattern GH #871
removed from core (=most_complete_row=: SELECT an observed row, never
compose one).  Composing here is worse than in core because the summed
=Quantity= and the single =Value_sold= are then divided into each other
downstream (=Value_sold / Quantity_sold= is a price; =Quantity= feeds
=harvest_kg=).

** Candidate fixes (either is a country decision; state which)

1. Key the collapse on =crop_variety= too (add it to =_grain=), so the two
   varieties stay two rows.  Cost: =(t, i, plot_id, crop, u, condition)= is
   then not unique for those 286 groups, and the wave-script uniqueness
   assert (=_GRAIN=) and the declared index in =data_scheme.yml= must
   either admit =crop_variety= as a level or the framework's declared-index
   collapse will fire on them instead (with a =GrainCollapseWarning=, which
   at least counts them).
2. Keep the grain and SELECT rather than compose: serve the most complete
   observed row per group (the GH #871 rule), summing nothing, and COUNT
   the dropped rows in a new =attrs= tally with a warning, so the loss is
   visible.  Cost: the served =Quantity= for those plot-crops becomes one
   variety's harvest rather than the plot total.

Either way the 27 sales must be counted before they are dropped; the
current silence is the defect.
