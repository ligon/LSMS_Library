* 2. Peru idiosyncrasies, QUOTED from =_/CONTENTS.org=

=Peru/_/CONTENTS.org= is 604 lines and contains *no* =LOGBOOK=, =WAITING=,
=TODO= or =DONE= entries (=grep= returns nothing) -- it is one long GH #601
evidence section on sampling universes.  It records *no* identifier convention,
*no* build-path note, and *nothing at all* about the =data_info.yml= /
=data_scheme.yml= defect.  The idiosyncrasies it *does* record, verbatim:

1. *The waves do not share a universe.*
   #+begin_quote
   Read the 1985 and 1990 entries before pooling ENNIV rounds.  Two of the four
   waves do *not* declare a national universe: 1985 subtracts three departments
   and 1990 is Lima-only.  Stacking all four rounds into one "Peru" panel
   without saying so averages across three different declared populations.
   #+end_quote

2. *1990 is Metropolitan Lima only.*
   #+begin_quote
   *This wave is not a national survey.*  It is the most consequential single
   fact in this section for anyone pooling ENNIV rounds ... A =Feature()= call
   that stacks Peru's four waves and takes a mean over =t= mixes a Lima-only
   population into a national series.
   #+end_quote

3. *1985 excludes three departments by design*, quoting catalog 597:
   #+begin_quote
   Cobertura nacional con la exepción de tres departamentos que se encontraban
   en emergencia, a saber: Ayacucho / Apurímac / Huancavelica
   #+end_quote
   and the file's own gloss: "a national aggregate computed from this wave is a
   national-minus-three-departments aggregate."

4. *The wave label mismatch is already recorded.*
   #+begin_quote
   (=_/data_info.yml= labels the first wave =1985-1986= while the directory is
   =1985/=; the survey ran twelve months from late 1985 into 1986.)
   #+end_quote
   This is load-bearing for the fix, but the mechanics are conditional and worth
   getting exactly right.  =Country.waves= (=country.py:1712-1731=) is an
   if/elif chain: it first tries =_/peru.py= for a =waves= / =Waves= attribute,
   and reaches =elif self.resources.get('Waves')= *only when
   =_/peru.py= does not exist*.  So:
   - *No country module* (today's state): carrying =Waves:= into
     =data_scheme.yml= makes =Country.waves= return =['1985-1986','1990','1991',
     '1994']=, naming a directory that does not exist -- a live breakage.
   - *With a country module* (which the =i()= fix requires, S4): the =elif= is
     never reached and a carried-over =Waves:= is merely *dead config* -- inert,
     but a second, wrong statement of the wave list sitting next to the real one.
   Either way, drop it.  Omitting it keeps directory discovery, which is what
   happens today and is correct.

5. *1994's two shipped documents give two different residence thresholds* --
   =programa.pdf= §4.4 "residen habitualmente más de 7 meses en los últimos 12
   meses"; =m-encues.pdf= §6.4 "si reside al menos 3 de los últimos 12 meses".
   #+begin_quote
   Anyone deriving a residence filter for Peru 1994 (cf. the =MonthsSpent= /
   =MonthsAway= handling described in =CLAUDE.md=) is choosing between two
   documented rules, not applying one.
   #+end_quote
   This is *directly actionable now*: I found the variable (=B08-AUSENTE=, see
   S3), so the choice is live rather than hypothetical.

6. *Lodgers and domestic workers are excluded from membership by rule* --
   =m-encues.pdf= §6.5 "excepto los pensionistas y trabajadores del hogar, que
   nunca se considera como miembros del hogar."  Peru's own
   =categorical_mapping.org= nevertheless ships codes 8 =Trabajador domestico=
   and 9 =Pensionista=, and 1994's =REG02= carries 134 and 17 such rows -- the
   instrument's rule and the delivered data disagree, and the roster will
   include people the manual says are not members.

7. *All four universe statements are catalog-only*: "the record is a moving
   target.  A WB metadata revision changes what Peru's declared universe is,
   with no diff in this repo."

** T2 -- the org-table =Code= column is int64; the =.dta= codes are strings

=DPTO.Code= and =AREA.Code= parse as *int64* (leading zeros lost: =01= -> =1=).
=REG01.a01= is =str= with values ='06'=, ='14'=; =a06= is =str= ='1'/'2'=.
So =[a01, {mapping: [DPTO, Code, Preferred Label]}]= builds ={1:'Amazonas',...}=
and matches *nothing*.  It fails *silently* -- you get the raw codes.

MEASURED, with the explicit org-table mapping in place:
=Region: {'15': 132, '06': 19, '13': 19, ...}= -- still department numbers.

=Rural: {'Urban': 2287, 'Rural': 1336}=.

This is the exact CLAUDE.md gotcha ("YAML mapping keys must be string keys ...
not numeric ... when the raw labels are strings"), and it bites =Region= and
=Rural= -- the two columns #684 singles out as "correctly-wired".

Detect: =df['Region'].value_counts()= after the build; digits mean it failed.

*Corroboration that the AREA direction (1=Urbano, 2=Rural) is right*: MEASURED

of 820 Lima + 1,380 urban (= 2,200) and 1,344 rural.  And 252 urban / 112 rural
clusters against CCPP0's 111 rural community records.  Both match.

(=Region: DPTO=, =Rural: AREA=, =Sex: Sex=, =Relationship: Relationship=).

last element is a dict.  Peru is the *only* place in the corpus with this syntax:

** T3 -- =v: segmento= is *sound*; the composite-key remedy would make it worse

MEASURED conflicts within =segmento=: 7 of 364 disagree on =a01= (DPTO), 10 of
364 on =a06= (AREA).  But look at the shape -- every single one is
*eleven-or-twelve households against one*:

segmento 125: a01 {'13': 10, '01':1,'12':1}  segmento 637: a06 {'1': 13, '2': 1}

corroborated: =CCPP0.DTA='s 111 rural community records give =segmento -> (dept,
prov, dist)= that is *1:1* (0 segmentos with more than one district), and *110 of

So Peru is *not* the Guatemala case.  Guatemala's =upm= was float32-rounded so
distinct PSUs genuinely collided; Peru's =segmento= is int16 and clean.  Adding
=(a01,a02,a03)= to the key does *not* remove the =a06= conflicts (still 10) and

around a mis-keyed household.

** T10 -- =Int_t= wired to the day field yields 100% NaT, silently

MEASURED with =Int_t: a17= (DIA): the delivered column is =NaT= for all 3,623
rows.  Mechanism: the *canonical* schema declares

** T13 -- the 1990/1991 =_i= / =_d= column pairs are NOT integer+decimal

I asserted this by analogy with 1994's dictionary-proven ENTER/DECIM split and
then tested it.  *The analogy is false.*  MEASURED on 1991 =N09A.DTA= (22,616 rows):

| column     | min  | max | >99 | fraction non-integer | distinct |
|------------+------+-----+-----+----------------------+----------|
| =fdexp_i=  | 0.01 | 300 |   1 |               0.6437 |      414 |
| =fdexp_d=  | 0.00 | 315 |   1 |               0.0000 |        5 |

=corr(fdexp_i, fdexp_d) = 0.0063=.  Three facts each independently kill the
integer/decimal reading: =_i= carries its own decimals (64% non-integer -- 2.50,
6.60, 1.20); =_d= takes only *five* distinct values and is almost always 0; and
=_d= exceeds 99 (max 315; =N05B1.amtpdb1d= reaches 5,000), which no decimal part
can.  The same shape holds in =N08A= (=expd_d=: 2 distinct values) and in 1990
(=FDEXP_D=: 5 distinct).  Note also that =N02B= orders its pair =lpmt_d,
lpmt_i= -- d before i.

I do *not* know what they are.  A plausible reading given the period -- ENNIV 90
was fielded in intis during hyperinflation, ENNIV 91 in =moi= 9-11, months after
the July-1991 nuevo sol reform, and =1990/DEFLATE.SSP= carries =EXRATEI= -- is
*intis vs. dólares* (amount reported in either currency).  That is a hypothesis,
not a finding.  Recorded in Gaps.

*Why this matters*: it is the difference between =Expenditure = i + d/100= and
=Expenditure = i= (with =d= a separate hard-currency amount needing conversion).
Getting it backwards silently corrupts every 1990/1991 expenditure number.

** T14 -- 1994 =ak03= has a 99999.99 sentinel the framework will not catch

* 6. The file-semantics split (Q2), stated from the code

| file                              | read by                                   | keys the reader looks for                                       |
|-----------------------------------+-------------------------------------------+-----------------------------------------------------------------|
| =countries/{C}/_/data_scheme.yml= | =Country.resources= (=country.py:1604=)   | =Data Scheme= (table registry, per-table =index:= + dtypes + =materialize=), =Waves=, =wave_folder_map= |
| =countries/{C}/{w}/_/data_info.yml= | =Wave.resources= (=country.py:567=)     | one block per table: =file=, =idxvars=, =myvars=, =dfs=, =derived=, =drop= |
| =lsms_library/data_info.yml=      | =feature._canonical_index_levels=, =country._no_v_join_tables=, =_load_canonical_spellings= | =Index Info=, =Columns=, =Join v from sample= |

The country-level =index:= key is *not* decorative -- =_declared_index_levels=
(=country.py:4059=) feeds =_normalize_dataframe_index= (=country.py:4852=), which
synthesises missing levels, reorders, drops undeclared levels, and collapses
duplicates.  That is where the =GrainCollapseWarning= comes from.

*Peru files on the wrong side of the line*:

1. =Peru/_/data_info.yml= is a *country* file with a wave-file *name*.  Its
   =Data Scheme:= belongs in =_/data_scheme.yml= -- this is #684, and it is right.
2. Its =Waves:= key must be *dropped*, not carried over (idiosyncrasy #4).
   It breaks =Country.waves= outright if there is no =_/peru.py=, and is dead
   config if there is one.
3. Its =Index Info:= is inert at country level.  Harmless (GhanaLSS/Nepal do it),
   but it is Nepal's block verbatim and should not be treated as a Peru decision.
4. =Peru/1994/_/data_info.yml='s table-level =mappings:= key is on *neither*
   side -- no reader anywhere consumes it (T2b).
5. The per-table =index:= values are Nepal's, and three of the five
   (=household_roster=, =individual_education=, =food_acquired=) declare =v= in
   violation of CLAUDE.md's rule (T5).
