#+TITLE: Rural: what the sources say, what the configs do, what the API delivers
#+DATE: 2026-08-21
#+AUTHOR: rural-values audit (branch docs/rural-values-audit)

* TL;DR

Every ~Rural~-producing cell in the corpus was measured three ways: the raw
source column (frequencies including nulls), the config mapping, and the
value the Country API delivers on a *cold* cache.

- *150 of 153 (country, wave, table) cells measured at source*; 3 could not
  be (Nepal, no microdata in the repo).
- *Class D (non-canonical delivered value) is EMPTY.*  Cold, over all 47
  countries and all three ~Rural~-producing tables, the corpus delivers
  exactly four things: ~Rural~, ~Urban~, ~Informal~, ~NA~.  GH #602's sweep
  holds; the Uganda ~'0'~ precedent does not recur anywhere.
- *Class A (collapse) is the real finding: 12 cells across 7 (country, wave)
  survey designs whose source draws MORE THAN TWO settlement categories and
  whose config delivers two.*  The worst is Iraq 2006-07 (a documented
  three-tier =rural / urban / metropolitan= stratification, 5,413 +
  5,736 households merged into one ~Urban~); then Ethiopia ESS2/ESS3
  (small town vs. large town), Kazakhstan 1996 (a three-stratum design),
  Azerbaijan 1995 (four codes to two).  Fourteen further cells have >2 raw
  categories and were *adjudicated out* with reasons (section 3).
- *Class C (delivered >=99% null) covers 11 cells / 7 country-waves*, of
  which *GhanaLSS 2005-06 is a pure config gap* -- the variable is in two
  shipped GLSS5 files at exactly the right grain -- and *GhanaSPS
  2013-14/2017-18 is joinable from wave 1* (same 334 EAs).
- *One cell is only a Rural by name*: India 1997-98 ~employment.Rural~ is a
  per-activity *workplace* location, 93% null, and sits outside the GH #602
  spellings check entirely.  Section 4A.
- *GH #659 is partially stale*: cold on ~origin/development~, GhanaLSS
  ~sample().Rural~ is null for *15,028 of 56,330 rows (26.7%), THREE of
  seven waves* -- not 21,026 / 37% / four waves.  The 5,998-row delta is
  GLSS4 (1998-99), fixed by GH #673.
- *Recommendation [SUPERSEDED 2026-08-22 --- see §7.1]*: this audit recommended
  adding ONE new canonical value, ~Semi-urban~, in exactly two places
  (GhanaLSS GLSS1/GLSS2, Kazakhstan 1996).  *That was not done.*  EL decided
  (2026-08-21) to keep ~Rural~ a strict binary and fold GhanaLSS's ~SU~ onto
  *~Rural~*; shipped in PR #690.  *Kazakhstan was not adjudicated and is still
  open.*  The parts of this bullet that still hold: do NOT generalise the
  ~Informal~ precedent (a formality/tenure class, not a settlement-size tier
  --- South Africa really is special), and the remaining class-A cells want a
  *separate column* rather than a third ~Rural~ value --- now being built as
  a per-country ~labels=~ selection in PR #697, not as a shared vocabulary
  (§7.3, as amended).

* 1. What was measured, and how

Three passes, all in this repo's worktree with
~PYTHONPATH~ asserted (~assert 'worktrees' in lsms_library.__file__~):

1. *Config parse* (structural, not grep).  Walk every wave ~data_info.yml~,
   every table block AND every ~dfs:~ sub-block, collecting any ~Rural~
   myvar; plus every script-path ~_/sample.py~ / ~_/cluster_features.py~.
   Result: *153 producing declarations* across 33 countries, and *34
   ~sample~/~cluster_features~ cells that declare no ~Rural~ at all*.

   Note the brief's "79 wave-level ~data_info.yml~ declare ~Rural~" is a
   *line*-grep count and over-counts: India 1997-98 matches only on a
   comment left by GH #602 when its ~Rural~ was deleted.  It also
   under-counts the universe, because the motivating cells (GLSS1/GLSS2)
   declare no ~Rural~ and are therefore invisible to that grep.

2. *Raw source counts.*  For each cell, read the declared source file with
   ~get_dataframe()~ and ~value_counts(dropna=False)~ on the declared source
   column, recording the Python *type* of each raw value (the Uganda ~'0'~
   and GLSS4 ~rural_dict.get(value)~ defects were both type mismatches).

3. *Delivered values, COLD.*  Fresh ~LSMS_DATA_DIR~ with only ~dvc-cache~
   symlinked to the shared blob store, all 47 countries, ~sample~ and
   ~cluster_features~.  Cold matters: GH #659's own method note records that
   *two of four* ~Rural~ defects reported in an earlier sweep were warm-cache
   artifacts.

Evidence files in this directory:

| file                          | grain                                  | rows |
|-------------------------------+----------------------------------------+------|
| ~rural_raw_values.csv~        | (country, wave, table, subdf, file, column, raw value) | 464 |
| ~rural_mapping.csv~           | the same, plus what the config maps it to | 459 |
| ~rural_delivered_values.csv~  | (country, table, wave, delivered value) | 397 |

*Raw and delivered counts are at DIFFERENT GRAINS and are not reconcilable
row-for-row.*  A raw count is over the rows of the source file (which may be
person-, household- or cluster-level); a delivered count is over the
table's declared index ((i,t) for ~sample~, (t,v) for ~cluster_features~).
Kazakhstan's ~type_loc~ reads 7,224 person-rows and delivers 1,996
households.  Both numbers are recorded; neither is wrong.

* 2. CONTENTS.org idiosyncrasies this audit relied on

Read before any claim was made about the country.  Quoted, not paraphrased.

** CotedIvoire -- the REGION->Rural mapping is the PRODUCER'S definition, not a proxy

My first reading was that mapping a *5-way agro-ecological ~REGION~* onto
~Rural~ was a proxy defect.  ~CotedIvoire/_/CONTENTS.org~ refutes that, and
the refutation is the result:

#+begin_quote
- 5 regions: ABIDJAN, OTHER CITIES, EAST FOREST, WEST FOREST, SAVANNA
- Urban = ABIDJAN + OTHER CITIES; Rural = EAST/WEST FOREST + SAVANNA
#+end_quote
-- ~CotedIvoire/_/CONTENTS.org~ ll.92-93

and, quoting the catalog's own Sampling Procedure (ll.240-246):

#+begin_quote
Regional classification was constructed so that clusters in Abidjan would be
classified as Abidjan; clusters in other major cities in East Forest, West
Forest and Savana would be classified as "Other Cities "; and only rural
clusters would be included in the East Forest, West Forest and Savanna.
Thus, the urban-rural classification is as follows: Urban - Abidjan and
Other Cities; Rural - East Forest, West Forest and Savanna.
#+end_quote

So the eight CIV 1985-89 cells that my ">2 source categories" screen flags
are *NOT class A*.  The distinction ~REGION~ draws that ~Rural~ does not is
*ecological zone*, which the same config already exposes as ~Region~.
Nothing is lost.  Recorded here so nobody re-files it.

** Iraq -- a documented THREE-tier stratification, folded to two

#+begin_quote
The survey was designed to visit 18,144 households-324 households in each of
56 strata, defined as the rural, urban, and metropolitan portions of each of
Iraq's 18 governorates. Baghdad, with five strata, was the exception.
#+end_quote
-- ~Iraq/_/CONTENTS.org~ ll.98-101, quoting IHSES Vol.1 ch.4 "A. Design"

The =xstrat= labels realise exactly that: =X - rural=, =X - other urban=,
=X - urban center=.  This is the strongest class-A cell in the corpus.

** Kazakhstan -- three DESIGN strata, folded to two

#+begin_quote
Three design strata, allocated by household share, not by population share ::

villages, middle-size and small cities / 36,02% rural regions, realized as
90 / 38 / 72 PSUs out of 200

* 3. Classification summary

Counted over *(country, wave, table)* cells.  A cell can appear in more than
one class -- C and E overlap heavily (E is the verified *cause* of most of C).
*Every member is enumerated below*, so the table is reconstructible from the
committed CSVs alone.

| class | what it means                                   | cells | country-waves |
|-------+-------------------------------------------------+-------+---------------|
| A     | source >2 substantive categories, config -> <=2 |    12 |             7 |
| B     | a non-null raw value the config sends to NA     | *0*   |         *0*   |
| C     | delivered >=99% null                            |    11 |             7 |
| D     | delivered value outside the canonical domain    | *0*   |         *0*   |
| E     | no urban/rural indicator in the shipped extract |    10 |             6 |

** Class A -- 12 cells / 7 country-waves

Iraq 2006-07 (sample, cluster_features); Ethiopia 2013-14 (both); Ethiopia
2015-16 (both); Kazakhstan 1996 (both); Mali 2017-18 (both); Azerbaijan 1995
(cluster_features); Niger 2014-15 (cluster_features).

26 cells have >2 substantive raw categories.  The other 14 were *adjudicated
out*, each for a stated reason:

- *CotedIvoire* 1985-89, 8 cells -- the 5-way ~REGION~ fold is the producer's
  own published definition (section 2).
- *Malawi* 2016-17 / 2019-20, 4 cells -- the 4 raw values are pure casing.
- *South Africa* 1993, 2 cells -- 3 raw categories, *3 delivered values*.  It
  is the one cell in the corpus that does not collapse.

Ethiopia 2011-12 is *not* counted (its source is genuinely 2-way) but carries
a related and arguably worse defect; see 4.2.

** Class B -- 0 cells

No non-null raw value anywhere resolves to NA.  *Two different strengths of
evidence*, and the distinction matters:

- For ~inline_dict~ cells the check is *mechanical* -- ~rural_mapping.csv~
  resolves every raw value against the declared dict, and no row lands on NA.
- For ~auto_function~ / ~script~ cells (GhanaLSS 1991-92 and 1998-99,
  GhanaSPS 2009-10, Ethiopia x5, Tanzania x2) the mapping is *code*, so the
  evidence is the delivered pass instead: every one of them delivers zero
  nulls, and every partial null anywhere in the corpus has an attributed
  cause that is not a lost mapping (Pakistan's documented PSU, Uganda's
  parish conflicts plus 20 raw nulls, Azerbaijan's merge).

Two *latent* B risks are recorded in 7.4 (Tanzania's ~.map(dict)~, Iraq's
~else 'Urban'~); neither fires on today's data.

** Class C -- 11 cells / 7 country-waves

GhanaLSS 1987-88 (both), 1988-89 (both), 2005-06 (sample); GhanaSPS 2013-14
(sample), 2017-18 (sample); Albania 2003 (both), 2004 (both).

Nine further cells have *partial* nulls and are deliberately NOT class C --
Azerbaijan 1995 ~cluster_features~ 45.8% (5.5), Pakistan 1991 ~sample~ 4.1%,
and seven Uganda cells (5.7).  Peru 1994 is a separate case again (5.6).

** Class D -- 0 cells

Cold, over all 47 countries and *all three* ~Rural~-producing tables
(~sample~, ~cluster_features~, and India's ~employment~), the delivered
domain is exactly {~Rural~, ~Urban~, ~Informal~, NA}.

** Class E -- 10 cells / 6 country-waves

GhanaLSS 1987-88 (both), 1988-89 (both), GhanaSPS 2013-14 (sample), 2017-18
(sample), Albania 2003 (both), 2004 (both).  Each verified by an exhaustive
file/dictionary sweep, not asserted -- counts in section 8.  *GhanaLSS
2005-06 is class C but NOT class E*: the variable is in the shipped extract
(5.2).

* 4. Class A -- COLLAPSE.  Worst first.

Every row below was read at source.  "collapsed" = households whose source
category is neither plainly urban nor plainly rural, or which are a distinct
tier the config merges away.

** 4.1 Iraq 2006-07 -- 11,149 households, a documented three-tier design folded to two

| raw =xstrat= suffix | households | config -> |
|---------------------+------------+-----------|
| =other urban=       |      5,736 | Urban     |
| =rural=             |      5,628 | Rural     |
| =urban center=      |      5,413 | Urban     |
| Baghdad =al-sader= / =al-rasafah= / =al-karekh= | 953 | Urban |
| =...- extra=        |         92 | Urban     |

Delivered: ~Urban~ 12,194 / ~Rural~ 5,628.  5,413 + 5,736 + 953 + 92 =
12,194 exactly.

Two separate problems.

1. *The collapse.*  The survey's own design document (quoted in 2.) names
   THREE portions -- rural, urban, metropolitan.  =urban center= is the
   metropolitan tier and =other urban= the urban tier; the config's 57-entry
   mapping in ~Iraq/2006-07/_/data_info.yml~ sends both to ~Urban~.  This is
   the *largest* collapse in the corpus by households (11,149 in two
   near-equal tiers) and the best-evidenced.

2. *92 households of =...- extra= are mapped to ~Urban~ with no evidence.*
   The wave's ~mapping.py~ used by ~cluster_features~ makes it worse: it is
   ~'Rural' if 'rural' in str(s).lower() else 'Urban'~ -- an =else= branch
   that will label ANY future or unrecognised stratum ~Urban~.  Per
   ~Iraq/_/CONTENTS.org~ l.172 the =extra= strata are "the extra Sulaimaniya
   metropolitan stratum", so ~Urban~ happens to be right *here*; the rule
   that produced it is not safe.

Iraq 2012 (=q00_16=) is a clean two-way ~URBAN~/~RURAL~ (14,884 / 10,262).
*I did not check whether 2012 has a metropolitan tier under another column.*

** 4.2 Ethiopia ESS2 / ESS3 -- 3,621 households, AND a cross-wave comparability trap

| wave    | raw                        |    n | config -> |
|---------+----------------------------+------+-----------|
| 2013-14 | Rural                      | 3323 | Rural     |
|         | Large town (urban)         | 1486 | Urban     |
|         | Small town (urban)         |  453 | Urban     |
| 2015-16 | RURAL                      | 3272 | Rural     |
|         | Meduim and large town      | 1255 | Urban     |
|         | SMALL TOWN                 |  427 | Urban     |

Both ~sample~ (script path, ~.map(dict)~) and ~cluster_features~ (YAML,
inline dict) do the same thing, from the same column.

*The folding itself is defensible*: the 2013-14 labels literally say
"(urban)", so the producer calls both tiers urban.  What is lost is the
small/large distinction that the ESS weighting is built on --
~Ethiopia/_/CONTENTS.org~ l.577 quotes the BID: "The results presented have
been weighted to be nationally representative for rural areas, *small
towns, and large towns*."  A user cannot reproduce the published
three-domain estimates from the delivered table.

*The comparability trap is worse and is not recorded anywhere.*
ESS1 (2011-12) is =rural= (3,466) + =small town= (503) and *nothing else* --
by design.  ~Ethiopia/_/CONTENTS.org~ l.450:

#+begin_quote
The ERSS sample is designed to be representative of rural and small town
areas of Ethiopia.
#+end_quote

So ~Rural == 'Urban'~ means *"small town, under 10,000 people"* in 2011-12
and *"any town"* from 2013-14 on.  Pooling Ethiopia waves and filtering on
~Rural~ silently compares two different populations.  The delivered urban
share jumps 12.7% -> 36.8% -> 34.0% -> 54.0% -> 53.9% across the five waves,
and most of the first jump is a definition change, not urbanisation.
(Measured delivered urban share by wave: 12.7 / 36.8 / 34.0 / 54.0 / 53.9.)

** 4.3 Kazakhstan 1996 -- 646 households in a stratum that is neither

| raw =type_loc= | persons | config -> |
|----------------+---------+-----------|
| =urban=        |   3,556 | Urban     |
| =rural=        |   3,022 | Rural     |
| =small town=   |     646 | Urban     |

Delivered (household grain): ~Urban~ 1,282 / ~Rural~ 714.

The three raw values are *exactly the three design strata* named in
~Kazakhstan/_/CONTENTS.org~ (quoted in 2.), and the middle one is described
by the survey's own final report as "villages, middle-size and small
cities".  Sending a stratum that explicitly contains *villages* to ~Urban~
is the clearest single wrong-direction fold I found.

** 4.4 Azerbaijan 1995 -- four bare codes to two, on an unsourced claim

| raw =inout= | PSUs | config -> |
|-------------+------+-----------|
| 1.0         |   40 | Urban     |
| 2.0         |   20 | Urban     |
| 3.0         |   13 | Urban     |
| 4.0         |   18 | Rural     |
| (null)      |    1 | NA        |

=inout= is a *bare numeric code*.  No value labels ship with =APP1.dta=; no

does not obviously map onto four codes.  I am recording that 73 of 91 PSUs
are called ~Urban~ on the strength of a repo comment that cites nothing, and
*I am not proposing a re-mapping*, because I cannot tell which code is
which.  The honest next step is one RA and the =APP1= questionnaire.

* 4A. Not a collapse, a MISNOMER: India 1997-98 employment.Rural

Every other ~Rural~ in the corpus lives in ~sample~ or ~cluster_features~.
India 1997-98 has a third: ~employment.Rural~, from =SECT02AD.DTA::v02a05b=.

Raw (16,089 activity rows): *14,998 null* (93.2%), Urban 728, Rural 363.
Delivered, cold, at the declared ~(t, i, pid, act)~ grain (6,158 rows):
5,067 ~NA~ / 728 ~Urban~ / 363 ~Rural~.

*The values are canonical; the concept is not.*  The config already says so,
in a comment left by GH #323 and quoted here in full because it is the
finding:

#+begin_quote
# NOTE (separate defect, not the GH #323 root cause): v02a05b is the
# per-ACTIVITY WORKPLACE location (14,998/16,089 NaN; 728 'Urban' / 363
# 'Rural'), NOT a household residence flag -- while India's `sample`
# deliberately carries NO Rural (GH #602: "India simply has no Rural
# indicator").  The silent collapse was laundering this sparse per-job
# field into a column that reads as the very household Rural indicator
# #602 removed.  Widening the index de-fangs it (it is now honestly
# per-activity rather than a fabricated person-level value), but the NAME
# still misleads and should become `Workplace_Rural` (or be dropped).
# Left in place here to keep this commit scoped to GH #323.
#+end_quote
-- ~India/1997-98/_/data_info.yml~

Three things this audit adds.

1. *It is the ONLY cell of its kind*, corpus-wide.  The rename to
   ~Workplace_Rural~ therefore costs nothing and collides with nothing.
2. *It sits outside the GH #602 safety net.*  ~_check_declared_spellings~
   keys on *(table, column)* as declared under ~Columns~ in
   ~lsms_library/data_info.yml~, and only ~sample~ and ~cluster_features~
   declare ~Rural~ there.  ~employment~ is not in ~Columns~ at all, so this
   ~Rural~ is *unchecked* -- it happens to be canonical, which is luck, not
   enforcement.  That is the #602 disease one level up: the check is real,
   the column is simply not in its domain.
3. *It does not contradict ~India/_/CONTENTS.org~*, and the distinction is
   the point.  That file says:

   #+begin_quote
   This is the documentation-side twin of the =sample.Rural= finding (#602,
   below in this file): the shipped data carry no urban/rural variable, and
   the documentation asserts no urban/rural universe.
   #+end_quote

   Read strictly -- no *household residence* urban/rural variable -- that is
   correct, and =v02a05b= is not a counter-example.  Read loosely it would be
   false, since a column literally labelled Urban/Rural does ship.  Worth
   tightening the wording when someone next touches that file.

   The same file's *"CAUTION -- do not upgrade 'rural' from inference to
   claim"* section is the model here: the WB abstract opens on "rural
   poverty" and every PSU is a village, and CONTENTS.org still refuses to
   record a rural-only universe because no document states one.

** 6.3 Is SU the same thing as ~Informal~?  No.

The discriminator that earned ~Informal~ its place was *source-internal*:
South Africa's own =metro= variable groups =type=3= with the non-metro
areas, so there was positive evidence against folding it into ~Urban~.

GLSS =SU= is a different kind of thing:

| | GLSS =SU= | South Africa =type=3= |
|---+---+---|
| what it classifies | *settlement size/function* -- a tier between town and village | *formality/tenure* -- squatter and informal settlements |
| where it lives | the cluster (EA), from the 1984 census frame | the household's area type in the sampling frame |
| ordered? | yes: U > SU > R is a size ordering | no: informal areas exist inside and outside metros |
| survives folding? | folding loses an ordered middle tier | folding asserts a formality fact the data denies |

They are *both* reasons not to force a binary, and they are *not the same
concept*.  ~Informal~ should not be reused for GLSS.

** 6.4 Is folding defensible for GLSS?

I do not think so, for three measured reasons:

1. *It is 52 of 263 clusters -- 20%.*  Whichever way you fold, you move a
   fifth of the sample frame across the only axis this column has.
2. *The stratification is built on it.*  Nine strata = 3 zones x 3 urban
   classes.  Fold, and the delivered ~Rural~ no longer reconstructs the
   design.
3. *The repo already wrote down a fold, for a different variable, and
   disagreed with itself.*  ~GhanaLSS/1988-89/_/categorical_mapping.org~
   carries an *unwired* 7-way table:

   #+begin_example
   | Code | Label         | Classification |
   |    1 | City          | Urban          |
   |    2 | Large Town    | Urban          |
   |    3 | Medium Town   | Urban          |
   |    4 | Small Town    | Rural          |
   |    5 | Large Village | Rural          |
   |    6 | Small Village | Rural          |
   |    7 | Other         | Rural          |
   #+end_example

   Nothing in the wave's ~data_info.yml~ references this table, and I could
   not determine which source variable it was written for -- *no shipped
   GLSS1/GLSS2 column has 7 categories of that shape*.  It is evidence that
   somebody met a >2-way Ghana locality variable, made a fold, and put the
   cut at "Medium Town is urban, Small Town is rural" -- a placement nobody
   can check, for a variable nobody can now find.  That is precisely the
   failure a visible third value prevents.  *I am recording this as an
   unexplained artifact and NOT acting on it.*

* 7. Recommendation on the canonical vocabulary

** 7.1 [SUPERSEDED 2026-08-22] Add exactly one value: ~Semi-urban~

#+begin_quote
*SUPERSEDED --- DO NOT ACT ON THIS SECTION.*  Decision by EL, 2026-08-21:
*no canonical ~Semi-urban~ value was added.*  ~Rural~ stays the binary
~{Urban, Rural}~, and GhanaLSS's ~SU~ folds to *~Rural~*.  Shipped in PR #690
(merged =22c66949=): GLSS1/GLSS2 ~sample().Rural~ 6,341 NA -> 0, delivered
vocabulary asserted as exactly ~{Urban, Rural}~.  ~lsms_library/data_info.yml~
is byte-identical to ~development~ --- no canonical value was introduced.

*The measurements below are unchanged and still correct.*  What is superseded
is the *recommendation*, not the evidence: the GLSS frame really is stratified
rural / semi-urban / urban, and ~SU~ really is 52 of 263 clusters.

*Three things learned after this section was written*, each of which would
have changed it:

1. *The fold now has survey evidence, and it points this way.*  The joint
   GLSS1/GLSS2 Basic Information Document §3.1 defines the tiers by *1984
   Census locality population* --- urban >5,000, semi-urban 1,500--5,000,
   rural <1,500 --- so the whole ~SU~ band sits *below the survey's own urban
   threshold*.  GSS itself nests Semi-urban under Rural in the GLSS3 report.
2. *The precedent this section leaned on does not exist.*  The GLSS2 7-way
   ~rural~ table's ~Classification~ column is *editorial --- it is not on the
   questionnaire*, and the table decodes ~Y01C:NRCPL~, a *non-resident
   child's* place of residence, not the household's settlement class (#692).
   Do not cite it for anything.
3. *Ghana's cutoff MOVES across waves* --- 5,000 -> 1,500 -> 5,000, with the
   middle band absorbed into ~urban~ for GLSS4 (#705).  So a third canonical
   value would not have made the series comparable anyway.

*Kazakhstan was NOT decided.*  The hedge below stands untouched: the
recommendation there was always weaker than for GLSS, nobody has adjudicated
it, and it remains open.  Whatever is done must not assume the Ghana decision
transfers --- Kazakhstan's producer does not use the word.
#+end_quote


Add to both ~sample.Rural~ and ~cluster_features.Rural~ ~spellings~ blocks in
~lsms_library/data_info.yml~:

#+begin_example
  Semi-urban: [semi-urban, Semi-Urban, SEMI-URBAN, SU, semi urban]
#+end_example

Use it in *two* places, and only two:

| country    | wave(s)          | source                      | n            | why |
|------------+------------------+-----------------------------+--------------+-----|
| GhanaLSS   | 1987-88, 1988-89 | Appendix I =SU= (transcribe)| 52 clusters  | the frame is literally stratified rural/semi-urban/urban; 20% of clusters |
| Kazakhstan | 1996             | =type_loc= = "small town"   | 646 hh       | a named design stratum containing *villages and small cities*; currently called Urban |

Both are *settlement-size tiers between town and village*, both are named as
such by the producer, and in both the current fold puts a village-containing
category on the urban side.  ~Semi-urban~ is the term GSS itself uses;
Kazakhstan's report says "villages, middle-size and small cities", which is
the same idea in more words.

I am *less* confident about Kazakhstan than about GLSS.  GLSS's own
documentation uses the word; Kazakhstan's does not, and someone may
reasonably prefer to leave =small town= folded and record the loss.  Say so
if you disagree -- the evidence is in section 4.3 and the CSVs.

** 7.2 Do NOT add a fourth value, and do NOT generalise ~Informal~

~Informal~ stays exactly where it is: South Africa 1993, 83 households, on
the source-internal =metro= evidence.  Nothing else in the corpus has a
formality/tenure stratum.  I checked Peru, the one other place the word
appears -- ~Peru/_/CONTENTS.org~ l.196 records that the ENNIV *1990* Lima

** 7.3 The other class-A cells want a SEPARATE COLUMN, not a third ~Rural~ value

#+begin_quote
*Amended 2026-08-22.*  This section's direction was right and is being built,
but two later findings bound it.

*Being built*: PR #697 generalises query-time ~labels=~ beyond food's ~j~
level, so a country can carry its own finer settlement labels alongside a
binary ~Rural~ *without a new canonical value*.  Iraq 2006-07 is the worked
example (7 raw ~xstrat~ tiers collapse to 2; 12,194 households in one bucket).
Note the mechanism had to live at ~_apply_categorical_mappings~, not at
~_relabel_j~: the latter keys on ~Preferred Label~, which is a function for
food (fine->coarse) but *one-to-many* for a settlement ladder (coarse->fine),
so it yields ~{Urban: 'Medium Town', Rural: 'Other'}~ --- last-row-wins garbage.

*Bounded by*: a corpus sweep (PR #704) found that *entity* variation, not
threshold variation, defeats cross-country settlement harmonisation.  Only
4 of 45 countries state a population rule at all, and the object measured
differs --- locality (Peru /centro poblado/), enumeration area, /commune/,
/corregimiento/, /cellule/.  Two countries could pick the *identical* number
and still not be comparable.  So a ~SettlementType~ column is defensible as
*per-country* detail; it is not a route to a shared vocabulary.
#+end_quote


Iraq (metropolitan / urban / rural), Ethiopia (large town / small town /
rural), Mali (Bamako / autre urbain / rural), Niger (Communaute Urbaine /
urbain / rural) are all *within-urban tier* distinctions, not a middle
category.  Squeezing them into ~Rural~ would make one column mean four
different vocabularies and would break the ~== 'Urban'~ filter that
currently works.

The right shape is a *new, optional, per-country column* -- call it
~SettlementType~ -- carrying the survey's own label verbatim, with ~Rural~
remaining the harmonised binary (plus ~Semi-urban~/~Informal~ where a genuine
third settlement class exists).  That gives:

- ~df[df.Rural == 'Urban']~ keeps working everywhere;
- Ethiopia's three ESS weighting domains become reconstructible;
- Iraq's metropolitan tier stops being invisible;
- no country is forced to invent a category it does not have.

*This is a design proposal, not a measurement.*  I have not prototyped it and
it is out of scope for this branch.

** One negative result worth stating plainly

*Class D is empty.*  Every delivered ~Rural~ value in all 47 countries, cold,
across ~sample~, ~cluster_features~ AND India's ~employment~, is ~Rural~,
~Urban~, ~Informal~ or ~NA~.  The specific defect the brief asked me to hunt
for -- the Uganda ~'0'~ shape -- does not exist anywhere in the corpus today.
GH #602's ~_check_declared_spellings~ plus its data fixes closed that class,
and this is independent cold confirmation of it.

One caveat on how much credit the *check* deserves, as opposed to the *fixes*:
~_check_declared_spellings~ is keyed on (table, column) as declared under
~Columns~ in the canonical schema, and only ~sample~ and ~cluster_features~
declare ~Rural~ there.  India's ~employment.Rural~ is outside its domain and
is canonical by luck (4A).  The check would not have caught a violation there,
and would not catch one in a *new* table that grows a ~Rural~ column.
