* Summary

The cheapest way to see what is wrong with =transformations.conversion_to_kgs=
is to ask it what a kilogram weighs.  On the nine countries measured here it
answers between 0.87 kg and 2.0 kg, and the deviation is not noise: it is the
median =Quantity= on the rows it looked at.  Step 2 of the chain takes the
median of =Expenditure= where the docstring promises a per-unit price, so the
number it returns is kilograms per transaction ROW, not kilograms per unit.
That is defect (b), and it is arithmetic, not judgement.

Defect (a) is the one the issue names.  No step of the chain carries =j=, so
the result is one factor per unit label for the whole country.  Malawi's
=Piece= is shared by 133 food items; estimated separately, the 72 the data can
support run from 1 g to 1.8 kg, and the library serves 0.50 kg to all 133.
GhanaLSS's =Heap= carries 125 items over a factor of 15,000.

Measuring those two turned up a third, which nobody has filed.  =KNOWN_METRIC=
does not contain =Millilitre=, =Grams= or =Litres=, and
=_parse_explicit_metric='s= regexes do not match the =lts=, =ltrs=, =lt= or
=kgs= spellings, so labels that state their own kilograms fall through to the
price-ratio inference and get invented ones.  The library currently believes a
Malawian millilitre weighs 743 grams, a Malian gramme weighs 708 grams, and a
Ugandan =Sack (50 kgs)= weighs 4.4 kg.  Call it defect (c).  It has no design question in it, it is
half a day's work, and it should land first, because fixing it shrinks the
inference set and enlarges the kg baseline that (a) and (b) are estimated
against.

Two things make this cheaper to land than the workplan assumed.  Nothing in
=transformations.py= is in any table's build fingerprint, and no derived food
table is ever written to a parquet, so *there is no re-warm for any of the
three*: the cost is a coverage regrade.  And =.coder/coverage/blessed.csv= is
empty, so no human blessing is invalidated.

One caveat, stated here rather than buried: defect (c) changes =harvest_kg='s
inferred layer on 47.7% of Uganda's =crop_production= rows, which is the one
place this recommendation crosses a line the brief drew.  See D7.

Recommendation: land (c), then (C) = (a) + (b) together, with (D)'s per-row
provenance in the same PR.  Section 4 lists the decisions and the number that
decides each.

* 1. What the code does today

** The chain, with its keys

=food_quantities(units='kgs')= and =food_prices(units='kgvalue')= are derived
at runtime from =food_acquired= (=country.py:4449-4471=); neither is ever
written to a parquet.  Both call =_get_kg_factors= (=transformations.py:1004=),
which builds one =dict[str, float]= in three layers:

1. =KNOWN_METRIC= (=transformations.py:924=), thirteen hand-coded tokens.
2. =_parse_explicit_metric= (=:964=) over every =u= label in the frame, for
   labels that name their own content ("50 kg Bag" -> 50.0).
3. the price-ratio inference, =conversion_to_kgs= (=:782=), for whatever is
   left.  Gated on =Expenditure= and =Quantity= both being columns (=:1045=),
   which is why =crop_production= never reaches it.

Layer 3 is the one at issue.  Reading its body (=:829-878=), with the group
keys as they are AT RUNTIME rather than as the signature's default suggests:

1. =pkg = v['Expenditure'].divide(v['Kgs'])=, then =.groupby(index).median()=
   (=:870-871=).  =index= is =group_levels = [n for n in ['t','m','i'] if n in
   idx_names]= (=:1048=).  =food_acquired= arrives indexed =(i, t, v, j, u, s)=
   in every country measured, and =m= is minted by =_add_market_index= inside
   =_finalize_result=, which runs AFTER the derived transform.  So the
   effective key is *=['t','i']=*: a per-HOUSEHOLD median price per kilogram,
   pooled across every item that household bought in a kg-known unit.
2. =po = v_infer['Expenditure'].groupby(index + ['u']).median()= (=:873=), key
   *=['t','i','u']=*, over rows with no survey-supplied =Quantity_kg=.
3. =kgper = (po / pkg).dropna()=, then =.groupby('u').median()= (=:874-875=),
   returned as =dict[str, float]=.  Final key *=u= alone*.

=_get_kg_factors= never widens any of it, and =_apply_kg_conversion= (=:1121=)
looks each row's factor up by =u.lower()= and nothing else.

** Defect (a): there is no item axis at any step

Not at the baseline, not at step 2, not in the result.  A "bunch" of bananas
and a "bunch" of groundnuts get one number.  The module's own comment at =:710=
states the general form of the problem ("=conversion_to_kgs= returns ONE factor
per unit label, while =1/price= varies over =(j, t, m)=") in the course of
explaining why =u='Value'= is excluded, and then the code does exactly that for
every other unit.

The crop side of the same library disagrees: =median_price_valuation= carries
=item_keys=('j',)= (=:3145=), and =harvest_kg_factors=' =survey_median= layer
groups =(country, t, u, condition)= before falling back to =(country, t, u)=
(=_survey_median_factors=, =:1855=).

The gap is also LOAD-BEARING somewhere it should not be.
=EthiopiaRHS/_/ethiopiarhs.py:48-50= says, in a comment justifying the GH #347
fix, that "the framework's price-ratio inference ... already groups by item
=i= within each unit"; =EthiopiaRHS/_/CONTENTS.org:543-544= and
=EthiopiaRHS/_/categorical_mapping.org:340-341= repeat the claim.  It is false.
=i= is the household id.  EthiopiaRHS stripped the food label off its unit
tokens on the strength of a property this function does not have, and the fix
proposed here would retroactively make that decision correct.

** Defect (b): step 2 medians Expenditure, not Expenditure/Quantity

The docstring says "expenditure is divided by quantity to get a per-unit price"
(=:791-793=).  Step 1 does divide, by =Kgs=.  Step 2 does not divide at all.
So =kgper= is kilograms per transaction row, and equals kilograms per unit only
where =Quantity == 1=.  Section 2(i) measures how often that holds: between 4.6%
and 40.5% of the rows, by country.

The self-test is the clean way to see it.  The inference mints a factor for
=u='kg'= as readily as for anything else (=_get_kg_factors= then discards it,
because =KNOWN_METRIC= seeded the key first), and the right answer is 1.0 by
definition.  Section 2(iii) reports what it actually returns.

** Defect (c): the metric vocabulary, found while measuring

Three spellings in wide use are missing from =KNOWN_METRIC=: =Millilitre= /
=Milliliter= / =Mili Liter=, =Grams=, =Litres=.  And =_parse_explicit_metric='s=
regexes end their unit tokens with =\b= after =l= and =kg=, so every label
whose metric content is spelled =lts=, =ltrs=, =lt= or =kgs= declines to match.
Verified directly:

: _parse_explicit_metric('Sack (100 kgs)')      -> None
: _parse_explicit_metric('Tin (Debe) (20 lts)') -> None
: _parse_explicit_metric('Cup/Mug(0.5lt)')      -> None
: _parse_explicit_metric('Basket (10 kg)')      -> 10.0     # matches
: _parse_explicit_metric('Bottle(750ml)')       -> 0.75     # matches

Those are Uganda's own container labels.  =Uganda/_/conversion_to_kgs.json=
already holds the right answers for all of them, which brings us to the one
thing here that is merely dead.

** What is dead, and what a document says about it

=Uganda/_/CONTENTS.org:810-813= says the json is "a kg-per-unit factor lookup
consumed by =food_quantities= derivation".  That is no longer true.
=uganda.food_acquired= joins it on =u= alone (=uganda.py:404-410=) into a
column named =Kgs=, and =uganda.food_acquired_to_canonical= then drops it: its
docstring lists =Kgs= among the columns that "are ignored" (=uganda.py:440-441=),
and the canonical frame it emits carries =[Quantity, Expenditure, Price]= only.
Confirmed on the warm build: =Country('Uganda').food_acquired()= has no =Kgs=
and no =Quantity_kg= column.  So the u-only table is live for anyone reading a
wave parquet directly and dead for the API, and 100% of Uganda's API
kilograms come from =_get_kg_factors=.

Two consequences.  The json is not a fix target, and =CONTENTS.org:810= needs a
correction.  But its CONTENT is a check: it is a hand-curated answer key for
exactly the labels defect (c) mishandles.

** What is NOT wrong

Worth saying, so the fix does not wander.  The =Quantity_kg= precedence rule is
right and stays (=:867-868=, =:1136-1141=): a survey-supplied per-row kilogram
beats any inference, and those rows are excluded from step 2 while still
anchoring the baseline.  The =u='Value'= exclusion is right and stays (GH #770).
The carry rule for unconvertible units in =food_quantities(units='kgs')= is
right and stays.  And =crop_production= does not reach the price-ratio
inference at all, so nothing in (a) or (b) can move =harvest_kg=.  Defect (c)
is different: it edits =KNOWN_METRIC= / =_parse_explicit_metric=, which
=_kg_factor_series= (=:1660=) does reach.  Section 2 measures how far.

* 2. Measurements

** How they were made

Warm =food_acquired= for nine countries, read through the public
=Country(...).food_acquired()= with =LSMS_DATA_DIR= pointed at a scratch root
holding copies of the L2-country parquets and a symlink to the shared DVC blob
cache.  Nothing was written to =~/.local/share/lsms_library=; verified after
the fact with =find ... -newer=, zero files (including zero new blobs).

Two fidelity checks, because a measurement of the wrong frame is worse than no
measurement.  First, the derived dispatch hands =transform_fn= the output of
=_aggregate_wave_data(waves, 'food_acquired')= rather than the public reader
(=country.py:4470=); on Malawi, Ethiopia, Niger, Tanzania and Nigeria the two
frames have identical length and an identical =u= vocabulary (symmetric
difference 0), so the public reader is a faithful stand-in.  Second, the
prototype re-implements the live chain as =infer_old= and compares its output
key by key against =conversion_to_kgs= itself; the agreement column below is
that check.

All prototypes run IN MEMORY on frames read from the cache.  No library code
was edited.  Scripts: =measure.py=, =metric_scoreboard.py=,
=mali_groundtruth.py=, =two_more.py=, =probe_hash_reach.py=, all in this
directory, all re-runnable.

and =pound= rows alone -- which means 0.70 is measuring how well the
inference's central assumption holds (one price per kilogram across all units

is weak, no amount of regrouping rescues the inference, and the honest answer
for such a country is a curated factor table rather than a better estimate.

* 3. Options

Defect (c) is a prerequisite rather than an option: it has no design content,
it is the only part with an external answer key, and it changes the input to
everything below.  Treat it as step 0 and measure (a) and (b) on top of it,
which is what section 2 does.

** (A) Item axis only

Baseline at =(t, j)=, step 2 at =(t, j, u)=, factor keyed =(j, u)=, with the
=u=-keyed factor as a fallback rung where the =(j, u)= cell is thin.

Appending =j= to the EXISTING =index= is not the same thing and would not work:
=(t, i, j)= asks one household to have bought item =j= both in kilograms and in
bunches within one wave.  The precedent for dropping =i= is on the crop side:
=_survey_median_factors= groups =(country, t, u, condition)= then
=(country, t, u)= and never carries the household (=:1893-1907=).

Two floors, and they gate different things.  =min_reports= is the count of
inference rows behind the =(t, j, u)= estimate, the direct twin of
=SURVEY_MEDIAN_MIN_REPORTS = 5=, whose own comment says five is "a starting
value, not a measured one" (=:1806-1813=).  =min_baseline= is the count of
kg-known rows behind the =(t, j)= price-per-kg reference.  The second one is
the one that bites; see the floor sweep.

One detail worth naming rather than burying: what the fallback rung IS.  The
prototype uses the fix-(b) =u=-keyed factor, which keeps the household baseline
=(t, i)= for that rung, so a row with no =(j, u)= cell gets today's key with
today's baseline and tomorrow's arithmetic.  The alternative is a =u= rung
built as the median ACROSS items of the =(j, u)= estimates, on the =(t, j)=
baseline.  That is unmeasured here.  The stakes are the fifth to half of
affected rows the fallback serves, which is why (D)'s provenance column matters
either way.

Fixing (a) alone leaves the returned factors in kilograms per row, so it does
not stand on its own.

** (B) Per-unit price only

One division at =:873=: =v_infer[price].divide(v_infer[quantity], axis=0)=
before the groupby.  Three lines, no signature change, no new keys.  It makes
the kg self-test come out right and rescales every inferred factor by roughly
the reciprocal of its unit's median quantity.  It leaves the item axis missing,
so Malawi's 133 =Piece= items still share one number, merely a better-scaled
one.

** (C) Both

(A) and (B) together, on top of (c).  This is what section 2(iii) measures.

** (D) C plus per-row provenance

The food twin of =harvest_kg_factors=: a function returning, per row of
=food_acquired=, the factor and which rung produced it, from a layer tuple in
the shape of =KG_FACTOR_LAYERS= -- say =('survey_kg', 'metric', 'item_unit',
'unit', 'none')=.  The counts ride in =attrs= exactly as
=attrs['kg_factor_sources']= does now (=:2062-2066=), and the layers partition
the frame.

This is not decoration.  Between a fifth and a half of the affected rows are
served by the fallback =u= rung rather than by their own =(j, u)= cell, and a
consumer currently cannot tell which.  It is also what makes the change
auditable after it lands: the same disagreement audit
=harvest_kg_factors= runs between the survey's reported factor and the
library's inferred one becomes available on the food side, wherever a country
ships a factor.

** Recommendation

*(c), then (C), with (D) in the same PR.*

The reasoning is that (b) alone is unarguable but small in the places that
matter, (a) alone is unusable, and the provenance column is what turns a
corpus-wide numerical change from an act of faith into something a reviewer can
check per row.  (D) costs one new public function and no change to any existing
return type, which is what keeps the crop side out of it.

- =KNOWN_METRIC= (=:924=) and =_EXPLICIT_METRIC_PATTERNS= (=:948=): defect (c).
  The new =lts= / =ltrs= / =ltr= / =lt= patterns are VOLUME and must carry
  =is_volume=True=, so =volume_as_mass=False= declines them exactly as it
  declines today's =ml= / =cl= / =l= (=:955-961=).  =kgs= and =gms= are mass.
  The prototype treats all of them as mass unconditionally, which is fine for
  a measurement and wrong as a specification.
- =_get_kg_factors= (=:1004=) keeps its =dict[str, float]= return, because
  =_kg_factor_series= (=:1660=) and therefore =harvest_kg= depend on it.
- Documentation: =Uganda/_/CONTENTS.org:810=, =EthiopiaRHS/_/CONTENTS.org:543=,
  =EthiopiaRHS/_/categorical_mapping.org:340= and =ethiopiarhs.py:48= all state

- =Mali/_/CONTENTS.org:786-794= records the contradiction with Mali's own

*** Cache cost: measured, and it is zero

=probe_hash_reach.py= walks =_build_registry._closure_parts=, the same graph
=build_transforms_fingerprint= walks.  The result is stronger than expected:
*not one callable in =transformations.py= appears in the closure of any table*,
=None=-scoped included.  Not =conversion_to_kgs=, not =_get_kg_factors=, not
the three =*_from_acquired= transforms -- and not =KNOWN_METRIC=,
=_EXPLICIT_METRIC_PATTERNS=, =_kg_factor_series= or =harvest_kg= either.  The
derived transforms are reached through =Country.__getattr__= rather than
through the tagged =_aggregate_wave_data= orchestrator, and nothing they
produce is written to a parquet: there is no =var/food_quantities.parquet=
anywhere under =data_root()=, and the =crop_production= parquets carry
=[Quantity, Quantity_sold, Value_sold, planting_month, harvest_month,
intercropped, perennial]= and no =Harvest_kg=.  =rg= confirms no wave script
imports any of these symbols, so =framework_imports_fingerprint= is unaffected
too.

So: *no re-warm, for any of the three defects.*  The WORKPLAN's "then a full
re-warm and regrade" (=WORKPLAN.org:219-220=) is half wrong, and the half that
survives is the regrade.

*** Re-warm and regrade plan

1. Nothing to re-warm.  =lsms-library cache clear= is not needed and should not
   be run.
2. =make matrix= and commit the =.coder/coverage/latest.csv= snapshot: the

3. =.coder/coverage/blessed.csv= currently holds a header and nothing else, so

4. Defect (c) DOES move =harvest_kg='s =inferred= layer through
   =_kg_factor_series=, on 47.7% of Uganda's crop rows.  It does NOT invalidate
   a cache: =harvest_kg= is a transformation applied to =crop_production= at
   analysis time, not a stored column, and =transformations.py= is outside
   every build fingerprint (measured above).  So the cost is a before/after on
   =harvest_kg_factors=' layer counts, not a rebuild.  Keep (c) in its own
   commit anyway -- it changes a different feature's output, and a reviewer
   should be able to see that change on its own.

*** What it must NOT do

- *Never store an inferred factor.*  No new json, no new column on
  =food_acquired=, nothing written to a parquet.  A factor is a function of the
  frame it was inferred from, and the three existing =conversion_to_kgs.json=
  files are the anti-pattern, not the model.  (Uganda's is a curated answer key
  and is a different thing; it still must not be READ by the framework.)
- *Never change =food_acquired= itself.*  It is registered, cached and hashed;
  the defect is downstream of it.
- *Never reorder or rename =harvest_kg='s ladder*, and never change what
  =KG_FACTOR_LAYERS= means.  The food-side layer tuple is a sibling, not a
  replacement.
- *Never let =_get_kg_factors= change its return type*, for the same reason.
- *No allowlist of known-bad units.*  Same argument as =_grain_strict= and the
  null-read guard: a registry of exceptions is the disease with paperwork.

* 4. Decisions for @ligon

Each with the number that decides it.

** D1.  Land defect (c) -- the metric vocabulary -- at all?

/The number/: the ground-truth table in section 2.  The library currently
serves =Millilitre= at 0.74 kg in Malawi and 0.27 kg in GhanaLSS, against a
true 0.001; =Grams= at 0.71 kg against 0.001; Uganda's =Sack (100 kgs)= and
=Tin (Debe) (20 lts)= are estimated rather than read.  Fixing it also removes
between 0 and 33,394 rows per country from the inference set and adds them to
the kg baseline.

There is no argument on the other side that I can see, which is why it is D1
rather than a section of its own.  The only question is packaging: see D7.

** D2.  Land (b) -- divide by Quantity at step 2?

/The number/: the kg self-test.  The current chain returns a factor for the
kilogram of 0.87 to 2.0 depending on the country, where the answer is 1.0 by
definition, and the deviation tracks the median =Quantity= on the rows it
looked at (2 to 5, with only 4.6% to 40.5% of rows at =Quantity == 1=).

/Read the self-test honestly/: where a country's only kg-known rows ARE its kg
rows, fix (b) returning exactly 1.000 is an identity, not independent
confirmation.  What is NOT an identity is the current code's answer, which
should have been 1.0 under the same construction and is not.  Malawi is the
country where the baseline genuinely mixes in non-kg rows through its
=cfactor= column, and there fix (b) alone returns 0.44 -- which is evidence
for (a), not against (b): the household baseline is pooling other items.

** D3.  Land (a) -- the item axis?

/The number/: Malawi's =Piece=.  133 food items share it; the 72 with enough
data to estimate range from 1 g to 1.8 kg, a factor of 1,833; the library
serves 0.50 kg to all of them.  GhanaLSS's =Heap= (280,242 rows, 125 items)
spans a factor of 15,000.  Corpus-wide, the corrected factor differs from the current one by
more than 10% on 89% to 100% of the affected rows and by more than 2x on 47% to
93%, and the total kilograms in =food_quantities(units='kgs')= move by the
percentages in the third (iii) table.

** D4.  =min_reports= for the =(t, j, u)= rung.

/The number/: the floor sweep.  Coverage is almost flat in this floor -- moving
it from 3 to 20 costs a few percent of inference rows in every country.  So
this parameter is nearly free and 5 (matching =SURVEY_MEDIAN_MIN_REPORTS=) is
defensible on consistency grounds alone.

** D5.  =min_baseline= for the =(t, j)= price-per-kg reference.

/The number/: also the floor sweep, and the =baseline N= column of the
top-expenditure table.  This is the floor that binds: it decides whether one
household's single kilogram purchase gets to set an item's price nationally,
and the largest price movements in section 2 sit on the thinnest baselines.
The crop side's precedent gates exactly this quantity (=_survey_median_factors=
counts the REPORTS behind the reference, =:1901-1907=), and the food side
currently gates nothing at all.

** D6.  Does the =(j, u)= factor carry a =t= axis?

/The number/: the D6 table.  For =(j, u)= cells estimated in three or more
waves, how far apart the per-wave estimates are.  A container's weight should
not move between waves; a large spread means the estimate is noise and pooling
over =t= is the right answer, a small one means we are throwing away real
variation by pooling.

** D7.  Defect (c) touches =harvest_kg=, which the brief said not to touch.

/The number/: 62,321 of Uganda's 130,606 =crop_production= rows -- 47.7% --
carry a label (c) would newly resolve.  Every other country is 0, 1 or 5 rows.

*This is the one place the recommendation contradicts a stated constraint*, so
it is a decision rather than a detail.  (a) and (b) provably cannot reach
=harvest_kg=: =crop_production= has no =Expenditure= column, so it never enters
the price-ratio branch (=:1045=).  (c) is different, because it edits
=KNOWN_METRIC= and =_EXPLICIT_METRIC_PATTERNS=, which =_kg_factor_series=
(=:1660=) reads.

The argument for doing it anyway: today those 62,321 rows get NO inferred
factor at all, so they are served by the survey's own =KgFactor= where one
exists and by the =none= layer where it does not.  Reading "5 lts" off a label
that says "5 lts" is not a change of method, it is the method finally applying.
And it costs no rebuild.  The argument against is simply that it is not what
this issue is for.  @ligon decides; a separate commit is the mitigation either
way.

** D8.  Per-row provenance -- option (D)?

/The number/: the share of affected rows served by the fallback =u= rung rather
than by their own =(j, u)= cell, which is 1 - the floor sweep's coverage: about
a fifth in most countries and about half in Niger.  A consumer today cannot
tell those rows apart from the well-estimated ones, and after this change the
difference between the two is larger than it was before.

** D9.  What to say about Mali?

/The number/: the Mali ground-truth table.  Against the questionnaire's own
unit list, the current factors are 0.21x to 0.52x of truth on the three sacks
and 708x on =Gramme=; after (c) + (C) they are 0.33x to 0.71x and 166x.  Every
one of the four moves the right way and none of them arrives.  So the fix is an
improvement and =Mali/_/CONTENTS.org:786-794='s advice ("use native units, or
=u = 'Kg'=") still stands afterwards.  Whether that is stated in the code, in
Mali's =CONTENTS.org=, or in the issue is a call for @ligon.

** D10.  Correct the three EthiopiaRHS documents in this PR?

/The number/: one false claim, in three places (=ethiopiarhs.py:48-50=,
=CONTENTS.org:543-544=, =categorical_mapping.org:340-341=), plus
=Uganda/_/CONTENTS.org:810-813=.  Cheap either way; the argument for doing it
here is that the claim is about THIS function and would become true.

* 5. Test plan

Unit tests on synthetic frames, so they state the property rather than pin a
corpus number.

1. *The kilogram weighs a kilogram.*  A frame whose only unit is =kg=: the
   inference's own factor for it must be 1.0 exactly.  Today it is
   =1 / median(Quantity)=.  This is the regression test for (b) and it is one
   assertion.
2. *A quantity of two is not a kilogram of two.*  Two frames identical except
   that every =Quantity= is doubled and every =Expenditure= doubled with it:
   the inferred factors must be IDENTICAL.  Today they double.
3. *Two items, one unit.*  A frame with items A and B both sold by =bunch=,
   where A's bunch is 10x B's: the fix must return two factors in a 10:1 ratio,
   and the =u=-pooled fallback must return one number between them.
4. *The thin cell falls back, and says so.*  A =(j, u)= cell with =min_reports
   - 1= rows must be served by the =u= rung, with =KgFactorSource == 'unit'=.
   A =(t, j)= baseline with =min_baseline - 1= kg rows must not produce a
   =(j, u)= factor at all.
5. *NA keys survive.*  A row with NA =j= must reach the =u= rung rather than be
   deleted by =groupby=; assert the row count out equals the row count in.
   (=CLAUDE.md= "Grain Collapse" section 3b.)
6. *The layers partition the frame.*  =sum(counts.values()) == len(df)=, the
   invariant =harvest_kg_factors= already documents (=:1988-1991=).
7. *Metric spellings are seeded, never inferred* (defect c).  Parametrised over
   =Millilitre=, =Grams=, =Litres=, =Sack (100 kgs)=, =Tin (Debe) (20 lts)=,
   =Cup/Mug(0.5lt)=: each must come back from =_get_kg_factors= at its stated
   value, and must NOT appear as a key of =conversion_to_kgs='s output.
   Uganda's =conversion_to_kgs.json= is the answer key; a test that reads it
   and asserts agreement on the labels the parser now handles is worth having
   even though the framework must not read the file at runtime.
8. *The crop side does not move except where (c) says it does.*  Pin
   =_get_kg_factors='s return TYPE (=dict[str, float]=), and pin
   =harvest_kg_factors=' layer counts on the Uganda fixture in
   =tests/test_crop_kg_factor.py=, with the (c)-affected labels enumerated in
   the test's own docstring rather than discovered later.
9. *No cache hash moves*, for all three commits.  Extend
   =tests/test_build_transform_hash.py=: assert that no callable or constant of
   =lsms_library.transformations= appears in =build_transforms_fingerprint='s
   closure for any table -- including =KNOWN_METRIC= and
   =_EXPLICIT_METRIC_PATTERNS=, which is what makes (c) free as well.  This is the
   assertion form of =probe_hash_reach.py= and it protects the "no re-warm"
   claim from a future refactor that quietly moves a derived transform onto the
   build path.
10. *=units='kgprice'= uses the same factors.*  The =:1442= branch maps
    =u -> factor= independently of =:1431=; a test with a per-item factor must
    show both branches agreeing on the same row.

Integration, on the corpus rather than a fixture, run once before merge and
recorded in the PR:
