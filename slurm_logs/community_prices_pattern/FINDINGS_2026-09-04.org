* Answer

*Yes, adopt it more widely --- but adopt it as a /second, independent price
series/, not as a replacement for the derived unit value, and fix the
cross-country plumbing first.*  Where the two can be compared at all, they
largely agree: on standardised units (=Kg=, =Litre=, =Gram=) the median ratio
of the household unit value to the surveyed community price is *exactly 1.00*
in Malawi, Ethiopia, EthiopiaRHS, Nigeria and GhanaLSS, with 17--35 % of
matched cells /identical to the last digit/.  So the derived price is not
systematically wrong and there is no case for changing what =food_prices()=
prefers by default.  The case for the feature is different and stronger, and
it rests on four measured facts.  (i) *Seven of the eight countries that
declare =community_prices= carry no reported household price at all* ---
=food_acquired.Price= is absent for Malawi, Nigeria, Ethiopia, EthiopiaRHS,
Mali, Niger and Tanzania, so =food_prices(units='unitprice')= returns an empty
frame for all of them and the surveyed community price is the /only/ reported
price the library holds.  (ii) The derived unit value is *undefined exactly
where it is most needed*: GhanaLSS is 100 %/100 %/54 %/82 %/87 % =u='Value''=
in 1987-88/1988-89/1991-92/1998-99/2012-13, and a community price exists for
every one of those waves --- this is GH #562 phase 3b and the #770 fallout in
one sentence.  (iii) Where the two /disagree/, the disagreement is a *unit-
basis collision, not a price disagreement*: Nigeria 2016Q1 =u='cl'= gives a
median ratio of *0.0167* over 1,241 cells (a factor of ~60), and Mali's =Tas=
/ =Unité= / =Boîte= give 0.22 / 0.11 / 0.35, while their =Kg= rows sit at
1.00.  The community instrument is the thing that can /fix/ that, because it
records the unit's size (Tanzania's =CM_SEC_G= literally ships a measured
weight per local unit).  (iv) The pattern is *cheap to extend and already
half-built elsewhere*: eight further declared countries hold an item-level,
cluster- or PSU-keyed, reported price file that nobody has wired.

What it would change, concretely: register =community_prices= in the canonical
=index_info= (today =Feature('community_prices')= *silently excludes
GhanaLSS's 660,168 rows --- 67 % of the corpus's community-price data* ---
whenever GhanaLSS is asked for alongside any other country); wire the eight
unwired countries; and build the =community -> household= price join as a
documented transformation, never as a default substitution.

* 2. Comparability: surveyed community price vs. derived household unit value

** Method

Household side: =Expenditure / Quantity= on =s='purchased'= rows of the
API-level =food_acquired= (i.e. the =units='unitvalue'= formula, *in native
=u=*), then the *median within =(t, v, j, u)=*.  Community side: the
per-native-unit price, reconstructed per country from the stored reported
columns (§3), then the median within =(t, v, j, u)= (this collapses GhanaLSS's
=obs=).  Ratio = household unit value / community price.  Deliberately *not*
routed through =food_prices(units='kgvalue')=: that mode is being edited right
now for #770, and kg conversion would import exactly the unit-inference
problem the comparison is meant to test.

* 3. What the pattern actually is --- and where it is not yet one pattern

** The shape

A =community_prices= row is: a *REPORTED* price, at *cluster* grain, for a
*named item* in a *named native unit*, with *no imputation and no aggregation
in core*, joinable to households through =v=.  It contrasts with the derived
unit value on four axes:

| axis          | =community_prices=                                  | derived =food_prices=                                    |
|---------------+-----------------------------------------------------+----------------------------------------------------------|
| provenance    | surveyed --- an enumerator recorded a market price  | computed --- =Expenditure / Quantity= from a household row |
| grain         | =(t, v, j, u)= --- cluster                          | =(t, v, i, j, u, s)= --- household                       |
| existence     | exists iff the survey fielded a price questionnaire | exists iff the household reported *both* a value and a quantity |
| failure modes | few vendors; the market may not be where the household shops | unit heterogeneity; quality/quantity discounts; degenerate rows (=u='Value'= -> 1, #770) |

The library already treats it correctly in one important respect: *no country
stores a per-unit price.*  Every one stores the reported numerator and (where
the instrument gives one) the reported denominator, leaving the division to a
transformation.  That is the same discipline =food_acquired= applies by
carrying =Quantity= + =Expenditure= rather than a price, and it is the part of
the pattern most worth propagating.

** But it is not yet ONE pattern --- three column conventions for one concept

Three consequences a "pattern" would have to settle:

1. *A consumer cannot write one expression.*  Getting a per-unit price out of
   =Feature('community_prices')= requires knowing, per country, whether to
   divide by =Quantity=, by =NumberOfUnits=, or not at all.  A canonical
   =PricePerUnit= /transformation/ (never a stored column) belongs in
   =transformations.py=.
2. *=obs= vs. selection.*  GhanaLSS keeps all vendor observations; Mali and
   Niger *select one* (documented, deterministic, never averaged); Malawi /
   Nigeria / Ethiopia / Tanzania have a single row per cell by construction.
   GhanaLSS's choice is the more honest one --- and it is the one the corpus
   penalises today (§3.3).  Note that the instruments the /others/ read also
   carry multiple observations: Tajikistan's =comm_r8= has SHOP 1/2/3,
   Albania 2002 has three prices, South Africa 1993 has =cfpri1/cfpri2=,
   Guatemala has =cotiza=.  Adopting the pattern widely means deciding =obs=
   corpus-wide, not per country.
3. *=u= is nominally harmonised but not semantically aligned* --- §2's
   container-unit collisions.  The instruments that record the unit's *size*
   (GhanaLSS =NumberOfUnits=, Liberia's "Standard Unit Equivalent",
   Tanzania's =CM_SEC_G= measured weight) are the ones that could close this.

Measured on =9e12753f=:

has *no =index_info= entry*, so assembly keeps the modal shape and drops the

so single-observation countries get =obs = <NA>=) is the right one, and the
measurement above is the argument for it: the alternative --- dropping =obs=

aggregation the design rules out.

* 4. Where else the pattern applies

The generalisable shape is *"a reported, cluster-level, item-level attribute
carried at its own grain, with no aggregation in core"*.  Three candidates,
with evidence:

1. *=community_unit_weights= --- a REPORTED local-unit -> weight table.*  This
   is the best candidate and it bears directly on #770.  Tanzania
   =CM_SEC_G.dta= / =cm_sec_g.dta= is a =(t, v, j, u, obs)= file carrying
   =vil_loc_unit= ("LOCAL UNIT"), =vil_loc_weight= ("LOCAL UNIT WEIGHT (KGS)")
   and =vil_loc_price= for 15 items x 3 observations --- 99 communities /
   207 priced rows in 2019-20, 488 communities / 3,906 priced rows with a
   weight in 2020-21 --- over exactly the container units the library cannot
   convert (=Heap (Fungu)=, =Piece (Idadi)=, =Bucket (sandoline)=, =Tin
   container (Debe)=, =Cane (Kopo)=).  It is *entirely unused*: Tanzania's
   feature reads =CM_SEC_F_ID= only.  Liberia's =comm_sect7_public= carries
   the same idea (=S7_4_quant= / =S7_4_unit=, "Standard Unit Equivalent").
   A reported, cluster-level unit-size table is precisely what
   =conversion_to_kgs= currently /infers/ from price ratios.
2. *=community_wages=.*  Uganda 2005-06 =csec4=/=csec41= carry agricultural
   and non-agricultural wages per person-day by sex at cluster grain
   (=c4q20..c4q23=, 312 clusters); Malawi's Module CK prices a =ganyu=
   day-wage alongside its goods.  Reported, cluster-level, item-like (the
   "item" is a labour category).  This is the natural home for the wage inputs
   =median_price_valuation= needs for =plot_labor= valuation, and Uganda ---
   which has *no* community food-price module --- would gain a
   =community_prices=-shaped feature from it.
3. *=community_land_prices=.*  Uganda 2018-19 / 2019-20 =CSEC2A= carries
   current and five-years-ago *rental* and *sale* prices for 1 ha of
   agricultural land and 200 m² of residential land, at cluster grain, in
   every one of ~305 communities.  Same shape; currently unread.

*Not* candidates, and worth saying so: Malawi =com_cd= / Ethiopia =sect4_com=
carry reported cluster attributes too (bus fares, distances to market), but
they are *one value per cluster*, not item-level --- they belong in
=cluster_features=, which already exists for exactly that.  And
Tanzania_Kegera's =price2004_cl.dta= is a Laspeyres index, i.e. derived: the
opposite of the pattern.

* 6. What I could NOT measure, and why

1. *31 % of the corpus was not swept for price modules.*  The corpus-wide
   label sweep read 4,799 of 6,941 DVC-tracked =.dta=/=.sav= sources; *2,142
   blobs were not in the local L1 cache* and I chose not to pull them (that
   would be thousands of S3 fetches into the shared cache).  Both sweeps are
   also *label-based*, so a source that ships no Stata variable labels is a
   silent miss --- as are fixed-width =.DAT= + =.DCT= pairs (CotedIvoire,
   GhanaLSS 1980s, Nicaragua 2001), which carry no metadata to sweep at all.
   (Spot-checked: Uganda's 2009-10 / 2010-11 =CSECTION*= files *do* carry
   labels, so their zero price hits are a real absence, not a blind spot.)
   The §1 unwired list is therefore a *lower bound* on all three counts.  A
   complete sweep is a bounded Slurm job.
2. *No questionnaire (C4) was read except Uganda's.*  Every "genuinely absent"
   claim in §1 other than Uganda 2011-12 / 2019-20 rests on the shipped data
   files (C1) plus the existing =data_scheme.yml= prose, not on the
   instrument.  By the repo's own rule that is =unsure=, not =not-asked=; I
   have written no verdicts.
3. *Niger and Tanzania have no comparability number at all* --- §2.  For
   Tanzania a region-level fallback comparison is possible and was not
   attempted (it needs the region crosswalk #113 describes as lossy).
4. *The "agreement" is not adjusted for anything.*  No weighting, no seasonal
   alignment (the community price is a point-in-time quote; the household unit
   value is a recall-period average), no outlier trimming beyond dropping
   non-positive values.  The exact-tie share is reported precisely so the
   reader can discount the median.
5. *I did not test whether the community price improves an estimate.*  The
   #562 phase-3b question --- does =Quantity = Expenditure / community_price=
   produce plausible quantities in the =u='Value'= waves --- is the decisive
   experiment and it is not run here.
6. *Blessings.* =.coder/coverage/blessed.csv= contains only its header: *no
   =community_prices= cell has ever been blessed*, in any country.  Nothing in
   this document is a blessing; I read distributions, not individual numbers
   against a source.

* 7. Contradictions found (both sides quoted)

1. *=GAP_RANKING.org:253= / =INCIDENCE_MAP.org:131= vs. the Uganda data.*  The
   docs say: /"Raw sources (data already in-repo, mostly unwired): ... Uganda
   Community =csec3*=..."/ and /"community price modules exist across LSMS-ISA
   (Uganda =csec3*=, Malawi =com_cc=, Nigeria =sectc*= Community, Ethiopia
   ESS)"/.  Measured: no Uganda wave's community data files carry a food-price
   module (C1, all seven waves; C4 confirms it for 2011-12 and 2019-20 --- see
   §1 for the scope of that claim).  Its =CSEC3*= files are *school* modules
   (=CFSchoolFacility=, =CFSchoolWater=, =CFSchoolPayment=, ...).  The
   2011-12 instrument PDF contains "price" zero times.  Malawi's price module
   is =com_ck=, not =com_cc= (=com_cc= is a different section).  I have not
   edited either file; this is the report.
2. *=Nigeria/_/CONTENTS.org:779= vs. the current build.*  The file says
   /"124,539 priced rows across 5 waves x 584 clusters x 139 food items x 30
   units"/.  A cold build on =9e12753f= gives *138,055 rows* over *870 distinct
   clusters* (487--517 per wave; 2,501 cluster-waves).  The numbers date from
   =e3b5ed6f= (2026-06-14); =b84b1922= subsequently re-keyed Nigeria's =v= to
   the composite =cluster_id(state, lga, ea)= for #323.  The prose about the
   composite key was updated, the counts were not.  Item and unit counts in
   the file (139 / 30) still match the corpus-wide totals I measure.
3. *=.coder/coverage/latest.csv= is stale for this feature, three ways.*  Last
   refreshed =9d2d6ce0=, 2026-09-02, before PR #762 merged.  It reports
   (a) =GhanaLSS,community_prices,,undeclared,absent= --- but GhanaLSS
   declares it and builds 660,168 rows; (b) =EthiopiaRHS,community_prices,<all
   8 waves>,absent= --- but EthiopiaRHS builds 2,691 rows over 6 waves (the
   grader cannot see a country-level-only script-path table); and (c) Nigeria's
   five post-planting quarters graded =dropped= ("declared for wave but absent
   from built t-index"), which is correct-by-design --- the price module is
   post-harvest only --- not a defect.  I did not regenerate the matrix.
4. *A claim I checked and could NOT break.*  Tanzania's wave script divides
   =cm_f063= by =cm_f062= and calls the result "currency per one native =u=",
   while the sibling file =CM_SEC_G= labels its analogous column "LOCAL UNIT
   WEIGHT (KGS)" --- which would make the quotient a per-*kg* price wearing a
   per-=Piece= label.  Measured on =CM_SEC_F_ID.dta=: median =cm_f062= is
   1.0 for KILOGRAMS, 1.0 for LITRES, 1.0 for PIECES, 370 for GRAMS and 225
   for MILLILITRES --- i.e. =cm_f062= is the amount *in the stated unit*, and
   the script is right.  Recorded because the label invites the wrong reading.

* 8. Recommendation

1. *Register the feature canonically.*  Add
   =community_prices: (t, v, j, u, obs)= to =data_info.yml= =index_info= and
   add =community_prices= to =fabricate_missing_levels=.  Without it the
   default cross-country call drops whichever side is in the minority --- today
   67 % of the data.  (Proposed on PR #762; measured here.)
2. *Add a =PricePerUnit= transformation*, never a stored column, that knows the
   three column conventions in §3.  Today a consumer must know per country
   whether to divide by =Quantity=, =NumberOfUnits=, or nothing.
3. *Do NOT change what =food_prices()= prefers.*  The measured agreement gives
   no basis for it, and a silent substitution would be the same disease as a
   silent pool.  Expose the community price as its own series and let the
   analyst choose --- exactly the #603 ruling on =Feature()=.
4. *Build the community -> household join as a documented transformation*, with
   the geography ladder =v -> District -> Region -> national= that
   =median_price_valuation= already implements, and use it for #562 phase 3b.
   The =u='Value'= table in §3 is the demand.
5. *Wire the cheap tier first*: Liberia 2018-19, Tajikistan 2007, South
   Africa 1993.  Each is a long-format, EA/PSU-keyed, item-level file; each
   would take one wave script and one item crosswalk.
6. *Treat the container-unit collision as a defect with a name.*  Nigeria
   =cl= and Mali =Tas=/=Unité= are not noise; they say the =u= axis is
   harmonised in label only.  The instruments that record unit size
   (GhanaLSS =NumberOfUnits=, Liberia's standard-unit equivalent, Tanzania's
   =CM_SEC_G=) are the fix, and §4.1 is the feature that would carry it.
7. *Fix Niger's asymmetry or say why not.*  Prices for 2011-12/2014-15 with no
   consumption, consumption for 2018-19/2021-22 with no prices, is the one
   built cell that currently buys nothing.
8. *Refresh =latest.csv=* so the three artefacts in §7.3 stop misreporting the
   feature.
