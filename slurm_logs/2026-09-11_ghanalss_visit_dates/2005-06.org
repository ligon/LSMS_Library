* Visit protocol

One line: eleven interviewer visits per household, every third day, a 33-day
EA cycle, identical in urban and rural areas; paper questionnaire (Part A +
Part B), manual data entry; purchases (9B) and own-produce consumption (8H)
both asked at visits 2..11 with "since my last visit" recall (9B's first ask
says "since my first visit"); a diary form existed (catalog 1064) but no
local document describes its use.

** Number of visits and spacing -- ESTABLISHED, from the DDI and from the data

=ddi-documentation-english-5.pdf=, PDF p.9, "Interviewer Workload":

#+begin_quote
A team of three interviewers worked in three EAs during a 33 day cycle. One
interviewer was assigned to work in one EA during a cycle. In both rural and
urban areas, each interviewer conducted five interviews per day. Thus, at the
end of each 33 day period (one cycle) a team will have interviewed 45
households. The Data Capture staff entered all 45 Part A questionnaires before
the team left for the next set of EAs.

An interviewer visited each household in the EA assigned to him/her every
third day. Thus an interviewer's workload of 15 households was divided into
three batches of five households.
#+end_quote

Same page, "Team Composition": "For both urban and rural areas, a field team
consisted of seven members: one supervisor, one senior interviewer, 3
interviwers, one data cature staff and a driver."  (sic, both.)

The survey's own visit record (=partb/secb2.dta=, below) corroborates it
exactly: median gap 3.0 days at every one of the ten steps, 94.9% of all
pooled gaps are exactly 3 days, 6,763 of 8,639 households have all nine
gaps v2..v11 equal to 3, visits fall flat across the seven weekdays
(12,211-12,418 per weekday: fieldwork ran through weekends), and the
v1 -> v11 span has median 30 days (p10 28, p90 30).  Urban and rural are
indistinguishable (median 3.0 at every step in both; span 30 in both).

So the eleven-visit / 3-day / 33-day-cycle schedule that
=GLSS7 Final Manual.docx= carries as boilerplate matches what GLSS5
actually did -- but UNSTRATIFIED.  GLSS3's 8-rural / 11-urban split does
not apply here.  (Whether the Manual TEXT descended through GLSS5 is a
separate question this evidence does not touch.)

Coverage: 10 asks x 3 days = 30 days, urban and rural alike.  Fieldwork
ran September 2005 through August 2006 (=sec0= =mdate= x =ydate=: ~895
first visits per month Sep-Dec 2005, 309-778 per month Jan-Aug 2006, a
handful of keying strays in other months).

** Recall phrase, per module

Section 9B, =G5QPartB.pdf= PDF p.36 (printed 9.13): ten columns headed
"2nd 3rd 4th ... 11th VISIT", each with a "dd mm" slot, numbered 1..10:
"How much was spent on ........ since my first visit?" (column 1) then
"... since my last visit?" (columns 2..10).  Amount only -- no quantity.

Section 8H, =G5QPartB.pdf= PDF p.17 (printed 8.11): fourteen columns.
1 "Did the household consume any own produced ........ in the past 12
months?"; 2 "How many months altogether was own produced ...... consumed
during the past 12 months?"; 3..12 headed "2nd 3rd ... 11th" with a
"dd mm" slot each: "How much of own produced ..... was consumed by the
household since my last visit?  No. OF UNITS"; 13 unit code; 14 "For how
much would you sell one unit of .... now?".

So on the instrument 8H's per-visit columns are q3..q12 -> visits 2..11,
exactly parallel to 9B's q1..q10 -> 2..11.  See Contradiction 2.

* Idiosyncrasies and contradictions

** Contradiction 1 -- visit spacing is now ESTABLISHED, and the boilerplate does describe GLSS5

CONTENTS.org 1531-1539 (=GLSS7 Final Manual.docx= is stale ...): "GLSS5
also fields eleven visits ... so it may have passed through that round
too; what the evidence settles is that the text is NOT GLSS7's, not which
earlier round it came from."  And =.coder/HANDOFF_recall_research.md:141=:
"GhanaLSS 2005-06 | 1 | needs GLSS5's own manual for the visit spacing".

Both can be closed without the manual.  GLSS5's DDI (PDF p.9, quoted
above) states "every third day", "33 day cycle", "in both rural and urban
areas", and =secb2= measures it: median 3.0 at all ten steps in both
strata, 94.9% of gaps exactly 3, span 30.  The eleven-visit / 3-day /
33-day schedule in the GLSS7 Manual's Table 2 MATCHES GLSS5's documented
and measured practice (and GLSS3's urban schedule).  That is a statement
about practice, not about the Manual text's lineage -- CONTENTS.org's
2026-09-09 retraction of the "carried over from GLSS5" attribution
stands; nothing here shows which round the text passed through.  Unlike
GLSS3 the GLSS5 schedule is NOT stratified: rural households get 11
visits at 3 days, not 8 at 2 days.  The recall window
for =food_expenditures= in 2005-06 is therefore 30 days, urban and rural
alike, the same figure as GLSS7's 6 x 5.

** Contradiction 2 -- 8H is asked at visit 2, and =s8hq3= is the 2nd-visit quantity, not a total

CONTENTS.org table (line 1261): "2005-06 (GLSS5) | 11 | q1..q10 -> 2..11 |
q4..q12 -> 3..11 | 10 / 9", and 1284-1288: "GLSS5 is why =visit= is the
calendar number ... There, purchases run from the 2nd visit but own
production only from the 3rd (=s8hq3= is 'number of units consumed', a
total, not a visit)".  =2005-06/_/food_acquired.py:132-135= implements
that: "s8hq3 is 'number of units consumed', a total, not a visit" and maps
=s8hq4..s8hq12 -> visits 3..11=, dropping =s8hq3=.

Three independent sources say otherwise, and the data settle it:

- The questionnaire, =G5QPartB.pdf= printed 8.11: columns 3..12 are headed
  "2nd 3rd 4th 5th 6th 7th 8th 9th 10th 11th", each "How much of own
  produced ..... was consumed by the household since my last visit?".
  Column 3 is the 2nd visit.
- The summary grids A.04 and B.04: "SECOND | 3, 8H, 9B" -- 8H is
  administered at the second visit.
- The DDI's literal question for =s8hq3= (PDF p.460, "Number of units
  consumed (s8hq3)"): "How much of own produced ... was consumed by the
  household since my last visit?"  Only the .dta variable label ("number
  of units consumed") reads as a total.  The labels on =s8hq4..s8hq12=
  ("... at 3rd visit" ... "at 11th visit") are consistent with the
  questionnaire (question 4 = 3rd visit, question 12 = 11th visit); it
  is =s8hq3='s label alone that fails to say "2nd visit".
- The data (=partb/sec8h.dta=, rows with =s8hq1= = yes, n = 48,270):
  =s8hq3= is non-null in 48,228 and positive in 21,440, against 18,266 for
  =s8hq4= and 17,874 ... 15,653 for q5..q12 -- the same magnitude as a
  visit column and a 1.17x first-ask excess, exactly the pattern every
  other wave shows.  It is NOT the sum of q4..q12: it equals that sum in
  18,578 rows, exceeds it in 2,740, and is SMALLER than it in 26,910.  A
  total cannot be smaller than the sum of its parts in 56% of rows.
  Median q3/q4 ratio 1.0; q3 == q4 in 62.8% of rows, the same share as
  q4 == q5 (66.6%).

Consequences, reported not fixed: (a) the library's 2005-06 produced side
drops the 2nd-visit ask -- 21,440 positive records -- and serves own
production from visit 3 only; (b) the "10 / 9" consumption-occasion cell
should read 10 / 10, and 8H covers the same 30 days as 9B; (c) the
Scott-Amenuvegbe passage (CONTENTS ~1462-1465: "GLSS5, whose own-production
module starts at the 3rd visit rather than the 2nd, shows the weakest
first-ask step (1.02x ...)") is an artefact -- the step it measured was
visit 3 vs 4, and the real first ask has a 1.17x step on positive counts;
(d) the ledger's Phase-3 note ("2005-06 -> purchased 2..11, produced 3..11
(the real asymmetry s5 predicted)") records the label, not the instrument.
=s8hq2= (months consumed in the past 12) is the only screener with a
numeric answer; =s8hq1= is the yes/no.

** Idiosyncrasies not in CONTENTS.org

- Two transcriptions of every visit date.  =secb1= (the A.01-A.03 boxes)
  and =secb2= (the A.04 grid) agree on 76,152 of 85,919 shared (hhid,
  visit) dates and disagree on 9,767, with +-365 days (year keyed as
  2005 vs 2006) and +-30/31 (month slips) dominating the differences.
  Prefer =secb2=: it is the form's single summary grid, it carries the
  result/duration on the same row, and its per-visit valid-date counts
  are marginally higher.  Where they disagree, one of the two is usually
  exactly the +-365 or +-30 shift of the other.
- Likewise =sec0= vs =secb0= for the first-visit date: 7,953 of 8,639
  equal; 686 differ, dominated by -1/+1/+3 days and +-30/31/365.
- Duplicate keys.  =secb1= has 314 rows (149 households) with a repeated
  (hhid, visitno); =secb2= 156 rows (78 households).  In the pairs
  inspected the two dates are 3 days apart -- consecutive visits keyed
  with the same visit number, not repeat visits.  Anyone pivoting on
  (hhid, visit) must dedupe or re-derive the visit ordinal from the date.
- 718 households have secb2's visit-2 date EQUAL to the sec0 first-visit
  date (gap 0 at step 1->2, mostly rural: 561 of 718).  Either the
  first-visit date on sec0 was written at visit 2, or visits 1 and 2 were
  same-day.  From step 2->3 on, zero-day gaps fall to ~1%.
- The DDI documents =urbrur= in all four survey-information files; none of
  the shipped files has it.  Use =parta/sec7.dta:loc2= as already wired.
- =secb2.vres= ships WITHOUT value labels although =cres/sinu/sentry= have
  them; the codes come from the printed grid (A.04).
- Part A and Part B print the same Survey Information / Summary pages;
  their SECOND-visit section lists differ ("3, 8H, 9B" vs "3, 5, 8H, 9B").
- 48 households are in =sec0= only (no Part B files, no 9B/8H rows).
  Whoever joins =secb*= to =sample= should expect 8,639, not 8,687.
- The =dd mm= slots on the 8H and 9B page headers mean each module page
  carries its own visit date on paper; that is the one genuinely
  per-section date GSS holds and did not distribute.
- No diary is mentioned in any local GLSS5 document, but the WB catalog
  lists a GLSS5 Diary form.  The GLSS7 Main Report's diary passage should
  not be back-projected onto GLSS5 without that form or the manual.
