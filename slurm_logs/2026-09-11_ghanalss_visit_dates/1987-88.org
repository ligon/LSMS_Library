* Visit protocol

One line: two interviewer rounds two weeks apart (Round 1 = Sections 0-8,
16A, 17A/C; Round 2 = Sections 9-15, 16B, 17B), paper questionnaire, decentralised
PC data entry BETWEEN the rounds, supervisor reinterview of a random ~25%, no
diary; Section 12 asked ONCE at Round 2 with the recall bounded by Round 1.

Why the expenditure module was asked only once although the household was
visited twice: Round One is the BOUNDING visit -- it fixes the start of the
recall window ("since my last visit ... during Round One"), and the module is
placed at Round Two so that the window is a realised, dated two weeks.  The
first contact collects the roster and designates the food respondent (Section
7); it does not collect consumption.  This is the same logic the multi-visit
waves use for their visit 1 (CONTENTS.org l.1281-1283), compressed to two
contacts.

Relation to CONTENTS.org's "1 visit": not a contradiction, but a term of art.
"Visit" in the =visit= level counts recall OCCASIONS; the household was
physically contacted by the interviewer at least twice (three times in urban
areas, counting the appointment call), by the supervisor once more in ~22% of
households (=REINTER= = 1 in 697 of 3,147, i.e. 24% of the 2,864 non-null;
=REINTER2= = 1 in 666 of 3,147, 24% of 2,824 non-null),
and by the anthropometrist at each round (=Y16A=/=Y16B= dates).  Worth a
sentence in CONTENTS.org so the next reader does not equate "single visit"
with "single contact".

* Where a visit DATE / TIME is recorded on the instrument

Every date box on the instrument is DAY / MONTH / YEAR (three two-digit
boxes).  *There is no time-of-day box and no duration box anywhere* -- not on
0A, 0B, 0C, on any section header, on the supervisor's verification or
reinterview forms, or in the data-entry system as described.  The nearest
thing to a time is the interviewer manual's "turn up on the day and at the
time agreed upon" (printed p.8), which is an appointment, not a record.

=Y16A=/=Y16B= are per-person anthropometry records with the measurement date
in each round: 13,343 of 14,943 valid Round-1 dates over 3,111 households;
8,411 of 9,603 Round-2 dates over 3,096 households.  Per household the
Round-1 measurement date equals =DAY1/MO1/YR1= exactly in 2,182 households
(median difference 0 days, p90 +1); the Round-2 date equals =DAY2/MO2/YR2= in
1,381 (median 0, p10 -2, p90 +8).  So the anthropometrist normally moved with
the interviewer, and =Y16B= is an INDEPENDENT second record of the Round-Two
visit date (used below).

** Visit slots, non-null counts, result vocabulary, gaps

Visit slots in =Y00A=: 2 interviewer visits (Round One, Round Two) + 1
proposed date + 2 supervisor verifications + 2 data-entry dates + 2 printout
supervisions = 9 dated events.  Valid (parseable, year in {87,88}) dates:
int1 3147, sup1 3141, op1 3140, supop1 3130, proposed 3132, int2 3131,
sup2 3133, op2 3136, supop2 3127.

Result-code vocabulary that shipped: =LOC= (dwelling found 1/2), =HHSAME=
(head same 1/2), =REPRES= (replacement reason 1 dwelling not found/vacant, 2
occupant not at home, 3 refusal), =REINTER= / =REINTER2= (supervisor
reinterview 1 yes / 2 no).  The 0B per-section COMPLETE/PARTIAL result codes
did not ship.

* Idiosyncrasies and contradictions

- *"Single-visit recall wave" vs. two interviewer rounds.*  CONTENTS.org
  l.1258 ("1987-88, 1988-89 | 1 | single recall ... | 1") and l.1265-67 are
  correct about recall OCCASIONS; the BID (PDF p.4, p.17) and the 0A grid show
  two dated interviewer rounds per household, ~14 days apart, plus a supervisor
  reinterview visit in ~22% of households and anthropometrist visits at both
  rounds.  Suggested one-line addition to CONTENTS.org: "one recall occasion,
  two interviewer contacts; the Round-One date bounds the recall".
- *=interview_date= wires Round One; the food module was asked at Round Two.*
  =1987-88/_/data_info.yml= =interview_date: DAY1/MO1/YR1=.  Section 12 is a
  Round-Two section (BID PDF p.4), dated by =DAY2/MO2/YR2= (=INTID2=).
  Whether =interview_date= should carry both (a =visit= level 1/2, as
  =melt_visit_intervals= does for later waves) is a decision for the
  maintainer; the material is in the file.
- *The recall window is conditional* (Interviewer manual printed p.70,
  quoted in s1): fixed "past two weeks" when the realised gap is < 1 week or
  > 3 weeks or there were several visits.  Not in CONTENTS.org.
- *Section 12B is NOT a "since my last visit" recall; only 12A is.*  CONTENTS.org
  l.1258 tabulates "1987-88, 1988-89 | 1 | single recall | single recall | 1"
  for both 9B/12A (purchases) and 8H/12B (own produce), and
  =1987-88/_/food_acquired.py= reads =Y12B.DAT= =VFOODCPD= as a "per-recall
  value".  The instrument (questionnaire PDF p.68, printed 43a, box =12B1=)
  asks q3 "During which months in the past 12 months (since...) did your
  household consume the [...] it produced?  WRITE THE NUMBER OF MONTHS CITED",
  q4 "How many times during these months did they eat home-produced [...]?"
  (TIMES + TIME UNIT: day 3, week 4, month 5, quarter 6, half year 7, year 8),
  q5 "How much would it cost to buy the amount they ate each time?"  The BID
  (PDF p.4, printed 2) says the same: "Section 12B asks the amount consumed and
  market value of foods grown or raised by the household in the last twelve
  months."  So 12B is a 12-month frequency x value-per-eating-occasion
  structure with no bounded two-week window, while 12A q2-q3 ("bought any
  [...] since my last visit?", "How much have they spent since my visit?") is
  the genuine bounded recall.  Stamping the produced side =visit = 1= beside a
  two-week purchase total is therefore a mixed-window row set.  Quoted both
  sides; not adjudicated here.
- *=Y00A::LOC= is "DWELLING FOUND?"*, not a location code.  =appendix_i_clusters.org=
  calls it "a fieldwork flag" without naming it; the 0A page names it.
- *The 336 copied Round-Two dates and 13 swapped pairs* (s3).  Anyone using
  =DAY2= as the Round-Two date must patch these from =DAY5= or =Y16B=.
- *0B and 0C are printed in the 1987 questionnaire scan* (PDF p.4-5) even
  though their data exist for 1988-89 only.  The scan is a different file
  from the 1988-89 one (DVC md5 =b679d62d...= vs =0a79d2e0...=, sizes 4,068,606 vs
  4,068,588 bytes) but the same 78-page instrument; the BID (PDF p.4) is
  explicit that "Section 0B and 0C are only available for 1988-89" as DATA.
  I did not find a statement of whether 1987-88's 0B pages were filled in
  and not keyed, or keyed and not released -- that is the question for GSS.
- *No Y17 files and no Y00B/Y00C in 1987-88* -- consistent with BID Appendix
  B's "88-89 ONLY" column (Y00B, Y00C, Y17A-C are second-year additions).
- *Scan defects to know about*: the Interviewer manual scan has no printed
  p.11 (PDF p.17 is printed 10, PDF p.18 is printed 12); PDF-to-printed offset
  in the Supervisor manual is +3 up to ch.6 and +1 by ch.8-9 (printed 44 =
  PDF 45, printed 46 = PDF 47), so index by printed number and check the
  header.  Interviewer, Supervisor and Flowchart scans render inverted.
- *=1988-89/Data/= currently holds untracked workspace copies* (=Y00A.DAT=,
  =Y00B.DAT=, =Y00C.DAT= and their =.DCT= beside the =.dvc= sidecars);
  =get_dataframe= warned "Refusing workspace copy of DVC-tracked file" and
  fell through to the cache.  Not touched; noting for whoever owns that tree.
- Not a contradiction but a caution: =CID= is constant 11 in every 1987-88
  file (it is the survey/round code), and the BID's =CLUST = Year*1000 +
  cluster= convention is what =appendix_i_clusters.org= uses (=1000 + yr1=).
