Scope: a READER's audit of the GLSS6 instruments, manuals, report and the
Section-0-type data files, for the GSS data request on interviewer-visit
dates.  No library table was built; no tracked file was edited.  All page
references give the PDF page of the file named and, where the page carries
one, the printed page label.

Mandatory reading done first (per the brief): =GhanaLSS/_/CONTENTS.org=
lines 1-32, 1235-1660 ("The repeated-visit design, wave by wave"), 2869-2932
(text layers), and every =2012-13= / =GLSS6= hit; =2012-13/_/data_info.yml=
(=interview_date= wires only =PARTA/SEC0.dta ddate/mdate/ydate=);
=2012-13/_/food_acquired.py= docstring (=s9bq1..6= -> visits 2..7, =s8hq3..8=
-> visits 2..7); =.coder/ledger/ghanalss-visit-offset.md=.  Idiosyncrasies
already recorded there and relied on here: GLSS6 fields seven visits with
9B at q1..q6 and 8H at q3..q8 (8H has two screeners), the q -> calendar-visit
map is carried in the Stata labels, =reint= flags 1,476 households, and the
GLSS7 manual's eleven-visit schedule is inherited boilerplate whose lineage
could not be pinned to a round.  Two of those are sharpened below
(=reint=; the manual lineage) and one belief in the brief is overturned:
*this wave ships complete per-visit dates for all seven visits*.

* Visit protocol

One line: seven paper-questionnaire visits per household at five-day
intervals inside a 35-day cycle, diary-assisted in all EAs, keyed in
regional offices on microcomputers (not CAPI).

- *Seven visits, five days apart, 35-day cycle.*
  =REPORTS/GLSS6_Main Report.pdf= Appendix 1 s2 "Survey Period" (PDF p.222,
  printed 201): "There were 10 cycles of 35 days each and one travelling
  day ... A cycle in both rural and urban areas lasted 35 days.  Interviewers
  in rural and urban areas all used the diary method as far as possible".
  s4 "Interviewer Workload" (same page): "An interviewer visited each
  household in the EA assigned to him/her every sixth day i.e. after every
  five days.  In other words, the interviewer visited each of the 15 selected
  household seven (7) times in order to complete the cycle."  Tables A1.3
  "Days of Visit" (Batch 1: DAY1 DAY6 ... DAY31) and A1.4 (PDF p.223, printed
  202).  =MANUALS/GLSS6 INTERVIEWERS MANUAL.pdf= s1.3 (PDF p.9, printed 4)
  agrees: "A cycle in both rural and urban areas is to last 35 days ...
  visit households at five-day intervals."  Fieldwork "took place from 18th
  October 2012 to 17th October 2013" (Report s1.5, PDF p.24, printed 3).
  *Measured* (below): median gap 5.0 days at every step 1->2 ... 6->7, p10 =
  p90 = 5, urban and rural alike; first-to-last span median 30 days.

- *Sections by visit* -- the questionnaire's own grid, "SUMMARY OF SURVEY
  RESULTS", page A0.3 (=QUESTIONNAIRES/GLSS6 Part A Questionnaire.pdf= PDF
  p.4; identical on =GLSS6 Part B Questionnaire.pdf= PDF p.4):
  | visit   | sections          |
  |---------+-------------------|
  | FIRST   | 1, 2, 5, 6, 7     |
  | SECOND  | 3, 8H, 9A, 9B     |
  | THIRD   | 4, 8H, 9B         |
  | FOURTH  | 8A-G, 8H, 9B      |
  | FIFTH   | 8H, 9B, 9C, 10    |
  | SIXTH   | 8H, 9B, 11        |
  | SEVENTH | 8H, 9B, 12        |
  (The manual's own table, PDF p.21, differs in detail -- 1st: 1, 2, 6, 7;
  5th: 5, 8H, 9A, 9B, 9C -- and runs to eleven visits; see the contradiction
  note below.)  So Part A (sections 1-7) is finished at the third visit and Part B
  (8-12) runs from the second to the seventh; 8H and 9B are asked at visits
  2..7 -- six consumption occasions, as CONTENTS.org's table says.

- *Recall phrase.*  Section 9B (Part B PDF p.35, printed 9.13, header
  repeated on every page to 9.26): columns headed "2nd 3rd 4th 5th 6th 7th
  VISIT"; col. 1 "How much was spent on ... since my first visit?", cols.
  2-6 "How much was spent on ... since my last visit?".  Section 8H (Part B
  PDF p.16, printed 8.11, repeated to 8.17): q1 "Did the household consume
  any own produced ... in the past 12 months?", q2 "How many months
  altogether was own produced ... consumed during the past 12 months?", q3-8
  "How much of own produced ... was consumed by the household since my last
  visit?" under the same "2nd ... 7th" heads, q9 unit, q10 "For how much
  would you sell one unit of ... now?".  Manual: "Part H is to be
  administered on every visit except the first" (PDF p.75, printed 70).

- *Diary: yes, in all EAs; paper, not CAPI.*  Manual "INSTRUCTION ON USE OF
  DIARY" (PDF p.76, printed 71): "Diaries will be provided ... At your next
  visit in five days time, you will record the entries made in the diary
  into the questionnaire ... Transfer of diary entries to the questionnaire
  will start from your second visit."  Part B instructions (PDF p.77,
  printed 72): "In all EAs the diary of household expenditure will be used.
  You must however, ask for expenditures on your second visit".  The diary is
  a transfer aid, not a dataset: no diary file ships.  Questionnaires are
  pencil-and-paper ("Write legibly in pencil without crossing out", Manual
  "Data Entry", PDF p.26, printed 21) keyed in two stages on regional-office
  microcomputers by the team's data-capture officer: Part A after the third
  visit, Part B after the last visit (Manual s2.3, PDF p.14, printed 9;
  Report Appendix 1 s5, PDF p.224, printed 203, "Microcomputers were
  installed in all data collection centres ... A data entry application
  system was designed to check the data automatically").  Team = supervisor,
  senior interviewer/editor, four interviewers, data-capture staff, driver
  (Manual s1.7 PDF p.11; Report PDF p.222 -- the Report's s1.5, PDF p.24,
  says "three interviewers", the appendix says four).

- *Contradiction inside the GLSS6 documents (and with CONTENTS.org's
  lineage note).*  The interviewer's manual carries, verbatim, the eleven-visit
  /three-day schedule CONTENTS.org attributes to the GLSS7 manual: s1.8 "a
  33-day cycle ... every fourth day", Table 2 "Batch 1: 1 4 7 10 13 16 19 22
  25 28 31" (PDF p.11, printed 6); s3.6 "Date of Next Visit ... exactly
  three (3) days apart" (PDF p.19, printed 14); s3.7 "spread over eleven
  visits" with an 11-row visit/section table (PDF p.21); 9B "administered 10
  times ... since the last 3 days" (PDF p.77, printed 72); "End of Cycle ...
  the last visit (that is, the 11th visit)" (PDF p.93, printed 88).  The same
  manual's s1.3 says 35 days and five-day intervals, and its s1.6 opens "The
  overall management of the conduct of GLSS-5 is under the Government
  Statistician" (PDF p.10, printed 5).  So the stale schedule demonstrably
  passed through GLSS6 and was lifted from the GLSS5 manual; CONTENTS.org's
  "it may have passed through that round too" can be firmed up for GLSS6.
  The questionnaire grid (A0.3) and the data both say seven visits.
  A second internal contradiction: the questionnaire's A0.3 schedules 9A
  (less-frequent purchases) at the SECOND visit; the manual (s3.7 and the 9A
  instructions, PDF p.77) says 9A is asked "only once during the last
  visit".  =sec9a.dta= carries no date, so the data cannot adjudicate.

*** An eighth contact that no document describes

Both interviewer/supervisor grids carry ONE MORE row than the section
schedule needs: =SECA01.visitno= 4 (15,965 rows) after Part A's three
visits, and =secb1.visitno= 8 (16,770 rows) after Part B's visits 2..7.
Measured:

- it is dated a median 12 days after the last section visit (SECA01 v4 -
  visit 3: IQR 9-16; secb1 v8 - visit 7: IQR 8-17), a broad spread, not a
  scheduled slot;
- the code in the interviewer slot is NOT the interviewer of record (differs
  in 98.8% of households in SECA01, 98.9% in secb1) and is never the
  supervisor's code.  It is the team's FIFTH interviewer code -- 17, 27, 37,
  47, 57, 87, 107 ... -- which has zero (or one or two) households of record
  at visit 1 and signs 98-100% of the team's extra rows (per-team count of
  visit-1 households by =intcode1= against extra rows; 96.1% of all extra
  rows corpus-wide are by a code with fewer than 30 households of record).
  That fifth code is the *Senior Interviewer/Editor* of Manual s1.7 (PDF
  p.11), who "in addition to assisting the Supervisor ... would relieve the
  regular Interviewers", and whose job the Report names as editing ("The
  senior interviewer also edited", Appendix 1 s5, PDF p.224).  The same
  person signs both files' extra row for 97.4% of households;
- they are TWO separate contacts, not one: the SECA01 row is dated ~12 days
  after visit 3 and the secb1 row ~12 days after visit 7, and the two dates
  coincide in only 0.1% of households;
- the supervisor date equals the interviewer date in about half of these rows
  (49% / 53%), against 1-2 days later for real visits;
- it is not the Module 13 (GPS) interview: =gps-sec0.intdate= equals it in
  0.8% / 0.0% of households; and it coincides with a keyed check-up date in
  0.4%.

So the extra row is the editor's pass over each booklet, timed like the
error-resolution step of Manual s2.3 (PDF p.14, printed 9): Part A is keyed
after the third visit and rejected answers "return[ed] ... to you by the 6th
or 7th visits"; Part B is keyed at the end of the cycle and the printouts
"given to you during the 5th visit of the next EA".  Whether the editor
actually re-visited the household on that date or edited in the office is
not stated anywhere (the supervisor date equals the editor date in half the
rows, which reads more like an office step); it is a question for GSS
below.

** Things that will trip the next person

- *Three files carry the first-visit date* and they disagree for ~5% of
  households: =SEC0= (=ddate/mdate/ydate=), =SECA04= visit 1, =secb2=
  visitcd 1.  =secb2= is the clean seven-visit calendar; =SEC0= is what the
  library wires today.
- *=SECA04.visit= / =SECA01.visitno= are mis-keyed* for ~1,000 households
  (duplicate visit-1 rows carrying visit-2/3 dates).  Sorting by date within
  household recovers the order; the code does not.
- *=SECA01.visitno= 4 and =secb1.visitno= 8 are not visits 4 and 8* of the
  section schedule; they are a later contact by another team member (see
  above).  A naive "count rows per household" gives 4 Part A and 7 Part B
  "visits", neither of which is the number of interviews.
- *=GOVERNANCE/gps-sec0.dta:intdate= is an integer ddmmyyyy* (18122013 =
  18 Dec 2013), not a Stata =%td=; =pd.to_datetime(unit='D',
  origin='1960-01-01')= produces years 4977-21404.  Decoded, Module 13
  (governance, peace and security) was fielded 2013-10 .. 2013-12 on 4,650
  households -- a separate return visit AFTER the main fieldwork, so its
  date is none of the seven visits and it is a median 238 days after visit
  7.  Its =month=/=year= columns are entirely null.
- *=sec8h.dta:s8hq9= says "2nd to 11th visits"*; the wave has 2nd..7th.  Do
  not read a visit count off that label.
- *=COMMUNITY/sec0.dta timeb/timec= are decimal hh.mm floats* (12.15 =
  12:15), the only clock times in the wave.
- *Dates outside the fieldwork window are keying errors*, not early starts:
  =SECA01= has 90 dates in Jan-Sep 2012 and =SEC0= 13 rows with =ydate= null;
  =secb2= itself stays within 2012-10-01 .. 2013-10-30.  Gaps of about
  -360 / +365 days in the per-visit files are year mis-keys.
- *Part A is keyed before Part B* (after visit 3 vs. after visit 7), which
  is why the Part A grids stop at three visits and why there are two
  physical cover sheets per household.
- *The Section 10 booklet has no date field at all*; =sec10_survinfo.dta=
  carries names only.  Section 10 is asked at the fifth visit (A0.3), so
  its date is =secb2= visitcd 5.
- *The Report's team size differs between its own sections*: s1.5 (printed
  3) "three interviewers", Appendix 1 s3 (printed 201) "Interviewers ... 4";
  the manual says four plus a senior interviewer who relieves.
- =Notes on how glss6 compares with other glss.docx= is a crosswalk of the
  =POV_GH_2013= aggregate variable names to GLSS3-5 names ("No correction
  made for GLSS6 based on recall"); nothing on visits.
- The 2012-13 =food_acquired.py= docstring calls =s8hq10= a "farmgate
  Price"; the instrument's wording is "For how much would you sell one unit
  of ... now?", and CONTENTS.org already records (measured on this wave)
  that respondents answer with the local retail price.  Not a visit-date
  matter; noted because the docstring and CONTENTS.org disagree.
