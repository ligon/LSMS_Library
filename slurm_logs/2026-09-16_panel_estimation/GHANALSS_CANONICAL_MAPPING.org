#+AUTHOR: Sue

Companion to =GHANALSS_SECTION9.org=, which inventories the source.  This note
answers: which *canonical* tables can Section 9 populate, on what precedent,
and where the canonical vocabulary has no slot for what the instrument asks.

Scope, per @ligon 2026-09-16: *the two panel waves only* (1987-88, 1988-89).
Later GLSS rounds do not extend the panel.

* Where the canonical descriptions live

- Index shapes :: =lsms_library/data_info.yml=, =Index Info > index_info=.
- Column notes, spellings, required/optional :: same file, =Columns=.
- Per-country declaration :: =countries/{C}/_/data_scheme.yml=.

Two things worth knowing before reading the table below, because they change
what "canonical" even constrains here:

1. =crop_production=, =plot_features= and =livestock= are registered in
   =index_info=.  *=plot_inputs= and =plot_labor= are NOT* -- that is open
   issue *#569*.  They are governed entirely by per-country =data_scheme.yml=,
   so their "canonical" shape is really the corpus convention.
2. The =Columns= block is *not* an exhaustive column list.  It carries only
   columns needing a note or a =spellings= block.  =crop_production.Quantity=,
   for instance, appears in no =Columns= entry but is declared by every
   country.  The operative vocabulary is what the countries agree on.

* Land: yes, the canonical tables answer it -- but not via =plot_features=

Canonically =Area= lives on *=plot_features=* (=(t, v, i, plot_id)=), as
hectares, beside =AreaUnit= for the original survey unit.  That table is
C4-validated *=not-asked=* for both panel waves: Section 9 has no farm-number
column and no per-farm roster part.  So the canonical home for area is a table
that legitimately does not exist here, and a synthetic =plot_id= is ruled out.

*The precedent that resolves it is EthiopiaRHS* -- the corpus's other
plot-less country (GH #512):

: crop_production:
:   index: (t, i, j, u)
:   Quantity: float        # production, kg harvested
:   Area_ha: float         # area planted, hectares

It carries *area on the crop row*, not on a plot table.  GLSS1/GLSS2 Part B
Q2 asks "how many *acres* of [crop] were harvested", i.e. exactly that shape --
area per household-crop.  So =crop_production.Area_ha= (acres / 2.47105) is
the conventional answer, with EthiopiaRHS as the named precedent.

=Y09A.ACRFRM= (household total acres farmed) is a *second*, coarser land
measure with no canonical home.  It should roughly equal the sum of =CROPH=
over crops; the two disagreeing would be a useful check.  Recommend not
wiring it initially.

* The mapping, table by table

** =crop_production= -- good fit, with one honest hole

Index: =(t, i, j, u)= on the EthiopiaRHS precedent.

| canonical column | GLSS1/2 | form | confidence |
|------------------+---------+------+------------|
| =Quantity=       | *none*  | never asked | *certain -- see below* |
| =Quantity_sold=  | =CROPS=   | Q4 quantity | high |
| =Unit_sold=      | =CROPSU=  | Q4 unit     | high |
| =KgFactor=       | =CROPSUK= | Q5 "how many kilograms per [UNIT]" | high |
| =Value_sold=     | =CROPSP=  | Q6 amount   | high |
| =Area_ha=        | =CROPH=/2.47105 | Q2 acres harvested | high |
| =intercropped=   | =CROPD=   | Q13         | high |

=Unit_sold= and =KgFactor= are canonical *optional* columns (Uganda declares
both).  =KgFactor='s canonical note -- "Kilograms per ONE unit of this row's
=u=, AS THE INSTRUMENT REPORTS IT" -- describes =CROPSUK= exactly.

*** =Quantity= is not asked, and must not be declared required

Every other country declares =crop_production.Quantity= (harvest, in unit
=u=).  Part B never asks it; it asks quantity *sold* only.  Declaring it
required and all-null would trip *Site B* of the null-read guard ("required
declared column 100% null in every wave") and raise =NullReadError= under
=LSMS_READ_STRICT=1=.  So: declare it =optional: true=, or omit it.  Either is
an honest statement that this instrument does not ask it; guessing a value
from the sold quantity would not be.

*** Open: what is =u=?

Canonically =u= is the unit the *harvest* was measured in.  With no harvest
quantity there is no such unit.  Two options, both defensible:

- =(t, i, j, u)= with =u= = the sold unit (=CROPSU=).  Keeps the canonical
  shape, but =u= then means something different than it does elsewhere --
  and =Unit_sold= already holds that value, so it is duplicated.
- =(t, i, j)=, no =u=.  Honest, and nothing forces =u=; but it departs from
  the shape EthiopiaRHS kept deliberately ("degenerate here, but the canonical
  shape carries =u= in the index uniformly").

*I lean to the second* -- a duplicated level that means two things in two
countries is worse than an absent one -- but it is your call.

*** No canonical home: the disposition values

=CROPKV= (value of crop kept for *seed*), =CROPPV= (value *given* to
labourers/landowner/gifts), =CROPLV= (value *lost* to insects/rodents/fire/rot)
are asked per crop and have *no counterpart anywhere in the corpus*.  They are
new columns whatever we do.  They are also what makes a value-based output
measure possible at all, so they are worth carrying.

** =plot_inputs= -- excellent structural fit, no slot for spend

Index: =(t, i, input, crop, u)= -- the *EHCVM precedent* (Benin, Togo, Burkina
Faso, Cote d'Ivoire, Guinea-Bissau, Niger, Senegal all declare exactly this),
which has *no plot level*.  GLSS1/2's input module is input x crop with no
plot, so the shape matches without any adaptation.

| canonical column | GLSS1/2 |
|------------------+---------|
| =Quantity=       | =FERTQNT=, =MANUQNT=, =INSCQNT= (fertilizer, manure, chemicals only) |
| =u=              | =FERTQNTU=, =MANUQNTU=, =INSCQNTU= |
| =Purchased=      | derivable -- the module asks whether the input was bought, and on credit |

*** The gap: input SPEND has no canonical column, anywhere

Swept every =data_scheme.yml=: the only value-bearing columns in any
agricultural table are =crop_production.Value_sold= and livestock's
=ValuePerAnimal= / =HerdValue= / =SalesValue=.  *No country's =plot_inputs=
carries a cost, value or expenditure column.*  The corpus records input
*quantities*.

GLSS1/2 records input *spend*, and for four of the seven inputs it records
*nothing else*:

| input                | quantity? | spend        |
|----------------------+-----------+--------------|
| fertilizer           | yes       | =FERTAMT=    |
| manure               | yes       | =MANUAMT=    |
| chemicals            | yes       | =INSCAMT=    |
| seeds / young plants | *no*      | =SEEDAMT=    |
| transport            | *no*      | =TRANAMT=    |
| containers           | *no*      | =CONTAMT=    |
| storage              | *no*      | =STORAMT=    |

Since "farm-level expenses" is the point of the exercise, dropping spend is not
an option.  Because =plot_inputs= has no =index_info= or =Columns= entry
(#569), adding a column is a *per-country declaration*, not a canonical schema
change -- the same latitude by which Malawi declares =Improved= and Ethiopia
declares =Applied=.  So this is cheap to do; what it needs is a *name we are
willing to see other countries adopt*.

*Open question*: the name.  =Value= is the least surprising given
=Value_sold=; =Cost= says more clearly that it is an outlay.  Your call, and
worth choosing as if it were canonical, because the next country that records
input spend should reuse it.

** =livestock= -- good fit

Index: =(t, i, animal)=, the shape every country uses.  =Y09F= carries an
animal code with owned / sold / bought counts and values, which lines up with
=HeadCount= / =HeadSold= / =HeadAcquired= / =HerdValue= / =SalesValue=.

*Confidence: medium.*  The mapping is inferred from variable names
(=LIVSTN/SN/BN= counts, =LIVSTNV/SV/BV= values); the Part F form (p.53) has
*not* been read yet.  Read it before wiring.

** =plot_labor= -- poor fit, needs a decision

Corpus convention is =(t, i, plot_id, source)= with =PersonDays= and =Wage=,
and *every* country that declares it carries =plot_id= -- including the EHCVM
countries that drop the plot level from =plot_inputs=.  There is no
plot-less precedent for this table.

GLSS1/2's labour (=Y09D3B=) is *household-level*: =CREXLAB= (hired-labour
spend) plus a male/female/child x 4-period worker-count grid.  So it has
neither a plot level nor person-days directly, and its headline number is
again a *spend*.

Options: a =(t, i, source)= variant (departs from a convention nothing else
departs from), fold hired-labour spend into =plot_inputs= as another input row
(structurally tidy, semantically a stretch), or leave labour unwired in the
first pass and add it once the shape question is settled.  *Recommend the
third* -- output, land, inputs and livestock are enough for a first Mundlak
specification, and labour deserves its own decision rather than being decided
by haste.

* Summary of what needs your decision

1. =crop_production= index: =(t, i, j, u)= with =u= = sold unit, or =(t, i, j)=?
2. Names for the three disposition-value columns (seed / given / lost).
3. Name for the input-spend column -- =Value=, =Cost=, or something else --
   chosen as if canonical, since it would be the corpus's first.
4. =plot_labor=: defer, or pick a shape now?
5. =Quantity= on =crop_production=: declare =optional: true=, or omit entirely?

* Not blocking, but worth knowing

- Each input's crop list is capped at *four* lines in the form, so a household
  cultivating more than four crops has its input spend truncated invisibly.
- Wiring anything moves *every* GhanaLSS cache hash (the country
  =data_scheme.yml= is hashed whole), so the bill is a whole-country cold
  re-warm including the 5.26M-row =food_acquired=.
- =crop_production= and =livestock= would be GhanaLSS's first agricultural

  cross-country check once built.

* Decisions taken (@ligon, 2026-09-16)

Index =(t, i, j)=, no =u=.  Input-spend column named =Cost=.  =plot_labor=
deferred.  =Quantity= included -- see the caveat under "Dispositions" below.

* Dispositions: what the four of them mean

Part B does not ask a harvest quantity.  What it asks is *where the harvest
went*, use by use, and what each part was worth.  Four uses are asked here and
a fifth is asked elsewhere:

| use            | asked | flag      | value     | question                                       |
|----------------+-------+-----------+-----------+------------------------------------------------|
| sold           | Q4/Q6 | --        | =CROPSP=  | "How much was the harvest of [...] sold for?"  |
| kept for seed  | Q7/Q8 | =CROPK=   | =CROPKV=  | "How much would the same quantity of seed cost in the market?" |
| given away     | Q9/Q10| =CROPP=   | =CROPPV=  | "Did you give part of the harvest to laborers, or to a land owner, or as a gift, or for ceremonies and fetishes?" |
| lost           |Q11/Q12| =CROPL=   | =CROPLV=  | "Have you lost any part of the harvested crop to insects, rodents, fire, rotting etc.?" |
| *eaten*        | *not in Section 9* | | | Section 12B, already wired as =derive_12b_fortnight_value= |

=CROPL= is not a yes/no but a *cause* code, verified against the form:
1 INSECTS, 2 RODENTS, 3 FIRE, 4 ROTTING, 5 OTHER/COMBINATION, 6 NO.  (A first
pass over the fax misread 4 as "NOTHING"; it is ROTTING.)

The flags and the values agree exactly -- GLSS1 =CROPK= YES = 6,152 and
=CROPKV= non-null = 6,152; =CROPP= YES = 6,803 against 6,804 -- so there are no
orphan values to reconcile.

** The valuation basis is NOT the same across the four

This is the part that matters economically and is invisible in the variable
names:

- =CROPSP= (sold) is what the household *received*.
- =CROPKV=, =CROPPV=, =CROPLV= each answer "how much would the same quantity
  *cost in the market*" -- an imputed *replacement* price, i.e. the buyer's
  side of the farmgate/market gap, normally above what the household would get.

So adding the four gives a total in *mixed prices*.  The repo already takes
this distinction seriously: =.coder/ledger/585-own-production-valuation.md=
requires a valuation's docstring to "state which side of the farmgate/market
gap it lands on", and =GhanaLSS/_/CONTENTS.org= records the same contrast
between Section 8H (farmgate) and Section 12B's =VFOODCPD= ("what it would cost
to buy").  Carrying the four separately, which is what the canonical wide shape
does anyway, keeps the choice with the analyst.

** What each disposition IS, economically

- *Seed* is an intermediate input, not final output: this season's harvest
  retained as next season's input.
- *Given* is a MIXED category and cannot be split.  Grain to labourers is a
  wage in kind and grain to a land owner is rent in kind -- both factor
  payments -- while gifts and ceremonies are transfers.  Counting the category
  as output without also counting the factor-payment part as a cost overstates
  profit.
- *Lost* is realized output that perished.  For a production function
  (technology) it belongs in output; for a revenue or profit function it does
  not.
- *Sold* is the only one measured at a transaction price.

** Practical hazard: =CROPSP= is a PRICE PER UNIT, not a total

=CROPSP= is denominated by =CROPSPU=, which draws on the same unit-code list as
=CROPSU= (1 pound, 2 kilogram, 3 ton, 4 minibag, 5 maxibag, ..., 22 ALL).  The
medians form a clean price ladder by unit size, which is what a per-unit price
looks like and not what a total looks like:

| =CROPSPU=       | median =CROPSP= (1987-88) |
|-----------------+---------------------------|
| 1 pound         |                        50 |
| 2 kilogram      |                        75 |
| 4 minibag       |                     1,600 |
| 5 maxibag       |                     4,400 |
| 3 ton           |                     7,500 |

So =Value_sold= is *not* =CROPSP=.  It is =CROPSP= when =CROPSPU= = 22 (ALL),
and =CROPSP= x (quantity expressed in =CROPSPU= units) otherwise -- and
=CROPS= is denominated in =CROPSU=, which is not always the same unit, so the
conversion has to go through =CROPSUK= (kg per sold unit) where it exists.

This is GH *#824* ("crop_production: sale value is per SOLD unit but the table
carries only the HARVEST unit =u=") appearing in a new country.  Read that
issue before wiring =Value_sold=.

Coverage note: =CROPSP= is populated on ~38% of rows (6,445 / 16,938 in GLSS1),
consistent with most crop rows recording no sale at all; =CROPS= is non-null
almost everywhere but is zero where nothing was sold.

** Consequence for =Quantity=

@ligon asked for =Quantity= to be included.  It has to be declared
=optional: true= and will be *entirely NaN*: Part B asks quantity SOLD
(=CROPS=) and never quantity harvested, and the other three dispositions are
asked as values, not quantities, so no harvest quantity can be reconstructed.
=optional: true= is also what keeps Site B of the null-read guard from raising
=NullReadError= on a required all-null column.

** Proposed names

Parallel to the established =Value_sold=:

| column        | source    |
|---------------+-----------|
| =Value_seed=  | =CROPKV=  |
| =Value_given= | =CROPPV=  |
| =Value_lost=  | =CROPLV=  |
| =LossCause=   | =CROPL=   |

=LossCause= is free information (insects / rodents / fire / rotting / other)
and worth keeping.  The one thing the names do not convey is the valuation
asymmetry above, which therefore belongs in the column notes.

* =Value_sold= is a DERIVATION, and here is the evidence for its rule

@ligon, 2026-09-16: compute =Value_sold= per GH #824, and register it through
the derived-value machinery.

** #824 is a RELATED but DIFFERENT problem -- do not conflate them

#824 is about the *unit*: =Value_sold= is a recorded total, =Quantity_sold= is
in the SOLD unit, and the table carries only the HARVEST unit =u=, so
=Value_sold / Quantity_sold= is a price per the wrong unit.  Its fix is to
carry =Unit_sold=.

GhanaLSS has a *stronger* problem: =Value_sold= is *not recorded at all*.  Q6's
=AMOUNT= is denominated by its own =UNIT= box (=CROPSPU=), so most rows carry a
*price per unit*, not a total.  #824's remedy (carry =Unit_sold=) is necessary
here but not sufficient -- the total has to be constructed, which is why this
is a derivation and not a column mapping.

** The evidence

The form (p.44, block =9B1=, col 6) reads "How much was the harvest of [...]
sold for?" over two boxes, =AMOUNT= and =UNIT=.  That is ambiguous on its own.
The data resolves it -- =CROPSPU= is essentially bimodal:

| relation of =CROPSPU= to =CROPSU= | 1987-88 | 1988-89 |
|-----------------------------------+---------+---------|
| equal (amount is per that unit)   |  91.3%  |  85.8%  |
| = 22, i.e. ALL                    |   9.6%  |  15.9%  |
| neither                           |   2.0%  |   0.95% |

A form whose =UNIT= box merely restated a total would not need the box, and
would not offer =ALL= as a distinct code.  Magnitudes agree: for maize, taking
=CROPSP x CROPS= on same-unit rows gives a median 11,100 against 10,000 on the
rows that report =ALL= (where =CROPSP= is unambiguously the total), whereas
=CROPSP= alone gives 3,550 -- three times too small.  Cassava and cocoa behave
the same way.

*Caveat, recorded rather than smoothed over*: yam and plantains *overshoot*
under the same rule (yam =CROPSP x CROPS= median 100,000 vs 8,000 on ALL rows).
The =ALL= and same-unit groups are different households selling different
amounts, so the comparison is confounded by transaction size and is
*suggestive, not conclusive*, for those two crops.  This is the thing to check
first if the derived values look wrong.

** Proposed rule

: Value_sold = CROPSP                  where CROPSPU == 22 (ALL)
:            = CROPSP * CROPS          where CROPSPU == CROPSU
:            = NaN                     otherwise (2.0% / 0.95% of sale rows)

Leaving the residual NaN rather than converting it is a deliberate choice: the
conversion would need a factor between two different container units that the
survey does not supply for that row.

* Settled after the validation pass (Sue, 2026-09-16)

** The yam and plantain "overshoot" was an artefact.  The rule stands.

Both apparent failures dissolve on inspection, and neither is a data problem:

- *Yam*: =CROPSU= is itself 22 (ALL) on 116 of the 214 rows my diagnostic
  counted as "same-unit", because =CROPSU == CROPSPU == 22= satisfies both
  masks.  Those rows belong in the ALL branch.  The diagnostic tested
  same-unit first; the *rule* tests ALL first, so the rule was never affected.
- *Cocoa* (62.5x): the same-unit rows sell in maxibags at a median 8,750 per
  maxibag, the ALL rows in kilograms at an implied 140 per kg.  8,750 / 140 is
  about 62 -- that ratio *is* the maxibag-to-kilogram conversion.  The two
  groups agree; they simply sell in different units.

Plantains, cassava and maize sit within ~1-3x with matching unit composition.
The per-unit reading of =CROPSP= is confirmed.  *Test ALL before same-unit.*

** =Quantity_sold= is in =Unit_sold=, NOT in kilograms

Worth stating in the column notes, because the analyst's first instinct is
=Value_sold / Quantity_sold= and that is a price per whatever container the
household sold in -- which varies row by row.  The kilogram conversion is
=Quantity_sold x KgFactor= for the unstarred (container) units and the metric
factor for the starred ones.

*And the canonical kg machinery does not reach this country.*
=harvest_kg_factors= keys the reported factor to =(u, condition)= and needs
=Quantity=; GhanaLSS has neither.  So =CROPSUK= is carried for the analyst's
use, not for =harvest_kg=.

*Small open point*: canonically =KgFactor= is "kilograms per ONE unit of this
row's =u=".  Ours is kilograms per =Unit_sold=.  With no =u= in the table
there is nothing else it could mean, so carrying it as =KgFactor= with a note
is defensible -- but if you would rather it not borrow a name whose canonical
definition points at a level this table does not have, say so.

** =plot_inputs=: =u= is a COLUMN here, not an index level

Both patterns exist in the corpus: Malawi and the EHCVM countries put =u= in
the index; *Uganda, Ethiopia and Nigeria declare it as a column* (=u: str=)
beside =Quantity=.

GhanaLSS must follow the second.  Four of the seven inputs -- seed, transport,
containers, storage -- are amount-only, so those rows have no quantity and no
unit.  A NaN on a declared index level is Site I territory, and the first
=groupby= deletes the row.  So: =index: (t, i, input, crop)= with =u: str= and
=Cost: float= as columns.

** =livestock= is in scope, and the mapping is now form-verified

Part F (p.53) is one row per animal -- cattle, sheep, goats, chickens, pigs,
other poultry, rabbits/guinea pigs, other -- with:

| Q  | question                                           | variable   | canonical column |
|----+----------------------------------------------------+------------+------------------|
|  4 | how many of all ages owned at present              | =LIVSTN=   | =HeadCount=      |
|  5 | if they sold one today, how much would they receive *altogether* | =LIVSTNV= | =HerdValue= |
|  7 | how many have they sold                            | =LIVSTSN=  | =HeadSold=       |
|  8 | how much received from sales in past 12 months     | =LIVSTSV=  | =SalesValue=     |
| 10 | how many did they buy                              | =LIVSTBN=  | =HeadAcquired=   |
| 11 | how much paid for all they bought                  | =LIVSTBV=  | (no canonical column) |
| 12 | how many born or received as gifts                 | =LIVSTAD=  | (none)           |
| 13 | how many *eaten* by the household                  | =LIVSTE=   | (none)           |
| 14 | how many lost, stolen, given, sacrificed or died   | =LIVSTDI=  | (none)           |

Five canonical columns map *directly*.  =ValuePerAnimal= is derivable as
=HerdValue / HeadCount= (Q5 is explicitly "altogether"), which makes it a
derivation, not a mapping.  =TLU= is a transform output and is declared by only
one country (EthiopiaRHS); per-country subsets are normal -- Benin and Togo
declare three columns.

Q13 is *livestock own-consumption*, the exact parallel of the crop
own-consumption gap, and has no canonical home.  Worth carrying.

| *...carry NO Section 9 value at all*  | *18.9%* | *19.5%* |
| households with >=1 such row          | 1,127 / 2,178 | 1,408 / 2,393 |
| share of harvested ACRES in those rows| 13.9%   | 14.4%   |

Those are crops grown and wholly consumed: acreage recorded, output valued
nowhere in Section 9.  They have no quantity either, so *no index choice
recovers them*.

*** Consequence: the Section 12B splice is LOAD-BEARING, not optional

An earlier section listed "splice 12B own-consumption, or use Section 9
dispositions alone and state the omission" as an open question.  This
measurement answers it.  Section 9 alone would serve *zero output* for ~19% of
cropped rows covering ~14% of harvested acres and touching *half the farming
households* (1,127 of 2,178; 1,408 of 2,393) -- and it would do so
*non-randomly*, concentrated in exactly the subsistence-oriented households a
production function is most likely to be about.  Regressing a value of output
that is structurally zero for the most subsistence-oriented half of the sample
would not be a noisy measure; it would be a selected one.

So own-consumption from =food_acquired= (=s='produced'=, already derived for
both waves by =12b-fortnight=) is required, not a refinement.  Its unit lives
in =food_acquired='s own index =(t, i, j, u, s, visit)=; =crop_production= does
not need one to make the join.

*What the splice costs*, stated so it is chosen and not discovered: it crosses
a recall window (Section 9 annual vs 12B fortnight annualised) and needs a
crop-code to food-item mapping, since the two modules use different =j=
vocabularies.  Both are real work and neither is affected by the index choice.

* Price sources, and a correction (@ligon, 2026-09-16)

@ligon: "You're proposing to use the produced values in food_acquired to get
prices to value the stock of harvest?  Because if so there are other possible
sources of prices."

*Correction first.*  What was proposed was using the 12B *values* directly as
the own-consumption component of output -- not deriving prices from them.  But
the question exposes that the proposal does more work than was admitted:
*Section 12B measures CONSUMPTION, not production.*  Substituting it is not a
recall-window splice, it is substituting a different concept.  Home-produced
food eaten in a year differs from the year's harvest by stocks carried, gifts
received, and every non-food crop.  It is a proxy, and must be labelled one.

** What there is to value, and what there is not

Section 9 records exactly one quantity: the quantity *sold*.  Seed, gifts and
losses arrive as values with *no quantity behind them*, and the ~19% of cropped
rows with no Section 9 value at all have neither.  So a better price source
does not rescue those rows -- but it does enable the *inverse* operation,
backing an implied quantity out of a recorded value.

** The four sources

| source | grain | coverage in the panel waves | what it is |
|--------+-------+------------------------------+------------|
| household's own sale price, =CROPSP= per =CROPSPU= | crop row | ~40% of crop rows (sale rows only) | farmgate, *received* |
| =community_prices= (=PRICE.DAT=) | =(t, v, j, u, obs)= | *sane* in both waves; 29,224 rows, 47 items | cluster market price |
| =food_acquired= / =food_prices()= | =(t, i, j, u, s)= | *sane* in both waves | household unit values |
| =median_price_valuation= ladder | geography median | existing machinery, ledger =585-own-production-valuation.md= | built for exactly this |

*** =community_prices= and the crop list are COMPLEMENTARY, not overlapping

Its 47 items are a consumer basket (it also carries aspirin, kerosene and
buckets).  *Only 13 of the 30 crop-form items are findable in it* -- Plantain,
Orange, Groundnut, Sugar, Cassava, Yam, Cocoyam, Maize, Rice, Millet, Sorghum,
Tomato, Pepper.  There is *no cocoa, coffee, rubber, timber, cotton, kola,
tobacco or pineapple*.

That gap is less damaging than it looks, because it falls the convenient way:
the missing items are the *cash* crops, which are the ones households *sell*
and therefore the ones that carry their own sale price; the items
=community_prices= does cover are the food staples that get eaten and carry no
sale price.  The two sources cover complementary halves of the problem.

** What prices can and cannot do here

Can:
1. *Invert values into quantities* -- =quantity_seed = CROPKV / price= -- giving
   physical output components where today there are only cedis.
2. *Re-value on ONE basis.*  Section 9 mixes a received price (sales) with a
   market replacement price (seed, gifts, losses).  Valuing every component at a
   single source removes an asymmetry the instrument built in.
3. *Cross-check =Value_sold=* -- the implied farmgate price against the
   community price for the same crop, cluster and round.  This is the
   =validated_against= this note already owed.

Cannot:
- Recover the ~19% of cropped rows that carry neither a quantity nor a value.
  Those have acreage and nothing else.

** The trap: do NOT impute output from acreage

The tempting fix for those rows is acreage x an assumed yield.  For a
*production function* that is circular -- output imputed from land, then
regressed on land, manufacturing the land coefficient.  Whatever is done with
those rows, it must not be that.  Leaving them missing is honest; the 12B proxy
is defensible if labelled; imputing from the regressor is not.
