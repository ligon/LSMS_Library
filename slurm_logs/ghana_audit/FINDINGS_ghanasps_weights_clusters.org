* 1. Idiosyncrasies found in the GhanaSPS documentation

Read in full: =_/CONTENTS.org= (377 lines), =_/notes.org= (406 lines),
=_/README.org= (32 lines).  Named idiosyncrasies:

1. *=_/README.org= is not about GhanaSPS at all.*  It is a variable table for
   GLSS food-expenditure sections for survey years 1988, 1989, 1991 and 1998
   (=SEC9B=, =S9B=, =y12a=, =fdexpcd=, =s9bq1=...).  Those are GhanaLSS
   rounds.  =PLAN_ghanasps_cleanup.org= already flagged it ("Stale (GhanaLSS
   content)"); it is still stale.  Do not treat it as GhanaSPS documentation.

2. *=_/notes.org= is a food-label harmonization worksheet*, not survey-design
   notes.  Its six headings are food/expenditure label crosswalks across the
   three rounds plus a demand-system estimation block.  It contains no
   statement about weights, clusters, EAs or =FPrimary=.  (Its only apparent
   "cluster" hits are substring artefacts in food names.)

3. *GSPS is a panel, not three national cross-sections.*  =CONTENTS.org=
   "Sampling universe" opens with this warning: only wave 1 has a documented
   probability sample; waves 2 and 3 are follow-ups plus split-offs, and no
   document states a universe for them.  This governs everything below: a
   =sample= feature for waves 2/3 cannot mean what it means for wave 1.

4. *Wave 1 design, quoted in =CONTENTS.org= from
   =Basic_Information_Dec_2015.pdf= s.3, printed p.8*: "5010 households from
   334 Enumeration Areas (EAs) were sampled.  Fifteen households were selected
   from each of the EAs."  Upper East (16 EAs) and Upper West (12 EAs) were
   deliberately oversampled, so *the design is not self-weighting*.
   5010 sampled vs 5009 interviewed is reconciled in the same document (s.5,
   printed p.11): one household was dropped for data incompleteness.

5. *"'2013-14' has no regions or urban/rural data."*  =CONTENTS.org=, under
   "Files in GhanaSPS/_/ > other_features.py", quoting the wave-2 Data Map.
   *This audit confirms it, and finds the trap that makes it easy to get
   wrong* -- see s.3.2: =2013-14/Data/01ea_employmentscreener.dta= declares
   =regioncode=, =regionname=, =districtname= and =communitycode=, and all
   four are 100% empty strings in all 13,643 rows.  A variable-name grep says
   "region exists"; the data say it does not.

6. *Wave-label discrepancy for wave 3.*  =CONTENTS.org= records that the
   directory is =2017-18= while the instrument's cover page says "WAVE 3
   (2018-2019)" and the IHSN entry is titled 2018-2019.  Affects citation only.

7. *Waves 2 and 3 are not in the World Bank catalog at all* (=CONTENTS.org=);
   only wave 1 is (catalog 2534).  So there is no WB DDI for waves 2/3, which
   is why no "Weighting" section exists for them (s.5).

8. *Pre-release data caveat* (GH #140, not =CONTENTS.org=): some GhanaSPS data
   on disk is a pre-release version.  Not re-verified here.  Every negative
   finding below is a statement about *the extract this repository holds*.

- "*2013-14 lacks EAs.dta*" -- true, but irrelevant: the EA is encoded in

*** Contradiction 2: GH #140 open question 2, "is FPrimary[:6] the canonical cluster ID?"

*No.*  =FPrimary[:6]= is right for panel households and wrong for split-offs.
The correct cluster key is =FPrimary[3:6]= (equivalently =FPrimary[1:6]=,
which prefixes the region).  Evidence in s.3.2.  The 583-prefix figure in
=slurm_logs/PLAN_ghanasps_cleanup.org= is reproduced here exactly and
explained: 334 real EAs + 249 spurious singleton prefixes manufactured by
the leading split-off digit.

* 2. Wave 2009-10

** 2.1 Weights

Every variable whose *name or label* matches
=weight|wgt|wt|sampl|expans|pond|inflat|raising|probab= was extracted from the
10,704-row inventory.  Wave-1 hits:

| File              | Variable    | Variable label               | Grain           |
|-------------------+-------------+------------------------------+-----------------|
| =key_hhld_info.dta= | =hhweight3= | "Household weight - revised3" | household (5009) |
| =key_hhld_info.dta= | =ppweight3= | "Population weight - revised3" | household (5009) |
| =S10AI.dta=       | =hhweight2= | "Household weight - revised"  | person           |
| =S5D3.dta=        | =hhweight=  | "Household weight"            | enterprise       |
| =S6B.dta=         | =s6b_5=     | "B5 Weight (kilograms)"       | body weight -- NOT a sampling weight |
| =S7A.dta=         | =s7a_27=    | "Q27 What was the weight of the child at birth-in kg" | body weight -- NOT a sampling weight |

Summary statistics (=key_hhld_info.dta=, n = 5,009 households, 0 NaN):

| Variable    |    n | min     | mean    | median  | max     | sum           |
|-------------+------+---------+---------+---------+---------+---------------|
| =hhweight3= | 5009 | 214.981 | 1419.20 | 1205.32 | 4751.10 |  7,108,790.10 |
| =ppweight3= | 5009 | 220.239 | 4829.02 | 3772.92 | 41505.3 | 24,188,566.86 |

*Both are expansion weights, not normalized weights* (mean is ~1400 and ~4800,
not ~1).  =sum(ppweight3) = 24.19 million= is close to Ghana's 2010 Population
and Housing Census count of ~24.66 million persons (an *approximate external
comparator*, not a repository fact), i.e. about 1.9 per cent low.

*Caveat worth recording*: =sum(hhweight3) = 7.11 million households= is well
above the ~5.47 million households of the 2010 census (again an approximate
external comparator).  The implied national mean household size is
24.19/7.11 = 3.40 persons, against ~4.4 in the census.  The weights look
calibrated on *persons*, and =hhweight3= should not be relied on as a
household-count expansion factor without further checking.

*=hhweight3= is a cluster-level design weight.*  It takes exactly one distinct
value within each of the 334 EAs (=nunique= per =id3= is 1.0 for all 334) --
consistent with a two-stage design where all 15 households in an EA share the
same inclusion probability.

*** Superseded revisions

- =hhweight2= ("Household weight - revised") appears only in =S10AI.dta=, a
  person-level module covering 4,998 households; 4,996 have a value.  Range
  246.5 to 4686.1, sum 6,986,607.  Correlation with =hhweight3= is 0.867 and
  *no household has an equal value* -- it is a genuinely different, earlier
  revision.
- =hhweight= ("Household weight") appears only in =S5D3.dta= (trade
  inventory), 1,804 households, sum 1,563,434, max 43,019.  Also unequal to
  =hhweight3= everywhere.

*Use =hhweight3=.*  It is the only complete, one-row-per-household weight, it
is the "revised3" generation, and it is the one the wave-2 panel documentation
names (s.5.2).

*** GH #140 open question 6, SETTLED: =ppweight3= is a per-person expansion weight, not a panel weight

Test: count roster members per household from =2009-10/Data/S1D.dta= (18,889
rows, exactly the 18,889 household members stated in
=Baseline_Descriptive_Report.pdf= s.2.1), join to =key_hhld_info.dta= on
=hhno= (0 unmatched), and compare =ppweight3/hhweight3= to household size.

- =ppweight3 / hhweight3 == household size= for *5,009 of 5,009 households*
  (=np.isclose= at =rtol=1e-6=; maximum absolute deviation 9.55e-07, i.e.
  float32 storage rounding).
- =sum(hhweight3 * hhsize) = 24,188,566.87= vs =sum(ppweight3) =
  24,188,566.86=.
- Mean household size 3.771; the ratio's mean is 3.771 with min 1 and max 20,
  exactly the roster's household-size distribution.

*=ppweight3= is arithmetically identical to =hhweight3= x household size.*  It
is therefore a per-person expansion weight and carries no longitudinal
information whatsoever.  It must NOT be mapped to =panel_weight=.

*No panel weight exists in wave 1* -- which is expected, since wave 1 is the
baseline.

** 2.2 Cluster / PSU identity

*Fully available, at household grain, in 118 of 124 files.*

=key_hhld_info.dta= (5,009 rows, one per household) columns:
=id1= (Region, labelled 1..10 Western/Central/Greater Accra/Volta/Eastern/
Ashanti/Brong Ahafo/Northern/Upper East/Upper West), =id2= (District),
=id3= ("EA NO."), =id4= ("HH ID"), =hhno=, =urbrur= (labelled 1 Urban / 2
Rural), =loc7= (labelled 1 Accra(GAMA) .. 7 Rural Savannah), =hhweight3=,
=ppweight3=.

| Quantity                              | Value                    |
|---------------------------------------+--------------------------|
| distinct =id3= (EAs)                  | *334* (documented: 334)  |
| households per EA                     | mean 15.00, min 14, max 15, sd 0.055 |
| distinct =id1=                        | 10                       |
| distinct =id2=                        | 115                      |
| distinct (=id1=,=id2=,=id3=)          | 335 (see note)           |
| =urbrur=                              | 2,010 Urban / 2,999 Rural |

=id3= is a *global* EA number (1..334), not nested within region.  One
anomaly: =id3=183= appears under two districts of region 6 (=id2=1= with 12
households, =id2=94= with 3).  So (=id1=,=id2=,=id3=) gives 335 groups while
=id3= gives 334.  Use =id3= alone as =v=; the extra district split is a
district-coding artefact, not a second cluster.  This is likely the same thing
the wave-2 Data Map means by "at least a few communities span two Eas".

*** The =hhno= digit scheme (this is the key to waves 2 and 3)

=hhno= is a 9-digit integer for all 5,009 households.  Decomposition, verified
against the id columns:

: hhno  =  S . RR . EEE . HHH
:          1   2    3     3     digits
:          |   |    |     +-- id4  (household within EA)   match rate 1.000
:          |   |    +-------- id3  (EA number, 001..334)   match rate 1.000
:          |   +------------- region (01..10)
:          +----------------- split/generation counter; always '1' in wave 1

So =hhno[3:6] == id3= exactly, and =hhno[:6]= partitions the 5,009 households
into *334 groups of ~15* -- the EA partition.

** 3.2 Cluster identity: FULLY RECOVERABLE, and =FPrimary[:6]= is the wrong key

=01a_consent.dta= is the household roster of the wave: 4,774 rows, 4,774
distinct =FPrimary=, all 9 characters.

*** Prefix census (the raw numbers, reproducing the PLAN's 583)

| prefix       | n distinct | hh/prefix min | median | mean | max |
|--------------+------------+---------------+--------+------+-----|
| =FPrimary[:2]= |         10 |             1 |   13.0 | 477.4 | 4189 |
| =FPrimary[:3]= |         40 |             1 |   12.0 | 119.4 |  770 |
| =FPrimary[:4]= |         52 |             1 |   10.5 |  91.8 |  507 |
| =FPrimary[:5]= |        127 |             1 |    8.0 |  37.6 |  149 |
| *=FPrimary[:6]=* |    *583* |             1 |   11.0 |   8.2 |   15 |
| =FPrimary[:7]= |        649 |             1 |    8.0 |   7.4 |   15 |

583 is *not* 334, and the size distribution is bimodal: 150 prefixes hold
exactly 1 household and 60 hold 2, while 96 hold exactly 15 and 73 hold 14.
That bimodality is the whole story.

*** Decomposition: panel households vs. split-offs

Split the 4,774 by whether =FPrimary= exactly equals a 2009-10 =hhno=:

| Group                        |    n | first char of =FPrimary= | distinct =[:6]= | hh/=[:6]= |
|------------------------------+------+--------------------------+-----------------+-----------|
| matches a W1 =hhno= (panel)  | 4366 | '1' (all)                | *334*           | 5..15, mean 13.07 |
| no W1 match (split-off)      |  408 | '2' (327), '3' (62), '4' (14), '5' (4), '6' (1) | 249 | 1..8, mean 1.64 |

- The panel group's 334 prefixes are *exactly* the wave-1 EA prefix set
  (=set(panel[:6]) == set(W1 hhno[:6])=, verified).
- *None* of the split-off group's 249 prefixes is a wave-1 EA prefix, and none
  coincides with any panel household's prefix in this wave (0 of 408).

So =FPrimary[:6]= scatters the 408 split-off households into 249 fictitious
one- and two-household "clusters" that no household of the origin EA belongs
to.  As a =v=, it would be silently wrong for 8.5 per cent of the wave.

*** The rule that recovers the true EA (exhaustively verified)

Replace the leading digit of =FPrimary= with ='1'=:

- *408 of 408* split-off ids then land exactly on a wave-1 =hhno=.  Zero
  misses.

That is, =FPrimary= follows the *same* S/RR/EEE/HHH scheme as wave-1
=hhno=, where the leading digit =S= is a split/generation counter (1 = the
original wave-1 household, 2..6 = households split off from it) and the
remaining 8 digits are the *origin* household's region, EA and household
number.  Three ids illustrate it directly: =408294015=, =508294015= and
=608294015= all descend from wave-1 household =108294015=.

Applying it to all 4,774 households:

| Implied EA (=FPrimary[3:6]=)      | value                |
|-----------------------------------+----------------------|
| distinct EAs                      | *334*                |
| all are wave-1 EAs?               | *yes*                |
| wave-1 EAs with 0 wave-2 households | *0*                |
| households per EA                 | min 6, median 14, mean 14.29, max 27 |

*Conclusion: cluster identity for 2013-14 is fully available.  Use
=FPrimary[3:6]= (or =FPrimary[1:6]= to carry the region), never
=FPrimary[:6]=.*

Consistency with the published sample account: =CONTENTS.org= quotes the IHSN
abstract, "5484 households were selected ... comprising of 5009 households
from the baseline survey and 475 households from split of households created
of which 4774 households were successfully interviewed."  Our decomposition
gives 4,366 originals + 408 split-offs = 4,774.  Consistent.

the raw column gives 336 values; over the well-formed ids, exactly 334).

*** =FPrimary_original= independently confirms the split-off rule

=FPrimary_original= ("Original HH ID if a split off") is populated for 920 of
5,669 rows (683 distinct), blank for 4,749.

- 894 of the 920 match a wave-1 =hhno=; 780 match a wave-2 =FPrimary=.
- *917 of 920 equal =FPrimary= with only the leading digit changed* -- the 3
  exceptions are exactly the 3 malformed 10-character ids above.
- It is populated for =round=2= (862 rows) and =round=0= (58 rows), i.e. it
  records splits created in *this* wave.  Households that split off in wave 2
  and were re-interviewed in wave 3 carry a '2'-leading =FPrimary= with a
  *blank* =FPrimary_original=.

So =FPrimary_original= is a partial, wave-3-only record of a rule that the id
scheme encodes universally.  The lead-digit rule is the general one; use
=FPrimary_original= as corroboration, not as the primary link.

*** Current-residence geography exists in wave 3 (unlike wave 2)

=00_hh_info.dta= carries populated =r_code= / =region= (10 regions, all named)
and =d_code= / =district= (real district names).

*Important: this is CURRENT residence, not the sampling cluster.*  The region
embedded in =FPrimary[1:3]= disagrees with =r_code= for *446 of 5,669
households (7.9 per cent)* -- those are the households that moved between
waves.  For a =sample= feature, =v= must come from the id (the sampling
cluster the household was drawn in); =region= / =district= from
=00_hh_info.dta= describe where it now lives.  They are different facts and
must not be conflated.

*=comm_code= is not usable as a cluster key*: 704 distinct values, 828 of
5,669 households coded =-666= (the wave's missing sentinel), and
(=r_code=,=d_code=,=comm_code=) gives 889 groups -- neither 334 nor a clean
nesting.

=00_comm_info.dta= and =rural-urban.dta= are *identical in content* (=DataFrame.equals= is True; 528
rows, same 5 columns =regioncode=, =regionname=, =districtcode=,
=communitycode=, =communitytype=), community-grain, with =communitytype= in
{1, 2} (246/282) and 12 rows carrying =communitycode = -666=.  Joining them
to households on (=regioncode=, =districtcode=, =communitycode=) covers only
3,953 of 5,669 households (70 per cent); dropping district raises it to 4,636
(82 per cent).  Usable as a *partial* urban/rural indicator with a documented
caveat; not a cluster key.

* 7. What this means for a =sample= feature

The blanket claim in =_/data_scheme.yml= -- "GhanaSPS has NO =sample= table
(cluster identity unavailable)" -- is not supportable.  Cluster identity is
available in *all three waves*.  What is missing is *weights in waves 2 and
3*, which is a different, narrower problem.

** 2009-10: IMPLEMENTABLE NOW, no caveat

Everything a =sample= row needs is in one household-grain file,
=2009-10/Data/key_hhld_info.dta= (5,009 rows, 0 NaN in any of these):

- =i= <- =hhno= (=format_id=)
- =v= <- =id3= (334 EAs; do NOT use (=id1=,=id2=,=id3=), which gives 335
  because of the EA-183 district split)
- =weight= <- =hhweight3=
- =panel_weight= <- =hhweight3= (baseline wave; no refreshment component yet).
  *Do NOT use =ppweight3=* -- proven above to be =hhweight3= x household size.
- =Rural= <- =urbrur= (labelled 1 Urban / 2 Rural)
- =strata= <- =id1= (region; the design is stratified by region, per the BID)

Note for whoever writes it: the =hhweight3= household total (7.11M) is ~30 per
cent above the 2010 census household count while the person total is within
2 per cent.  Record that in the wave's docs; do not silently rescale.

** 2013-14: IMPLEMENTABLE WITH A DOCUMENTED CAVEAT (cluster only, no weight)

- =i= <- =FPrimary= (=01a_consent.dta=, 4,774 rows)
- =v= <- =FPrimary[3:6]= -- *334 EAs, every one a wave-1 EA, none lost.*
  Equivalently =('1' + FPrimary[1:])[:6]=.  *Never =FPrimary[:6]=.*
- =weight= <- *unavailable.*  Exhaustive sweep of 89 of 89 =.dta= files finds
  none, and =key_hhinfo_panel.xlsx= assigns =hhweight= / =ppweight= to "W1"
  with all wave-2 cells blank.
- =panel_weight= <- unavailable.
- =Rural=, =strata= <- unavailable (region derivable from =FPrimary[1:3]=, but
  it is the *origin* region, which for a mover is not where they live).

*Caveat to write into the config*: =v= is the *wave-1 sampling EA*, i.e. the
cluster the household (or its parent household) was originally drawn in.  For
the 408 split-off households and any mover, it is a lineage cluster, not a
geographic one.  That is the right thing for design-based variance estimation
and the wrong thing for "which market is this household near".

*Acquisition target*: =w2_hh_geodata_w3preloads= (named by
=key_hhinfo_panel.xlsx=, absent from =2013-14/Data/=) carries wave-2
=eacode=, =comm_code_w2=, =d_code_w2=, =district_w2=.  Getting it would give
wave 2 a current-residence geography.  It would *not* give it a weight.

** What is genuinely blocked

*Weights for 2013-14 and 2017-18.*  Not blocked by this environment, and not
blocked by anything a config edit can fix:

1. The microdata do not contain them (313 of 313 files read; the sweep is
   complete, with zero availability failures).
2. The panel construction map records the weights as wave-1-only.
3. Waves 2 and 3 are not in the WB catalog, so there is no DDI "Weighting"
   section and no producer-published weight to go fetch.

Whether ISSER/Yale ever computed wave-2/3 weights is a question for the data
producer, not for the repository.  The honest configuration is: wave 1 gets a
=sample= table with weights; waves 2 and 3 get a =sample= table with =v= and a
=weight= column of NaN, and a comment that says *why*, citing
=key_hhinfo_panel.xlsx=.

Given =CONTENTS.org='s standing warning that no universe statement exists for
waves 2 and 3, a NaN weight there is not merely an inconvenience -- it is
arguably correct.  A weight without a universe is a number without a meaning.
