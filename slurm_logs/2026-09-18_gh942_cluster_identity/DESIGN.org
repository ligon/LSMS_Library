* 1. The GLSS2 contradiction, and its resolution

** The contradiction

=GhanaLSS/_/CONTENTS.org:389-390= (the per-round keyspace table):

: | GLSS1     | 1xxx             | 200 EAs from 1984 census frame    |
: | GLSS2     | 2xxx             | 200 EAs, fresh draw               |

#942 measures a clean 79-to-79 bijection between GLSS1 and GLSS2 clusters with
=Region= and =Rural= agreeing 79/79.  A fresh draw does not produce that.

** It is already flagged in the same file, and marked unresolved

=GhanaLSS/_/CONTENTS.org:1109-1121=, "Tensions with the existing text in this
file", item s2 --- which the section header at =:1076= records as *unresolved*
(only s1 was resolved in place):

#+begin_quote
The documentation says GLSS1 used master-sample replicates 1 and 5 (2 x 100
EAs), and that GLSS2 was *half* retained GLSS1 workloads plus *half* from
replicate 6 (100 EAs).  "Fresh draw" is therefore accurate for at most half of
GLSS2.
#+end_quote

So the file contradicts itself at two line numbers.  The brief's "the file does
not say so" is true of the table but not of the file.

** The document that settles it is in the repository

=GhanaLSS/1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3report.pdf=, Appendix 1, "Sample design
for GLSS1 and GLSS2", printed page 110 (quoted verbatim at =CONTENTS.org:927=):

#+begin_quote
The original idea was that a rotating panel design would be used, with half of
the sample being retained each year for re-interview and the other half being
replaced. ... Half the workloads from GLSS1 were therefore retained for GLSS2,
and attempts were made to re-interview the same households. The other half of
the GLSS2 sample was taken from the 100 EAs in replicate 6 of the master
sample.
#+end_quote

And the master-sample construction, same appendix, printed page 109
(=CONTENTS.org:933=): 800 EAs drawn PPS from the 1984 census, assigned to eight
replicates of 100; GLSS1 used replicates 1 and 5.

** The measurement that corroborates it

Realised keyspaces, warm =sample.parquet= (=tmp/gh942/glss.py=):

| quantity                         | GLSS1 (1987-88) | GLSS2 (1988-89) |
|----------------------------------+-----------------+-----------------|
| households                       |            3147 |            3194 |
| distinct =v=                     |         *176*   |         *170*   |
| =v= appearing in the 79-pair set |              79 |              79 |
| =v= appearing in no pair         |          *97*   |          *91*   |

That is the documented design, realised: about half of GLSS1's clusters recur
in GLSS2 under the =2xxx= numbering (79), and about half of GLSS2 is new (91,
against replicate 6's design figure of 100).  Household linkage is real and by
design --- 712 households at the API, 714 in =PANELC.DAT=
(=CONTENTS.org= §"Panel Linkage (GLSS1 to GLSS2)").

** Verdict

*GLSS2 is not a fresh draw.*  It is a half-retained rotating panel.
=CONTENTS.org:390= is wrong as written and should read something like:

: | GLSS2 | 2xxx | ~170 EAs; ~half retained from GLSS1 (renumbered), ~half from master-sample replicate 6 |

*Two caveats a crosswalk builder must carry.*

1. /The household-derived crosswalk is a LOWER BOUND on retained clusters./
   Half of 176 is ~88; we recover 79.  Either nine retained workloads yielded
   zero successful re-interviews, or "workload" is not exactly "EA".  Nothing in
   the repository distinguishes these, so a derived crosswalk must be labelled a
   floor, not the design.
2. /It is complete for what it can be./  The 97 GLSS1 and 91 GLSS2 unpaired
   clusters are genuinely different EAs; there is no missing linkage to find.
   The bijection is not a partial view of a 176-to-170 mapping.

*Consequence for #942.*  The 79 pairs are the *panel half* of one boundary, not
evidence that GLSS renumbers faithfully in general.  GLSS2 -> GLSS3 has *zero*
household linkage (measured; and =CONTENTS.org:959= quotes GSS abandoning the
rotation at GLSS3), so no crosswalk is derivable there by any method.

* 2. The corpus survey

** Method, and the two ways it lies

Per country, per consecutive wave pair: households sharing =i=, the share
keeping the same =v=, whether the induced cluster-pair relation is a bijection,
and the distinct =v= count each side.  Measured through =Country(C).sample()=,
so =updated_ids= has already re-keyed =i=.

Two false-positive modes fire in this corpus.  Both are reported per row so no
cell is read as evidence it cannot bear.

*** F1 --- spurious linkage: =i= is renumbered 1..N each wave

=Tajikistan/_/CONTENTS.org:331= quotes the WB documentation:

#+begin_quote
The TLSS99, TLSS03 and TLSS07 data cannot be used as panel data. The same
households were not visited in all surveys.
#+end_quote

Yet the measure finds 960 "shared" households at 2003->2007 and 1,503 at
2007->2009.  Their =i= values are =['1','10','100', ... '998','999']= in both
waves --- sequential household numbers restarted each round.  Panama is the
same.  *Neither country has any =panel_ids= configuration at all* (no
=panel_ids.json=, no =panel_ids.py=, no =data_scheme.yml= entry).

So Panama's 1.0%/0.0% and Tajikistan's 0.0%/0.6% are not evidence of a
wave-scoped =v=.  They are evidence of nothing.  Same for CotedIvoire
1988-89 -> 2018-19: 1,097 "shared" households across thirty years and a change
of survey programme (CILSS -> EHCVM), =nA=100=, =nB=1084=.

*** F2 --- =i= embeds =v=: what it does and does NOT disqualify

Where the household id is built from the cluster code (EHCVM =grappe_menage=,
Malawi, GhanaSPS, ...), a household matched on =i= must carry the same =v=.
Measured corpus-wide as "does the row's =v= occur as a substring of its =i=":

: Azerbaijan Benin Burkina_Faso China CotedIvoire GhanaAHIES GhanaSPS
: Guinea-Bissau Guyana Kosovo Malawi Mali Niger Pakistan Senegal Serbia
: "Serbia and Montenegro" South Africa Timor-Leste Togo    = 1.00
: Iraq 0.997   EthiopiaRHS 0.983   Albania 0.844   Ethiopia 0.714   Liberia 0.561

*It is important not to over-read this.*  Where the linkage is genuine, "the
household's id embeds its cluster number and it linked across waves" means the
survey wrote the *same cluster number* for that household in both rounds ---
which is exactly what =stable= asserts for #942's purposes.  What F2 actually
disqualifies is the *mover / bijection* diagnostic: a household that changed
cluster would have changed =i= too, so it cannot appear in the matched set at
all.  Those countries can be read as =stable= for the API contract, with
*movers unobservable*, and cannot be read as evidence about the draw.

What F2 *does* void is a =same_v= reading where the linkage itself is
unverified: there, id-composition and id-collision are indistinguishable.

*** Why the three must stay separate

Different fixes, different truth conditions.  (a) is a 322-entry recode the
library can apply with no survey input and no loss.  (b) is a sentinel that
should be *labelled*, not linked.  (c) admits no fix and can only be declared.
A single "wave-scoped" tag over Uganda would promise a crosswalk for (c) that
cannot exist and would hide that (a) is ours to fix.  *GhanaLSS renumbers
faithfully; Uganda does three different things at seven boundaries.*

** Tanzania: the brief's premise needs one correction

The brief says Tanzania's 0% continuity is the NPS refresh and is by design.
Measured, *the 0% cell and the refresh are different cells*:

- =2019-20 -> 2020-21=: *zero shared households*.  This is the refresh --- the
  extended and refresh sub-panels are disjoint
  (=.claude/skills/tanzania-panel-design.md=: "2019-20 and 2020-21 have zero
  household overlap ... correct by design").  It says nothing about =v=, because
  there is no household through which to observe it.
- =2014-15 -> 2019-20=: *849 shared households, 0.0% same =v=, 310 pairs, 131
  non-bijective*.  These are *extended-panel* households --- tracked by design
  --- that kept =i= and lost =v=.  =2019-20/_/data_info.yml:24= maps
  =v: clusterid=; =2020-21/_/data_info.yml:19= maps =v: y5_cluster=; 2012-13 and
  2014-15 are script-path (the =2008-15/= multi-round folder,
  =.claude/skills/multi-round-waves.md=) and declare no =v:= in YAML at all.

So Tanzania is =wave-scoped= at 2014-15->2019-20 and =refresh-by-design= at
2019-20->2020-21, and unlike GhanaLSS and Ethiopia its wave-scoped boundary is
*not a bijection* (131 violations over 310 pairs) --- tracked households
genuinely moved between clusters.  *A crosswalk is not recoverable there even in
principle.*

*Main structural finding of this survey:* refresh-by-design is a fact about
*household* continuity; wave-scoped is a fact about *cluster-id* continuity.
They are orthogonal, they occur at different boundaries of the same country, and
a one-dimensional regime enum cannot express both.

** Recommendation: the =panel:= block of =sampling.yml= (#793)

*Not =data_info.yml=.*  That file holds cross-country *conventions* --- required
columns, canonical spellings, canonical indexes.  "Ethiopia renumbered its EAs
between W1 and W2" is a per-country, per-wave fact about a survey, the category
=population.yml= and =sampling.yml= exist for.  It would be the first
per-country datum in a file whose discipline is that it holds none.

*Not a new =cluster_identity.yml=.*  #793 already proposes =sampling.yml= with a
=panel:= key (its Uganda exemplar has =panel: {role: panel-baseline, rotation: null}=).
Cluster-id continuity across rounds *is* a draw-and-rotation fact; it belongs in
the same record as "half the workloads were retained".  A separate file would put
the GLSS1/GLSS2 rotation in one file and its consequence for =v= in another.

*But do not block #942 on #793's full schema.*  #793 is a large curation project
(stages, PPS, oversamples, ~24 countries with no documentation, an unverified
NADA DDI acquisition route).  #942 needs three fields per boundary.  Recommend:
*#942 lands =sampling.yml= with only the =panel:= block populated, plus the
=PopulationRecord=-style loader and =Country(...).sampling=; #793 grows the same
file with the design fields.*  #793 gets its loader for free; #942 does not wait.

** The shape

Per *wave*, describing that wave's relation to *the preceding wave in
=Country.waves=* --- the grain is the boundary, not the country.  GhanaLSS
renumbers at 1988-89 and is unlinkable thereafter; Uganda changes regime at seven
consecutive boundaries; Tanzania is panel-stable, then wave-scoped, then
refreshed.  *A single tag per country is wrong for all five informative
countries.*

#+begin_src yaml
Sampling:
  '1988-89':
    panel:
      household_continuity: rotating-panel   # | panel | refresh | independent | unknown
      cluster_id_continuity: wave-scoped     # | stable | wave-scoped | redefined | mixed | unobservable
      cluster_id_note: |-
        GLSS2 renumbers retained GLSS1 EAs under the 2xxx scheme.  79 of
        GLSS1's 176 clusters recur; the other 91 GLSS2 clusters are from
        master-sample replicate 6.  The 79 is a LOWER BOUND (derived from
        household linkage), not the design figure.
      source_type: local-documentation       # | wb-catalog | derived-from-data | not-found
      confidence: high                       # | medium | low
      source_file: '1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3report.pdf'
      locator: 'Appendix 1, printed page 110'
#+end_src

| =cluster_id_continuity= | means                                      | instance                             |
|-------------------------+--------------------------------------------+--------------------------------------|
| =stable=                | the same EA keeps its id                   | GhanaSPS, GhanaAHIES, Nigeria, CILSS |
| =wave-scoped=           | renumbered; a crosswalk is meaningful      | GhanaLSS 1988-89, Ethiopia 2013-14   |
| =redefined=             | =v= denotes a different KIND of thing      | Uganda 2013-14, 2018-19              |
| =mixed=                 | some clusters synthetic                    | Uganda 2009-10 (339/656 =@lat,lon=)  |
| =unobservable=          | no verified linkage; cannot be determined  | GhanaLSS 1991-92 onward, Iraq        |

*=refresh-by-design= is NOT a =cluster_id_continuity= value.*  It is
=household_continuity: refresh= (Tanzania 2020-21) --- exactly the distinction
the brief requires the scheme to express, and the one-dimensional enum in #942's
"Possible resolutions" cannot.

*There is deliberately no =rendered-differently= value*, although §2.3(a) is a
real corpus phenomenon.  Q5 recommends treating it as a library defect to fix;
declaring it in =sampling.yml= would record a bug as a survey design fact ---
#793's own "launder a screen into a record" objection.  Until Q5 is decided,
Uganda 2009-10 and 2011-12 *measure* as wave-scoped and must not be *declared*
so; leave them =unobservable= with the note, or hold the two entries.

*The triple travels with the record*, as =PopulationRecord.from_config= requires
(=population.py:130=) and =capability.audit()= requires: =source_type= /
=confidence= / =locator=, refused if absent.  A value reached by §2's
measurement is =source_type: derived-from-data=, and by #793's own rule a
=derived-from-data= record must not be readable as documentation.  Given §2's
two false-positive modes that rule is load-bearing: a =derived-from-data=
reading for a country with unverified linkage is unfalsifiable by construction
and should be written =unobservable= with the reason, not =stable=.

*A possible third field, flagged not decided.*  Malawi serves 100% continuity
because the IHPS tracks households and reports the *baseline* EA; Tanzania
serves 79.6% because it reports the household's *current* cluster.  Both are
defensible and they mean different things --- and for clustering standard
errors, the *baseline* EA is the design cluster.  A
=cluster_reference: baseline-ea | current-location= field would name it.  Out of
scope here; raised for @ligon.

* 4. What the library should DO with it

** The hazard is pooling WAVES, not pooling COUNTRIES

=PopulationHeterogeneityWarning= (=feature.py:896=, =population.py:122=) warns
at =Feature()= and is *silent* at =Country()=, because the hazard it names only
arises when countries are pooled.

#942's hazard is the opposite shape.  Its own reproduction is a *single-country,
two-wave =Country()= call*:

#+begin_src python
hc = g.household_characteristics(waves=["1987-88", "1988-89"])
#+end_src

So the warning must fire at =Country=, not at =Feature=.  Copying the population
precedent's *placement* would put it exactly where the bug is not.

** Proposal

*Warn, never fence, never rewrite.*

1. =ClusterIdentityWarning(UserWarning)= in a new =lsms_library/sampling.py=
   (sibling of =population.py=).
2. Raised from =Country._finalize_result= when *all* of: the frame carries =v=;
   it spans more than one =t=; and some consecutive boundary among the
   *returned* waves has =cluster_id_continuity= in
   ={wave-scoped, redefined, mixed}=.
   *Deliberately NOT on =unobservable=.*  GhanaLSS 1991-92 onward are genuinely
   different EAs with distinct prefixes and no linkage, so grouping on =v= across
   them is *correct*; warning there would false-alarm on every multi-wave
   GhanaLSS call and train the reader to ignore it.
3. *Dedup key required.*  =_join_v_from_sample= re-enters =sample()=, which runs
   its own =_finalize_result=, before the outer table's runs --- so a naive
   "once per call" fires twice.  Key the emission on
   =(country, frozenset(waves))= for the duration of the outermost call.
4. Message names country, boundaries and values, states that *nothing was
   dropped or rewritten*, and gives the remedy the workshop found by hand:
   #+begin_quote
   GhanaLSS: `v` is not comparable across 1987-88 -> 1988-89
   (cluster_id_continuity: wave-scoped -- GLSS2 renumbers retained GLSS1 EAs).
   Rows are returned unchanged.  Group within a wave, or `.droplevel('v')`
   before a cross-wave reshape.
   #+end_quote
5. =Country.sampling= property returning ={wave: SamplingRecord}=, mirroring
   =Country.population=.
6. =df.attrs['sampling']=, and a *fourth entry in =feature._ATTRS_CARRIERS==
   (=feature.py:98-117=) so the record survives =Feature()='s concat.  CLAUDE.md
   records that =recall= and =derivations= had their =merge_attrs= "written,
   documented as load-bearing, and never called" until #873 --- do not repeat it.
7. =sampling_reports(country=, table=)=, twin of =null_read_reports()= /
   =quantity_reports()=.
8. =LSMS_CLUSTER_STRICT=1= -> =ClusterIdentityError=.  *Its own lever*, for the
   reason CLAUDE.md gives for each of the others: a destroyed row, an empty
   column, an impossible value and an incomparable key are separate concerns and
   must ratchet separately.

*No returned data changes* --- not one row, not one index value, under any
setting except the opt-in strict lever.  Cache cost zero: =_finalize_result= is
in =_build_registry._EXCLUDED_CALLABLES= (CLAUDE.md §Population: "Attach point is
load-bearing ... so it costs no cache invalidation"), and the =.yml= is invisible
to the hasher (§3.1).

** Deliberately NOT proposed

- *No allowlist of known-bad boundaries.*  Same reasoning as =_grain_strict=,
  =null_read_audit= and =quantity_audit=: an allowlist is the same disease with a
  registry.  =unobservable= is a recorded verdict with a reason, not an
  exemption.
- *No default =droplevel('v')=.*  That is a default that silently drops data ---
  what @ligon declined in #603.
- *No auto-derivation of the record from §2's measurement.*  §2 has two
  false-positive modes; a generated record would encode the artefact.

* 5. Out of scope, and why

*The crosswalk is out of scope for #942.  Recommendation: declare the regime;
make the crosswalk a follow-up.*

1. *It is derivable for two boundaries in the whole corpus.*  GhanaLSS
   1987-88->1988-89 (79 pairs, a lower bound, §1) and Ethiopia 2011-12->2013-14
   (333 pairs, complete).  Tanzania 2014-15->2019-20 has 131 non-bijective pairs
   over 310 --- not derivable *in principle*, since tracked households genuinely
   changed cluster.  Uganda's zero-agreement boundaries are (a) a recode, (b) a
   sentinel and (c) a meaning change; a household-derived crosswalk addresses
   none.  A crosswalk built on #942's method covers *two cells* and would be read
   as covering the class.
2. *The regime declaration is the prerequisite.*  You cannot decide where to
   apply a crosswalk without a machine-readable statement of which boundaries
   want one.  Today that is prose no consumer reads --- the whole of #942's
   complaint.
3. *Uganda's cheapest real fix is not a crosswalk at all* (§2.3a): a 322-entry
   table fixing two of seven boundaries with no survey input.  Its own issue.
4. *Rewriting =v= changes served data*, and that decision has not been taken ---
   §6 Q3.

Also out of scope: #793's design fields (stages, PPS, oversamples); the Ethiopia
geovars crosswalk read (§2.4, worth doing, needs a cold read); any change to
=_join_v_from_sample=; the =cluster_reference= field (§3.3).

* 6. Open decisions for @ligon

** Q1.  Is the unit of declaration the wave boundary, or the country?

- *Per boundary* (recommended).  Expresses Uganda's seven different answers and
  Tanzania's three.  Cost: ~104 wave entries, most =unobservable= or =stable=
  with a one-line reason.
- *Per country.*  38 entries.  Cost: wrong for GhanaLSS, Uganda, Tanzania,
  Ethiopia and Nigeria --- five of the five countries where the question is
  answerable.  Recommend against.

** Q2.  One field or two?

- *Two orthogonal fields* (recommended): =household_continuity= and
  =cluster_id_continuity=.  Required to state that Tanzania's 2019-20->2020-21
  break is a refresh (a household fact) while its 2014-15->2019-20 break is
  renumbering (a cluster fact).  The brief requires exactly this distinction.
- *One enum.*  Simpler; collapses the two Tanzania rows and loses the
  by-design/defect distinction the skill file exists to preserve.

** Q3.  If a crosswalk is later built, WHERE does it apply?

- *(a) Rewrite =v= at =_join_v_from_sample=.*  Symmetric with =id_walk= on =i=,
  read-path, zero cache cost.  *Changes served index values* for GhanaLSS and
  Ethiopia --- every existing cross-wave result moves.
- *(b) Add a =v_panel= level alongside =v=.*  Nothing served changes; the user
  opts in.  Cost: a new index level on every household table of the affected
  countries, plus a =Feature()= alignment decision for countries lacking it.
- *(c) A =cluster_ids= table, twin of =panel_ids=.*  Nothing changes anywhere;
  the user joins it.  Cheapest and most honest; also the easiest to not use,
  which is how #942 arose.

Recommend *(c)* first and *(b)* as the opt-in ergonomic layer.  (a) is a silent
change to returned data of exactly the kind #603 declined.

** Q4.  Does =CONTENTS.org:390= get corrected in this issue or its own?

The "fresh draw" row is wrong (§1); correcting it is a two-line edit that also
resolves =Tensions= s2, recorded in the file as unresolved.  Read-only here.
Recommend folding into #942's PR, since #942's crosswalk rationale cites the row.

** Q5.  Should Uganda's rendering defect (§2.3a) become its own issue?

Recommend *yes*, and *not* folded into #942.  It is a library-side formatting
inconsistency with a 322-entry deterministic fix, it currently reads as a
wave-scoped =v= and would be mis-declared as one, and it is the only one of the
three Uganda defects that can simply be repaired.  Note the 272/50 split (§2.3a):
the fix is the pair table, not a digit rule.

** Q6.  Does the Ethiopia geovars file already ship the crosswalk?

=CONTENTS.org:978= lists =Pub_ETH_HouseholdGeovars_Y2= as carrying
"household_id(2), ea_id(2)" --- both id generations side by side.  If so,
Ethiopia's crosswalk is a *read* rather than an inference, and should be read
from the survey rather than derived from panel households.  Unverified here
(needs a cold source read).  One bounded check before any derivation code is
written.

** Q7.  Should =cluster_reference: baseline-ea | current-location= be a third field?

Malawi serves the baseline EA (100% continuity), Tanzania the current cluster
(79.6%).  Both defensible, different meanings, and for clustering standard errors
the baseline is the design cluster.  Adding it makes the record right; it also
widens the curation.  Raised, not recommended either way.
