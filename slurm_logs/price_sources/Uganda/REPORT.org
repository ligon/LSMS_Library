** The question wording (this is what sources 2 and 3 actually are)

UNPS GSEC15B (2013-14 household questionnaire, "Part B: Food, Beverage, and
Tobacco (During the Last 7 Days)", PDF p. 7) is one grid per food item.  Its
columns, verbatim:

: 3C Unit of Qty | Consumption out of Purchases: Household [4 Qty, 5 Value],
: Away from home [6 Qty, 7 Value] | Consumption out of home produce
: [8 Qty, 9 Value] | Received in-kind/Free [10 Qty, 11 Value] |
: 12 Market Price | 13 Farm gate /produ cer price

So =market= (=h15bq12=) and =farmgate= (=h15bq13=) are *columns 12 and 13 of
the same row*, asked in the same unit as column 3C.  The library maps 12 ->
=Price= for =s='purchased'= and 13 -> =Price= for =s='produced'=
(=uganda.food_acquired_to_canonical=).

*The interviewer manual settles what column 9 means* -- and it is the answer
to section 5's question.  The file is stored at
=2011-12/Documentation/UNPS.HouseholdAndWomanQx.Manual.pdf=, but the document
itself is titled /"DRAFT Interviewer Manual for Uganda National Panel Survey
2010/11"/ -- cite both, since the path and the document disagree.  Section
T6aeQ5 (the same words recur at T6nfQ5, and in the 2005-06 UNHS Manual of
Instructions under "Food consumed out of home production"):

#+begin_quote
Home production refers to items produced or grown by the household which have
been consumed by the household during the past 7 days. ... Ask the respondent
to estimate the amount of money that he/she would normally sell the quantity
reported. *This should be valued at farm gate/producer price. Farm gate price
refers to the price a farmer receives for his or product at the boundary of
the farm. This price excludes any cost transport and marketing services.*
#+end_quote

and, for contrast, the market price the same manual defines at T6aeQ6:

#+begin_quote
Valuation of the quantity consumed should be based on the market price. The
market price refers to the price prevailing in the market that *includes cost
of transport, marketing services and profit margins.*
#+end_quote

So source 3 is, *by instruction*, "what would you have RECEIVED if sold" --
a farmgate valuation, and the marketing margin is exactly the object of study.
Whether households obeyed is section 5.

Crop sales (source 4), 2013-14 agriculture questionnaire, "SECTION 5A CONT'D:
QUANTIFICATION OF PRODUCTION": /"How much of the [...] you harvested during
the first season of 2013 was sold and in what condition/state?"/ with *Qty,
Condition/State Code and Unit Code*, then /"What was the value?"/ in UShs.
That compound question is the root of defect D2.

94.4-99.9% of purchased rows have the reported "Market Price" *exactly* equal
to =Expenditure/Quantity=.  The pooled matched set gives a median gap of 0.000
with an IQR of 0.000 across 4,686 cells.

*This is not a library artefact.*  I re-ran it on the raw =.dta= before the
library touches anything, computing =h15bq12= against
=(h15bq5+h15bq7)/(h15bq4+h15bq6)= directly:

| wave | market vs Exp/Qty | farmgate vs Value_own/Qty_own | market AND farmgate both populated |
|------+-------------------+-------------------------------+------------------------------------|
| 2005-06 | 1.72% differ | 1.36% differ | 160 of 28,401 |
| 2011-12 | 5.21% | 4.75% | 110 of 26,030 |
| 2013-14 | 1.51% | 1.01% | 124 of 34,211 |

Two conclusions, both consequential:

1. *Source 2 is dropped.*  UBOS's columns 12 and 13 are back-calculated unit
   values, not independently elicited prices, whatever the questionnaire
   asks.

   *The residual few percent is NOT the home/away fold, and I checked rather
   than assumed.*  The obvious explanation is that the library folds the two
   purchase legs, so a price matching only one leg would differ from the
   folded ratio.  Measured on the raw file, among rows where the reported
   price differs: the share with BOTH a home and an away leg populated is
   0.8% (2005-06), 0.4% (2011-12), 0.2% (2013-14) -- against a baseline of
   0.15-0.24% among all priced purchased rows, i.e. essentially no
   enrichment.  The share whose reported price matches ONE leg exactly is
   0.2-0.8%.  *The fold explains none of it.*  What the residual is remains
   unexplained -- keying noise, rounding at entry, or a genuinely independent
   report in a small minority -- and I do not claim to know.
2. *Columns 12 and 13 are populated DISJOINTLY* (fill 71-75% vs 22-27%,
   overlapping on ~0.4% of rows).  So UNPS does *not* deliver a
   within-household market-vs-farmgate price pair, which is what one would
   have hoped from a questionnaire that prints both columns side by side.

Source 3 survives this: it is =value_own / quantity_own=, which is a real
quantity (the household's valuation of what it ate from its own harvest).
What it is *not* is a separately elicited price question.

* 4. Logs: the model, read out

=models.csv=, one pooled row plus one per =geo_level=, per wave and pair.
Standard errors are HC3 and *unclustered*; with cells nested in districts and
regions the true SEs on the source-4 pairs are wider than printed, so I read
only the sign and rough magnitude there.

*The primary test is =between=* (protocol): a constant markup or iceberg
transport cost predicts beta = 0, an additive per-unit cost predicts beta < 0.

| pair | median =between= over waves | median =within= | median R^2 of item+geo effects |
|------+-----------------------------+-----------------+--------------------------------|
| 1 vs 3 | *+0.013* (se ~0.04) | +0.046 | 0.32 |
| 1 vs 4 | *-0.189* (se ~0.14-0.25) | -0.051 | 0.93 |
| 1 vs 4b | *-0.224* | -0.197 | 0.90 |
| 3 vs 4 | *-0.197* | -0.326 | 0.84 |
| 3 vs 4b | *-0.233* | -0.357 | 0.83 |

In words:

- *1 vs 3 behaves like a pure proportional markup.*  =between= is +0.013 with
  a standard error three times its size -- indistinguishable from zero -- and
  =within= likewise.  The gap between what a household pays and what it says
  its own produce is worth does not shrink as the food gets dearer.  Item and
  place explain only 32% of that gap's variance, i.e. it is mostly
  household-level noise, which is what one expects of two self-reported
  valuations of the same commodity.
- *Every pair involving the crop sale price has a clearly negative =between=*,
  -0.19 to -0.23.  A 10% dearer crop carries a ~2% smaller proportional gap:
  there is a real *additive per-unit* component in the farmgate-to-market
  wedge, on top of a proportional one.  That is the signature of a per-kg
  handling or transport cost, and it is the protocol's discriminating result.
- *R^2 of item + geo effects is 0.83-0.93 on the source-4 pairs* against 0.32
  on 1 vs 3.  Almost all of the farmgate-to-market gap is "which item, and
  where" -- it is structural, not household noise.
- =within= is *not interpretable* for any source-4 pair and I do not read it:
  source 4 is matched at a threshold of 5 against a household sample of 10, so
  the two sides have unequal noise, which the protocol shows drives the within
  slope negative under a pure markup (synthetic: -1.27 with a thin b). The
  =within= column above is printed for the record only.  For 1 vs 3, where
  both sides are household samples of >= 10, =within= *is* readable, and it
  agrees with =between= at ~0.
- Noise, from =sd_log_price= in =cells.csv=: source 4 is the noisier side of
  every pair it appears in, which is why =between= is the only slope I trust
  there.

*The answer to the headline claim: no, it is higher.*  The farmgate-to-market
gap in Uganda is *60%*, against the literature's 30-50%.  =claim_30_50= is
=False= for every pooled (=geo_level='all'=) wave-pair row but one -- 2019-20
=unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price_soldunit=, whose median gap is 0.405, i.e.
exactly =log 1.5=, the band's upper edge.  Across all 167 model rows it is
True in 12, all of them single rungs with 1-15 cells and all sitting at the
top of the band (0.288-0.405).  So the band is touched from above, never
occupied.  Taking the market side as source 1 (the
purchase unit value, which the manual defines as including "transport,
marketing services and profit margins") and the farmgate side as source 4 (an
actual sale, not a valuation), the wedge is 1.60x pooled and ranges 1.50-2.00
across waves with no trend.

** Does a household value its own-consumed harvest at the sale price, the purchase price, or between?

*Between -- and nearer the purchase price.*  On the common cell set the
own-consumption valuation sits at *0.585* of the log distance from the crop
sale price to the purchase price (median).  Prices are heavily rounded, so
exact ties are common and the strict/non-strict choice matters; all three
categories, strictly:

| own-consumption valuation vs ... | strictly below | exactly equal | strictly above |
|----------------------------------+----------------+---------------+----------------|
| the crop sale price (source 4) | 13.9% | 21.5% | 64.6% |
| the purchase price (source 1) | 94.2% | 20.6% (of =gap_1_3=) | 5.8% |

Read the first row: in two thirds of cells the household values its own
produce strictly above what a sale actually fetched, and in only one seventh
below.

*So the valuation does not land where the instruction points.*  The manual is
explicit that column 9 "should be valued at farm gate/producer price ...
excludes any cost transport and marketing services".  If it were followed to
the letter, 3 vs 4 would be ~1.0.  It is 1.23.

Two readings fit that equally, and the data cannot separate them: households
ignored the instruction and valued at something nearer what they would pay;
or they followed it, but their *notion* of "the price I would get" is the
price at the nearest trading centre rather than at the field boundary, which
already contains part of the margin.  Source 3 is a valuation the *respondent*
produced (=value_own/quantity_own=), not a price anyone quoted, so the
behavioural attribution is genuinely open.  *For a consumption aggregate the
two readings say the same thing* -- roughly 23% of margin is baked in either
way.  Consequence for anyone
using =food_acquired.Expenditure= on =s='produced'= rows: own-consumption is
valued roughly 23% above what the household would actually have received, so
consumption aggregates built on it carry a partial marketing margin that the
questionnaire intended to exclude.

*A sharper, cell-free version of the same test, with a caveat that partly
undoes it.*  For households that both bought and produced the same item, in
the same unit, in the same 7 days, the log ratio of the purchase unit value to
the own-consumption unit value has median *exactly 0.000 in all eight waves*
(n = 59-124 per wave, 696 total): those households value own consumption at
precisely what they paid.  *But 49-76% of those observations are exact zeros*,
which is as consistent with the enumerator entering one number in both column
blocks as with a genuine valuation.  I report it as suggestive, not as
behaviour, and the cell-based 0.585 is the number I would defend.

  within processing state, not across it.
- *Sweet Bananas at 0.37x is a unit artefact.*  A "bunch" sold at the farm and
  a "bunch" bought in a market are not the same object, and the negative gap
  is not a negative margin.

one pre-check that came back clean, and it is why Uganda needed no unit fix.

* Appendix: what a future reader should not repeat

- =food_prices(units='unitprice')= and =units='unitvalue'= return *the same
  number* for Uganda on 94-99.9% of rows.  Do not treat them as two sources.
- The three pairwise gap rows in section 3 are on different cell sets.  Use
  =three_way_cells.csv= for anything that needs them to be consistent.
- 2015-16 crop sale values are unusable until D1 is explained.  They are
  excluded here, not corrected.
