** Sources 2 and 3 do not exist in the API, and the absence is silent

=food_acquired= for Malawi declares only =Quantity= and =Expenditure= -- no
=Price= column (ledger s.4).  =Country('Malawi').food_prices(units='unitprice')=
therefore returns an *empty frame*, 0 rows, and *does not raise*.  A caller who
does not check =len()= gets a silent nothing where six other countries get a
price.  Reported under Data problems (D6).

Consequence for this exercise: Malawi cannot compare a household's valuation of
its own harvest (3) with anything.  The farmgate side is *only* source 4, the
crop sale price.

** What the instruments actually ask (quoted)

*Source 5*, IHS3 Community questionnaire, "SECTION CK: PRICES"
(=Malawi/2010-11/Documentation/IHS3.Community.Qx.FINAL.pdf=):

#+begin_quote
PROMPT FOR EACH ITEM ON THE LIST.

Is [ITEM] available for sale in your community or nearby at the present time?
YES..1  NO...2 >> NEXT ITEM

What is the price for the item?   MK | NUMBER OF UNITS | UNIT CODES
#+end_quote

So source 5 is a *current local market selling price*, and its basis is
explicit: the per-unit price is =Price / NumberOfUnits=.  Do not assume
=NumberOfUnits= is 1 -- it is 50 for 2,345 rows, 250 for 1,085, 500 for 875.

*Source 4*, IHS3 Agriculture questionnaire Module I q6/q7, and the Stata
variable labels of =ag_mod_i.dta=:

#+begin_quote
Did you sell any of the harvested [CROP] during the 2008/2009 rainy season?

How much of the harvested [CROP] was sold in total?   QUANTITY | UNIT | S/U

ag_i03 = What was the total value of [CROP] sales?
#+end_quote

"What was the total *value of sales*" is a *received* price: source 4 is a
genuine farmgate price, not a "what would you have paid" valuation.  Note the
UNIT and S/U columns -- see D1 and D2.

** The idiosyncrasies I found, named

1. *=crop_production='s sale carries its OWN unit, and it was never read.*
   The single most consequential finding; see D1.  =Malawi/_/CONTENTS.org=
   documented neither =crop_production= nor =community_prices= at all before
   this task (1,409 lines on plot_features, livestock, housing and sampling
   design); both are now written up there, commit =5eebadfc=.
2. *2004-05's =v= was not a cluster key until GH #323* -- it was =ea=, the EA's
   sequence number within its Traditional Authority, 110 distinct values for
   564 EAs.  Now =psu=, the fully-qualified 8-digit code.  *Frame vintage
   changes twice*: 1998 census EAs (IHS2), 2008 (IHS3/IHS4 + the IHPS
   baseline), 2018 (IHS5), and 2004-05 shares only *3* codes with 2010-11.  A
   cross-wave join on =v= is meaningful only from 2010-11.  This analysis never
   pools waves, so it is unaffected -- and 2004-05 has neither source 4 nor
   source 5, so it contributes source-1 cells and nothing else.
3. *2016-17 and 2019-20 are each ONE library wave carrying TWO declared
   universes* -- a national cross-section (IHS4 / IHS5) and a tracking-defined
   IHPS panel.  From 2016 the IHPS half is, by the survey's own statement, no
   longer regionally representative ("the IHPS 2016 cannot be tabulated by
   region ... the IHPS domains of analysis will be limited to the national,
   urban and rural areas").  Every Region-rung cell in those two waves pools
   both universes.  *Read the =v= and District rungs in preference.*
4. *2013-14 (IHPS) =ea_id= is the IHS3 BASELINE EA, and the survey tracks
   movers.*  588 of 4,000 households report a district different from the one
   encoded in their own =ea_id=, and those 588 sit a *median 68 km* from their
   IHS3 location against 0.03 km for the 3,412 that match.  At the =v= rung a
   mover's purchase is therefore paired with its *baseline* EA's community
   price -- the wrong market.  This is the sharpest caveat on 2013-14's
   cluster-grain cells, and 2013-14 is thin for other reasons too (next point).
5. *2013-14 prices almost no food.*  The IHPS Module CK list is the reduced
   subset CK1, CK3, CK4, CK22-CK50; it omits CK5-CK21, i.e. *every perishable
   food*.  Of its 32 priced items essentially one -- maize grain -- is a food.
   The 2013-14 rows in =models.csv= with =n_cells= of 1, 2 and 8 are that, not
   a wave-level result.  Do not read them as one.
6. *Region-specific unit factors, and =conversion_to_kgs.json= has never been
   generated* (CONTENTS.org TODO, GH #116; IHS2 has no conversion information
   at all).  Malawi's kg conversion is region-keyed and lives in the wave
   scripts' CSV, so =food_acquired.Quantity_kg= is a *region-specific*
   conversion.  =crop_production= and =community_prices= carry no
   =Expenditure=, so =_get_kg_factors=' price-ratio inference has nothing to
   work on for them and only metric labels survive.  *Effect on the per-kg
   view*: using the library's =units='kgvalue'= for source 1 and metric labels
   for sources 4 and 5 would put the two sides of every gap on different
   factor bases.  See "Deviations" below for what I did instead.
7. *=crop_production= names its item level =crop=, not =j=* (D5).
8. *Two "must consult" documents I did not read*, stated rather than left
   silent: the per-wave =Documentation/SOURCE.org= files (I went to the
   questionnaires and the catalog PDFs directly, and to =CONTENTS.org='s
   verbatim universe quotes, which are re-extracted from those same PDFs), and
   =slurm_logs/DESIGN_food_prices_units_kwarg_2026-05-06.org= (the four
   =units== modes are documented in =CLAUDE.md= and in
   =transformations.py=, which is what I worked from).  Neither changes a
   number here; both would have to be read before anyone extends this to the
   =*price= modes, which Malawi does not have.
9. *=community_prices= carries a redundant =i= level equal to =v=* purely to
   stop =local_tools.map_index()= swapping =j= into =i= (a Malawi-legacy
   =food_acquired= inversion).  Documented in =malawi.py:2482=; it works, and
   the spurious =i= never reaches the API.

** (i) Unit vocabulary across tables

| table              | distinct =u= | comment                                        |
|--------------------+--------------+------------------------------------------------|
| =community_prices= |           20 | clean =harmonize_price_unit= Preferred Labels  |
| =crop_production=  |           13 | clean =harmonize_crop_unit= Preferred Labels   |
| =food_acquired=    |      *640*   | 17 shared labels + *623* others                |

The intersection of =food_acquired.u= and =community_prices.u= is *17 of 20*
(all but =90 kg Bag=, =Basket (Dengu)=, =Ox-Cart=, which households do not buy
in).  So the *vocabulary* is fine; the problem is the other 623, and *it is not
one problem but three*:

- (a) *Spelling variants of a shared label* -- =Kg= (35,805 rows, all 2004-05),
  =kg=, =Kgs=, =Kilogram=, =Kkilogram=, =No. 10 plate= vs =No 10 Plate=,
  =No.12 Plate= vs =No 12 Plate= (the last pair are *two spellings of one unit
  inside the shared =u= mapping table itself*, codes 07 vs 07a/07b).  2004-05
  uses an entirely different label set from 2010-11 onward.  *This blocks
  nothing here*: 2004-05 has neither source 4 nor source 5.  Reported (D4), not
  fixed -- fixing it would cold-rebuild the corpus for no gain to this task.
- (b) *Size-qualified labels* -- =Heap (Large)=, =Heap (Medium)=,
  =Piece (Medium)=, =No.10 Plate (Flat)=, =No.10 Plate (Heaped)=.  These are a
  *real granularity difference, not a rename*: the community survey asks the
  price of a generic "heap", the household reports the size of the heap it
  bought.  *Do not collapse them.*  The cost is concrete and asymmetric: in
  2010-11 and 2013-14 =food_acquired= is size-qualified and =community_prices=
  is not, so =Heap= and =Piece= barely match in those waves; in 2019-20 both
  sides use the bare label and they match in volume (=Heap= / Tomato alone is
  294 matched cells).
- (c) *Free-text junk* -- =Tina Falt=, =Smallpaper=, =Nsima And Beef=,
  =Shecookedonherown=.  Hundreds of labels, individually tiny.

*The unit-side blocker I did fix in the analysis is a different one*:
=u = 'Other (Specify)'= is *not a unit*, it is the absence of one, and matching
two sources on it pairs a price per (unknown thing A) with a price per (unknown
thing B).  Left in, it produced =community_price_vs_crop_sale_price= cells with
ratios of *0.004* (Maize), *0.007* (Rice) and *0.033* (Tomato).  Excluded on
all three frames (3,333 + 453 + 2,867 rows).

*The one that most restricts the answer is not a vocabulary defect at all*:
households essentially never *buy* in =50 kg Bag= -- 53 rows in 2010-11, *0* in
2013-14, *1* in 2019-20 -- while the crop *sale* is dominated by it (65,989
rows).  The only unit in which all three sources coexist in volume is
=Kilogramme=.  *That is why the per-kg view is load-bearing for Malawi rather
than a secondary check.*

* 3. Levels: median ratio per pair and wave

=ratio = exp(median gap_log)=; "a" is the market side, so >1 means a is dearer.
=n= is matched cells at =geo_level='all'=.  Thresholds: source 1 n>=10 (a
household sample statistic), source 4 n>=5 (a household sample, but *thin* --
lowered per PROTOCOL and stated here), source 5 n>=1 (a measurement: one
surveyed price per cluster and item).

|---------+--------+------+-------+----------+------------------------|
| 2010-11 | native |  837 | 1.000 |    0.125 |                  0.170 |
| 2019-20 | native | 1156 | 1.000 |    0.223 |                  0.245 |

*This is the best-powered result in Malawi and it is a striking one: the
household purchase unit value and the independently surveyed community
reference price agree to within 0-5% at the median.*  They are separate
instruments (=hh_mod_g1.dta= vs =com_ck.dta=), separate respondents (the
household vs a community informant panel), and they land on the same number.
The exact 0.000 medians are not an artefact of a shared source; they reflect
heavy price rounding (a 100 g packet of tea at MK 50), so two independent
medians coincide exactly.  The dispersion is small too: IQR 0.125-0.259 in
logs, i.e. a half-width of about 6-13%.

* 4. Logs: the model table, read out

With the =MIN_DOF=5= gate in the coordinator's revised =common.py=, *almost
every source-4 slope is NaN*, and that is the right answer, not a failure: 10
to 41 matched cells against an item-plus-geography effect block leaves nothing
to identify a slope with.  The earlier run reported =r2_fe = 1.000= with
=n_cells = 12= and =between = 5.74=; those were saturated fits, and they are
now correctly suppressed.  *For the two farmgate pairs, read section 3 and the
per-item table in section 5; there is no interpretable regression.*

The slopes that survive are all on =unit_value_vs_community_price=:

| wave    | basis  |    n | between (se)     | within (se)     | r2_fe | resid |
|---------+--------+------+------------------+-----------------+-------+-------|
| 2010-11 | native |  837 | *-0.045 (0.015)* |  0.029 (0.027)  | 0.576 | small |
| 2019-20 | native | 1156 |  -0.008 (0.015)  | -0.456 (0.324)  | 0.384 |       |
| 2010-11 | kg     |  772 |  -0.064 (0.058)  | -1.226 (0.236)  | 0.523 |       |
| 2019-20 | kg     |  575 |  -0.128 (0.101)  | -1.691 (0.278)  | 0.355 |       |

In words, one sentence each:

- =between= is the primary test and it is *economically zero* in all four
  rows: -0.045 (2 se from zero, but 4.5% of a log point) to -0.128 (1.3 se).
  A constant markup or an iceberg cost predicts 0; an additive per-unit
  handling cost predicts a *strongly* negative slope.  *The gap between the two
  market sources does not fall with the price level*, which is what one would
  expect of two measurements of the same thing.
- =within= is *not interpretable here* and I am reporting it only because the
  protocol says to.  Source 5 is a measurement matched at cluster grain with
  =n = 1= per cell (its =sd_log_price= at =v= is *NaN* -- a single observation
  has no dispersion), which is precisely PROTOCOL's "THIN b" case where the
  Bland-Altman midpoint stops cancelling the shared noise and the within slope
  takes the sign of =var(noise_a) - var(noise_b)=.  The synthetic benchmark for
  that case is -1.27; the two kg rows come out at -1.23 and -1.69.  *Those are
  the artefact, not a finding.*
- =r2_fe= 0.36-0.58: item identity and place explain between a third and
  three-fifths of the variance in the gap -- so most of what is left is
  cell-level noise, not a systematic price-level effect.
- Which source is noisier: at the District rung the median =sd_log_price= is
  *0.310* for the community price, *0.445* for the unit value and *0.490* for
  the crop sale price.  The community price is the *least* dispersed, as a
  single surveyed price rather than a household sample should be.
- Standard errors are HC3 and *unclustered*.  With 294-495 distinct =v=
  values and multiple items per cluster this understates the true uncertainty;
  it does not change the sign or the order of magnitude of a slope this close
  to zero.

* 5. The claim: is the farmgate-to-market gap 30-50%?

Farmgate is *source 4* (Malawi has no source 3).  Market is *source 1* or
*source 5*.

*Pooled, no.*  =claim_30_50= is true in only 3 of 80 =models.csv= rows.  The
per-kg gaps are 1.65x-2.73x, i.e. *65% to 173%* -- two to five times the
claimed margin.

*By item, the answer splits cleanly, and the split is the finding.*  Per-kg
basis, all waves pooled within pair:

*Maize -- the staple, the most-traded crop, and by a distance the
best-measured cell -- sits squarely inside 30-50%: 1.33x on 39 cells against
the community price and 1.36x on 7 cells against the purchase unit value.  Two
independent market sources, the same answer.*  So the 30-50% claim is not
wrong; it is *right for the commodity it was presumably formed on, and wrong
for most others*.

*The items at the top of the table are probably not carrying a bigger margin;
they are a different commodity at the two ends.*  Rice at 2.9-3.1x and
groundnut at 5.0x are what *processing and shelling* would look like: the
farmer sells paddy and unshelled groundnut, the household buys milled rice and
shelled nuts, and the milling / shelling yields (about 65% each, i.e. ~1.5x on
weight) account for a good part of the gap before any margin.  The survey
*records* shelled-vs-unshelled -- =ag_i02c= on the sale, =ag_g13c= on the
harvest -- and the library reads *neither* (D2), so the two ends of these gaps
are provably not pinned to the same physical basis.  *An analyst comparing
farmgate and retail for groundnut or rice in Malawi should not use these cells
until the =condition= level exists.*

I checked, and could not close, the obvious competing explanation.  D8 shows
=harmonize_food= folds *=Boiled groundnuts=* -- a vendor snack -- into the
Preferred Label =Groundnut= in 2016-17 and 2019-20, which would make that pair
farmgate-nut-vs-cooked-snack rather than a margin.  *But all six of the
per-kg Groundnut cells come from 2010-11 (5) and 2013-14 (1)*, the waves in
which =Groundnut= is the raw nut, so *D8 does not explain the 5.0x*.  Rice's
33 cells do span all four waves and have no such label problem.  With six
cells I will not adjudicate further: the shelling basis is the live
hypothesis, D8 is a real defect that happens not to bite here, and neither is
demonstrated.

*The rule I applied for exclusions, stated so it is not mistaken for
cherry-picking*: exclude an item from the source-4 pairs only where *no* wave
has the same physical form at both ends -- green leaf is never packeted tea,
cherry is never instant coffee, so Tea and Coffee are out (283 rows).  Where
*some* wave does (rice is rice, groundnut is groundnut, the difference is
milling and shelling state that the survey records and the library drops),
keep the cells and flag them, which is what the paragraph above does.  Maize,
sorghum, millet, beans and pigeon pea are sold and consumed in the same form
and are the trustworthy part of the table.

This is exactly the confound PROTOCOL warned the between-item slope would
conflate, and Malawi cannot separate it: the additive-cost hypothesis and the
item-heterogeneity hypothesis both predict a big log gap on the cheap unshelled
goods, and with 10-41 cells there is no power to tell them apart.  *Phase 2
should test the slope within the "same form at both ends" class* (maize,
sorghum, millet, pulses) rather than across all items.

Sources 3 and 4 cannot be compared to each other in Malawi -- source 3 does not
exist here.  That comparison is Uganda's.

** D1's residual: the defensive collapse, measured not assumed

The commit body for =6c2afa99= says the ratio is a price per the row's unit
"by construction".  That is true of the merge, but =assemble_crop_production=
still ends with a defensive collapse on =(t, i, plot, crop)= (=u= is a column
at wave level) that fires when a plot-crop was harvested in *two* units, and
=.first()= skips NA per column -- so it could in principle re-create exactly
the mislabelling D1 removes, by taking =u= from one row and =Quantity_sold=
from another.

Measured on the raw modules rather than argued about:

*32 plot-crops in 135,410 (0.024%)*, all but four of them in the perennial

one, and "by construction" overstates it by that margin.  Recorded here and

Also from the pre/post comparison: *3 rows changed value rather than
appearing or disappearing* (e.g. a 2010-11 Maize plot going 166.67 -> 750

63-99%)".  Malawi's 77.8% agreement is the worst measured so far.

** Deviations from PROTOCOL, stated

1. *Per-kg basis.*  PROTOCOL prescribes =food_prices(units='kgvalue')= for
   source 1's per-kg view.  I used *one metric-only factor map for all three
   sources* (=Kilogramme= 1, =Gram= 0.001, =50 kg Bag= 50, =90 kg Bag= 90,
   =Litre= 1, =Millilitre= 0.001).  Reason: sources 4 and 5 carry no
   =Expenditure=, so =_get_kg_factors=' price-ratio inference is unavailable to
   them and only metric labels survive; combining that with =kgvalue='s
   region-keyed CSV factors on the source-1 side would put the two sides of
   every gap on different bases.  The uniform map is also *non-circular* --
   no factor is inferred from source 1 -- which is the worry PROTOCOL raises.
   Cost: the per-kg view covers only the metric units.
2. *Source 4 threshold lowered to 5* (PROTOCOL permits it "for a thin source;
   say so").  Source 4 is a household sample, but a thin one.
3. *=u = 'Other (Specify)'= excluded* on all three frames -- see s.1a.
4. *=Tea= and =Coffee= excluded from the source-4 pairs only* (283 rows): the
   harvested form (green leaf, cherry) is not the commodity that shares its
   label in =food_acquired= / =community_prices= (packeted tea, instant
   coffee).  Tea came out at a ratio of 41.3 on 2 cells.  They stay in sources
   1 and 5.
5. *=cells.csv= is restricted to the comparable item universe* -- items present
   in at least two sources in that wave -- rather than every purchased food
   label, to keep the file the analysis universe.  188k rows as it is.
6. *=gaps_by_item.csv=* is an extra output beyond the three PROTOCOL schemas,
   carrying the section-5 per-item table.
