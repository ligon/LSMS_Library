* 0. What pools, and how the basis was recovered

=gap_log= is unit-free, so it pools across countries without conversion; waves
never pool within a country.  Two mechanical problems had to be solved before
any of that could be done, and both are recorded in =pooled_gaps.csv=:

1. *=gaps.csv= carries =u= but no =u_basis=* (=common.GAP_COLS= has none), so
   the per-kg view is identified by the unit label, per the conventions each
   agent converged on: =u == 'kg'= (Ethiopia, Malawi, Tanzania), =u ==
   'per_kg'= (Mali), and no per-kg rows at all in Uganda / GhanaLSS / Nigeria /
   Niger.  =basis_class= normalises ={kg, per_kg} -> kg=.  Nigeria's third
   basis =native_ex_misscaled= is *reconstructed exactly* as =native= minus
   =u in {g, cl}= (346 / 177 / 124 / 108 cells by round, matching its
   =models.csv= to the row); its =kg= basis is the =u == 'Kg'= subset (301 /
   164 / 76 / 55, likewise matching).  Mali's =native_sized= is reconstructed
   as its sized-unit subset (202 cells, matching).
2. *=gaps.csv= carries no thresholds*, and the countries did not use one
   regime.  Each =(country, t, pair, u_basis)= group's row count was matched
   against that group's =models.csv= =n_cells= at =geo_level='all'= to
   identify which regime =gaps.csv= holds; *124 of 124 groups matched exactly*
   (=synthesis_run.log=).  The regimes actually in the pooled file are
   Ethiopia (household side 5, community 1), Tanzania (crop side *10*, not 5),
   and 10 / 5 / 1 elsewhere.  This is a pooling caveat no country listed and
   it is why =threshold_a= / =threshold_b= are columns of =pooled_gaps.csv=.

Four slices exist only in a country's =models.csv= and *not* in
=pooled_gaps.csv=, because the country wrote =gaps.csv= at one regime:
Niger's =kg_def= (13 cells), GhanaLSS's threshold-5 sensitivity rows,
Tanzania's threshold-5 rows, and Nigeria's =kg= rows (the last is derivable,
see above).  Where this synthesis needs them it cites the country's
=models.csv= directly and says so.

** Structural fact 1: source 2 does not exist anywhere in the corpus

*A reported purchase price independent of =Expenditure / Quantity= is not
available in any of the eight countries.*  This is a corpus fact, not a
finding about one country:

- *Seven countries have no =Price= column on purchased rows at all.*
  GhanaLSS measured it: =food_acquired.Price= is populated on *0.0000%* of the
  4,331,168 =s='purchased'= rows, in all seven waves (GhanaLSS §2).  Ethiopia,
  Malawi, Tanzania, Nigeria, Mali and Niger have no =Price= column in
  =food_acquired= at all, so =food_prices(units='unitprice')= returns an empty
  frame (Ethiopia §2, Malawi D6, Tanzania §2, Nigeria N-01, Mali §2, Niger D3).
- *Uganda has one, and it fails the protocol's independence test in the RAW
  Stata files.*  UNPS prints a "Market Price" column (=h15bq12=) beside a
  "Farm gate/producer price" column (=h15bq13=) in the same grid, but
  94.4-99.9% of purchased rows have =h15bq12= *exactly* equal to
  =(h15bq5+h15bq7)/(h15bq4+h15bq6)= (Uganda §2, computed before the library
  touches anything).  Uganda's agent retained the 4,686
  =reported_purchase_price_vs_unit_value= gap rows deliberately so the failure
  is visible in the pooled data: in =pooled_gaps.csv= their median gap is
  *0.0000* and *95.13%* are exactly zero.  They are flagged
  =source2_not_independent_retained_as_evidence= and excluded from every
  statistic below.

Consequence for the library: =transformations.py='s docstring for
=units='unitprice'= ("the survey's own price question") is *false wherever it
returns anything*.  That is Uganda D5, and it is unfiled.

Why each basis: GhanaLSS ships no kg table (GH #770), so its native =Kg= cells
*are* the per-kg view; Ethiopia's three tables share one =u= vocabulary;
Malawi's native cells are matched overwhelmingly on =Kilogramme=; Tanzania's
native basis pools =Kg= and =Piece= in one regression, which is what produces
its -0.789 native slope (Tanzania §4, D3); Nigeria's =g= and =cl= community
rows are package prices, not per-unit prices (N-02); Mali's unsized units
fabricate a +0.15 price-level gradient (Mali §4).

*What it means.*  In *11 of these 16 country-waves the median gap is exactly
0.0000* -- the household's purchase unit value and the independently surveyed
community price are the same number -- and four of the remaining five sit
within ±6% (Nigeria +2.7%, +5.3%, -5.5%; Mali -5.8%).  The fifth, Malawi
2013-14 at 0.91, is *one cell*: the IHPS Module CK list omits every perishable
food, so that wave prices essentially one item.  The
household recalls what it spent; an enumerator visits a named marketplace and
quotes a price.  They share no file, no respondent and no question, and they
agree at the median.  *The community price is therefore a validated yardstick.*
It does **not** follow that the two are interchangeable downstream: §3 shows
they give different farmgate answers, and an earlier draft of this file said
otherwise.  Two measurements can agree on the level of a market price and
still select different cells.

** Four qualifications on the identity, none of which overturn it

1. *The instruments are independent in the file system, and that was checked
   rather than assumed*: no wave's =food_acquired.py= reads a community file,
   =community_prices= is not in =country.py='s =_FOOD_DERIVED=, and
   =transformations.py= never mentions it -- so there is no imputation path in
   either direction (red-team, =indep/findings.md=, all six countries).
2. *Nigeria 2019Q1 is partly agreement BY CONSTRUCTION.*  =nigeria.py='s W4
   comment block says the purchased-quantity denominator was chosen because
   "median ratio 0.95 on matched (t, j, u) cells, vs 1.05 and far noisier when
   q5a is used".  The community price was the criterion, so that round's -5.5%
   is not fully independent corroboration.  The other three Nigeria rounds and
   the other five countries are unaffected.
3. *"No respondent in common" is verified for four countries, not six.*  Malawi's
   Module CK and Nigeria's C8 are *informant*-reported community instruments;
   whether an informant is ever also a sampled household respondent is not
   established here.
4. *The cells being compared are not at the same rung in every country*, and
   the pooled median hides it: Ethiopia 91% and Malawi 88% of their matched
   cells are at =v= (a genuine cluster-grain comparison), but Tanzania is 83%
   District, Nigeria 73% District, GhanaLSS 63% Region and Mali 98% Region.
   *Nigeria's agreement falls as the rung gets finer* -- its tie share is 3.2%
   at =v= against 13.7% at District -- which is the opposite of what a
   cell-level identity predicts, and it is the one country where the finer
   comparison is well populated enough to notice.

The exact zeros are not a pipeline artefact.  Between 5% and 40% of cells are
*byte-identical* on both sides, and each report checked the mechanism
independently: food prices concentrate on round figures and both instruments
record them (GhanaLSS: Egg at =Single= in cluster 70099, 0.60 GHS on both
sides, 11 households vs 3 vendors; Ethiopia: a cluster's median Banana unit
value lands on exp(2.302585) = 10.0 Birr/kg and so does the surveyed price;
Tanzania: the tied cells sit at 1,000 / 1,200 / 2,000 / 2,400 / 5,000 / 6,000
TZS per kg).  Dispersion rules out a duplicated frame: Mali's Kg IQR is 0.37
with 26.9% of cells beyond 1.5x, i.e. the *medians* agree and the individual
observations do not.

*But the tie share itself is weak evidence, and the red-team's measurement is
the reason.*  Re-pairing cells at random within =(t, j, u)= -- destroying any
true cell-level agreement while keeping the price grid -- still produces most
of the observed ties: Ethiopia .165 observed vs .092 random (1.8x), Nigeria
.117/.074 (1.6x), Tanzania .184/.143 (1.3x), Malawi .296/.239 (1.2x), Mali
.216/.180 (1.2x), GhanaLSS .227/.212 (1.1x).  The tie share is largely a
property of a coarse round-number grid.  *The median gap of 0.0000 is the
result; the tie share is colour, not corroboration.*

*Pooled, the identity has no price-level gradient.*  On the native sized
subset -- 9,760 cells, 6 countries, 2,648 country x wave x geography effects --
the median gap is 0.0000, 21.6% of cells are exact ties, and
=coef_between = -0.010 (se 0.012)= (=pooled_models.csv=,
=spec='market_identity'=, =basis='native'=, =unit_scope='sized'=).  That is
zero to two decimal places: the two market measures track each other
one-for-one from the cheapest food to the dearest.  On the kg basis (16,540
cells, 3 countries) it is -0.085 (0.011) -- small, but the kg basis is where a unit-coding
miscode is mechanically largest in logs on cheap, small-unit goods (GhanaLSS
D2 names exactly this confound for its own -0.167).  *Both pooled figures are
dominated by one country*: the native-sized 9,760 cells are 76.4% Ethiopia
(then Malawi 11.3%, Nigeria 7.4%, Mali 2.1%, GhanaLSS 1.6%, Tanzania 1.2%) and
the kg-sized 16,540 are 90.5% Ethiopia.  Read the per-country-wave table above,
not the pooled slope, as the result.  The kg basis here is
Ethiopia + Malawi + Tanzania only: *Mali's per-kg rows are flagged out*,
because Mali's own report disowns them (=_get_kg_factors= returns =Sac moyen=
-> 26.2 kg where the label says 50 and =Gramme= -> 0.708 where it should be
0.001, M3) -- see §7.

*the native basis is the marketing margin proper; the per-kg basis includes the
bulk-breaking premium.*

** The between slope: pooled, within class, and whether it is identified

The pooled fit uses =common.fit_gap_model= *unmodified*, with =geo= rewritten
to =country|t|geo_level|geo= (country x wave x geography effects, as the spec
prescribes) and =j= rewritten to =country|t|j=.  *The item key must be
country-and-wave specific, and this is load-bearing rather than cosmetic*:
=between= regresses the gap on the item's mean midpoint, a log *price level*,
and pooled raw a UGX level and an XOF level are not comparable while a label

wave, the FWL projection absorbs any country-wave-level constant from both =y=
and =x=, so the currency scale cancels exactly.  All fits are on *sized* units.

*Deaton & Zaidi, in one paragraph.*  Deaton, A. and S. Zaidi (2002),
/Guidelines for Constructing Consumption Aggregates for Welfare Analysis/,
LSMS Working Paper 135, World Bank -- §on the valuation of home-produced
consumption -- treat the choice as between two defensible prices: the producer
(farmgate) price, which is what the household forgoes, and the local market
(purchase) price, which is what the consumption is worth to it; and they warn
that valuing own-consumption at market prices inflates the aggregate relative
to purchases by the marketing margin.  The questionnaires quoted in §1 ask for
the farmgate one -- that is a fact about the instruments, not a claim about
what Deaton and Zaidi caused.  The corpus says the choice is not the analyst's
to make: *the respondents have already made it, and they answered with
something at or near the market price.*  The practical consequence is concrete
and holds under either reading of *why*: **in Uganda, own-consumption is valued
about 23% above what a sale actually fetched (3 vs 4 = 1.234 on the 223-cell
transitive set), so a consumption aggregate built on =food_acquired.Expenditure=
for =s='produced'= rows carries part of a marketing margin the questionnaire
intended to exclude.**  *That 23% is Uganda-only*: GhanaLSS has no source 4, so
no comparable number exists for it, and an earlier draft wrongly wrote "in
Uganda or GhanaLSS".

** Caveats that apply to every number above

- *The standard errors are HC3, and PROTOCOL.org says HC1.*  =common.py='s
  =_fwl_slope= passes =cov_type="HC3"= to =metrics_miscellany.estimators.ols=,
  so every country's =models.csv= and every row of =pooled_models.csv= carries
  HC3, while PROTOCOL.org §Statistics says "HC1 standard errors
  (unclustered)".  The code is what ran; the protocol text is being corrected
  by the coordinator.  HC3 is the more conservative of the two in small
  samples, so no interval here is narrower than the protocol intended.
- *They are unclustered*, on the pooled fits as on the country ones.  Cells within a country-wave-district share households and share a
  single community-price measurement; the true intervals are wider than
  printed.  No conclusion here rests on a slope being two standard errors from
  zero -- the conclusions rest on slopes being *near* zero and on medians.
- *=within= is not read anywhere.*  For every pair with source 5 or source 4 at
  cluster grain the protocol's own synthetic shows the thin-=b= pathology
  drives it to -1.27 under a pure markup, and the countries' estimates
  (-0.90 to -1.72) sit squarely in that range.  It is reported in
  =pooled_models.csv= and is not evidence.
- *=n_items= is large under the =country|t|j= key and =within= is mostly NaN.*
  Expected, and not a loss.
- *The cold-build caveat.*  Every country's analysis read the shared warm cache;
  none of the guards on the build path (=_assert_built_required_columns=, the
  =dfs:= required-column check, the grain audit) is exercised by a warm read.
