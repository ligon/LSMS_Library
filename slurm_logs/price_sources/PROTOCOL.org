#+title: Protocol: five sources of food prices, compared
#+author: Sue (Claude Fable 5.1), scrum master
#+date: 2026-09-08

Every country agent follows this protocol so that the per-country outputs
pool into one cross-country analysis.  Where your country departs from it,
say so in the report, do not improvise silently.  Shared code:
=slurm_logs/price_sources/common.py= (use it; if it is wrong, report the
defect rather than forking it).  Ledger: =.coder/ledger/price-sources-comparison.md=.

* The question
Food expenditure is quantity times price, but /which/ price?  A household
that eats its own harvest gave up the farmgate price; a household that
buys paid a market price; the two differ by a marketing margin that the
literature puts at 30--50%.  We have up to five price observations for the
same food, place and time.  Are their /levels/ similar?  In /logs/, is the
gap between two sources a constant (a proportional markup, or iceberg
transport cost, both of which are additive in logs and independent of the
price level) or does it fall with the price level (an additive per-kg
transport or handling cost)?  And is the 30--50% claim borne out?

* Grain and geography
- Cell keys: =(t, j, u, geo_level, geo)=.  Native unit =u= is a key: **match
  sources within the same native unit first** (Kg where both have it).
  The per-kg view is a second, flagged view, because the library's kg
  factors are /inferred from/ =Expenditure/Quantity= ratios
  (=_get_kg_factors=) -- a per-kg comparison against source 1 partly
  compares it with itself.
- Geography ladder (the repo's existing procedure, =median_price_valuation=):
  cluster =v= first, then =District= (where =cluster_features= has it), then
  =Region=, then national.  A cell is /usable/ when it has at least the
  source's threshold of observations -- and the threshold is PER SOURCE,
  because the sources differ in kind: a household-side source (1, 2, 3, 4)
  is a sample statistic and needs 10 observations (lowerable to 5 for a thin
  source; say so); a community price (5) is a /measurement/ -- one surveyed
  price per cluster and item -- and is usable at 1.  A symmetric 10 would
  push every community-price pair up to Region and lose the cluster-grain
  comparison this exercise is about.  =common.match_gaps(threshold_a=,
  threshold_b=)= records both in =models.csv=; =common.model_rows= writes
  one pooled row and one row per =geo_level=, because =finest_only= mixes
  cluster ids, districts and regions in one geography effect and the
  per-rung rows show whether the answer depends on the rung.
- **Two checks BEFORE any matching, in section 1 of the report**, because
  their failure otherwise surfaces as "zero matched cells": (i) the unit
  vocabulary across tables -- =community_prices= says =Kilogramme= /
  =Gramme=, does your =food_acquired= say =Kg=?  Tabulate both =u= sets and
  their intersection; a mismatch is a defect to report and an obvious
  blocker you may fix (a categorical mapping, not a rename in your script).
  (ii) the share of =community_prices.v= present in =cluster_features.v=
  (and in =sample().v=) per wave, so the ladder's bottom rung is real.
- Geography columns come from =cluster_features= joined on =(t, v)=
  (=common.attach_geo=).  Households whose =v= is missing from
  =cluster_features= are counted and reported, never dropped silently.
- One wave at a time within a country; waves are separate rows, never
  pooled, because item lists and universes change.

* Statistics (all produced by =common.py=)
1. Per source and cell: =n=, median log price, its SD (=sd_log_price=), median price, currency
   (from =food_prices(currency='column')= for the wave).
2. Per matched pair of sources: =gap_log = median_log_a - median_log_b=,
   =ratio = exp(gap_log)=.  Orientation: =pair= is "a_vs_b" and a is the
   /downstream/ price (market side) so a marketing margin is positive:
   =unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price=, =community_price_vs_crop_sale_price=,
   =unit_value_vs_own_consumption_valuation=, =unit_value_vs_community_price=,
   =reported_purchase_price_vs_unit_value=, =own_consumption_valuation_vs_crop_sale_price=.
3. Per (country, wave, pair, u-basis): median and IQR of =gap_log=; share
   of cells with abs(gap) > log(1.5); and two Frisch-Waugh-Lovell
   regressions (the fixed effects partialled out exactly with a dummy
   matrix, as =Metrics_Miscellany.fwl_regression= does; vendored in
   =common.py=, numpy only):
   - =within=: =gap_log ~ item FE + geo FE + beta_w * midpoint=, where the
     regressor is the Bland-Altman midpoint =(log p_a + log p_b)/2= of the
     cell -- NOT =log p_b=: noise in b sits in both the gap and =log p_b=,
     and a pure markup then produces a spurious negative slope (measured
     on synthetic data: -0.11, se 0.03).
   - =between=: =gap_log ~ geo FE + beta_b * item_mean_midpoint= -- item
     effects are dropped because an additive per-unit cost shows up
     chiefly across items (a big log gap on cheap goods, a small one on
     dear goods), which item effects would absorb.
   A constant markup or iceberg transport cost predicts both betas = 0; an
   additive per-unit cost predicts both < 0, the between one strongly.
   **=between= is the primary test.**  The midpoint trick cancels shared
   noise only when the two sources are equally noisy; with unequal noise
   the =within= slope has the sign of var(noise_a) - var(noise_b) even
   under a pure markup (synthetic, a slightly noisier: +0.08, se 0.03; a
   THIN b of two observations per cell: **-1.27, se 0.07**, while =between=
   stays at -0.002).  So for any pair in which one side is a measurement
   source matched at cluster grain (source 5 at =v=, source 4 at =v=)
   =within= is not interpretable: report it, but READ only =between= for
   those pairs.  =within= is meaningful where both sides are household
   samples with >= 10 observations per cell (e.g. 1 vs 3 at Region).
   Say which source is noisier from =sd_log_price= in =cells.csv=.
   Also reported: the R^2 of item + geo effects alone (how much of the gap
   is "item and place"), the residual SD (how tight the relation is), HC3
   standard errors from =metrics_miscellany.estimators.ols= on the
   residualised regression (unclustered -- say so if it matters; an earlier
   draft said HC1, corrected 2026-09-08).  =claim_30_50=
   is whether the median gap lies in [log 1.3, log 1.5] = [0.262, 0.405].
4. Say what each number means in the report in one sentence each; do not
   paste tables without reading them.

* Outputs (exact paths and schemas; =common.write_outputs= enforces them)
- =slurm_logs/price_sources/<Country>/cells.csv= -- =common.CELL_COLS=
- =slurm_logs/price_sources/<Country>/gaps.csv=  -- =common.GAP_COLS=
- =slurm_logs/price_sources/<Country>/models.csv= -- =common.MODEL_COLS=
- =slurm_logs/price_sources/<Country>/REPORT.org= -- sections in this order:
  1. Sources present, with the idiosyncrasies you NAMED from
     =CONTENTS.org= and the questionnaire (quote wording; cite file and
     page or question number).
  2. Independence test for source 2 (or "not present").
  3. Levels: for each pair, median ratio per wave and the spread.
  4. Logs: the model table, read out in words.
  5. The claim: does the farmgate-vs-market gap sit in 30--50%?  Which
     farmgate (3 or 4), which market (1 or 5)?  **Report the gap by item**
     (a table: item, n cells, median gap, ratio) and name which items sit
     in 30--50% and which do not -- the between-item slope conflates an
     additive cost with item-heterogeneous margins (perishables carry
     higher margins than grains at any price), and only the per-item table
     lets Phase 2 test the slope within item classes.  This is where
     "critically evaluate" lives.  Where 3 and 4 both exist (Uganda),
     compare them to each other: does a household value its own-consumed
     harvest at the sale price, the purchase price, or somewhere between?
  6. Data problems, as a table (id, table, wave, symptom, count,
     evidence =path:line= or command, proposed fix, status
     fixed-in-worktree / reported).  Counts, not impressions.
  7. Reproduce: the exact commands.
- =slurm_logs/price_sources/<Country>/analysis.py= -- the script that made
  the CSVs, =__main__=-guarded, never named =*_test.py=.

* Fixes: obvious blockers only, in your worktree
You MAY fix an obvious blocker that stops a comparison -- a =j= label that
does not join, a unit code that does not decode, a =NumberOfUnits= basis
not applied -- when the fix is CONFIG (a country =_/*.org= table,
=data_info.yml= mapping, a wave script's label map) or a small edit to that
country's own module/scripts.  Rules:
- Work on branch =fix/prices-<country>= in your worktree; commit with the
  standard trailer; do NOT push; list the commits in the report.
- Verify a fix with =LSMS_COUNTRIES_ROOT=<worktree>/lsms_library/countries=
  and a PRIVATE =LSMS_DATA_DIR= (see your brief) so the shared cache is not
  rebuilt under a hash the main checkout does not have.  Analysis reads
  before any fix may use the shared cache.
- NEVER edit =lsms_library/country.py=, =transformations.py=,
  =local_tools.py=, =feature.py=, =data_info.yml='s required-column
  declarations, any test, =pyproject.toml=, or another country's files.
  If the blocker needs that, STOP on that point, record it under Data
  problems as "reported", and continue the analysis without it.
- A fix changes what the analysis sees: say which numbers were produced
  after the fix.
