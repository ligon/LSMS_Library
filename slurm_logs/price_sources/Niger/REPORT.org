*What these mean, one sentence each.*  The native-basis ratio is how much
more a buyer pays for one *bowl (Tiya) or kilogram* of a food in a
cluster/district than a farmer received for a bowl or kilogram of the same
crop sold there: 6% to 25%.  The kg_def ratio is the same comparison after
converting both sides to kilograms using only exact, name-derived factors:
44% to 84%.  The IQR is the spread of that gap across cells -- 0.30 to
0.53 in logs, i.e. the middle half of cells spans a factor of ~1.4-1.7,
which is as large as the gap itself.  =share_abs_gap_gt_log1p5= is the
fraction of cells where the two sources disagree by more than 50% in
either direction: a quarter to a third of them, so the *median* is doing
real work and individual cells should not be trusted.

*The two bases disagree systematically, and the reason is economic, not
technical.*  =gaps.csv= shows the native matches are almost entirely one
unit -- =Tiya= carries 22 of 24 cells in 2011-12, 29 of 30 in 2018-19 and
22 of 23 in 2021-22 -- while the kg_def matches are dominated by
=Sac de 50/100 kg= on the crop side against =Gramme=/=Kg= on the market
side.  A native-unit match compares *the same container on both sides*; a
per-kg match compares a *wholesale sack sold at the farm* with a *retail
gram or kilo bought at the market*, so it contains a bulk-breaking margin
on top of the marketing margin.  That is why the per-kg gap (1.44-1.84) is
roughly double the native gap (1.06-1.25).  Anyone quoting a single Niger
"marketing margin" must say which basis they mean.

*The gap widens up the geography ladder*, which is what a spatial
arbitrage story predicts.  From =gaps_by_rung.csv=, 2011-12 community vs
crop sale, native basis: =v= 1.20 (21 cells) -> =District= 1.27 (10) ->
=Region= 1.43 (9) -> =national= 1.67 (5).  The 2018-19 pair does the same
more weakly (=District= 1.06 -> =Region= 1.12 -> =national= 1.21).  Two
prices compared within a cluster differ least; the further apart the
market and the farm can be, the larger the wedge.

*2014-15 is unusable* and should be read as missing, not as a negative
ratio.  It has two matched cells, one of which (=District= =Dogondoutchi=,
=Niebe/Haricots secs=, =Tiya=) is corrupted by the gram-weight defect D5:
a community row records =Price=600= for =Quantity=2687= on unit =Tiya=,
i.e. 0.22 FCFA per bowl.  Dropping the 111 corrupted rows corpus-wide
moves that wave's median gap from -1.969 to -0.515 and leaves 2011-12
*exactly unchanged* at +0.223 -- so the defect is confined and the other
three waves are unaffected.

*Which source is noisier* (median =sd_log_price= over cells with n>=5, from
=cells.csv=): =unit_value= 0.84 / 0.82 (2018-19 / 2021-22),
=community_price= 0.22 / 0.47 (2011-12 / 2014-15), =crop_sale_price= 0.30

* 4. Logs: the model table, read out

*Every one of the 25 =models.csv= rows reports NaN for =coef_within=,
=se_within=, =coef_between=, =se_between=, =r2_fe= and =resid_sd=.*  This
is =common.fit_gap_model='s =MIN_DOF = 5= guard (commit 58088820) firing,
and it is firing correctly: Niger's matched cells never leave five
residual degrees of freedom after the item and geography effects.

The arithmetic is in =gaps_by_rung.csv='s =resid_dof= column
(=n_cells - n_items - n_geos - 1=), which is *negative or zero in 22 of
its 23 rows*.  The worst-case examples: 2011-12 at =v=, 21 cells over 2
items and 21 geographies (dof -3); 2018-19 at =District=, 26 cells over 2
items and 24 geographies (dof -1).  The single best rung is 2011-12 at
=Region= -- 9 cells, 2 items, 5 geographies, dof +1 -- still four short.

*The cause is structural and worth stating for Phase 2.*  Source 4 yields
only 1,281 / 497 / 3,005 / 896 priced rows per wave after the sold-unit
restriction, spread over 35 crops, 6-9 units and 115-338 clusters.  A cell
must clear 5 observations for a household-side source, so the cells that
survive are few and land in *different* geographies -- the geography
effect then has almost one level per observation.  Niger cannot identify
a Bland-Altman slope without either a coarser geography (which the
protocol's ladder deliberately avoids) or many more sale observations.

*So for Niger, read the medians and the by-item table, as the protocol
says.*  A prior run of this script against the pre-58088820 =common.py=
"fitted" these designs and returned =r2_fe = 1.000000=,
=resid_sd ~ 1e-15= and =coef_within = -7.6 (se 6e-13)= -- saturated
nonsense.  Those numbers appear nowhere in the delivered outputs; every
number in this report was produced against the current =common.py=.
Niger contributes *no evidence either way* on constant-markup versus
additive-cost.

* 5. The claim: is the farmgate-to-market gap 30-50%?

*Which farmgate, which market.*  Niger has no source 3, so the farmgate
side is always source 4, the crop *sale receipt* -- a price actually
received, not a valuation.  The market side is source 5 (a surveyed
CONSUMER price, quoted in section 1) in the ECVMA waves and source 1 (the
household purchase unit value) in the EHCVM waves.  The two market-side
sources are not interchangeable: source 5 is one enumerator observation
per cluster and item, source 1 is a median over many households' purchases.

*Answer: it depends on the basis, and neither answer is comfortably inside
the band.*

- On the *native-unit* basis -- like container against like container --
  the gap is *6% to 25%*, i.e. *below* the 30-50% claim in all three
  usable waves.  =claim_30_50= is False for every native row.
- On the *per-kg* basis it is *44% to 84%*, i.e. at or *above* the top of
  the band.  Only one row (2018-19 kg_def, +0.365, a single cell) lands
  inside it.

The honest reading is that the 30-50% figure sits *between* Niger's two
measurements, and the gap between them is the bulk-breaking margin
(section 3), not measurement error.
