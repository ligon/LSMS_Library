*Source 3's question wording, quoted, decides what the pair means.*  GLSS
Section 8H, column 14 (GLSS3, =1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3QPartB.pdf=,
Section 8H block; column 13 is =ENTER UNIT THAT THE RESPONDENT CHOOSES / SEE
LIST OF UNIT CODES=):

#+begin_quote
For how much could you sell one unit of......now?
#+end_quote

GLSS6 (=2012-13/Documentation/QUESTIONNAIRES/GLSS6 Part B Questionnaire.pdf=)
and GLSS7 (=2016-17/Documentation/GLSS7_MODULE B _14_10_16.docx=, Section 8H
Q9) both read:

#+begin_quote
For how much would you sell one unit of ..............now?
USE MOST OCCURING UNIT OF MEASURE
#+end_quote

So source 3 is a *farmgate / seller-side valuation* ("would you sell"), not
"what would it cost to buy".  The pair =community_price vs
own_consumption_valuation= is therefore a genuine marketing-margin test.
*The same sentence also explains pre-check (i)*: the unit is the one the
RESPONDENT chooses, while the price survey weighs the item.

*Source 5's basis, quoted.*  The GLSS3 price questionnaire
(=1991-92/Documentation/pdf/G3QPrice.pdf=, section =I. FOOD PRICES=) is
=CODE | ITEM | 1ST OBSERVATION (KG, PRICE) | 2ND ... | 3RD ...=; GLSS7's
(=GLSS7_price questionnaire.xlsx=) is =PRICE | QTY | UOM= per observation.
So the reported price refers to a stated quantity, and the per-unit price is
=Price / NumberOfUnits=.  Verified across waves on maize at =u='Kg'=:
1987-88 71.4, 1988-89 62.2, 1991-92 100, 1998-99 534 (GHC/kg); 2012-13 0.909,
2016-17 1.32 (GHS/kg) -- a consistent series through the 2007
redenomination.  This settles the =CONTENTS.org= note that GLSS3/GLSS4
distribute only =p = PRICE/KG=: their =NumberOfUnits= is the form's stated
basis (1.0 for weighed foods, 0.17 for the evaporated-milk tin, 9.0 for
firewood), so dividing does not double-count.

*Currency.*  =currency='column'= gives GHC for 1987-88 ... 2005-06 and GHS for
2012-13 and 2016-17 (the 2007 redenomination, 1 GHS = 10,000 GHC).  *Every
comparison in this report is WITHIN a wave, so the redenomination is
irrelevant to every gap reported here* -- a log gap is unit-free, and no cell
pools two waves.  The =currency= column in =cells.csv= carries the code so a
cross-wave levels table stays possible.

** 1.2 Idiosyncrasies NAMED from =CONTENTS.org= (3,185 lines, Traps 1-9)

1. *Trap 6 / GH #753 -- verification hazard.*  The wave =mapping.py= modules
   build their search path from =files('lsms_library')=, the IMPORTED
   package, so =LSMS_COUNTRIES_ROOT= alone silently decodes against the main
   checkout.  Handled: every run here sets =PYTHONPATH= to the worktree from
   a neutral cwd and asserts ='wt-prices-ghanalss' in lsms_library.__file__=.
   The fix below is verified the same way.
2. *Trap 9 -- a duplicate index in =food_acquired= silently REWRITES =Price=
   table-wide.*  =food_acquired= is in =_ADDITIVE_MEASURE_COLUMNS=, so a
   non-unique index makes core SUM Quantity/Expenditure and re-derive
   =Price = Expenditure/Quantity= *on the whole frame* -- destroying 839,677
   recorded farmgate prices and fabricating a price on ~4.33M purchased rows,
   with the grain audit correctly silent.  This is why the unit fix below was
   *measured for duplicates before being made* (0 rows in duplicate groups).
3. *Trap 8 / GH #782* -- 2005-06's =j= shipped the survey's own labels for
   years because =convert_categoricals=True= turned the code into a label and
   =.replace()= passes an unmatched value through.  The lesson used here: the
   =j= and =u= axis checks in section 1.3/1.4 are run against the *BUILT*
   table, never against the config.
4. *=harmonize_price_item= and =harmonize_food= are ONE vocabulary* -- a
   price item flagged =Food: yes= is asserted to sit on the wave's
   =harmonize_food= axis.  That is why the "166 of 662" label gap is not what
   it looks like (section 1.4).
5. *2005-06 is =asked-not-distributed=* for the price survey (=G5QPrice.pdf=
   exists, 9 pp; no price file in the DDI inventory or the tree).  It is the
   one wave with source 3 and no source 5.
6. *GhanaLSS ships NO kg conversion table* (=conversion_to_kgs.json --
   intentionally NOT used=), so every kg factor it has is *inferred from
   =Expenditure/Quantity== -- which exists only in 2016-17.  See "Scope
   deviations" for what that costs.
7. *2012-13 prices are stamped per EA but collected per market*: 1,015 EAs,
   323 =(region, district, market)= keys, ~41 markets; EAs sharing a key carry
   identical prices.  So =n_geos= at the =v= rung overstates the number of
   independent price measurements in that wave, and the unclustered HC3
   standard errors on its rows are optimistic.
8. *=obs= is a fourth index level* -- three vendor observations per item, in
   every wave.  Source 5's =n= per cell is a count of vendor observations,
   not of households; that is exactly why its threshold is 1 and not 10.

What each number means:

- =coef_between= is the *primary* test: the slope of the log gap on the
  item's mean Bland--Altman midpoint after geography effects.  Zero means a
  proportional markup or an iceberg cost; negative means an additive
  per-unit cost (a fixed handling charge is a big log gap on a cheap good).
- =coef_within= adds item effects and uses the cell midpoint.  *It is not
  interpretable for any pair with source 5 on one side*, because a community
  price is 1--3 vendor observations and the protocol measured (on synthetic
  data) that a thin side drives the within slope to -1.27 under a pure
  markup.  The first draft of this report had to say that of 1991-92's
  =coef_within = -2.33=; under the corrected =common.py= that row is NaN
  because 31 cells cannot support 16 item plus 19 geography effects at all.
- =r2_fe= is the share of gap variance item and geography effects explain
  alone; =resid_sd= the residual SD of the within specification.
- Standard errors are HC3 on the residualised regression, *unclustered*.  For
  2012-13 that matters: its =v=-rung cells reuse ~41 markets' prices across
  1,015 EAs (idiosyncrasy 7), so the effective number of independent price
  measurements is far below =n_geos=.

*Where the slopes are NaN, read the medians and the by-item table* (sections
3 and 5), as the protocol says.  1998-99 has 22 cells against 5 items and 21
geographies and now reports NaN throughout; before the guard it "fitted"
=coef_between = -13.66= with =se = 1.8e-14= and =r2_fe = 1.000000= -- a
rank-deficient design, not a slope.  The same is true of the per-rung rows
1991-92 =v= (12 cells / 11 geos), 2016-17 5v3 =v= (1 cell) and every
=national= row with fewer than 20 items.

*The one well-identified row is 2016-17 =unit_value_vs_community_price=*:
516 cells, 105 items, 94 geographies, =coef_between = -0.167 (se 0.045)= --
significantly negative, and it stays negative at every rung except =v=
(Region -0.230 (0.051), national -0.131 (0.048), =v= +0.039 (0.048)), and at
the looser threshold (808 cells, -0.205 (0.039)).  Taken at face value this
says the gap between a household's purchase unit value and the surveyed
market price *falls with the price level*, i.e. an additive per-unit
component.  But both sides are market prices whose median gap is exactly
zero, so what the slope is more likely picking up is the unit-coding noise of
D2, which is mechanically larger in log terms on cheap, small-unit goods (a
"1 gram" or "1 ml" miscode).  *We do not claim an additive transport cost
from this number*; we report it and name the confound.  The sign also
reverses at the finest rung, which a real additive cost would not do.

For 2016-17 =unit_value_vs_own_consumption_valuation= -- the only pair where

unit_value 0.310, own_consumption_valuation 0.292, community_price 0.298).
The honest reading of this pair is =between=: *no detectable relation between
the gap and the price level*, on a median gap of zero.

* 5. The claim: is the farmgate-vs-market gap 30--50%?

*No, not in GhanaLSS, in any wave, on any pairing.*  =claim_30_50= is
=False= on every one of the 24 model rows: the median gap never lands in
[log 1.3, log 1.5] = [0.262, 0.405].

Which farmgate, which market?  GhanaLSS has *no source 4* (no
=crop_production=), so the farmgate side is source 3 -- the household's own
"for how much could you sell one unit" -- and the market side is source 5
(the surveyed vendor price) or source 1 (what households actually paid).

| pairing | waves | median ratio | verdict |
|---------+-------+--------------+---------|
| market survey / own-consumption valuation (5 vs 3) | 1991-92, 1998-99, 2012-13, 2016-17 | 1.05, 2.09, 0.87, 1.20 | no stable margin; two waves below 1 |
| household purchase price / own-consumption valuation (1 vs 3) | 2016-17 | 1.000 | *no margin at all* |

The 1-vs-3 result is the sharpest and it is not noise: 322 cells, and the
median household values a unit of its own produce at *exactly* what its
neighbours paid for the same item in the same unit in the same cluster.  Ask
a Ghanaian household what it could sell a bowl of maize for and it answers
with the price of a bowl of maize.  Whatever the true farmgate--retail wedge
is, *this instrument does not measure it*: it measures the respondent's
knowledge of the local retail price.  That is a caution for anyone valuing
own-consumption in GhanaLSS at =food_acquired.Price= and calling it a
producer price.

*Both sides are sane and the spread is real.*  It is not a units mis-code:
the mechanism is the *size of the lot the price survey weighed*.  The
enumerator buys a retail portion, and a retail portion of pepper is 110
grams while a retail portion of yam is 2.85 kg.  The per-kg price falls with
the lot -- an ordinary quantity discount -- so the market/farmgate ratio
orders itself by *perishability and lot size*, not by price level.  That is
precisely the item heterogeneity the protocol warns will be confounded with
an additive per-unit cost by a between-item slope, and it is why 2012-13's
=coef_between = +0.435 (0.232)= should not be read as economics.

* 8. Scope deviations

1. *A pair the protocol does not list.*
   =community_price_vs_own_consumption_valuation= (a = community price, the
   market side; b = own-consumption valuation).  GhanaLSS's source 1 exists
   in one wave and it has no source 4, so without this pair the country
   contributes no farmgate-vs-market comparison at all.  Orientation follows
   the protocol's own rule.  *No other country in the sweep has both 3 and 5*
   (Uganda has 3 without 5; Ethiopia / Malawi / Tanzania / Nigeria / Mali /
   Niger have 5 without 3), so these rows pool with nothing and should be
   read as GhanaLSS-only.
2. *No per-kg view.*  The protocol asks for a flagged per-kg second view.
   GhanaLSS ships no kg conversion table and =_get_kg_factors= infers factors
   from =Expenditure/Quantity=, which exists only in 2016-17 (GH #770; six of
   seven waves' =food_prices(units='kgvalue')= are correctly EMPTY).  A per-kg
   view would therefore exist for one wave and would be doubly circular
   there.  Native-unit matching is the whole analysis; where both sides carry
   =Kg= (which is 100% of the 2012-13 matched cells and 77% of 1991-92's) the
   native view already IS the per-kg view.
3. *A screen on source 5 in 2016-17* (D1): keep an observation when =u= is a
   metric mass/volume unit or =NumberOfUnits == 1=.  Constant-free,
   wave-scoped, and counted (5,351 of 220,908 rows).  Without it a single
   content-weight row moves a cell's log gap by 8.  *Constant-free is not
   lossless*: ~1,095 =Piece= / =Single= rows whose count is probably genuine
   (a crate of 24 eggs) are dropped with it, conservatively.  The rest are
   non-food.
4. *No local statistics.*  The =common.py= defect this country hit (D5)
   was reported, briefly worked around, and then FIXED in the shared module
   by the coordinator; the wrapper has been removed and every number in
   sections 3--5 comes from =common.cell_medians= / =common.match_gaps= /
   =common.model_rows= unmodified.
5. =cells.csv= is 39 MB (355,688 cells, dominated by 189,215 community-price
   cells at three ladder rungs).  That is the honest inventory at the
   protocol's schema; say so if the pooling step needs it trimmed.
