   2 and 3 are absent for an evidenced reason, not a wiring gap -- see §2.
2. *Only two of six waves can be compared.*  =crop_production= and
   =community_prices= exist for 2019-20 and 2020-21 ONLY.  Source-1 cells are
   emitted for all six waves; every matched pair is 2019-20 or 2020-21.  The
   four 2008-15 rounds are separate waves throughout and are never pooled
   (=wave_folder_map=; =.claude/skills/multi-round-waves.md=).
3. *The protocol's source-5 geography does not exist in Tanzania and had to be
   built from the repo's own crosswalk.*  Pre-check (ii) returns *0.0%* in
   both waves: =community_prices.v= is the community questionnaire's
   =interview__key= and shares nothing with =sample().v=.  I used the
   =community_cluster_xwalk= feature (Tanzania's own, GH #113) to reach the
   survey cluster.  §1 has the rates.
4. *=crop_production= DOES have =u=* -- as a *column*, not an index level.  The
   brief said "no =u= level", which is true of the index and misleading about
   the data: =u= is ='Kg'= on *all 2,885* sale rows.  Source 4 therefore
   matches natively on Kg and no unit assumption had to be invented; the
   questionnaire confirms it (§1).
5. *The =u= vocabularies do not match and I case-folded them in my script*
   rather than fixing the config: =food_acquired.u= is ={Kg, piece}=,
   =community_prices.u= is ={Gram, Kg, Litre, Millilitre, Piece}=.  Tanzania's
   =categorical_mapping.org= explicitly *forbids* registering
   ='piece' -> 'Piece'=, so this is NOT an obvious blocker I may fix.
   Reported (D3).
6. *=common.CELL_COLS= / =GAP_COLS= carry no =u_basis= column* (only
   =MODEL_COLS= does), so per-kg rows are written with =u='kg'= (lowercase)
   and native-kilogram rows with =u='Kg'=.  Reported to the coordinator
   rather than forked, per the protocol.  *Consequence to expect, not to file
   as a bug*: in =cells.csv= the source-1 and source-4 rows appear twice, once
   under =u='Kg'= and once under =u='kg'=, with identical content -- those two
   sources are already per kilogram, so the kg view is the identity on them.
   Only source 5 differs between the bases.  *Any statistic taken from
   =cells.csv= or =gaps.csv= must pick a basis first* (=analysis.split_basis=);
   pooling them double-counts.
7. *Most model rows are non-identified* -- the fixed-effect block saturates on
   9-39 matched cells.  These outputs were regenerated against the
   =common.py= revision of 2026-09-08 (the coordinator's =MIN_DOF= guard,
   prompted by Nigeria), which reports NaN rather than a number below 5
   residual degrees of freedom: *80 of 97 rows have no =coef_within=* and *50
   have neither slope*.  I did not wrap or fork =fit_gap_model=; my

** The idiosyncrasies I hit, NAMED (from =Tanzania/_/CONTENTS.org=, 1,719 lines)

1. *=community_prices.v= is not a cluster id and never was.*  It is
   =interview__key=, a Survey-Solutions *per-interview* token.  CONTENTS.org
   §"Why the community-price <-> household link cannot be made cleanly"
   documents zero overlap with =sample().v=; my pre-check (ii) reproduces it
   exactly: *0.0%* of 94 (2019-20) and 484 (2020-21) community =v= appear in
   =sample().v= or =cluster_features.v=.  Without the crosswalk, source 5 has
   *no geography at all* and the whole 1-vs-5 and 5-vs-4 comparison is
   impossible -- this is the single fact that decides whether Tanzania can
   take part in this study.
2. *The crosswalk exists, and it is asymmetric between the two waves.*
   =community_cluster_xwalk= (grain =(t, v)=) resolves the community to a real
   =sample().v= for *51 of 99* communities in 2019-20 (=(region, ward)= +
   interview-date overlap) and *458 of 488* in 2020-21 (deterministic: the
   survey cluster is baked into the community admin codes, =(RR,WWW,EE,CCC)=).
   Measured on the priced rows: 1,417 of 2,769 (2019-20) and 12,280 of 13,062
   (2020-21) reach a cluster; the remaining 2,134 rows fall back to region.
   *Every resolved cluster is a real =cluster_features= row* (0 missing), which
   I verified rather than assumed.  2019-20 is structurally capped -- 37
   communities sit in wards with no surviving baseline-pinned household -- so
   its bottom rung is genuinely thinner, not badly built.
3. *2019-20 and 2020-21 are DIFFERENT sub-panels with different universes.*
   2019-20 follows the Extended Panel only (989 targeted households, 1,184
   realised); 2020-21 follows the Refresh Panel *plus a 545-household urban
   booster* drawn in Mbeya, Arusha, Mwanza, Tanga and Dodoma -- 11.6% of that
   wave is a purpose-built urban oversample.  The two waves have *zero
   household overlap by design*.  I therefore report every number per wave and
   never pool them; where the two waves disagree (they mostly do not), read it
   as two populations, not as sampling noise.
4. *=cluster_features= geography is a household attribute projected onto a
   cluster, in a tracking panel.*  The GH #323 Site-2 fix cut contested cells
   from 2,106 to 385, but the collapse still destroys 2,687 rows corpus-wide,
   and my run raised =GrainCollapseWarning= for five of six waves (605 /
   1,304 / 404 / 197 / 177 rows).  District is the rung most affected; Region
   is "essentially solved" (706 contested clusters -> 3).  I normalise both
   with =tanzania.normalize_place= (§ below) and lean on Region where the two
   disagree.
5. *=2008-15/= is one folder, four rounds.*  Handled by the framework via
   =wave_folder_map=; my cells are per =t= and the four rounds are four rows
   everywhere.  Related and NOT ignored: the =UPHI= / =r_hhid= back-casting
   means a 2008-15 household count is a Round-4-panel count, so source-1
   =n= for those waves counts *panel* households, not interviewed ones.  It
   does not bias a median price, but it makes =n= incomparable to 2019-20 /
   2020-21 =n=.
6. *=food_acquired.u= is a 2-token sentinel vocabulary, not a unit label
   axis.*  =new_harmonize_units= collapses every unit to ={kg, piece}= and
   rescales the quantity; the framework then maps =kg -> Kg= but leaves
   =piece= alone by explicit design.  Consequence in §5/D3.

*Three idiosyncrasies were NOT recorded and I have added them* (commit
=38e988ee=): the stale #112 premise (D1), the integer-truncation defect (D2),
and the fact that Tanzania is the only country reading a *build table* out of
the cache-excluded =CONTENTS.org= (D5).

** Geography, and how the three sources were put on one ladder

- Ladder: =v= (survey cluster) -> =District= -> =Region= -> national, via
  =common.attach_geo= on =(t, v)= against =cluster_features=.
- =Region= and =District= are normalised on *every* source with
  =tanzania.normalize_place= (uppercase, punctuation -> space).  Without it the
  region-code fallback ('DAR ES SALAAM', 'MJINI MAGHARIBI UNGUJA') and
  =cluster_features= ('Dar Es Salaam', 'Mjini/Magharibi Unguja') are different
  vocabularies and *0 of 27* region names match; after it they are one axis.
  This also folds away some of the documented district spelling variants
  ('MBINGA (NYASA)' / 'MBINGA(NYASA)').
- Source 5 unresolved communities keep a Region from their own admin code
  (=TZ_REGION_BY_CODE=, zero-padded -- the raw codes are unpadded '1', '7').
  *0 rows end with no region at all.*
- Households whose =v= has no =cluster_features= row are *counted, not dropped*:
  source 1, *3,468 rows* (2019-20: 2,924; 2014-15: 544) -- these are the 326
  split-off households carrying a =clusterid= they were never sampled in, and
  the 5 2014-15 clusters lost in =id_walk=, both already recorded in
  CONTENTS.org §"Known, NOT fixed here".  Source 4: 102 rows.  They still
  contribute at the national rung.

*The 1-vs-5 median gap is exactly zero, and that is a result, not a bug.*  I
checked it: on the native basis 25 of 131 matched cells (19.1%) have a
*byte-identical* median log price on both sides -- 41 of 227 (18.1%) on the kg
basis -- because TZS food prices are quoted on coarse round points (the tied
cells sit at 1,000 / 1,200 / 2,000 / 2,400 / 5,000 / 6,000 TZS per kg) and a
median lands on one of them from both directions.  45.8% of native cells agree
within +-10% and 74.0% within +-25%; the IQR of the log gap is 0.27 (2019-20)
and 0.15 (2020-21).  Two independently collected sources -- one a household
recall of what it spent, one an enumerator's price quote at a named
marketplace -- agree to within a rounding tick.  *This is the strongest
internal-consistency evidence in the Tanzania data and it validates both
sources at once.*

and 1/5 = 1.00 on the kg basis): =finest_only= keeps a different geographic

common cell set.  The national table above is the transitive one.

* 4. Logs -- the model table, read out

=models.csv= holds 97 rows (pooled + one per rung, x 3 pairs x 2 bases x 2
threshold regimes).  *Most are non-identified*: the =within= spec spends
=n_items + n_geos - 1= parameters plus a slope, and every crop-sale pair has
only 9-39 matched cells over 9-14 items, so the fixed effects would fit the gap
*exactly*.

Under the previous =common.py= this showed up as =r2_fe= = 1.000000,
=resid_sd= ~ 4e-16 and a =coef_within= of *6.886* -- a saturated-dummy
artefact presented as an estimate.  The coordinator's 2026-09-08 =MIN_DOF=5=
guard now returns NaN instead, and these outputs are regenerated against it:
*80 of 97 rows report no =coef_within=, 50 report neither slope*, and the
largest surviving =|coef_within|= is 1.72.  My independent check
(=analysis.identification()=, which recomputes =n_cells - (n_items + n_geos -

Sentence by sentence:

- *=between= is the primary test and it is essentially zero on the kg basis*
  (-0.046, -0.084, +0.031, -0.084).  Two of the four differ from zero at
  conventional levels but the *magnitude* is 4-8%, an order below the -0.24 the
  synthetic additive-cost benchmark produces.  So the (tiny) wedge between the
  household unit value and the community price does *not* fall with the price
  level: it behaves like a constant proportional relationship -- which, given
  the median gap is 0, means the two sources track each other one-for-one
  across cheap and dear foods alike.
- *Read the kg basis, not the native one, for this pair.*  The native basis
  gives -0.789 (2019-20), which looks like a strong additive cost and is not
  one: the native basis pools =Kg= and =Piece= cells in a single regression, so
  the item's mean midpoint partly encodes *which unit* the item is priced in
  rather than how dear it is.  The kg basis is unit-homogeneous and the effect
  collapses to ~0.  This is a real trap for the cross-country pooling and I
  flag it rather than quietly reporting the bigger number.
- *=within= is not interpretable for any Tanzania pair* and the protocol
  predicts exactly this: source 5 is a measurement matched at cluster grain
  with =n=1=, which is the protocol's "THIN b" case (synthetic: within -1.27
  while between stays -0.002).  My within estimates (-1.72, -1.39, -0.43,
  -0.20) sit right in that predicted range and should be read as noise
  asymmetry, not economics.  =sd_log_price= in =cells.csv= says which side is
  noisier: source 5 cells are mostly =n=1= and carry no SD at all.
- *=r2_fe= 0.27-0.98*: item and place identity alone explain most of the 1-vs-5
  gap where thresholds are tight, i.e. what little disagreement there is, is
  systematic to particular items in particular places rather than random.
- *For the farmgate pairs there is no readable within model at all*, and the
  surviving =between= slopes are worthless as evidence: they scatter from
  -0.405 to +1.665 across wave, basis and threshold on 9-39 cells, changing
  sign between neighbouring specifications of the same pair.  The honest
  statement is that Tanzania *cannot* discriminate a constant markup from an
  additive per-kg cost on the farmgate-to-market gap: 2,885 crop sale rows over
  33 crops is too thin once it is cut by wave, item, unit and geography.  The
  per-item table in §5 is what Tanzania contributes to that question instead.
  Standard errors are HC3 and unclustered throughout; with 9-39 cells that is
  the least of the problems.

** The =claim_30_50 = True= rows in models.csv, and why I do not stand behind them

A coordinator filtering =models.csv= on =claim_30_50= will find Tanzania
saying *yes* on *12 of 97 rows*, which contradicts everything above, so it
must be explained rather than left to be discovered.  They are not 12
independent findings: =model_rows= writes one pooled row plus one per
geographic rung, so the 12 collapse to *two pooled results*, both
=community_price_vs_crop_sale_price=, *native* basis, *2019-20*, thresholds
(1,10) and (1,5); median gap 0.262 / 0.278, i.e. ratio 1.30 / 1.32 -- sitting
exactly on the bottom edge of the interval.  Of the remaining rung-level rows,
*three rest on a single cell* (=n_cells= = 1) and one more on six.

They rest on *nine* native-Kg cells, and here they are in full:

artefact, not a finding.

Everything else on the same wave says otherwise: the kg basis, which adds the

national ladder gives *2.76* with *0 of 21* items in range.  *I stand behind
the national ladder and the kg basis.*  Read Tanzania as a firm *no* on the
30-50% claim, and treat those two =claim_30_50= cells as flagged, not as a
dissenting vote.

*What the per-item table shows that a pooled slope hides* -- and this is the
part Phase 2 needs.  The ordering is not by price level, it is *by
perishability and by processing distance*:

- *Bulk storable grains and roots sit lowest*: Sugarcane 1.37, Cassava Fresh
  1.50, Pulses 1.83-2.00, Maize (grain) 2.86-3.51.
- *Perishable fruit and vegetables sit highest*: Ripe Bananas 3.35, Citrus
  3.71, Other Fruits 3.87, Groundnuts 3.82.
- Fruit is *not* systematically dearer than grain in level (Citrus 267 TZS/kg
  farmgate is *cheaper* than Maize's 350), so the item-level slope a =between=
  regression estimates is picking up *margin heterogeneity by commodity class*,
  not an additive per-kg cost.  Tanzania is a clean illustration of the
  confound the protocol warns about, and it is why the per-item table -- not
  the slope -- is the deliverable here.

*Sources 3 and 4 cannot be compared to each other* (the Uganda centrepiece):
source 3 does not exist for Tanzania.  So the question "does a household value
its own-consumed harvest at the sale price or the purchase price?" is
*unanswerable from Tanzanian data* -- the instrument never asks.  What Tanzania
*can* say instead, and Uganda cannot, is that its two independent *market-side*
measures agree to a rounding tick (§3), which puts a tight bound on the
measurement error in the market leg of any margin estimate.
