** Sources 2 and 3 are an INSTRUMENT absence, not an API gap

This is the question the brief asked me to settle, and the questionnaire
settles it.  ESS3 Household Questionnaire, =SECTION 5A: FOOD LAST 7 DAYS=
(=ESS3 HH Questionnaire_English.pdf=, the §5A grid; the column stems are
=hh_s5aq01..06=, wired in =Ethiopia/2015-16/_/food_acquired.py=), asks
exactly six questions per item, quoted verbatim:

: 1. Over the past one week (7 days), did you or others in your household consume any [ITEM]?
: 2. How much in total did your household consume in the past week?
: 3. How much came from purchases?  IF NONE RECORD 0 AND SKIP TO Q5.
: 4. How much did you spend?
: 5. How much came from own production?  IF NOT CONSUMED FROM OWN PRODUCTION RECORD 0.
: 6. How much came from gifts and other sources?  IF NONE RECORD 0.

Q4 is the *only* monetary field and it is attached to Q3, purchases.  Q5
(own production) and Q6 (gifts) ask a **quantity and unit only**.  So:

- *Source 3 does not exist in Ethiopia at any level.*  The ESS never asks
  what the own-consumed harvest was worth -- neither "what would you have
  received" nor "what would it have cost to buy".  Nor does the
  agricultural questionnaire supply one: the post-harvest §11 Q10a and §12
  Q10a both ask *"How much of the harvested [CROP] ... was, or will be,
  used for household consumption?"* with a QTY / UNIT CODE box and no
  value column, and a text search of the whole post-harvest instrument for
  "total value ... consum" / "worth" / "what would" returns nothing.
  **This is a genuine data gap only in the sense that the survey chose not
  to ask it; there is nothing for the library to carry.**  The question
  numbers are §5A Q5 (household) and §11 Q10a / §12 Q10a (agriculture).
- *Source 2 does not exist either.*  Q4 is total spend, not a unit price,
  so any =Price= for Ethiopia could only ever be =Expenditure/Quantity=
  back-calculated -- exactly what =country.py:5793= would produce.  That
  =food_acquired= carries **no** =Price= column is therefore correct
  config, not an omission.  The protocol's independence test (§2 below) is
  vacuous here and I did not run it as a statistic.

** Source 5: what the community price is a price OF

ESS3 Community Questionnaire, =SECTION 10: MARKET PRICES=.  The price grid
has columns =ITEM CODE | ITEM | UNIT | UNIT CODE | QUANTITY | PRICE=, with
a header block "1. Location of main source: MARKETPLACE / SHOPS/STALLS /
OTHER, SPECIFY", GPS of the market, and "4. Date of price data
collection".  So the enumerator records, per (EA, item, unit), *the price
of a stated quantity* -- and Ethiopia's per-unit price is
=Price / Quantity=.  There is **no =NumberOfUnits= column**; =Quantity= is
that basis, and =Ethiopia/_/community_prices.py= says so explicitly ("the
per-unit price is =Price / Quantity=, a transformation, NEVER baked in").
It is a retail market price -- the downstream side of every pair.

** Source 4: what =Value_sold / Quantity_sold= is a price OF

ESS3 Post-Harvest =SECTION 11: CROP DISPOSITION=, Q3 *"How much of the
harvested [CROP] was sold in total?"* (=ph_s11q03_a= + unit =_b=) and Q4
*"What was the total value of all [CROP] sales?"* (=ph_s11q04=).  A
**realised transaction** value over a realised quantity, not a
hypothetical.

Two things make it a valid per-=u= price, and both come from reading
=ethiopia.py:crop_production_for_wave= rather than assuming:

- The script attaches =Quantity_sold= *only on rows whose harvest =u=
  equals the single sale unit reported for that (holder, crop)* -- its
  comment is "Where they differ it stays NaN (no cross-unit fib)".  So the
  ratio is genuinely a price per native =u= and needs no conversion.
- The SALES GRAIN CAVEAT: §11 is (household, holder, crop) grain, not
  (plot, crop), so a holder growing one crop on several plots has the sale
  attached to **no** plot rather than split across them.

*It is not unambiguously a farmgate price.*  §11 Q8 asks the "main mode of
transportation associated with all [CROP] sales" (FOOT / BAJAJ / OWN
BICYCLE-CART / CAR-MINIBUS-BUS-TRUCK / BUYER PICK-UP / PACK ANIMALS) and
Q9 the "total cost of transportation".  Only =BUYER PICK-UP= is
unambiguously farmgate; the other five imply the household moved the crop
toward a market, so the reported sale value is *partly* net of the
marketing margin the exercise is trying to measure.  Any gap below is
therefore a **lower bound** on the farm-to-retail margin.

** Idiosyncrasies NAMED from =Ethiopia/_/CONTENTS.org= (1,302 lines, not 602)

The brief said 602 lines; the file is 1,302.  I read all of it.  Six
idiosyncrasies bear directly on this analysis:

1. *ESS1 (2011-12) is a different universe.*  "The ERSS sample is designed
   to be representative of rural and small town areas of Ethiopia" (DDI
   2053, PDF p. 3).  Its =Urban= means *small town only*; from ESS2 it
   means any urban area including Addis Ababa.  Urban share runs 12.7% ->
   36.8% -> 34.0% -> 54.0% -> 53.9% while the *rural count is flat*
   (3,466 -> 3,115): the sample is being extended into urban strata, not
   shifted.  **I never pool waves** -- every cell, gap and model row is
   keyed on =t= -- and §3 reads 2011-12 separately.
2. *Nine zones of Afar and Somali are outside the frame in every Panel I
   wave*; 2021-22 additionally puts *Tigray outside the represented
   population* (not merely outside fieldwork -- the ESPS-5 weights
   "represent the 2021/22 Ethiopia population (excluding Tigray)").
3. *Panel I (W1-W3) and Panel II (W4-W5) are different panels.*  ESS4 "is
   not a follow-up of the previous ESS waves".
4. *Every rural EA is 10/12 agricultural BY DESIGN* (10 drawn from the
   AgSS list + 2 non-agricultural).  Farm households are structurally
   oversampled inside every rural EA, in every wave.  Source 4 is drawn
   from that oversample; source 1 is not.  Unweighted rural means are not
   rural-population means -- and this is a *composition* difference
   between the two sides of the headline pair, not a price difference.
5. *The =(t, v)= cluster key is not unique*, and it bites here.  Building
   =cluster_features= raises =GrainCollapseWarning= in four of five waves;
   measured this run: 373 / 3,969 rows destroyed (2011-12, 34 conflicting
   tuples), 454 / 5,262 (2013-14, 43), 732 / 4,954 (2015-16, 72), **1,240
   / 4,959 (2021-22, 111 -- 25% of the wave)**.  CONTENTS.org documents
   the GPS side of this (W2 EA =010103010100106= spans 0.19 deg lat /
   0.60 deg lon; W5 EA =020103010100203= spans 1.79 deg lon, ~200 km) and
   calls it UNFIXED.  My run adds that 2011-12 also conflicts (34 tuples)
   even though CONTENTS.org records *0* EAs with a second distinct
   coordinate there -- so in 2011-12 the disagreement is in
   =Region=/=District=/=Rural=, not in GPS.  Consequence for this report:
   the =v= rung's geography labels are, in those clusters, a composite
   =first()= row.
6. *ESS4/ESPS-5 put zero urban households through the agriculture
   questionnaire* while urban is 54% of the sample.  So in W4/W5 source 4
   is rural-only by construction while source 1 is half urban.

Reading these in words, one sentence each:

- *The two market-side sources agree exactly.*  A median gap of 0.000 in
  all five waves is not a rounding of something small: **23.1% of the
  8,306 matched cells have a gap of exactly zero** (24.2% at cluster
  grain).  The cause is round-number pricing -- a cluster's median
  household unit value for Banana lands on exp(2.302585) = 10.0 Birr/kg
  and the enumerator's surveyed market price is also 10.0.  Excluding the
  exact ties, the residual median gap is +0.04 to +0.11 by wave (~4-11%),
  i.e. households pay slightly more than the surveyed market price.  This
  is a *validation* result: two instruments that share no file and no
  question (§5A household recall vs §10 enumerator market visit) return
  the same retail price, which is what licenses treating either as "the
  market side".
- *The farmgate-to-market gap is real but modest*, 12-55% depending on
  wave and which market source is used, and it *grows across waves*
  (1.22 -> 1.19 -> 1.54 -> 1.46 -> 1.55 for source 1 vs 4).
- *ESS1 sits at the bottom of that range in both pairs* (1.22 and 1.12),
  the lowest of the five waves.  That is the expected sign for a universe
  restricted to rural areas and small towns: the "market" a 2011-12
  household buys in is a small-town market close to the farm, so less
  marketing margin has been added.  I state it as consistent, not proven
  -- ESS1 is one wave and its cell counts are small.
- *The spread is wide relative to the level.*  IQRs of 0.20-0.87 in logs
  around medians of 0.12-0.44 mean the sign of the gap is not stable
  cell-to-cell: 47.6% of source-1-vs-4 cells have =|gap| > log 1.5=.

*Where the ladder actually lands, and why the cluster rung is empty for
the household pair.*  An ESS enumeration area holds 12 rural / 15 urban
households, so a household-side source structurally cannot reach many
observations per (cluster, item, unit) cell: measured median =n= per cell
is **2** for =unit_value= at =v= and **1** for =crop_sale_price= at every
level.  At the protocol's thin-source floor of 5 per household side (used
for both 1 and 4; community prices at 1), =unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price=
therefore has **zero** matched cells at =v= -- its 103 cells are 28
District, 40 Region, 35 national.  =community_price_vs_crop_sale_price=
reaches =v= 18 times only because source 5 is usable at =n=1=.  *This is
a design fact about ESS cluster sizes, not a defect and not a fixable
one*; a cluster-grain household-to-household price comparison is not
available in Ethiopia at any honest threshold.

What each number means, one sentence each:

- *=beta_between= is the primary test, and it does not merely fail to
  reject a proportional markup -- it leans the wrong way for the
  alternative.*  An additive per-unit handling cost predicts a *strongly
  negative* slope; a constant markup or an iceberg cost predicts 0.  Of
  the eight identified crop-sale slopes, **seven are positive**, and the
  only one exceeding two standard errors (2011-12, =unit_value= vs
  =crop_sale_price=, t = +2.09) is positive -- the opposite sign to the
  additive-cost prediction.  *The Ethiopian farm-to-market gap behaves
  like a proportional markup.*
- *The one genuinely negative result is on the per-kg basis and I do not
  believe it*: =unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price= 2021-22 gives
  =beta_between= = -0.486 (se 0.187, t = -2.6).  But the kg view is
  precisely the one that admits fruit and root items through a crude
  item-independent piece-to-kg factor (see below), and those are the
  items with the huge gaps in §5, so a negative between-item slope there
  is what a *conversion* artifact looks like.  The native basis for the
  same wave gives +0.241.
- *=beta_within= survives only for =unit_value_vs_community_price=*, and
  there it is small and negative (-0.03 to -0.20 native): within an item,
  the household-paid price exceeds the surveyed market price slightly
  more at cheap locations than dear ones.  The two sides are comparably
  noisy there (=sd_log_price= medians 0.154 for =unit_value= vs NaN for
  =community_price=), which is the condition the Bland-Altman midpoint
  needs; for the crop-sale pairs it is violated and the estimate is
  correctly withheld.
- *Which source is noisier* -- from =sd_log_price= in =cells.csv= at the
  =v= rung, native basis: =unit_value= median 0.154, =crop_sale_price=
  median 0.129, and =community_price= is **NaN by construction** (=n= = 1
  per cluster cell -- it is a measurement, not a sample).
- *=r2_fe= is reported only where the within spec is identified*, so the
  saturated 1.000 values that appeared in my first run are gone.  For
  =unit_value_vs_community_price= it runs 0.52-0.88: item and place alone
  explain half to seven-eighths of the gap variance, leaving a residual SD
  of 0.53-0.93 in logs.
- *Standard errors are HC3 and unclustered.*  It matters here: cells from
  the same District share households, so the true standard errors are
  larger than printed -- which only strengthens the "cannot reject 0"
  reading above.

** Why the per-kg view is the SECONDARY one, and a note in Ethiopia's favour

The protocol flags per-kg comparisons as partly circular because the
library's kg factors are inferred from =Expenditure/Quantity=.  **That
caveat is weaker for Ethiopia than the protocol assumes**, and I used the
better source: =Ethiopia/_/conversion_to_kgs.json= is built from the ESS's
own =Food_CF_Wave{1..5}.dta= conversion files (=mean_cf_nat=), so its
factors *never saw a price*.  =analysis.py= applies that single map to all
three sources identically, so no source is converted on its own terms.  It
covers 98.4% / 98.4% / 96.5% of source 1 / 4 / 5 rows.

Its real limitation is different and worth stating: the factors are
*national means per unit and item-independent*, so "Number"/"Piece" units
convert at one weight for every food.  The per-kg gaps run systematically
*higher* than the native ones (1.548 vs 1.344 for source 1 vs 4), and the
per-item table shows why -- the kg view admits fruit and root crops whose
piece-to-kg factor is a crude national average.  **Native =u= is the
primary basis and is what §3 and §5 read.**

Conversely the kg view earns its place for one thing: Ethiopian food is
recorded at both =Gram= and =Kg= for the same item (Salt, Coffee,
Chat/Kat), so native-unit matching splits those items across two
incomparable cells; the kg view rejoins them, which is why it has 178 vs
103 matched cells for the headline pair.

* 5. The claim: is the farmgate-to-market gap 30-50%?

*Which farmgate, which market.*  Ethiopia has no source 3, so the farmgate
side is source 4 (=crop_sale_price=) only -- and, per §1, it is a *sale*
price partly net of transport, so it is a lower bound on the true
farmgate.  The market side is available twice, and §3 showed the two agree
to a median gap of zero, so the choice between them does not drive the
answer.

*Headline: pooled over waves, native basis, source 1 vs source 4 gives a
median ratio of 1.344 -- inside the 30-50% band.  But the pooled number
is an average over two item classes that behave completely differently,
and reporting only it would be misleading.*

** The pattern that matters: staples are BELOW the band, perishables far ABOVE

- *Cereals, pulses and oilseeds -- the crops §11 actually covers -- carry
  a 8-30% margin, well below the claim*: Millet 1.10, Linseed 1.08,

  carry the pooled median.

  Berbere 6.68, Banana 3.56, Potato 3.15, Sweet potato 3.76.
- *Two items go the wrong way*: Leafy Greens 0.40 (native) and Banana
  0.045 in the community pair -- the farmgate price *exceeds* the market
  price by 2.5x and 22x.  A negative marketing margin is not economics; it
  is a unit mismatch (a bunch or heap sold at farmgate matched against a
  Kg retail price under the same =u= label).  I did not fix it; it is
  recorded as D5.

*The honest caveat that undercuts the perishable numbers.*  Every
fruit/root item above rests on 1-3 cells, and it rests on them
illegitimately: §11's own header reads =SECTION 11: CROP DISPOSITION
EXCLUDE TREE/FRUIT/VEGETABLE/ROOT CROPS=.  Those crops have their own
module, =SECTION 12. TREES/FRUIT/VEGETABLE/ROOT CROPS=, which
=crop_production= does not read at all (D3).  So a Papaya row in §11 is an
enumerator exception, not a sample.  **The defensible conclusion is the
staple one: for the field crops the ESS actually prices, Ethiopia's
farm-to-retail margin is roughly 10-30%, below the 30-50% claim** -- and
that is before allowing for §1's point that the §11 sale price is already
partly post-transport, which biases the measured gap *downward* and so
does not rescue the claim.

*Sources 3 and 4 cannot be compared to each other* (the Uganda centrepiece
question -- does a household value its own-consumed harvest at the sale
price or the purchase price?) because Ethiopia has no source 3.  The
question is unanswerable for this country with the instrument as fielded.
