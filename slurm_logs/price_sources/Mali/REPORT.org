** Idiosyncrasies NAMED (from =Mali/_/CONTENTS.org= and the questionnaires)

1. *EHCVM indexing.*  =v= is =grappe=; each grappe is visited in exactly one
   =vague=; =i= is =(grappe, menage)=.  Verified: =community_prices= is keyed on
   the native grappe and =_join_v_from_sample= does not fire on it.
2. *Only 2018-19 and 2021-22 are EHCVM.*  2014-15 and 2017-18 are EAC-I
   (Enquete Agricole de Conjoncture Integree), a different instrument -- which is
   precisely why the price modules are where they are.  The Overview section of
   =CONTENTS.org= still blurs this and says so itself.
3. *In rural 2014-15 the observation unit is the agricultural holding, not the
   household* (=Documentation.pdf=, Sampling Procedure, p.5: "exploitations
   agricoles en milieu rural, menage en milieu urbain").  Source 1 for that wave
   is therefore not a uniform household sample.
4. *Kidal is excluded by design* from 2014-15, 2017-18 and 2018-19.  The Region
   rung -- which is where all of Mali's price comparisons land, see below -- is a
   country-minus-Kidal geography.
5. *3.85 households per grappe in 2014-15* (design: 3 per rural EA, 9 per urban).
   A household-side source needs 10 observations per cell, so *no source-1 cell
   at the cluster rung qualifies in the only wave that has a market price*.  The
   ladder starts at Region.  This is arithmetic, not a failure, and it is the
   single most important structural fact about Mali here.
6. *=crop_production= names its item level =crop=, not =j=* (aliased in
   =analysis.py=), and -- correcting the ledger's *§4* and the brief -- it *does*
   carry a =u= column.  "No =u= level" is true of the index only.

** The community price question, quoted

=EACI2014_COM_P2_11082014.pdf=, "SECTION 4. RELEVE DES PRIX A LA CONSOMMATION",
columns (4.03)-(4.11).  The block is *three readings*, each with three fields:

#+begin_quote
Unité | Relevé 1 Poids (KG) | Prix | Unité | Relevé 2 Poids (KG) | Prix | Unité | Relevé 3 Poids (KG) | Prix
#+end_quote

#+begin_quote
CODES UNITES: 1=Kilogramme  2=Gramme  3=Litre  4=Centilitre  5=Unité  6=Sachet  7=Paquet  8=Boîte  9= Sac large (100kg)  10=Sac moyen (50kg)  11=Sac petit (25kg)  12=Verre  13=Bidon  14=Tas
#+end_quote

Two consequences, both load-bearing:

- *"Poids (KG)" is a WEIGHT, not a NumberOfUnits.*  The protocol's "per-unit
  price is =Price / NumberOfUnits=" does not apply.  For Mali,
  =price per native unit = Price= and =price per kg = Price / Quantity=.  This
  is a *deviation from the protocol's wording* and is flagged as such.
- The sacks name their own size, so the survey supplies its own kg conversion
  and the per-kg community price does *not* depend on the library's inferred kg
  factors.

*The library reads only relevé 3.*  Measured over both passages: relevé 1 has
66,742 priced rows, relevé 2 has 49,081, relevé 3 has 40,237 -- and *26,745
product-variety rows are priced in relevés 1-2 and empty in relevé 3*, so they
are lost.  It is a coverage loss, not a bias: where relevés 1 and 3 share a unit
and a weight (n = 31,408) the median log(P3/P1) is *exactly 0.000, IQR 0.000*.

* 3. Levels

Thresholds: source 1 and source 4 are household-side sample statistics, source 5
is a measurement.  =threshold_a / threshold_b= = 10 / 1 for 1-vs-5, 10 / 5 for
1-vs-4, 1 / 5 for 5-vs-4.  *Source 4 is lowered to the protocol's permitted 5 and
this is said out loud*: Mali has 1,346 crop rows in the whole corpus with both a
positive =Value_sold= and a positive =Quantity_sold=.

|--------------------------------------+--------------+-------+--------------+----------+-----------------------|
| =unit_value_vs_community_price=      | native       |   527 |        0.955 |    0.606 |                 40.0% |
| =unit_value_vs_community_price=      | native_sized |   202 |        0.942 |    0.317 |                 25.7% |
| =unit_value_vs_community_price=      | *u = Kg only* |  160 |    *1.0000* |    0.367 |                 26.9% |
| =unit_value_vs_community_price=      | unsized u    |   325 |        1.000 |    0.784 |                 48.9% |
| =unit_value_vs_community_price=      | per_kg       |   543 |        0.725 |    1.496 |                 63.4% |
| =unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price=      | native (Kg)  |     2 |        4.926 |    0.654 |                100.0% |
| =community_price_vs_crop_sale_price= | native (Kg)  |     1 |        4.211 |        0 |                100.0% |

*Read the Kg row.*  On the one unit where both sources demonstrably measure the
same physical quantity, the household unit value and the surveyed market price
have a median log gap of *exactly 0.000* over 160 (item x region) cells: at the
median, Malian households pay the price the community survey records.  That is a
genuine cross-validation of two independently collected sources, and it is the
strongest positive result in this country.  The dispersion is real though -- IQR
0.37 in logs, and 26.9% of cells differ by more than 1.5x.

*Why so many gaps are exactly 0.000 -- it is price discreteness, not a join
artefact.*  Seven items in the per-item table below sit at exactly 1.000 across
7-8 region cells each, which looks too clean to be real.  It is not a duplicated
frame: both sources quote *round numbers* (rice 350 or 400, salt 200, sugar 500
FCFA/kg), so with a few tens of observations the two medians land on the same
round number.  The check that distinguishes the two explanations is dispersion --
a duplicated frame would give IQR 0 as well, and the Kg IQR is 0.37 with 26.9% of
cells beyond 1.5x.  The medians agree; the individual observations do not.

The =per_kg= row (ratio 0.725) is *not trustworthy* and is reported only for
completeness: it converts source 1 through =_get_kg_factors=, which for Mali
2014-15 returns Sac moyen -> 26.2 kg (label says 50), Sac petit -> 5.3 (says 25),
Sac large -> 33.0 (says 100) and Gramme -> 0.708 (should be 0.001).  The honest
per-kg comparison is the =u = Kg= row above, where the factor is 1.0 on both
sides and no inference occurs.

*Which source is noisier* (median =sd_log_price= over cells with n >= 10):
community price *0.635* < unit value *0.935* < crop sale price *1.254*.  The
market survey is the tightest measurement and the crop sale price by far the
loosest -- which is the other reason source 4 cannot carry a conclusion here.

* 4. Logs -- the model, read out

Primary pair, =unit_value_vs_community_price=, 2014-15:

| basis        | rung   | cells | items | geos | median gap | within (se)     | *between (se)*    | r2_fe |
|--------------+--------+-------+-------+------+------------+-----------------+-------------------+-------|
| native       | Region |   491 |    36 |    8 |     -0.002 | -0.136 (0.076)  | *+0.152 (0.047)*  | 0.146 |
| native_sized | Region |   197 |    32 |    8 |     -0.057 | -0.300 (0.083)  | *-0.062 (0.091)*  | 0.422 |
| per_kg       | v      |   486 |    26 |  115 |     -0.347 | -1.395 (0.096)  | -0.423 (0.086)    | 0.564 |

In words, one sentence each:

- =median gap= -0.057 on comparable units: the household unit value sits about
  5.6% *below* the surveyed market price -- a small negative, not a margin.
- =between= = *-0.062 with a standard error of 0.091* is statistically
  indistinguishable from zero.  *This is the primary test, and it says the
  relation between these two prices is proportional* -- whatever separates them
  does not shrink as goods get dearer, so it is not an additive per-kg handling
  cost.  Since the level is also ~1, the honest reading is "these two sources
  measure the same thing, up to noise".
- =between= = *+0.152 (0.047)* on the unsized-inclusive =native= basis is
  significantly *positive* -- and it is an artefact of unit incomparability, not
  economics.  Dearer items are disproportionately the ones sold in heaps, boxes
  and packets whose size differs between a household's purchase and the market
  survey's quote.  *Restricting to sized units removes the whole effect.*  A
  country agent who ran only the =native= basis would have reported a positive
  price-level gradient that does not exist.
- =within= = -0.300 (0.083) is *not interpretable here* and is reported only
  because the protocol asks for it: source 5 is a measurement with one surveyed
  price per cluster-item, exactly the thin-b case =common.py='s self-test shows
  driving the within slope to -1.27 under a pure markup.  Note also that
  source 1 is the *noisier* side (0.935 vs 0.635), which under a pure markup
  predicts a *positive* within slope -- so the observed negative is not explained
  by the noise asymmetry either.  Read =between=.
- =r2_fe= = 0.42 on the sized basis: item and region effects explain 42% of the
  variance of the gap; =resid_sd= 0.47 says what is left is still substantial.
- The =per_kg= row's strongly negative =between= (-0.423) is the broken-kg-factor
  artefact of section 3 compounding the thin-b artefact.  Do not read it.

Standard errors are HC3 and *unclustered*.  It matters here: the 197 sized cells
are 32 items across 8 regions, so cells within an item share a common source-5
measurement error.  The between coefficient is insignificant anyway, so
clustering would only widen an interval that already contains zero -- but a
future analyst wanting to *reject* zero on Mali should cluster by item first.

* 5. The claim: is the farmgate-to-market gap 30-50%?

*Mali cannot test the claim, and the reason is worth more than a number.*

The claim needs a farmgate price.  Mali has exactly one candidate, source 4, and:

- source 3, the other farmgate candidate, *was never asked* (section 1);
- source 4's unit axis is unsized, so only =u = 'Kg'= is comparable -- 78 rows in
  2014-15, over 32 clusters and 20 crops, 16 of them a *non-food* crop (Coton);
- at the protocol's lowered threshold of 5, that yields *2* matched cells against
  source 1 and *1* against source 5.

Those cells, in full, are the entire evidence base:

| pair                                 | item         | u  | cells | ratio |
|--------------------------------------+--------------+----+-------+-------|
| =unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price=      | Igname       | Kg |     1 |  9.47 |
| =unit_value_vs_crop_sale_price=      | Patate douce | Kg |     1 |  2.56 |
| =community_price_vs_crop_sale_price= | Igname       | Kg |     1 |  4.21 |

Retail at 2.6x to 9.5x farmgate is far outside 30-50%, but *n = 3 single-cell
comparisons is not evidence*, and a yam sold in bulk off the farm against a yam
bought by the kilo in a market is only nominally the same "Kg".  =claim_30_50= is
=False= on every Mali row of =models.csv= and should be read as "not tested".
