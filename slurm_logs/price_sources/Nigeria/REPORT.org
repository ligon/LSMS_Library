*Sources 2 and 3 are absent, not merely thin.*  Nigeria's =food_acquired=
carries no =Price= column at all (index =(i,t,v,j,u,s)=, columns =Quantity=,
=Expenditure=, =Quantity_kg=), so =food_prices(units='unitprice')= returns
*zero rows*.  There is nothing to run an independence test on (section 2).

** Post-planting / post-harvest: rounds are never pooled

Every Nigeria wave directory holds two rounds and =Country.waves= ARE the
round labels (=['2010Q3','2011Q1','2012Q3','2013Q1','2015Q3','2016Q1',
'2018Q3','2019Q1','2023Q3','2024Q1']=).  =t= is a cell key everywhere in
=analysis.py= and every model row is fitted within one =t=.  The three
price-bearing modules sit in *different* rounds, which is why the sources
overlap so little:

- =food_acquired= (consumption) -- *both* rounds, W1-W4 only.
- =community_prices= (C8) -- *post-harvest only*, W1-W5.
- =crop_production= (A3 harvest) -- *post-harvest only*, W1-W5.

So sources 1 and 5 can be compared in 2011Q1 / 2013Q1 / 2016Q1 / 2019Q1
only, and source 4 only in 2011Q1 / 2013Q1 (below).

** Idiosyncrasies NAMED from =Nigeria/_/CONTENTS.org= (852 lines, read in full)

1. *=ea= alone is not the cluster id* (GH #323).  =v= is the composite
   =state/lga/ea= built from raw CODES, published identically by =sample=,
   =cluster_features= and =community_prices=.  A label-built key matches the
   household side 0% of the time in W2.  Moved households (=ea == 0=) get
   singleton =v = moved-{hhid}= rather than a NaN that =groupby= would drop.
2. *The questionnaire was renumbered after wave 1* (GH #591).  From W2 on
   =q5a= is consumption out of PURCHASES, not own production; the pre-#591
   build labelled purchases as own production, leaving the purchased row with
   ~0 quantity and the expenditure still on it, so =Price = E/0 = inf= and
   =food_prices= kept 161-1,106 rows per wave out of 120k-160k.  Fixed; my
   source 1 has 52k-67k rows per round, which is the post-fix world.
3. *W4 reference-period asymmetry.*  In 2018Q3/2019Q1 the purchased row is
   the most recent purchase within *30 days* (q9a/q10) while produced and
   in-kind are 7-day consumption.  Pairing q10 with q9a is what makes the
   unit value a price; the cost is that =Quantity= summed across =s= is not a
   7-day total for W4.  Irrelevant to a price comparison, stated because it
   bounds what else these rows can be used for.
4. *=u= is stored as raw codes and decoded at API time* against
   =categorical_mapping.org= =#+name:u=; the wave scripts' =.replace(unitcodes)=
   is a no-op (float codes, string-keyed map).  All my unit work is on the
   *API-side* labels, which is the only place the vocabulary is comparable.
5. *=community_prices= was designed as the independent yardstick* -- it is the
   surveyed community price, not the WB's household-median imputation.  Ledger
   591 used it to adjudicate which purchased quantity pairs with the
   expenditure.  My section 3 is, in effect, that validation run at full scale.
6. *W1/W2 price items are resolved by NAME* onto =harmonize_food= (the
   community questionnaire has its own disjoint code scheme); W3-W5 resolve by
   Code.  118 of 139 community items match a =food_acquired= label.
7. *Universe caveats that bear on two of my four rounds.*  W3 (2016Q1) carries
   a *conflict-area oversample* (+5 households per conflict EA) and W4
   (2019Q1) *excludes rural Borno by design* with inaccessible EAs replaced.
   Unweighted cell medians in those rounds are over/under-weighted in opposite
   directions; my cells are unweighted medians, so this is a caveat on the
   levels, not on the within-cell gaps.
8. *W5 BID footnote 1*: "The Community Questionnaire does not collect
   information from communities in the sociological sense.  The data cannot be
   used to represent communities in Nigeria."  Source 5 is the sampled
   households' shared price environment, not a community estimate.

** Questionnaire wording (quoted, as the protocol requires)

*Source 5 -- what basis?*  There is no =NumberOfUnits= column in Nigeria's
=community_prices=, and none is needed: the form asks for the price of ONE
unit.  W4 post-harvest community questionnaire (=ph_w4_community_quest.pdf=,
section C8, p. 13), verbatim:

#+begin_quote
1. Is [ITEM] available for sale in your community or nearby at the present
time?  YES...1  NO.....2(-> NEXT ITEM)
2. PLEASE USE A QUANTITY OF ONE (1) FOR [ITEM] AND THE UNIT OF MEASURE YOU
ARE USING.   [sub-columns: UNIT OF MEASURE | SIZE]
3. What is the price for the item?   [NAIRA]
#+end_quote

W1 (=GHS_PANEL_COMMUNITY_POST_HARVEST_FINAL.pdf=, C8, p. 14) is the same
question with the unit *printed per item* instead of asked -- "1. Is [ITEM]
available for sale in your community or nearby at the present time? / 2. What
is the price for the item?" over a printed "Unit of measure" column, with
group headers stating the basis ("Grains and Flours (1 KG/1 PIECE)", "STARCHY
ROOTS, TUBERS & PLANTAIN (1 MEDIUM PIECE/1KG)", "OIL AND FATS (1 LITRE/1
MEDIUM CONTAINER)").  So source 5 is a per-unit price by construction -- *and
the SIZE column in W3-W5 is not read by the build*, which is problem N-02.

*Source 4 -- is it a farmgate price?*  Yes.  W1 Agriculture post-harvest
questionnaire (=GHS_PANEL_AGRICULTURE_POST_HARVEST_FINAL.pdf=, section A3,
p. 13), verbatim column heads: q9 "Did you sell any unprocessed crop since the
harvest?", q10 "To whom did you mainly sell the harvest of [CROP]?", q11 "How
much did you sell to or through this [BUYER]?" [QUANTITY + PROD. UNIT CODE],
q12 "What was the value of sales to or through this [BUYER]?" [NAIRA].  A
first-buyer sale of unprocessed crop: farmgate.

*The own-consumption valuation Nigeria asks and the library does not carry.*
Same form, p. 14, q18, verbatim: "If you had sold all [CROP] harvested since
the last visit, what would be the total value?" [NAIRA].  That is a
*received-side* ("what would you have got") valuation -- a farmgate
valuation, not a market one -- and it is populated on *9,857 of 13,018*
(75.7%) plot-crop rows in W1 and *9,868 of 12,948* (76.2%) in W2, against only
~3,500 rows per wave with an actual sale.  =crop_production= does not carry
it.  This is problem N-07 and it is the single most valuable thing a future
pass could wire for Nigeria.

In words, one sentence each:

- *=median gap= is the typical log difference between the two sources* in a
  matched cell; =exp= of it is the ratio in section 3.
- *=coef_between= is the primary test.*  A constant proportional markup (or an
  iceberg transport cost) predicts 0; an additive per-unit cost predicts a
  strong negative.  For *1 vs 5* it is -0.12, -0.16, -0.10 and +0.00 -- small,
  within about two standard errors of zero, with no consistent sign across
  rounds.  Read: *no additive-cost signature between the two market-side
  sources*, which is what you would expect of two measurements of the same
  price rather than of two points in a supply chain.
- *=coef_within= for 1 vs 5 is -0.90 to -1.00 and must not be read as a
  result.*  It is the protocol's own documented thin-=b= pathology: source 5
  is a *measurement* with one surveyed price per cell, so the Bland-Altman
  midpoint no longer cancels the shared noise, and the protocol's synthetic
  with a two-observation =b= produced *-1.27* under a pure markup while
  =between= stayed at -0.002.  My -0.90/-1.00 sits squarely in that regime.
  Cited as confirmation the diagnostic works, not as an economic finding.
- *The farmgate pairs' regressions are NOT IDENTIFIED and I do not interpret
  them.*  The designs are saturated: 5 vs 4 in 2013Q1 has 17 cells and *16*
  geography effects (=r2_fe= = 1.000000, =coef_between= = -2.000 with a
  standard error of 9e-15 -- an arithmetic artefact of a rank-deficient
  design, not a slope); 5 vs 4 in 2011Q1 has 53 cells and 46 geos; 1 vs 4 in
  2013Q1 has 19 cells against 14 items + 9 geos.  Only 1 vs 4 in 2011Q1 (72
  cells, 19 items, 28 geos) has residual degrees of freedom, and there
  =coef_between= = +0.165 (se 0.126) is indistinguishable from zero.  *For the
  farmgate pairs, read the median and the by-item table, not the slopes.*
- *=r2_fe= is the share of gap variance item and geography effects explain on
  their own*; at 0.42-0.92 for the identified 1-vs-5 rows, most of the gap is
  "which item and where", not a price-level relationship.
- *=share_abs_gap_gt_log1p5= and =claim_30_50=* are in =models.csv=.
  =claim_30_50= is =True= on 8 of the 139 model rows -- 1 of them pooled --
  and which ones depends on the threshold and the rung, not on the round.
  See section 5, where this is the main finding rather than a footnote.

*Standard errors are HC1/HC3 and unclustered.*  It matters here: cells within a
District share households, so the 1-vs-5 standard errors are optimistic.  It
does not change the reading, because the coefficients are near zero anyway.

*Threshold sensitivity.*  The GHS-Panel draws 10 households per EA, so a
=(t, j, u, v)= cell with >= 10 purchasers of the same item in the same unit is
structurally almost unattainable; with =threshold_a = 10= the 1-vs-5 pair
matches at =District= for 204/234/254/99 of its cells and reaches =v= only
132/22/16/0 times.  Re-running at =threshold_a = 5= (rows present in
=models.csv=) multiplies the cells 4-7x and moves the 2019Q1 median gap from
-0.057 to 0.000 -- i.e. it does not change the answer, it sharpens it.  The
5-vs-4 pair *does* reach cluster grain (43 of 53 cells at =v= in 2011Q1),
because a community measurement is usable at =n = 1=.

* 5. The claim: is the farmgate-to-market gap 30-50%?

*For Nigeria, no -- it is larger, and it is strongly item-heterogeneous.*

Which farmgate, which market?  Nigeria has *no* source 3, so the farmgate is
*source 4*, the crop sale price (=Value_sold / Quantity_sold=, a first-buyer
sale of unprocessed crop).  The market is available *twice over* -- source 1
(what households paid) and source 5 (what the community market charged) --
and section 3 showed those two agree at a ratio of 1.000 on Kg and l.  That
agreement is what makes the farmgate comparison trustworthy: two independent
market measures give the same answer.

First, the single most comparable slice -- =u = 'Kg'= cells only, *pooled
over both rounds and all geography rungs* (so this is an orientation number,
not the per-round result; those follow):

| comparison                       | Kg cells | median ratio | implied margin |
|----------------------------------+----------+--------------+----------------|
| market (source 1) / farmgate (4) |       89 |        1.630 |          *+63%* |
| market (source 5) / farmgate (4) |       66 |        2.000 |         *+100%* |

Both sit *above* the 30-50% band ([log 1.3, log 1.5] = [0.262, 0.405]).

- *At =District= grain the gap is 1.21-1.67* -- i.e. right at or just above the
  claimed band, and three District cells fall inside it.
- *At cluster (=v=) grain it is 1.45-2.60* -- mostly well above.
- *Pooled, 7 of the 8 settings exceed 1.5* and one (5 vs 4, 2013Q1, thresholds
  (1,3), 51 cells) lands at 1.455, inside the band.

Why the rung matters is not mysterious and it is not an artefact of the
statistics: a =v=-grain pair compares a farm's own sale price with the market
price *in that same cluster*, which is the transport-and-margin wedge the
claim is about; a =District=-grain pair compares two medians each already
pooled across clusters, which averages away the within-district dispersion in
both, and the more heterogeneous side (the thin crop-sale source) is pulled
further toward the district middle.  The cluster-grain number is the one that
answers the question as posed; the district-grain number is the one with
enough observations to be stable.  *Nigeria cannot adjudicate between them* --
the farmgate source has 17 to 118 matched cells.

** So: does the claim hold for Nigeria?

*The honest answer is "the central estimate is above the band, but Nigeria's
farmgate data are too thin to reject 30-50% cleanly."*  Specifically:

1. The best-populated cluster-grain estimate (5 vs 4, 2011Q1, 115 cells at
   =v=) gives *+75%* -- clearly above.
2. The best-populated single setting overall (5 vs 4, 2013Q1, (1,3), 51 cells)
   gives *+45.5%* -- inside the band.
3. Every District-grain estimate is 1.21-1.67, straddling the band's top.

What is *not* ambiguous, and is the durable result, is the *item*
heterogeneity below: whatever the pooled number, it is a mixture of cereals
near or below +30% and perishable / processed goods at +100% to +1000%.  A
single 30-50% figure is the wrong shape of answer for Nigeria regardless of
where the pooled median lands.

*The reading.*  The 30-50% band is not a bad summary so much as a bad
*single* summary: 1 of 21 and 2 of 15 items fall inside it, and the misses go
in *both* directions.  This is the finding that survives the threshold and
rung sensitivity above -- the pooled median moves between 1.45 and 2.00
depending on how the cells are selected, but the item ordering does not.  The pattern is legible and it is the one the protocol
anticipated -- *cereals sit at or below the band, everything else sits far
above it*.  Guinea corn/sorghum (+33%, +27%), Millet (+9%, +25%) and Maize
(+19% / +62%) are bulk storable grains with dense trading networks; Rice--local
(+170%, +207%), Groundnuts unshelled (+230%), Okra--fresh (+213%),
Kola nut (+1025%) and Tomatoes (+108%) are either perishable, bulky-per-naira,
or processed between the farm gate and the plate (local rice is *milled*
between q11 and the consumer -- part of that gap is a processing margin, not a
marketing margin, and it should not be read as transport).

This is exactly why the protocol asks for the item table: a between-item slope
fitted across these items would be reading a mixture of an additive handling
cost and genuinely item-heterogeneous margins, and for Nigeria it cannot
separate them because the farmgate designs are saturated (section 4).  The
per-item rows are the input Phase 2 needs to test the slope *within* item
classes (grains vs. perishables vs. processed).

*Two caveats on the size of these numbers, both of which cut the same way.*
(i) Community prices and crop harvests are both post-harvest, when producer
prices are seasonally *low*, while purchases in source 1 span both rounds --
if anything this *understates* the annual average margin.  (ii) Source 4's
unit is the *harvest* unit applied to the *sale* quantity by assumption
(problem N-03), correct on ~96% of rows; the ~4% filed under a wrong unit
add noise but no systematic direction.

*Sources 3 and 4 cannot be compared to each other for Nigeria* (the Uganda
centrepiece), because source 3 does not exist here.  =sa3q18= would supply the
missing half -- see problem N-07.

** Items to treat with suspicion

In the 1-vs-5 pair, Onions (ratio 3.97, 20 cells) and Garden eggs/egg plant
(7.07, 17 cells) are far outside the otherwise tight distribution (the other
30 items are between 0.74 and 1.59).  Both are typically sold by heap or
basket; I read them as residual size-variant confounds surviving into the
=Kg=/=Tuber= cells rather than as price facts, and do not interpret them.
