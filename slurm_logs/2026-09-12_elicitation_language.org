* Scope and method

Four categories, two waves, verbatim wording from the *instruments*, not from
the data dictionaries:

1. food expenditures, 2. non-food (non-durable) expenditures,
3. consumer durable assets, 4. rent / housing costs.

** Two caveats on "verbatim"

1. *Uganda's questionnaire is a printed grid with very narrow columns, so
   header text wraps mid-word.*  Quotes below are reflowed: a line break is
   closed up only when the trailing fragment is not itself a word and the
   concatenation is one (=househol= + =d= -> =household=), otherwise a space is
   inserted.  Every quote used here was checked against the raw line list and
   against the shipped Stata variable label.  Where the printed cell is
   genuinely garbled the garble is reproduced and flagged.
2. *GLSS7's Section 7 Parts A-C are auto-numbered in Word*, so the printed
   question numbers are not in the extracted text.  Numbering below comes from
   the shipped variable names (=s7cq1a=, =s7cq2=, ...) corroborated by the
   questionnaire's own cross-reference "( >> PART C Q7)".

* Summary: the four categories side by side

| category | GLSS7 2016-17 | UNPS 2019-20 |
|----------+---------------+--------------|
| food | S9 Part B, *6 asks* "since my last visit" (~5 d each), diary-assisted; own produce in S8 Part H on the same visit grid | S15 Part B, *1 ask*, "During the Last 7 Days"; four acquisition channels in one grid |
| non-food non-durable | S9 Part A, *past 12 months*, asked once; frequently-purchased non-food rides inside Part B | S15 Part C, *last 30 days*; S15 Part D, *last 365 days* |

* 1. GhanaLSS 2016-17 (GLSS7)

The organising split in GLSS7 is *frequency of purchase, not food vs non-food*.
Section 9 is titled =HOUSEHOLD EXPENDITURE (FOOD AND NON-FOOD EXPENSES)= and the
manual states it plainly:

#+begin_quote
Section 9 is segmented into Part A (Less Frequently Purchased Items and Part B
(Frequently Purchased Items)

Please note that questions on frequently purchased items will be asked per
visit while questions on less frequently purchased items will be asked once.
The two categories have been clearly marked in the questionnaire.
#+end_quote
-- /GLSS7 Final Manual, Section 9/

** 1.1 Food expenditures

*** Purchases -- Section 9 Part B, =EXPENSES (FREQUENTLY PURCHASED ITEMS)=

One question, repeated across six visit columns headed =2nd 3rd 4th 5th 6th
7th= (each with a =day month= date box):

#+begin_quote
1. How much was spent and what quantity was acquired of ………... since my last visit?
#+end_quote

Response columns per visit: =AMT (GH¢)= | =QTY= | =UNIT=.
Respondent block at the top of every page: =PERSON RESPONSIBLE / COPY FROM
SECTION 6 Q.8=, i.e. the household members identified as mainly responsible for
purchases.

Manual, Part B:

#+begin_quote
Questions 1-6: Solicit expenditure on food and non-food items which are
purchased more frequently.  Responses for this part will be solicited during
all visits with the exception of the first visit (Question).  In other words,
it will be administered 10 times in all.  In your second visit you will ask for
how much was spent by the household since the last 3 days, starting from the
day of your first visit.

For your next visits (Question 2-6), you will ask for how much was spent by the
household since your last visit.  In all EAs the diary of household expenditure
will be used.  You must however, ask for expenditures on your second visit in
order to capture the three days prior to your second visit.  If the household
did not spend anything on the particular item, skip to the next item.
#+end_quote

*That manual paragraph is stale -- see [[*4. Findings worth acting on][Finding 3]].*  The printed form has six
visit columns, not ten, and the Main Report's field design is five-day spacing,
not three.  The correct design, and why the Manual must not be used for it, is
already written up in =GhanaLSS/_/CONTENTS.org=: "GLSS7's recall period: six
five-day recalls, diary-supported" and "=GLSS7 Final Manual.docx= is stale
where it describes the visit schedule".

*** Own production -- Section 8 Part H, =CONSUMPTION OF OWN PRODUCE=

Same visit grid, asked of the holder identified in Section 6 Q.7:

#+begin_quote
1. Did the household consume any own produced ……….. in the past 12 months?
   Yes… .1  No……2  Next Item

2. How many months altogether was own produced ……….. consumed during the past
   12 months?

3.-8. How much of own produced ……. was consumed by the household since my last visit?

9. For how much would you sell one unit of ………….. now?
   USE MOST OCCURING UNIT OF MEASURE
#+end_quote

Q1/Q2 are screeners; Q3-Q8 are the six visits; Q9 is the farmgate valuation
price.  Note that the value of own produce is *not* asked -- only a quantity
per visit and a current selling price per unit.

*** The diary

=DIARY OF CONSUMPTION AND EXPENDITURE=, left with the household, header
=CLUSTER NUMBER / HOUSEHOLD NUMBER / NAME OF HOUSEHOLD HEAD / DATE=:

#+begin_quote
INSTRUCTION: RECORD ALL PAYMENTS MADE AND ESTIMATE ALL OWN PRODUCED GOODS CONSUMED.
#+end_quote

Columns: =Date= | =Place of Purchase/Own Produce= | =Description of Item
Purchased or Consumed= | =Qty= | =Unit= | =Price= | =Amount=.

The diary is an *aid to the visit questions*, not a separate instrument: the
manual says "In all EAs the diary of household expenditure will be used", and
nothing in the shipped extract is keyed to a diary line.

straight to Part C Q7.

*** Rent paid on OTHER dwellings -- Section 9 Part A item 168

Under the COICOP heading =Housing, water, electricity, gas and other fuels
(excluding payments captured in Section 7)=:

#+begin_quote

#+end_quote

so the 12-month non-food module deliberately de-duplicates against Section 7.

*** No imputed rent

*GLSS7 asks no hypothetical or imputed rent question for owner-occupiers.*
The rent block's only monetary variables in =g7sec7.dta= are =s7cq1a/1b=
(cash paid), =s7cq3a/3b= (value of goods and services given in exchange) and
=s7cq6a/6b= (paid by a non-member); the section's other money questions are
construction and repair (=s7cq7=, =s7cq10=) and the Part D utility bills
(water, refuse, toilet).  Nothing asks an owner what the dwelling is worth to
live in.  The Main Report's repeated
"(actual and imputed)" refers to *own-produced food and other own-produced
goods* (S8H and S9A Q3/Q4) -- its housing tables report only occupancy status
and who pays the rent.  Any imputed rent in a GLSS7 welfare aggregate is a
statistical construction downstream of the instrument, not something a
respondent was asked.

* 2. Uganda 2019-20 (UNPS wave 8)

Section 15 =Household Consumption Expenditure= is one section in four recall
tiers: Part A (household size over the reference week), Part B food/7 days,
Part C non-durables/30 days, Part D semi-durables and durables/365 days,

=[TO BE ANSWERED BY HEAD OF HOUSEHOLD AND IN HIS/HER ABSENCE, BY AN ADULT

Everything from Q24a to Q32c is gated =IF OWNER OCCUPIED (CODE 10 IN Q.2)= and
concerns the *parcel*: area, acquisition, tenure type, formal documents,

#+end_quote

whole section is

#+end_quote

*There is no rent question in Section 9.*

*** Rent lives in Section 15 Part C, as three consumption items

| code | printed label | channel that actually carries the value | HHs | median UGX/30 d |
|------+---------------+------------------------------------------+-----+-----------------|
|  301 | Rent of rented house | =CEC05= purchased value | 321 | 50,000 |
|  302 | Imputed rent of owned house | =CEC07= home-produced value | 2,570 | 30,000 |
|  303 | Imputed rent of free house | =CEC09= in-kind value | 186 | 30,000 |

(measured on =gsec15c.dta=, 3,066 households; =CEC04/06/08= quantities and
=CEC10= unit price are empty for all three items.)

So *Uganda does elicit imputed rent, but has no dedicated prompt for it.*  The
owner-occupier is asked CECO 7 -- "What would be the total value of these [Q8]
CECO 3] of [ITEM] if you were to sell them at the farm gate ... during the last
30 days?" -- with =[ITEM]= filled as "Imputed rent of owned house".  The
rent-free occupier gets CECO 9, "…if you were to sell them in the market".  The
elicitation language is the generic own-production / in-kind valuation prompt,
verbatim, applied to housing.

* 3. Recall windows, stated precisely

| | GLSS7 2016-17 | UNPS 2019-20 |
|-+---------------+--------------|
| food purchases | 6 asks x "since my last visit"; Main Report: "every sixth day i.e. after every five days … seven (7) times" -> ~5 d/ask, 30 d covered in a 35-d cycle | 1 ask, 7 days |
| food own-produce | same 6 visits (S8H q3-q8), plus a 12-month screener and months-consumed | same 7 days, same grid |
| non-food frequently purchased | inside the same 6 visits | -- |
| non-food less frequently purchased | 12 months, asked once at the last visit | 30 days (Part C) |
| semi-durables / durables (flow) | inside S9A's 12 months -- verified: 9A's item list carries =Air conditioners and air coolers=, =Computer (new)= / =(second hand)=, =Fan (Desk Fan)=, furniture and =Bicycles= | 365 days (Part D) |
| non-consumption (taxes, transfers) | no Part-E analogue; nearest is S11 Part A =TRANSFER PAYMENTS MADE BY HOUSEHOLD (INTERNAL REMITTANCES)=, 12 months.  Direct taxes were not located in GLSS7; this cell is *not* an exhaustive audit | 365 days (Part E) |
| durable stock | S12B, no window (age + purchase price + resale value) | S14, no window (number + current value) |
| rent | a *rate*: amount + time unit (daily…yearly) | 30 days |

Note the categories are not commensurable: GLSS7's rent is an amount *per a
time unit the respondent chooses*, so it is not a recall window at all, while
Uganda's is a 30-day flow like any other consumption item.

* 4. Findings worth acting on

Each is a statement about this repository, not about the surveys.

1. *Uganda's =recall.yml= says =basis: not-recorded= for all eight waves*, with
   the note "CONTENTS [RECALL] discussion is GSEC15C non-food, not the GSEC15B
   food module".  For 2019-20 the food module's window is now
   questionnaire-grade and unambiguous: the Part B banner reads
   "Part B: Food and Beverage (During the Last 7 Days)" on every page of the
   grid, and every quantity/value prompt repeats "during the last 7 days".
   That is =recall_days: 7=, =n_asks: 1=, =basis: questionnaire=,
   =recall_verbatim: 'During the Last 7 Days'=.  *Not changed here* --
   =recall.yml= is generated from =.coder/coverage/food_recall.csv=.

2. *Uganda's =CONTENTS.org= warning about the =[RECALL]= CAPI fill is about
   Part C and remains correct*, and 2019-20's labels confirm its last sentence:
   the placeholder is gone and the labels say only "CEC05. Value purchased".
   But the *printed* Part C questionnaire supplies what the extract dropped --
   every Part C prompt says "during the last 30 days", and the Part D prompts
   say "during the last 365 days".  The item-specific window the CONTENTS note
   describes is not visible in the 2019-20 printed form; if it still varies by
   item it does so inside CAPI, below the printed instrument.  Worth a
   re-check before anyone fills =RecallWindow= for this wave.

3. *A third stale passage in the GLSS7 manual -- a new instance of a recorded
   problem, not a new problem.*  =GhanaLSS/_/CONTENTS.org= already has a
   section "=GLSS7 Final Manual.docx= is stale where it describes the visit
   schedule", cataloguing §3.7 ("eleven visits", Table 2 at three-day spacing,
   "33-day cycle") against §3.6 ("exactly five (5) days apart"), the Manual's
   own seven-visit section table, the Main Report and the data, and tracing
   the eleven-visit text verbatim to GLSS3's =G3Intman.pdf= §1.9.
   *The Manual's Section 9 Part B paragraph quoted above belongs on that list
   and is not on it*: "it will be administered 10 times in all" and "since the
   last 3 days" are the same inherited eleven-visit / three-day schedule,
   surviving in a third place inside the same document.  Conclusion unchanged
   -- use the Main Report; =recall.yml= 2016-17 already does
   (=recall_days: 5=, =n_asks: 6=, =basis: questionnaire=).

4. *GLSS7 Section 9 Part B is not a food module, and the size of the gap is
   measurable.*  It is the frequently-purchased basket across all twelve
   COICOP divisions.  Measured on =g7sec9b_small.dta= against this wave's own
   =harmonize_food= table: *484 distinct =freqcd= item codes, of which 320
   carry a food Preferred Label and 164 do not* and are discarded by
   =food_acquired.py='s "Drop rows whose food code did not harmonize" step.
   So a third of the module is non-food, and the =Code_9b= column is the only
   thing separating the two -- an item added there by mistake pulls a haircut
   into =food_expenditures=.  =CONTENTS.org= already treats that column as
   load-bearing (Trap 8: "=.map()= is necessary but NOT sufficient …  The
   script asserts coverage explicitly"); what is written down nowhere is *how
   much* of 9B the column is holding back.

   /Answered 2026-09-12./  *1.931e6 cedi over the 30-day exposure, which is
   44.0% of 30-day non-food spending* (9A contributes 2.457e6 on the same
   basis; 2012-13 gives 38.7%).  Annualised, 9A + 9B-non-food is 71% of GSS's
   own =TOTNFD=, the remaining 29% being rent, utilities, education and health
   from other sections -- so the partition closes.  The exact counts:
   =harmonize_food= declares *485* =Code_9b= entries, *320* with a food
   Preferred Label and *165* blank; *164* of the 165 actually occur in
   =g7sec9b_small.dta= (one is declared but unused), which is the 164 above.
   The held-back vocabulary is now written down -- =nonfood_items_9b= in
   =GhanaLSS/_/categorical_mapping.org=, 204 harmonised items across the three
   waves with per-wave codes and verbatim labels.  It is not wired to any
   feature; serving 9B needs a =visit=-grain decision.

5. *Uganda Part D (=gsec15d=) and Part E (=gsec15e=) are read nowhere in the
   repo* -- =grep -rn 'gsec15d\|gsec15e'= over =lsms_library/= returns nothing.
   Uganda's =nonfood_expenditures= is Part C alone, i.e. the 30-day non-durable
   tier only; the 365-day semi-durable/durable flow and the non-consumption
   tier are unwired.  For a country whose =assets= table carries only a stock
   value, the missing Part D is the durables *flow*.

6. *Neither instrument asks an owner-occupier what their dwelling would rent
   for.*  Uganda reaches imputed rent by re-using the own-production valuation
   prompt on a housing item; GLSS7 does not reach it at all.  Anyone comparing
   a housing share across these two waves is comparing a number with a number
   that is not there.

7. *Printed-vs-shipped variable-name drift in UNPS8*, all verified against the
   shipped labels: =CECO*= -> =CEC*= (letter O to zero); =CEB12/CEB13= ->
   =CEB012/CEB013=; =CEB14A/B/C= -> =CEB14a/b/c=; printed =CEB02= absent from
   the file.  Anyone reading the questionnaire to wire a new Uganda wave should
   expect to translate.
