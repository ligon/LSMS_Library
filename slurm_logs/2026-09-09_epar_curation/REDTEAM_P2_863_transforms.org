* 1. =gross_crop_revenue= -- PASS

*The attack.*  If =Value_sold= were a household-crop total STAMPED onto every
=(u, condition)= row, the plain =sum(min_count=1)= would double-count.

*Ruling, from Uganda's own documentation.*  =Uganda/_/CONTENTS.org= §"Harvest
condition on =crop_production= (GH #323 / #637)" (lines 950-996): question 6
is compound -- /how much, in what unit, in what condition/ -- and "a household
that took some of a crop off a plot fresh and the rest after drying it answers
TWICE.  Both answers are separate source rows (2009-16) or separate parallel
column slots (2018-20)."  The sale questions ride the SAME slot: CONTENTS.org
line ~1198 enumerates slot 2's columns as =s5bq06a_2= / =s5bq06b_2= /
=s5bq06c_2= / =s5bq06d_2= / *=s5bq07a_2= / =s5bq08_2=* -- i.e. each condition
record carries its own quantity-sold and value-sold.  So =Value_sold= sits on
the condition grain as a DISTINCT fact per row, not as a repeated total.
Summing across =u=/=condition=/=season= is correct.

*Measured warm (Uganda, =crop_production=, 130,606 rows, index unique):*

| quantity                                                          |          value |
|-------------------------------------------------------------------+----------------|
| non-zero sale rows                                                 |         45,592 |
| exact repeats within =(t,i,j)= on Quantity_sold AND Value_sold     |  895 (1.9%)    |
| ... their summed value                                             |  198,824,150   |
| groups at the finest key =(t,i,plot,j,season)=                      |         44,598 |
| ... with >1 non-zero sale row                                       |            963 |
| ... of those, ALL rows share ONE Value_sold (the stamping shape)    |         *37*   |
| ... of those, rows DIFFER                                           |            926 |

The implementer's 895/45,592 reproduces exactly.  The decisive number is the
new one: at the finest key the stamping shape appears in *37 groups*, 926
carry genuinely differing values.  Not a stamped total.

=sum(Gross_crop_revenue) = 10,709,007,434.04=, byte-equal to
=sum(Value_sold)= and to the figure CONTENTS.org:1220 pins.  =by='j'= sums
back to the household total; on Malawi =by='j'= correctly returns a level
named =crop=.  Never-zero-fill verified (an all-NaN household is absent).

*Sharpening on the slot-2 citation.*  The =s5bq07a_2= / =s5bq08_2= columns
prove the INSTRUMENT asks 7a/8 once per harvest record; CONTENTS.org also
records that slot 2 is *not yet wired* (=CROP_COLMAPS= reads slot 1 only,
"Still not wired: it ADDS ~1 435 rows").  So the wired 2018-20 waves carry
one condition record per plot-crop-season; the multi-condition exposure is
the 2009-16 LONG format.  The 37-vs-926 measurement is load-bearing either
way, and it is measured over the wired data.

** Sub-finding (CONCERN): on MALAWI the risk runs the OTHER way -- under-count

Malawi's =data_scheme.yml= says the sale is a household-crop question
"attached to single-plot crops only".  =gross_crop_revenue='s docstring cites
that same sentence as "the shape that would produce" stamping -- *the wrong
direction*.  Measured warm:

| Malawi =(t,i,crop)= groups | count  | with any non-null Value_sold |
|----------------------------+--------+------------------------------|
| grown on ONE plot          | 87,440 | 23,206 (*26.5%*)             |
| grown on MORE than one     | 19,311 |    514 (*2.7%*)              |

A crop grown on two or more plots almost never carries a sale row at all, by
instrument design.  So Malawi's =Gross_crop_revenue= is a *systematic lower
bound*, not a double count, and the docstring says the opposite about the
very sentence it quotes.  Fix the framing; it changes what a Malawi user
should conclude from the number.

*Carry-over caveat, not this branch's defect*: CONTENTS.org §"WAITING 2015-16
=crop_production.Value_sold= was ~100x too small (#829)" -- fixed upstream,
but anyone quoting a LEVEL from this transform should read that entry.

* 2. =livestock_income= -- PASS on behaviour, FAIL on the name

** Rename verdict: RENAME, and =livestock_sales_value= is the right name

The implementer's self-criticism is correct and understated.  The function
computes exactly one of EPAR's seven terms (=value_lvstck_sold=,
=EPAR_UW_Malawi_IHS_W1.do:3345=), a GROSS SALES VALUE; EPAR's
=livestock_income= (=:5959=) is a NET income over seven terms.  Six are not
computable from our schema.  A name that promises "income" and delivers a
gross flow is exactly the class of error the repo's own coverage doctrine
(=sane= != =blessed=) exists to prevent.

*Recommend =livestock_sales_value=.*  It already matches the output column
=Livestock_sales_value=, so the call site and the column agree, and it is
unambiguous about gross-vs-net.  The one alternative worth naming is
=value_livestock_sold= (EPAR-literal, easiest to diff against their table);
it is worse here because it breaks the repo's =noun_phrase= transform naming
and does not match the column.  =livestock_sales_value= wins.

** What =hdds= counts -- STATED

Groups with at least one row where =Quantity > 0= *OR* =Expenditure > 0=
(=_consumed_mask=, an OR over whichever of the two columns exist).  Not
"any row"; not quantity alone.

** Hand case (3 households, verified)

| HH | rows                                                  | HDDS | expected | FCS  | expected |
|----+-------------------------------------------------------+------+----------+------+----------|
| A  | Maize q10 d5; Beef q1 d2; Milk q0 e0 d0                |    2 | 2        | 18.0 | 2*5+4*2  |
| B  | Maize q0 e300 d7; Rice q2 e200 d4 (same group)         |    1 | 1        | 14.0 | 2*min(11,7) |
| C  | Milk q=NaN e=NaN d3                                    |    0 | 0        | 12.0 | 4*3      |

A's Milk row (0 quantity, 0 expenditure) correctly does NOT count; B's
expenditure-only row does; B's two staples cap at 7 group-days, not 11.

=days== is keyword-only with *no default* (=fcs(food_acquired, *, groups,
days, weights=None, cap=7)=) and the missing-column error names the reason
(no country's =food_acquired= carries a days column; Tanzania
=hh_sec_j3.hh_j08= is the unwired source).  Corroborated: =grep 'Days'= over
the 40 =data_scheme.yml= finds it only under =food_coping=.

** Docstring divergences -- both present and correct

24-hour FAO recall vs. the LSMS 7-day window (so our HDDS is systematically
higher and not comparable to a published FAO number), and acquisition vs.
consumption (a pure purchase module makes the score a lower bound).  =fcs=
points at =hdds='s note 2 for the second.  Both stated plainly.

** One undocumented asymmetry (minor CONCERN)

=hdds= gates on the acquisition mask; =fcs= ignores it entirely and scores on
=days= alone.  Household C above therefore scores =HDDS 0= and =FCS 12= from
the same rows.  Each is defensible on its own terms (an FCS *is* a frequency
score) but the two functions are documented as siblings and the difference is
not stated in either docstring.

* 4. =fertilizer_rate= -- CONCERN (arithmetic right, two silent-emptiness defects)

- =nutrient='product'= -> p1 = 100/2 = *50.0 kg/ha*.

- p2 (Area NaN) and p3 (Area 0) are *ABSENT* from the output -- not NaN, not

** DEFECT A -- =fertilizer_rate(Malawi)= returns 331 rows, ALL EXACTLY 0.0

Measured warm:

#+begin_example
fertilizer_rate(Malawi plot_inputs, plot_features)  ->  331 rows
  Nitrogen_kg_per_ha:  mean 0.0  std 0.0  min 0.0  max 0.0
nitrogen_kg(Malawi plot_inputs)                     ->  331 rows, 331 zeros, 0 positive
#+end_example

*Root cause* (pre-existing in =nitrogen_kg=, GAP 2, NOT introduced here).
=nitrogen_kg= matches the =input= label by exact lower-cased equality against
=_NITROGEN_CONTENT='s keys.  Malawi's canonical labels and their fate:

| Malawi =input= label     |   rows | key in =_NITROGEN_CONTENT=? |
|--------------------------+--------+-----------------------------|
| NPK Fertilizer           | 26,775 | no (key is ='npk'=)         |
| Urea Fertilizer          | 24,125 | no (key is ='urea'=)        |
| Organic Fertilizer       | 11,075 | no                          |
| CAN Fertilizer           |  2,037 | no (key is ='can'=)         |
| D Compound Fertilizer    |    726 | no                          |
| DAP Fertilizer           |    273 | no (key is ='dap'=)         |
| *Other Fertilizer*       |    403 | *yes -- N share 0.0*        |

So the ONLY label that resolves is the one whose nominal N content is zero.
53,936 real fertilizer rows contribute nothing; 331 survive and are
identically 0.  =nutrient='product'= is hit the same way (the override
=({k: 1.0 for k in _NITROGEN_CONTENT})= reuses the same key set), so its
"median 137 kg/ha" is a statistic over =Other Fertilizer= alone.

*Why this is a red-team finding and not merely an inherited bug*: the branch
ships =test_malawi_warm_fertilizer_rate=, which asserts =len(out) > 0=,
=isfinite(...)= and =>= 0= -- *all three pass on a column of 331 zeros*.
That is precisely the "right shape, no content" failure CLAUDE.md devotes a
whole section to (=null_read_audit=, Site B).  The =_NITROGEN_CONTENT=
comment also advertises the product vocabulary as covering "Tanzania /
Ethiopia / Malawi / Nigeria", and =fertilizer_rate='s docstring repeats the
product list -- both read as coverage claims that Malawi does not honour.

*Recommend*: (a) warn (or refuse) when the numerator resolves to an
identically-zero column, or when the share of fertilizer-labelled rows that
match =_NITROGEN_CONTENT= falls below some floor; (b) strengthen the warm
test to assert a positive value, not merely a finite one; (c) file the
=_NITROGEN_CONTENT= key gap as its own issue (it is =nitrogen_kg='s, and it
also silently affects the already-shipped =nitrogen_kg=).

** DEFECT B -- =on='plot'= default returns an EMPTY frame on Uganda, silently

#+begin_example
fertilizer_rate(Uganda plot_inputs, plot_features)                 ->  (0, 1)   -- empty, no warning
fertilizer_rate(Uganda plot_inputs, plot_features, on='parcel')    ->  (509, 1), median 2.47 kg N/ha
#+end_example

Uganda's ag-module plot key is ={hhid}-{parcel}-{plot}= while
=plot_features.plot_id= is ={parcel}_{suffix}= -- exactly the divergence the
docstring names as the reason =on='parcel'= exists.  But the DEFAULT is
='plot'=, and the inner merge simply returns nothing.  Note that the sibling
transform this one is modelled on, =yield_kg=, defaults to =on='parcel'=.
The two defaults disagree, and the failure mode is a silent empty result on
one of the corpus's two flagship ag countries.  There is no Uganda warm test
for =fertilizer_rate= (the warm tests cover Malawi only), so nothing catches
it.

*Recommend*: raise (or warn) on an empty merge naming the two key
vocabularies, and either default to ='parcel'= as =yield_kg= does or
auto-select by measured match rate.

* 5. =crop_diversity= -- CONCERN (behaviour right and pinned; one docstring sentence is false)

** What counts as one crop -- MEASURED

An "occurrence" is one distinct =(t, i, crop, <plot level>, season)= tuple:
the =(u, condition)= rows are de-duplicated away, but *plots and seasons are
NOT*.  Hand case -- one household, maize on p1 (season A, in two conditions),
p1 season B, p2 season A, p2 season B; beans on p1 season A:

| basis                                  | shares    |        H |
|----------------------------------------+-----------+----------|
| implementation (occurrences)           | 4/5, 1/5  | *0.50040* |
| distinct crops per (t,i)               | 1/2, 1/2  |  0.69315 |

The implementation returns *0.500402*, i.e. the occurrence basis.  This is
documented in the Parameters block ("share of the household's crop
OCCURRENCES ... =(plot, season)= in Uganda, =(plot,)= in Malawi") and pinned
by =test_crop_diversity_count_separates_plots_and_seasons= and
=test_crop_diversity_count_dedups_the_u_and_condition_rows=.

** The false sentence

The Notes say of EPAR: "It also takes the shares over plot-crop ROWS rather
than over crops, so a crop on three plots contributes three terms.  *Neither
choice is reproduced here*".  The second half of that is *not true*: a crop
on three plots contributes three terms here too (three occurrences).  What
IS de-duplicated is only =u=/=condition= *within* a land-season unit.  The
Parameters block is right and the Notes sentence contradicts it; a reader who
reads the Notes will believe the index is over distinct crops and will
misread every number.  *Fix the sentence, not the code.*

** Undocumented comparability caveat

The occurrence key includes =season= where the country has that level
(Uganda) and does not where it doesn't (Malawi).  A Uganda household growing
an asymmetric crop mix across two seasons therefore gets a different H from
an otherwise identical Malawi household.  The count basis is *not
cross-country comparable*, and =Feature('crop_production')= users will not
be told.

** Everything else checks out

- =weight='value'= uses =Value_sold= and covers sold crops only; the
  docstring says so at length ("a household that sold one crop and ate three
  scores H = 0 ... NOT its cropping diversity").  Hand case matches
  (45/75, 30/75 -> 0.67301).
- =weight='area'= raises =NotImplementedError= naming the missing column.
- Sign: warm Uganda 16,555 households, H in [0, 2.6581]; warm Malawi 36,329,
  H in [0, 2.6593]; *no negative values*.  A single-crop household scores
  exactly =0.0= (the =h + 0.0= normalisation kills the =-0.0=).
- The docstring states EPAR's =sdi= is un-negated and equals =-H=, with the
  =.do= line references.  Correct.
