* Purpose and stance

This note documents a second open-source harmonisation effort over the
LSMS-ISA surveys, alongside the World Bank's own =LSMS-ISA_Ag= panel that
=slurm_logs/2026-06-13_wb_incidence_map/= already covers.  The stance is the
same for both: neither is canonical.  Each is a useful point of comparison,
built by careful people from the same raw files we read, and each therefore
tells us something when its numbers or its construction decisions differ
from ours.  What follows is a description of the EPAR project, a statement
of how it differs in kind from the WB panel and from this library, and a
pointer to the ranked learnings in =LEARNINGS.org=.  The survey behind it
was read-only: eight extractors and three verifiers over the EPAR code, our
=CONTENTS.org= files and our open issues; no data was built.

The one thing to hold on to while reading: EPAR's product is a fixed,
citable set of household-level summary statistics, and everything about
its design follows from that goal.  Ours is re-derivable item-level data
with the aggregation in code.  Neither is wrong for its purpose.  The
places where EPAR is useful to us are almost never "adopt their number";
they are "they already found the survey defect", "they already wrote the
conversion table", or "they already worked the construction decision we
have been deferring".

* What EPAR is

- Project :: Evans School Policy Analysis & Research Group (EPAR),
  University of Washington.  "Agricultural Development Data Curation",
  EPAR Technical Report #335.  Project leads C. Leigh Anderson and Didier
  Alia; some thirty-five named contributors over "over a decade" of work.
  Page: https://epar.evans.uw.edu/agricultural-development-data-curation/
- Licence :: BSD 3-Clause (University of Washington, 2017).  No barrier to
  reading, quoting, or adapting.
- Citation :: University of Washington, EPAR (2025), /Living Standards
  Measurement Study - Integrated Surveys on Agriculture: Processed Datasets
  for Ethiopia ESS, Malawi IHS/IHPS, Nigeria GHS, Tanzania NPS, and Uganda
  NPS from 2009-2022/.  The 2017 yield study carries its own DOI,
  10.5281/zenodo.1093718.
- Repositories :: cloned read-only under
  =/global/scratch/fsa/fc_jevons/ligon/reference/epar/=; commit hashes,
  dates and licences are in =NOTES.md= there.  Shallow clones, so the hash
  is the only vintage marker.
  - =LSMS-Agricultural-Indicators-Code= (v1.5, 2026-05-21): the TR#335
    pipeline.  One Stata do-file of roughly 300 KB per country-wave, a
    ~30 KB =readme.md= per wave, per-country nonstandard-unit conversion
    tables (Uganda, Malawi), panel keys (Malawi, Ethiopia), a master
    indicator list and a summary-statistics do-file.
  - =Household-Consumption-Data= (2026-06-10): a sibling product valuing
    household food consumption by source (purchases, own production, gifts)
    for sixteen countries, including six EHCVM countries, GhanaSPS, Kenya,
    India and Sierra Leone.  Fourteen of the sixteen are countries we hold.
  - =358_Estimating-Crop-Yields= (2017, frozen): a sensitivity study of
    yield construction decisions for Ethiopia ESS W2-W3 and Tanzania NPS
    W1-W3.
  - Not cloned: =LSMS-Data-Dissemination= (about 3 GB of final =.dta= per
    wave).  Its published estimates workbook,
    =EPAR_UW_335_AgDev_Indicator_Estimates.xlsx=, was fetched on its own
    and is recorded in =NOTES.md=.  Also noted but not surveyed: the
    AgQuery / AgQuery+ Shiny front ends and an NSSO-India indicator repo.

* Coverage

EPAR's TR#335 covers the five anglophone LSMS-ISA countries.  Against our
waves the match is close: EPAR holds every ISA wave we hold and none of
the two pre-ISA waves.

| Country  | EPAR waves                                         | Ours, beyond EPAR |
|----------+----------------------------------------------------+-------------------|
| Ethiopia | ESS W1-W5 (2011-12 ... 2021-22)                    | none              |
| Malawi   | IHS/IHPS W1-W4 (2010-11, 2013, 2016, 2018-19)      | 2004-05 (IHS2)    |
| Nigeria  | GHS-Panel W1-W5 (2010-11 ... 2023-24)              | none (we split each wave into its post-planting and post-harvest rounds) |
| Tanzania | NPS W1-W5 plus the 2019-20 SDD extended panel      | none              |
| Uganda   | UNPS W1-W5, W7, W8 (2009-10 ... 2019-20)           | 2005-06           |

The consumption-by-source repository reaches further: Benin, Burkina Faso,
Cote d'Ivoire, Guinea-Bissau, Senegal and Togo (EHCVM), GhanaSPS 2009-10,
Mali, Niger, and India, all of which we hold, plus Kenya and Sierra Leone,
which we do not.  That overlap is the same EHCVM set GH #438 names as the
first tier for extending our agricultural stack.

* What it produces

The unit of output is the indicator.  The master list
(=_Summary_statistics/EPAR_UW_335_master_list_indicators.xlsx=) enumerates
201 distinct indicators, expanded to 6,285 rows once every disaggregation
is counted: by sex of household head, by sex of the plot decision-maker
(male, female, or mixed), by farm-size bin (0, 0-1, 1-2, 2-4, over 4 ha),
by currency (local, consumption-PPP, GDP-PPP), and by weight type.  By
grain, 138 indicators are household-level, 32 are population-level means,
30 are individual-level, and 2 are household-plot.  By theme: crop
production 58, income 46, livestock 34, agricultural activity 18, adoption
15, inclusion 12, general household 10, gender equality 8.  (Every count
in this section was recomputed from the workbooks by the verifier.)

Each wave's do-file writes three final datasets, at household, individual
and plot grain, each assembled by a long chain of one-to-one merges over
some thirty already-collapsed intermediate files.  The published workbook
(47,927 rows over 27 country-wave instruments) reports mean, design-based
standard error, quartiles and N for rural households only, and is
permitted by the README to lag the code.  Only 150 of the 201 master-list
indicators appear in it, and nothing published is absent from the master
list.

Two of the disaggregations deserve a sentence each, because we have no
counterpart.  1,569 of the 6,285 rows are keyed on the sex of the
/plot decision-maker/, a plot-season categorical built from up to three
named household members with a head-of-household fallback.  And the
farm-size bins are a reporting cut, not a sample restriction: every rural
household is kept and tabulated by bin.

* How it is built

The shape of the pipeline is closer to ours than the WB panel is.  There
is one script per wave and one prose document per wave, and the prose is
organised as a fixed sequence of about fifty =### SECTION= headings, each
ending in a =KNOWN ISSUES= line.  Those readmes are the most directly
useful artefact in the repository for us: they are a second, independent
=CONTENTS.org=, written by people who read the same questionnaires.

The construction decisions that recur in every country, with the tag we
gave each one (reported / imputed / aggregated):

- Plot area :: imputed.  Parcel area times the plot's share of area
  planted within the parcel (Uganda), or GPS with a self-report fallback
  where the GPS value is zero or missing (Tanzania, Ethiopia).  Our
  =plot_features.Area= is whatever the wave reports, once.
- Harvest and sales in kilograms :: imputed.  Region-level median
  conversion factors, pooled across all seven Uganda waves, with a national
  fallback; a distinct table for the /sold/ unit.  Malawi borrows the
  IHS5 2019-20 conversion files for every earlier wave and interpolates
  shelled from unshelled by a per-crop ratio.  Nigeria and Ethiopia use
  the per-row or per-wave factors the WB ships.  EPAR reads Uganda's
  survey-reported per-row factor (=a5?q6d=) only to pool it into those
  medians; our =harvest_kg= keeps the row's own value first.
- Valuation of unsold harvest, livestock products and own-consumed food ::
  imputed.  The same finest-cell-first median-price ladder every time
  (enumeration area, then successive admin levels, then national), gated
  on ten or more observations.  This is the ladder our
  =median_price_valuation= already reproduces from the WB panel; three
  teams have now built it independently with the same threshold.
- Weights :: imputed.  Survey weights are rescaled to World Bank
  population totals per wave (rural and urban separately).  Ours are
  normalised to within-wave mean one at read time; the two agree on every
  weighted ratio and disagree on every level.
- Outliers :: imputed.  Winsorisation at the 1st and 99th percentiles,
  variable by variable, sometimes on a ratio and on its numerator and
  denominator separately, never on proportions, never within a
  sub-population.  We winsorise nothing and count instead.
- Priority crops :: a curated list (the do-file comment names twelve, the
  operative global fourteen: maize, rice, wheat, sorghum, millet, cowpea,
  groundnut, common bean, yam, sweet potato, cassava, banana, cotton,
  sunflower, pigeon pea) gets its own monocrop, input and yield tables.
  We carry every crop the survey asked about.
- Aggregation :: everything is collapsed to household grain inside the
  script and persisted.  This is the single structural axis on which EPAR
  and this library differ, and it is a difference of goal, not of care.

* Three products, one raw source

| | WB =LSMS-ISA_Ag= | EPAR TR#335 | LSMS_Library |
|---+---+---+---|
| Built by | WB LSMS team | EPAR, UW | this repo |
| Countries | 7 ISA | 5 ISA (+16 in the consumption repo) | 40, of which 7 ISA |
| Unit of output | one cross-country panel, four grains | per-wave indicator sets, three grains, plus a summary workbook | item-level tables per country, aggregation in code |
| Where aggregation lives | in the data | in the data | in =transformations.py= |
| Outlier policy | "deliberately refrained" from extensive correction | winsorise 1/99 | count, never clip |
| Weights | survey weights | raked to WB population | within-wave mean one |
| Unit conversion | WB factors | pooled regional medians, WB factors, interpolation | reported factor, then survey median, then inferred |
| Documented per-wave defects | supplementary tables (sparse) | =KNOWN ISSUES= per section per wave | =CONTENTS.org= per country |
| Silent corrections of source defects | yes (Uganda 2015-16 x100) | no (defect flagged in one section, consumed in the next) | fixed with a guard that asserts the defect is present |

The last row is the cautionary tale this survey was briefed with.  The
Uganda 2015-16 sale values are divided by one hundred in the distributed
release (GH #829).  The WB panel multiplies them back silently.  EPAR's
readme says "Sold values are inaccurate in W5" under =ALL PLOTS= and "No
issues in this section" under =GROSS CROP REVENUE=, which consumes them;
the published estimates carry the defect.  We fixed it with a guard.
Three defensible teams, three different behaviours on one defect, and the
only one visible from outside is the one written down.  "No issues in
this section" is not clearance, on their side or ours.

* What this survey produced

- =BRIEF.md= :: the shared brief (stance, sources, reading recipe, our-side
  crib, the reported/imputed/aggregated tag, the three-cell register).
- =FINDINGS_{Uganda,Malawi,Nigeria,Tanzania,Ethiopia}.org= :: per-country
  extracts.  Each reads our =CONTENTS.org= first, then EPAR's readmes and
  do-file sections, and places every EPAR known issue in one of three
  cells: found by both, found by EPAR only, found by us only.
- =FINDINGS_conversion_factors.org= :: EPAR's kg-per-unit tables and
  interpolation rules against our =harvest_kg= layers and =u= tables.
- =FINDINGS_consumption_by_source.org= :: the consumption-by-source
  repository against our =s= axis and =food_expenditures(basis=)=.
- =FINDINGS_taxonomy.org= :: the indicator inventory, a section-by-section
  incidence table against our feature vocabulary, the final dataset
  grains, the estimates workbook, the 358 yield study, and a mapping of
  EPAR's gender constructs onto the GH #439 LSMS+ design.
- =REDTEAM_A.org=, =REDTEAM_B.org=, =REDTEAM_C_439.org= :: verification of every cited line
  behind the ranked learnings and new-issue candidates.
- =LEARNINGS.org= :: the ranked, verified learnings, with what each would
  touch and its risk.

Not done here, by design: a numeric parity run against EPAR's final
=.dta= files or the estimates workbook.  The join keys and the workbook
are on disk; that is a separate dispatch.
