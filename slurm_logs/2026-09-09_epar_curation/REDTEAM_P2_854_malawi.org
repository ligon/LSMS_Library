** Is an ox-cart of maize at 400-700 kg plausible?  Yes, and the table checks itself

Two independent arguments.

1. *Physical.*  A Malawian two-ox cart bed is roughly 2.0 x 1.2 x 0.4-0.6 m
   (~1.0-1.4 m^3); shelled maize grain bulk density is ~750 kg/m^3, so a
   two-thirds-loaded cart is 400-700 kg.  Draft-animal literature rates a pair
   of oxen at roughly 500-1,000 kg payload on a flat track.  468 kg is squarely
   inside that.

** What a higher cap would admit on the reported side

The screen has three INDEPENDENT rules and only rule 2 is the cap:

1. unit is the kilogram and factor is not ~1 -- catches Uganda's ="u='Kg'"=
   claiming 260,000 *regardless of the cap*;
2. factor > =KG_FACTOR_MAX=;
3. factor is an integral calendar year in the year band on a non-kg unit --
   catches Uganda's 2,017 / 2,018 *regardless of the cap*.

So the Uganda pathologies the docstring names are caught by rules 1 and 3, or
are so far above any sane cap (147,000; 260,000; 860,000) that raising 250 to
1,000 does not admit them.  Measured on what I could reach: Nigeria's
=crop_production= (19,678 non-null reported =KgFactor= in the shared cache) has
*zero* rows above 250, so a cap of 1,000 changes nothing there.  I could NOT
measure Uganda: no =Uganda/var/crop_production.parquet= exists in the shared
cache and building it was out of scope for a read-only pass.  *That is a stated
gap, not a clean bill* -- Uganda is the country the cap was tuned on, and
whoever changes the cap must measure Uganda's (250, 1000] band first.

** Recommendation for @ligon

*Add a per-unit cap, do not raise the global one, and add a floor.*  Concretely:
keep =KG_FACTOR_MAX = 250= as the default and add a small
=_KG_FACTOR_MAX_BY_UNIT= table for bulk-transport containers
(={'ox-cart': 800, 'cart': 800, 'wheelbarrow': 200, ...}=, matched on the same
stripped-lower-cased unit text =_KG_UNIT_KEYS= already uses).  Reasons:

- the cap exists to catch *quantity-column-re-keyed* and *year-column* errors,
  which live on kg/bag/tin units, and a per-unit table leaves those untouched;
- a global 800 would let a mis-keyed 500 through on a =Kilogramme= row -- except
  rule 1 already catches that, so the real exposure is bag/tin units, exactly
  what a per-unit table protects;
- it recovers 4,639 rows that currently get *no* weight at all;
- it costs one dict and no new concept: the screen already reads the row's unit
  from =df=, so the lookup is one line inside =_screen_reported_factors=.

Under an ox-cart cap of 800 all 12 factors are admitted including the 682.  If
@ligon wants the 682 excluded too, 600 admits 10 of 12 (all but 610.0/631.4
Sugar Cane and 682.0) -- but Sugar Cane at 610 kg/cart is *more* defensible than
maize, so 800 is the coherent choice and the 682 should be flagged in
=CONTENTS.org= rather than screened out by an arbitrary number.

Separately: *the screen has no floor*, and Citrus / Ox-Cart = 18.9 kg is the
live instance.  Worth its own line in the ledger.
