* How to read this

Every item below survived a red-team pass (=REDTEAM_A.org=, =REDTEAM_B.org=,
=REDTEAM_C_439.org=) that opened the cited lines.  Where the extractors
were corrected, the corrected reading is what appears here; the original
findings files keep the uncorrected text and should be read with the
red-team tables beside them.  The ranking is by leverage for this library:
what it unblocks, how many countries it touches, and how cheap it is
relative to what we already have.  "Cheap" means the raw material is on
disk and the target function exists; it never means the design decision
is free.

The register discipline mattered.  The extractors placed 36 rows in
"EPAR found it and we did not"; the verifiers moved 16 of them back to
"both found it" because the row's own evidence cited our =CONTENTS.org=
or an open issue (=REDTEAM_A.org= section 0: Uganda 2 of 2, Malawi 3 of
11, Nigeria 3 of 7, conversion factors 0 of 2; =REDTEAM_B.org= register
spot-checks: Ethiopia and consumption 8 of 11, Tanzania 0 of 3).  What
survives as genuinely new is listed in section 3.  Uganda contributes
nothing new; that is a compliment to =Uganda/_/CONTENTS.org=, not a gap
in the survey.

Three things EPAR does that we should not import are recorded once, in
section 4, so nobody rediscovers them and proposes them as fixes.

* 1. Ranked learnings

** L1. Two conversion-factor sources are already in our DVC tree, unread

Ethiopia and Malawi both ship WB conversion files that EPAR reads
throughout and we never open.

- Ethiopia :: =Crop_CF_Wave{2,3,4}.dta= and =crop_cf_wave5.dta= (crop by
  unit by region) have sidecars in four waves; no Ethiopia script reads
  them, and =Ethiopia/_/data_scheme.yml:120= says outright "NO unit->kg
  conversion" for =crop_production=.  =ET_local_area_unit_conversion.dta=
  has sidecars in 2011-12 through 2018-19, two of the four blobs already
  in the local cache, and zero references anywhere; =Ethiopia/_/CONTENTS.org:1036-1039=
  says these area factors "are NOT in the repo".  That sentence is wrong,
  and it is the one register row in Ethiopia that is a genuine new
  finding: we hold the file and document a wrong reason for not using it.
  EPAR's W5 do-file (=EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W5.do:324=) explains the
  missing 2021-22 sidecar: the WB did not ship the file for W5 and EPAR
  borrowed the earlier waves'.
- Malawi :: =ihs_seasonalcropconversion_factor_2020.dta= and
  =ihs_treeconversion_factor_2020.dta= sit under
  =Malawi/2019-20/Data/Cross_Sectional/= with sidecars; only the food
  file (=ihs_foodconversion_factor_2020.dta=,
  =2019-20/_/food_acquired.py:17=) is wired.  =Malawi/_/CONTENTS.org:653-660=
  lists the DVC sources and omits both crop files.  EPAR borrows the
  IHS5 files for every earlier Malawi wave
  (=Malawi IHS/Nonstandard Unit Conversion Factors/README.md=).

/Touches/: =harvest_kg= gains a layer ("WB-shipped factor") whose rank
relative to =reported= and =survey_median= is a decision, not a default;
Ethiopia's do-file records a duplicate row in =Crop_CF_Wave5= (crop 74,
unit 62, factors 4.34 and 6.125) that EPAR resolved by hand
(=EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W5.do:349=), so the loader must de-duplicate
deliberately rather than average.  /Risk/: medium; real scoped work,
two countries, two loaders (the fallback ladders differ).

** L2. The food-side kg inference has no item axis, and it is live

=conversion_to_kgs= (=transformations.py:782=) groups by =['t','m','i']=
then =['t','m','i','u']= and returns a factor keyed on =u= alone; a
"bunch" of bananas and a "bunch" of groundnuts get one pooled factor.
The crop-side ladder, =median_price_valuation=, carries
=item_keys=('j',)= (=transformations.py:2853=).  The same library
disagrees with itself.  GH #850 already names =_get_kg_factors= and
"needs j in the baseline group", so this is not a new issue; what the
survey adds is the measurement that the gap is in production today for
every country whose price-ratio inference fires, that
=Ethiopia/_/conversion_to_kgs.json= was built with =groupby('unit_cd')=
alone, and (red-team A's own observation, unmeasured) that step 2 takes
the median of =Expenditure= rather than =Expenditure/Quantity=, so the
inferred factor is kilograms per transaction row, equal to kilograms per
unit only where =Quantity == 1=.

/Touches/: =transformations.py:782,1051=; every cached =food_quantities=
and =food_prices=.  /Risk/: high, because the output moves corpus-wide;
that is also why it ranks second.

** L3. Per-row layer provenance on the food side, matching the crop side

=harvest_kg_factors= discloses which layer served each row
(=KgFactorSource=, =transformations.py:1899=).  The food side returns a
bare number.  EPAR's Malawi factor table ships a =source= string with
every factor (=EPAR_UW_conversionfactors.do:482,486=, six values).
/Touches/: the return shape of =_get_kg_factors= / =conversion_to_kgs=,
a public signature.  /Risk/: medium; it pairs naturally with L2.

** L4. The plot decision-maker grain is built, saved, then thrown away

EPAR's Uganda, Tanzania and Ethiopia do-files each construct a
person-grain intermediate, =Uganda_NPS_W5_dm_ids.dta= with columns
=(hhid, parcel_id, plot_id, season, id_no, pid, formal_land_rights,
personid, female, age, hh_head)= (=EPAR_UW_Uganda_UNPS_W5.do:413-424=),
and its Tanzania (=W3.do:261=) and Ethiopia (=W4.do:515=, without a role
slot) twins, then =collapse (mean) female= to one plot-season
categorical (=W5.do:433-439=: male-only, female-only, anything
fractional is mixed, head's sex where nothing matched).  Roughly 1,569
of the 6,285 master-list rows hang off that categorical.

This is the LSMS+ axis of GH #439 worked from the ISA side.  Verified:
all twenty-one raw decision-maker variables (=a3aq3_3=, =a3aq3_4a/b=,
=ag3a_08_1/2/3= and their =ag3a_08b_*= spelling in 2019-20, =s3q13=,
=s3q15_1= and =s3q15_2= in Ethiopia) are zero-hit across the repo, and
the files carrying them are already opened by our wave scripts for other
columns (Uganda =plot_labor.py:25-26=, =plot_inputs.py:22-23=; Ethiopia
=plot_inputs.py:28=, =plot_labor.py:21=, =plot_features.py:20=; plus
=sect8_1_ls_w4= and =sect2_pp_w4= for the asset side).  Column-adds,
not new acquisitions, for Uganda 2015-16, Ethiopia 2018-19 and Tanzania
2019-20/2020-21.  Not cheap for Tanzania 2012-13, which lives in our
=2008-15/= tree with no =AG_SEC_*= files at all.

The design cost is real and the extractor understated it.  The proposed
=(t, i, plot, pid, role)= grain omits =season=, by which both Uganda
(=:433=) and Tanzania (=:263=) collapse; under our index rule that is a
missing level, and =_normalize_dataframe_index= would =first()= away the
second season.  And the repo has already reserved the table: "who farmed
it this season" is what =data_info.yml:227-231= assigns to a future
=plot_cultivation=, with a filing procedure in
=slurm_logs/DESIGN_erhs_plot_features_2026-05-20.org:54-57= that says do
not retrofit =plot_features=.  Ethiopia's =holder_id= is not a problem:
=ethiopia.py:747,1000= already fold it into =plot_id=.  The tie rule
needs a documented decision; the 2017 358 study and the 335 pipeline
partition identically (red-team C checked all 27 combinations) and
differ only in whether an all-missing plot falls back to the head's sex.

The same do-files build women's asset-ownership constructs from
=sect11_hh_w4= (Ethiopia, =(hhid, asset_cd)= by twelve owner slots,
=W4.do:3497-3600=) and Uganda's =AGSEC2A=/=AGSEC6A=/=gsec14=
(=W5.do:3174-3274=), and Tanzania W3 has the full set of women's
sections too (=W3.do:2930,3049,3241,3387=), contrary to the extractor's
first reading.  EPAR drops the item code before saving, so the claim
"one collapse from =individual_assets (t,i,j,pid)=" is two collapses;
the upside is that the raw file is item-grain by owner slot, finer than
anything EPAR kept.  #439's flagship feature is constructible from data
we already hold, without Cambodia.

/Touches/: a =plot_cultivation=-family schema proposal under #167/#439,
one column-add per wave script in three countries, one transform for
=dm_gender=.  /Risk/: medium on design, low on wiring.

** L5. The median-price ladder: three teams, one design, one real delta

EPAR's crop-price ladder, its livestock-price ladder and its
consumption-by-source ladder, the WB panel's =valuation_median_crops=,
and our =median_price_valuation= all select the finest geographic cell
with at least ten observations (EPAR's Ag repo gates =obs > 9=; the
consumption repo =obs > 10=, so eleven; the Uganda readme's "at least 9"
is a prose slip).  The loop direction in EPAR's Stata is an
implementation detail; the selection is ours.  The differences that are
real, stated once (=REDTEAM_A.org= "Cross-file reconciliation"):

1. EPAR's medians are weighted, by the population-raked
   =weight_pop_rururb=, and Nigeria's are quantity-weighted on top
   (=W4.do:696,731=); ours is unweighted.
2. EPAR's national rung is gated by the same threshold; ours is an
   unconditional fallback (=transformations.py:3018-3022=).
3. EPAR's livestock ladder fills narrow-to-broad, prefers the
   household's own observed price, and ends unconditional
   (=Malawi W1.do:3339-3343=), the opposite idiom.
4. The consumption repo's first rung is the household's own purchase
   price for the same item (=EthiopiaW5_...do:423=, =UgandaW4_...do:693=),
   undisclosed in its README; the Ag repo keeps that as a parallel
   =value_harvest_hh= series instead.

/Learning/: a =weight_col= kwarg on =median_price_valuation= is additive
and closes the only construction delta; a docstring line recording the
convergence is worth having.  Do not write "EPAR's ladder" without saying
which of the two.  /Risk/: low.

** L6. Own-production valuation as an opt-in transform, with its bias stated

Three extractors (Ethiopia, Tanzania, consumption) independently arrived
at the same proposal: value =food_acquired='s =produced= and =inkind= rows
through =median_price_valuation=, as =food_expenditures(basis='total',
valuation='median_price')= or similar, never stored.  GH #585 already asks
for it, so this is corroboration, not discovery.  Two things the survey
adds.  First, EPAR's ladder is built entirely from the purchase side
(=food_purch_value/food_purch_qty=), and our own price study measured the
consequence: in Uganda, own-consumption valued at the purchase price sits
about 23 percent above what a sale actually fetched
(=slurm_logs/price_sources/SYNTHESIS.org:750-753=; Uganda only, and the
farmgate instruction is at =:732-736=).  Any such kwarg must say which
side of the farmgate-market gap it lands on.  Second, the same-household
same-item rung (learning L5, item 4) is cheaper and less objectionable
than the spatial ladder and could be offered first, as =valuation='own_price'=.

For Tanzania this bears on #846: the instrument asks no price for
own-consumption, and EPAR imputes one because nothing was asked.  The
=not-asked= verdict stands; the imputation is a separate, analyst-facing
choice.  /Risk/: low as an opt-in, medium if it ever became the default.

** L7. The SOLD-unit conversion, independently confirmed as buildable

EPAR keys sale and harvest conversions separately everywhere it converts
at all: Uganda has a distinct =UG_Conv_fact_sold_table= (waves 3-5 only,
=Master_Conversionfactor_table.do:271,286=) merged on
=(crop_code, sold_unit_code, region)= (=W5.do:474-498=).  This is GH #824,
which Malawi closed on 2026-09-08 and Uganda and Nigeria have not.  Do
not copy EPAR's 2018-19 wiring: it feeds the condition column as the unit
for season A (=W7.do:520-523=), and red-team A showed that all twenty of
Uganda's condition codes are also valid unit codes in EPAR's table, so
the merge fabricates plausible kilograms rather than missing ones.
/Risk/: medium; the fix must re-derive the 2018-19 column choice from our
own =CONTENTS.org:1009-1027=.

** L8. Small transforms we lack and can write from stored items

- =livestock_income= :: EPAR imputes it from =number_sold= and
  =number_slaughtered= times a laddered price (=Malawi W1.do:3339-3346,5959=).
  We have no such function.  Caveat: our =livestock.Value= is a reported
  hypothetical ("if sold today"), not a transaction price; a port would
  silently change what the number means.
- =rcsi= :: =rcsi = s1 + s2 + s3 + 3*s4 + 2*s5= (=Malawi W1.do:4525=), from
  =food_coping= counts we already store.  EPAR builds it for Tanzania W5
  (=W5.do:2614=) and not for Uganda W5 (a wave-specific absence, per its
  readme).  Note EPAR's phase cutoffs leave
  =rcsi == 19= in no phase (=:4531,4535=).
- HDDS / FCS :: food-group counts over diet recall; EPAR's group mapping
  (=Tanzania W5.do:2538-2560=) is a starting point to re-derive, not copy.
- =dm_gender= :: see L4.
- Fertilizer application rate, gross crop revenue, Shannon crop
  diversity :: one-line reductions over items we hold.

** L9. Malawi's shelled/unshelled ratio is a technique our =condition= axis lacks

EPAR computes a per-crop shelled-to-unshelled ratio once and uses it to
fill a missing condition's factor from the other's
(=EPAR_UW_conversionfactors.do:381-390=), then hardcodes which crops the
ratio applies to (=:442-444=).  Nothing in =harvest_kg_factors= infers
across conditions.  A principled version (its own N threshold, no crop
allowlist) could be a fifth layer.  Related: =malawi.py:915='s
=_harvest_block(condition=None)= accepts the parameter and never uses it
anywhere in the module, while EPAR keys every Malawi factor merge on
=(region, crop, unit, condition)= (=W1.do:1326=).  /Risk/: medium-high.

** L10. Plausibility counts where we have none

Tanzania 2020-21 =crop_production= carries raw quantities up to
7,500,000 kg with no screen in =crop_production_for_wave=
(=tanzania.py:1163=; numbers verified against the warm parquet).  The
learning is the gap, not EPAR's remedy, which is a hand-coded
single-household deletion (=Tanzania W5.do:578-579=).  A report-only
count, as =harvest_kg='s factor screen already does, fits the house
rule.  Also worth a look: EPAR's theory that implausible GPS areas are
unit errors rather than decimal errors (=W5.do:254=) against our
2,500-acre clamp at =tanzania.py:1046-1054=.

** L12. TLU tables: a provenance question, not a coefficient swap

- Condition-blind collapse :: every EPAR wave sums across harvest
  condition (=Master_Conversionfactor_table.do:248=); an independent
  codebase falling into the trap #323/#637 fixed.
- Kg-unit factors must equal one :: 6 of 128 kg-unit region medians in
  EPAR's own Uganda table deviate from 1 by more than 1 percent, two with

  the written one.

  is built from it, =Master_Conversionfactor_table.do:84-91=) but pools
  it into medians; we keep the row.  The Uganda findings file says the
  opposite and is wrong on that line.
- #820, confirmed and bounded :: the section 12 tree-crop module exists
  as a separate file only in W1-W3; ESS4/5 folded it into section 11
  (=Ethiopia W4.do:759=; no =sect12_ph= sidecar for 2018-19 or 2021-22).
  The issue's 2015-16 measurement stands; the fix is a W1-W3 wiring job,
  and the later waves need a check that section 11 now carries the tree
  crops.  EPAR dropped nothing; its extractor's claim that it had was
  wrong.
- =months_food_inadequate= :: the same months-count as EPAR's FOOD
  PROVISION; a clean match, while FIES stays ours only.

* 4. Not to import, recorded once

- Winsorisation :: 1st/99th percentile into a new =w_= column, raw
  preserved (hand-rolled =_pctile= plus =replace=; =winsor2= appears only
  in the 358 study).  The 358 study's own side-by-side of winsorising
  against a MAD rule is the sharpest argument for never storing one
  choice.  We count and never clip.
- Population-raked weights :: every EPAR wave rescales to WB rural and
  urban totals, and that raked weight is what weights every EPAR median.
  Ratios agree with ours; levels do not.  A one-line note in each
  country's =CONTENTS.org= under weights is enough.
- Household-level intermediates :: EPAR persists roughly thirty
  collapsed =.dta= files per wave and merges them.  Reconstruct from our
  items; never import one.
- The brief's three tags were one short :: filters (rural-only, priority
  crops), scalar rescales, and value/quantity ratios fit none of
  reported / imputed / aggregated.  A future pass should add =selected=
  or =derived=.
