1. *Malawi mints four kg spellings and only one of them returns 1.0.*  The
   inference groups on the RAW label, so Malawi's map contains
   ='Kg' -> 1.000=, ='Kilogram' -> 1.432=, ='Kilogramme' -> 1.125= and
   ='kg' -> 0.440=.  None of these is ever DELIVERED (all four are seeded
   =KNOWN_METRIC=, and the metric rung outranks both inference rungs -- the
   ledger 4 note about test-plan item 7 is correct), so this is a diagnostic,
   not a defect.  But it is a diagnostic worth reading: measured,
   *604,600 of Malawi's 971,531 rows anchor the baseline, and 419,996 of those
   (69%) are survey =Quantity_kg= rows in NON-kg units* against only 146,280
   kg-spelled rows.  A ratio of 0.44 says those cfactor rows imply a price per
   kilogram 2.3x higher than Malawi's own kilogram rows do.
2. *At item level the self-test is a real test, and it does not pass
   everywhere.*  1.000 with 100% of items within 10% in Tanzania, Niger, Mali
   and EthiopiaRHS; 0.962 of items in Uganda; but 0.415 (Malawi), 0.451
   (Nigeria) and 0.158 (GhanaLSS).  Where the baseline is pure-kg the answer
   is an identity; where it mixes units the deviation measures how badly the
   inference's central assumption (one price per kilogram across all units of
   one item) holds among units where the conversion is KNOWN.  DESIGN.org says
   this about GhanaLSS and it is right; it is equally true of Malawi and
   Nigeria and the design does not say so.

*Verdict: PASS on the arithmetic, with the ceiling stated.*  The docstring
should say that the self-test is an identity, not a confirmation, in a country
whose kg-known rows are its kg rows -- it currently implies more than it earns.

| case | served? | tag |
|------+---------+-----|

| n_base=4, spread *1.24* | *yes* | *True* |
| n_base=4, spread *1.25* (boundary) | *yes* | True (the comparison is =<=)|
| n_base=4, spread *1.26* | *no* | -- |
| n_base=3, spread 1.24 | yes | True |
| n_base=3, *one report with a NaN price* | *no* | -- effective n=2 |
| n_base=2 | no | -- |
| n_base=1 | no | -- |

3 refused.  Every case is as specified.  *PASS.*

** CONCERN: =min_reports= gates the POOLED support, not the =(t, j, u)= estimate

=conversion_to_kgs='s docstring says "*min_reports* is the number of step-2 rows
behind a ``(t, item, u)`` estimate".  The code computes
=detail['support'] = n.groupby(['j','u']).sum()= and then screens
=support >= min_reports= -- i.e. the *total across every wave*.  The same is
true of the strict-baseline screen (=strict_n.groupby(ju).sum()=).  Constructed:

| frame | per-wave support | pooled support | served? |
|-------+------------------+----------------+---------|
| 5 waves x *1* Bunch row each, fat kg baseline in each | *1* | 5 | *yes*, =support = 5= |
| 1 wave x *4* Bunch rows, fat kg baseline | 4 | 4 | *no* |

One report per wave in five waves clears a floor that four reports in one wave
does not.  The crop side's precedent, =_survey_median_factors=, gates
=(country, t, u, condition)= -- *per wave* -- so this is a divergence from the
pattern the ledger says it reused.

D6 arguably licenses it: if the per-wave estimates are noise around one
container weight, then pooling their support is pooling evidence about the same
quantity, which is what pooling the estimates already assumes.  I do not think
it is wrong.  I think *the docstring says something the code does not do*, and
on a floor this is exactly the sentence a future reader will rely on.  One-line
fix; no behaviour change.

** The IQR argument is a scale error.  The conclusion survives; the sentence does not.

=FOOD_KG_TIGHT_TOLERANCE='s docstring says an =IQR/median= gate "admits 21 of
Uganda's 28 in-band cells at a tolerance of 1.10 and 26 at 2.00 -- a statistic
that barely moves across a two-fold change in tolerance is not discriminating".
I reproduce 21 and 26 exactly.  *But 1.10 and 2.00 are not comparable
tolerances for the two statistics.*  =max/min <= 1.25= means the reports lie
within about +/-12%; =IQR/median <= 1.10= means the interquartile range is 110%
of the median -- an enormously looser gate.  Swept at ITS own scale:

| country | IQR/med <= 0.05 | <= 0.10 | <= 0.25 | <= 0.50 | (<= 1.10) | (<= 2.00) |
|---------+-----------------+---------+---------+---------+-----------+-----------|
| Uganda | 0 | 2 | 6 | 16 | 21 | 26 |
| Malawi | 2 | 2 | 5 | 10 | 20 | 24 |
| Nigeria | 0 | 1 | 5 | 10 | 16 | 20 |
| Ethiopia | 0 | 0 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 8 |
| EthiopiaRHS | 1 | 2 | 5 | 8 | 10 | 13 |

At =IQR/median <= 0.10= Uganda admits 2 cells whose =max/min= p90 is *1.32* --
a perfectly discriminating gate, and the "cells whose three reports differ by
25x" only enter at the absurd tolerances.  So the docstring's stated evidence
does not support its claim.

*The decision still stands, on the other ground the same docstring gives*: at
N = 3 or 4 the interpolated IQR is the range wearing a robust name, =max/min=
is scale-free and reads as exactly the sentence the exception is for.  Keep
=max/min=; *rewrite the paragraph* -- as written it is a measurement a reviewer
can falsify in ten minutes, sitting in a module constant.

** 6.4 Niger: CONCERN (strong).  Disclose, do not block -- and the reason generalises

*First, what Niger's own =CONTENTS.org= says, because it constrains the check.*
Its section "Prices: what Niger can and cannot compare (2026-09-08)" states:

#+begin_quote
=food_acquired= is EHCVM-only and =community_prices= is ECVMA-only, so a
household unit value can never be compared with a surveyed community price in
Niger.
#+end_quote

That is correct and I reproduce it: =food_acquired= exists only in 2018-19 and
2021-22, =community_prices= only in 2011-12 and 2014-15, and my own generic
=u = 'Kg'= check returned *zero* overlapping =(t, j)= cells for Niger where the
other five countries returned 33-175.  So the answer key below *crosses eras*.
I use it anyway, and say why: it is a RATIO of two prices inside one wave
(price per Tiya / price per kg), which is inflation-free, and a tiya is a
physical bowl whose size does not move in a decade.  It is *suggestive, not
decisive*, and every load-bearing claim below is restated on era-free evidence.

*The answer key.*  Niger's 2011-12 =community_prices= price the same items in a
SIZED unit (=Sac de 100 kg=) and in the =Tiya=, giving kilograms per Tiya:

| j | Tiya rows in =food_acquired= | truth (kg/Tiya) | OLD factor | NEW factor | NEW / truth | rung |
|---+------------------------------+-----------------+------------+------------+-------------+------|
| Mil | 10,107 | *2.56* | 2.08 | *0.328* | *0.13* | =item_unit= |
| Mais en grain | 3,932 | *2.50* | 2.08 | *0.264* | *0.11* | =item_unit= |
| Sorgho | 3,240 | 2.56 | 2.08 | 1.250 | 0.49 | =unit= |
| Niebe/Haricots secs | 4,775 | 1.17 | 2.08 | *1.111* | *0.95* | =item_unit= |
| Oignon frais | 508 | 1.87 | 2.08 | *1.950* | *1.05* | =item_unit= |
| Tomate sechee | 582 | 1.11 | 2.08 | 0.450 | 0.41 | =item_unit= |

The item axis nails Niebe (0.95x) and Oignon (1.05x) -- exactly what it was
built to do -- and is *8-9x too small on the two items that carry 27% of
Niger's food expenditure*.  (Note the OLD column is one pooled number, 2.08,
for every item; it is near the cereal truth by luck and wrong for the rest.)

*The era-free evidence, which is what the concern actually rests on.*  Per
wave, for the three biggest Tiya items:

| j | wave | baseline reports | median price/kg from them | =Kg= rows | Tiya rows | median price/Tiya | per-wave kg/Tiya |
|---+------+------------------+---------------------------+-----------+-----------+-------------------+------------------|
| Mil | 2018-19 | *0* | -- | *0* | 4,886 | 750 | *no estimate* |
| Mil | 2021-22 | *9* | *2,285.7* | 13 | 5,221 | 750 | *0.328* |
| Mais en grain | 2018-19 | *0* | -- | *0* | 1,743 | 700 | *no estimate* |
| Mais en grain | 2021-22 | *12* | *2,840.3* | 13 | 2,189 | 750 | *0.264* |
| Niebe | 2018-19 | *0* | -- | *0* | 2,655 | 700 | *no estimate* |
| Niebe | 2021-22 | *16* | *675.0* | 20 | 2,120 | 750 | *1.111* |

Four facts, none of which needs the 2011-12 file:

1. *2018-19 contributes NO estimate at all* -- it has zero kg-known rows for
   any of these items.  So the delivered "cross-wave median" (D6) is a single
   wave's number applied to both, and 4,886 of the 10,107 Mil Tiya rows are
   served a factor estimated on a wave they are not in.
2. *Nine reports govern 10,107 rows.*  The =(2021-22, Mil)= baseline clears
   =min_baseline = 5= with nine usable price-per-kg reports, unconditionally
   and with no dispersion check, and that number is then the denominator for
   every Tiya, Boite, Tongolo and Moudou row of millet in the country.
3. *Those nine reports disagree with the 5,221 rows they govern by 7.6x, in the
   same wave, on the same item.*  A Tiya of millet trades at 750 FCFA; the Kg
   rows say millet is 2,286 FCFA/kg; so the Kg rows assert a Tiya is 0.33 kg.
   Niger's cereal retail price in 2021-22 was 250-450 FCFA/kg (WFP/FEWS), and
   at 300 FCFA/kg a 750-FCFA Tiya is 2.5 kg -- the size a tiya actually is.
   *The 13 Kg rows are the anomaly, not the 5,221 Tiya rows.*
4. *Where Niger's Kg rows are sane the item arm is right.*  Cowpea at 675
   FCFA/kg is a plausible 2021-22 price, and its Tiya comes back at 1.111 kg
   against an answer key of 1.167.

*So the diagnosis is not "the branch is wrong".*  The item arm is exactly as
good as the item's own kg-labelled rows, and it removes the dilution that used
to hide a bad set of them.  The defect is 13 rows of Niger data; the branch is
what makes it visible and localised.  That is an argument FOR the change, and
an argument against reading every movement as a correction.  Niger's delivered
17.5 kg per household-week (5.85 persons, so ~0.43 kg/person/day on a 7-day
recall, before the natively-carried unconvertible rows) is low rather than
impossible, and I do not lean on it.

*The structural point, which is bigger than D5's tolerance.*  =min_baseline= is
an ABSOLUTE floor.  Nine reports clear it and then govern 10,107 rows with no
relation between the two counts and no signal when they disagree.  The design
spent its D5 effort on whether to admit 3-4 reports when they agree within 25%
-- worth 0-0.7 pp of coverage -- and did not ask whether a cell that clears the
floor should be checked against the cell it governs.  A relative gate
(=baseline_n= against the rows it serves), or simply a warning when the
baseline's implied price per kilogram is far from the step-2 rows' own implied
one, is the design-level response.  *Section 3's ungated strict rung and this
are the same finding seen from two sides*, and together they are the D5 critique
worth the maintainer's attention.

*Actions*: (a) say in the PR that Niger's -56.1% is not signed off as a
correction; (b) file the =u = 'Kg'= defect (13 rows, millet at 2,286 FCFA/kg,
maize at 2,840) as a Niger issue and add it to =Niger/_/CONTENTS.org=, whose
price section is otherwise the best in the corpus and does not yet record it;
(c) file the relative-floor question as a =transformations= issue.

* 10. Cross-country pooling

*The premise is not live.*  =Feature.__call__= (=feature.py:579-620=) loops over
countries, calls =getattr(Country(name), table)()= *per country*, and only then
=pd.concat=s.  The derived food transform therefore never sees a frame with
more than one country in it, so nothing pools =(t, j)= across countries.

What IS there: =food_kg_factors= hardcodes
=group_levels = [n for n in ('t','m','i') if n in idx_names]= -- unchanged from
the pre-#850 =_get_kg_factors=, so *pre-existing* -- and would pool countries if
someone hand-built a concatenated frame and called the transform on it.  The
new =_WAVE_LEVELS = ('country', 't')= anticipates that case but is
*currently unreachable*, because =country= is never in the =index= list that
reaches the filter.

*Recommendation: not an issue on its own.*  It is a latent hazard with no live
caller and a dead guard.  Either drop ='country'= from =_WAVE_LEVELS= or add
it to the =group_levels= scan so the guard is real; a one-line comment saying
which would be enough.  Filing a GitHub issue for a code path nothing calls
would add backlog without adding safety.
