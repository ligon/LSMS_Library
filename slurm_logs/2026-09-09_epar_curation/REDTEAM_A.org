** "What I did not read" -- the parked =region!=.= item.  ADJUDICATED; the framing is WRONG

The findings park this as "W7/W8 =if region!=.= vs W5's =if region==.=" and call
it "a possible EPAR-internal inconsistency ... flagged, not adjudicated".  Both
the pattern and the mechanism are now established, and the wave-level framing is
wrong -- *the inversion varies WITHIN a file*:

| wave | sold (ALL PLOTS) | harvest (ALL PLOTS) | sold (GROSS CROP REV) |
|---+---+---+---|
| W1 | =region==.= (:523) OK | *=region!=.=* (:562) | *=region!=.=* (:684) |
| W4 | =region==.= (:505) | =region==.= (:541) | =region==.= (:775) |
| W5 | =region==.= (:497) | =region==.= (:528) | =region==.= (:678) |
| W7 | *=region!=.=* (:556) | =region==.= (:592) | =region==.= (:738) |
| W8 | *=region!=.=* (:730) | *=region!=.=* (:764) | =region==.= (:1014) |

*Mechanism.*  The line reads =replace s_conv_factor_X = sn_conv_factor_X if
<cond>=, where =s_= is the REGIONAL merge result and =sn_= the NATIONAL one, and
the trailing comment in *all fifteen* instances is identical: "We merge the
national standard conversion factor for those HHID with missing regional info."
With =region!=.= the national factor overwrites the regional one for every
household that HAS a region -- i.e. the regional table is discarded exactly
where it applies, and the intended national fallback for region-missing rows
never fires.  Five of fifteen call sites are inverted, across three waves, in a
way no wave-level or format-level explanation covers.  This is a copy-paste
defect, not a long-vs-wide artefact, and it changes published kilograms in W1
(harvest + gross crop revenue), W7 (sold) and W8 (sold + harvest).

Two caveats I can state and one I cannot.  (a) The findings cite =W7.do:552= for
this line; it is =:556=.  (b) W7's section carries =W7.do:549= =*Region is not
present= immediately above the regional sold merge, so if =region= is genuinely
absent for every row there, =region!=.= is never true and the W7 *sold* effect
is "the national fallback never fires" -- missing kilograms rather than wrong
ones.  (c) Which of the two obtains in W1 and W8 depends on how populated
=region= is after their merges, which cannot be settled without the microdata.
The defect is real either way; its numeric sign is not settled here.

** The EPAR median-price ladder -- the correct facts

There is *no N>=9 vs N>=10 disagreement in these four files*: all three
crop-price ladders gate on =obs > 9=, i.e. N>=10.  Verified:
- Uganda =W5.do:542= =replace price_unit=price_unit_`i' if obs_`i' > 9=
- Malawi =W1.do:1347-1348= =replace price_unit = price_unit_`i' if obs_`i'_price > 9=
- Nigeria =W4.do:863-864= =replace price_unit = price_unit_`i' if obs_`i'_price>9 & price_unit_`i'!=.=

*Iteration order and selection rule.*  All three iterate *broad to narrow*
(=foreach i in country region district county parish ea= at =W5.do:534=;
=country region district ta ea= at =W1.do:1346=; =country zone state lga ea=
at =W4.do:862=) with an unconditional overwrite at each qualifying level.  The
net selection is therefore *the finest cell with >=10 observations* -- which is
*exactly* the rule our =median_price_valuation= implements
(=ours: transformations.py:2869-2875=: "the median observed price of the finest
cell whose count >= threshold").  Consequently
=FINDINGS_Uganda.org:102='s "pools the price ladder narrow-overrides-broad in
one pass rather than our EA-first cascade" is *OVERSTATED*: the loop direction
is an implementation detail; the selection is identical.

*The =hhid= rung is not a rung.*  In all three countries =hhid= appears in the
*collapse* loop but not in the override loop; it is renamed to a *parallel*
column and used as a separate valuation basis:
- Uganda =W5.do:545-546= (=replace price_unit_hh=price_unit if price_unit_hh==.=
  / =replace price_unit=price_unit_hh if price_unit==.=)
- Malawi =W1.do:1351-1354=
- Nigeria =W4.do:866-867=
So =FINDINGS_Nigeria.org:126='s "household -> EA -> LGA -> state -> zone ->
country" is *CORRECTED*: the household is not a ladder rung, and the code's
order is the reverse of the listed one.

*The real differences from ours, stated once:*
1. *EPAR's medians are weighted; ours are not.*  =collapse (median) ...
   [aw=weight]= at =W5.do:506/512=, =W1.do:969/975/980/985=,
   =W4.do:704/710=.  And =weight= is EPAR's *population-raked*
   =weight_pop_rururb= in every case: =W5.do:482-483= (=merge ... keepusing(
   weight_pop_rururb)= / =ren weight_pop_rururb weight=), =W1.do:504-505=
   (=ren weight weight_sample= / =ren weight_pop_rururb weight=),
   =W4.do:695-696=.  Nigeria goes further and makes the median
   *quantity*-weighted: =W4.do:696= =gen weight=qty*weight_pop_rururb=, and
   again at =:731= =replace weight = weight_pop_rururb*qty_kg=.
   Ours takes an unweighted median (no weight parameter on
   =median_price_valuation=, =ours: transformations.py:2847-2856=).
2. *EPAR's national rung is gated by the same >9; ours is unconditional.*
   =ours: transformations.py:2878-2879=: "The national median is the
   unconditional fallback".  Under EPAR, a crop whose country cell has <10

   household's own price (or nothing).

   obs_X >= 10=, preferring the household's own observed price and ending in an
   *unconditional* country rung.  Any statement of the form "EPAR's ladder"
   should say which of the two.
