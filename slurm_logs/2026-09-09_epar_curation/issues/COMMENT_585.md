EPAR's ladders and our `median_price_valuation` converge on the same selection rule -- the finest geographic cell that clears an observation gate -- so the design here is corroborated rather than novel. Ag-repo paths below are relative to `reference/epar/LSMS-Agricultural-Indicators-Code/` (HEAD `abfbdc9301242527b7543606aad1ad6eed2e6530`); consumption-repo paths to `reference/epar/Household-Consumption-Data/` (HEAD `0e9ccf2981e7cb8448605925a5111d2835bf1e31`).

The gates, verified line by line: the Ag repo tests `obs_X > 9` -- for crop prices, and on the same gate for its input-price and wage cascades (`EPAR_UW_Nigeria_GHS_W4.do:1375`, `:1511`, `:1186`), i.e. N>=10 (Uganda `EPAR_UW_Uganda_UNPS_W5.do:542`, Malawi `EPAR_UW_Malawi_IHS_W1.do:1347-1348`, Nigeria `EPAR_UW_Nigeria_GHS_W4.do:863-864`); the consumption repo tests `obs_X > 10`, i.e. N>=11, one stricter (`Tanzania_W5_Food Consumption by source.do:348-372`). Ours is `cell_count >= threshold` with `threshold=10` (`transformations.py:3025-3026`, default declared at `:2864`), so N>=10, matching the Ag repo. The Ag-repo loops run broad to narrow with unconditional overwrite, which nets out to the same "finest qualifying cell wins" as our finest-to-coarsest `imputed.isna()` guard.

The deltas any `valuation=` kwarg must disclose:

1. **Weighting.** EPAR's medians are weighted by `weight_pop_rururb`, its population-raked weight, not the survey weight (Uganda `W5.do:482-483`, Malawi `W1.do:504-505`, Nigeria `W4.do:695-696`). Nigeria's unit-price collapse is further quantity-weighted (`W4.do:696`, applied at `:704`), though its per-kg collapse at `:735` uses the unweighted-by-quantity form despite `:731`. `median_price_valuation` (`transformations.py:2858-2867`) takes no weight argument; a `weight_col` kwarg closes this cleanly.

2. **The national rung.** EPAR gates it by the same threshold, so a country cell below it yields no ladder price at all. Ours is an unconditional fallback (docstring `:2892-2893`, implemented `:3029-3033`). That is a different failure mode at the coarsest rung, not just a different number.

3. **The two repos put the household's own price at opposite ends of the ladder.** In the Ag repo it is not a rung: a parallel `value_harvest_hh` series is built beside the main `value_harvest` (Uganda `W5.do:548`, Malawi `W1.do:1357`, Nigeria `W4.do:874`, Ethiopia `W3.do:1262`, Tanzania `W5.do:573`). Only two of the five let it touch the main series, and then only after the ungated country rung has failed (Uganda `W5.do:546`, Ethiopia `W3.do:1265`); Malawi never does, and Nigeria and Tanzania fall back to a reported value instead (`W4.do:873` `val_harvest_est`, `W5.do:574` `ag4a_28`). The consumption repo inverts this: the household's own purchase price for the same item is the FIRST rung, tried before any spatial cell and conditional on the consumed unit matching the purchased unit (Ethiopia `EthiopiaW5_...do:423`, Uganda `UgandaW4_...do:693`). A `valuation='own_price'` option built on the wrong repo's ordering would silently invert which price wins when both exist.

4. **The purchase-side bias.** Every EPAR rung is a purchase transaction. Our five-source price comparison measured what that costs in Uganda: own-consumption valued at the purchase price sits about 23% above what a sale fetched (`slurm_logs/price_sources/SYNTHESIS.org:750-753`; the farmgate instruction motivating the concern is at `:732-736`). That number is Uganda-only -- GhanaLSS has no comparable sale-price source. A `valuation=` kwarg landing on the purchase side of this gap must say so in its docstring, not merely note that a bias exists.
