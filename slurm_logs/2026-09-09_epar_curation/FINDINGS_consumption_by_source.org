* Coverage (their waves vs ours; what they build for this scope)

EPAR's =Household-Consumption-Data= ships one =.do= file per country-wave
(16 country series, several multi-wave) and appends all of them
(=Append all surveys_SSA.do:18-30=) into one pooled
=ALL_SURVEYS_food_consumption_value_by_source.dta=. Of the 16, we hold 15 of
their countries under a different name; Kenya (KIHBS) and Sierra Leone (IHS)
are genuinely absent from our corpus (matches the BRIEF's list). Their
"Ghana" is GhanaSPS (2009/10, GhanaW1) -- their table calls it
=Ghana_SPS_W1=, one wave only; we have more GhanaSPS waves declared for other
features but this pass did not check =food_acquired= coverage there.

What EPAR builds (README.md:1-40, verbatim methodology, five bullets under
"Consumption"): per-household, per-crop-category annual VALUE of food
consumed, split into =food_consu_value= / =food_purch_value= /
=food_prod_value= / =food_gift_value=, each in nominal LCU and 2017 PPP, plus
a top-1%-winsorized variant of each, at the =(hhid, crop_category1)= grain
-- confirmed by the metadata-only read below. No item (=j=) grain survives
into the final product; no unit (=u=) axis; no acquisition-source index
level -- source is columns, not an index level, and is fixed at 3
(purchase/production/gift; EPAR's own README omits "other").

Metadata-only read, =Final Data/Uganda_UNPS_W4_food_consumption_value_by_source.dta=
(pyreadstat, values never touched): 39,032 rows x 41 columns. Grain is
=(hhid, crop_category1)= -- there is no =item_code= column in the final
product; it existed at intermediate stages
(=UgandaW4...do:604-758=) and is aggregated away before the file is written.
Columns: identity/demographic block (=hhid=, =fhh=, =age_hh=, =hh_members=,
=adulteq=, =nadultworking*=, =nchildren=, =nelders=), geography
(=adm1=,=adm2=,=adm3=,=GID_1=), =weight=, =rural=, interview date parts,
=crop_category1=/=crop_category2=, =conv_lcu_ppp=, then eight value columns
(=food_{consu,purch,prod,gift}_value_{lcu,ppp}=) and their eight winsorized
counterparts (=w_food_*=). No standard errors, no per-source unit, no
provenance flag distinguishing "value came from a median-ladder impute" from
"value came from the household's own report."

* Construction decisions by feature area (each tagged reported/imputed/aggregated, with .do:line)

- *Item/source extraction* (=reported=). Uganda: three source blocks
  (=food_purch_*=, =food_prod_*=, =food_gift_*=) read directly off GSEC15B
  columns =h15bq4/5=, =h15bq8/9=, =h15bq10/11= (=UgandaW4...do:290-300=).
  Nigeria (post-harvest visit shown, W4): =s10bq9a/b= purchase,
  =s10bq6a= production, =s10bq7a= gift (=NigeriaW4...do:676-689=). Senegal
  EHCVM: =s07bq07a/b= purchase, =s07bq04= production, =s07bq05= gift
  (=SenegalW1...do:392-399=). All three are one-to-one =rename= from the
  raw module -- =reported=, matching our =food_acquired= extraction
  philosophy exactly.
- *Unit harmonization* (=reported=, with a large hand-built lookup, =imputed=
  only where the survey's local unit is converted to kg/litre without a
  reported conversion factor). Uganda's 99-value =unit_new= recode
  (=UgandaW4...do:315-448=) and volume-to-mass conversions
  (=UgandaW4...do:590-596=, e.g. =/0.05= for 50ml sachets) are =imputed=
  conversion FACTORS, analogous to our =conversion_to_kgs=/=_get_kg_factors=
  but with no disclosed provenance layering (no reported-vs-median-vs-
  library-inferred distinction the way =harvest_kg= documents at
  =data_info.yml:560-583=).
- *Purchase price* (=imputed=, mislabeled as reported by the README). See
  KNOWN ISSUES #1. =price_unit=food_purch_value/food_purch_qty= at
  =UgandaW4...do:604=, =NigeriaW4...do: (equivalent block ~line 390s)=,
  =SenegalW1...do: (equivalent ~line 400s)=.
- *Own-production / gift valuation ladder* (=imputed=, the marquee
  construction of this repo). Three rungs before the spatial ladder: (a)
  household's own price if same item+unit (KNOWN ISSUES #3); (b) the
  EA->...->national median-of-observed-purchase-price ladder gated at >10
  obs per cell (KNOWN ISSUES #2); (c) unconditional national median as final
  fallback (=UgandaW4...do:733-737= mirrors our =median_price_valuation='s
  national fallback at =transformations.py:2986-2989=).
- *Food-away-from-home* (=aggregated=, a single crop bucket, KNOWN ISSUES #6).
- *Annualization* (=aggregated=, a fixed x52/x12 multiplier per country,
  KNOWN ISSUES #7): =UgandaW4...do:778-781=, =NigeriaW4...do:531-534=,
  =SenegalW1...do:502-505=, =GhanaW1...do:432= (x12).
- *PP/PH averaging, Nigeria only* (=aggregated=, KNOWN ISSUES #5):
  =NigeriaW4...do:886-895=.
- *Winsorization* (=imputed=, top-1% clip replaced with the p99 value, per
  country-wave, KNOWN ISSUES #4): =UgandaW4...do:797-806=,
  =NigeriaW4...do:903-910=, =SenegalW1...do: (equivalent block ~519)=.
- *Crop-category aggregation, two levels* (=aggregated=): =crop_category1=
  (raw-item read time, ~80 buckets, e.g. =UgandaW4...do:200-284=) then
  =crop_category2= (a coarser ~15-bucket fold, =UgandaW4...do:882-908= and
  identically in =NigeriaW4...do:958-987=). Note the =crop_category2= recode
  block is *character-for-character identical* between Uganda and Nigeria
  (same 30+ =replace= lines, same category names) -- a genuine cross-country
  crosswalk EPAR maintains by hand, not derived from any shared config file.
- *PPP conversion* (=imputed=, a per-country-wave scalar): =gen conv_lcu_ppp=,
  a single hardcoded constant per Instrument value
  (=UgandaW4...do:928-930=: =0.000991261= for W4; =NigeriaW4...do:1004-1006=:
  =0.007958188=; =SenegalW1...do:604-606=: =0.004172273=), applied to both
  the raw and winsorized value columns. No PPP handling exists anywhere in
  =lsms_library= (out of scope for us; noted only as a capability gap, not a
  bug to fix here).

* Ranked learnings (what we could take, why, what it would touch in our repo, and the risk)

1. *Offer the median-price valuation ladder as an OPT-IN transform for
   =food_acquired='s own-production/gift rows, reusing =median_price_valuation=
   verbatim rather than re-deriving it.* We already built this exact ladder
   for =crop_production= (GAP 7, =transformations.py:2847-3030=) and have an
   open ticket asking for precisely this on the food side (GH #585, GH #575).
   EPAR is a second independent implementation of the same idea (EA/grappe->
   ...->national, >10 obs, median of observed purchase unit-value) --
   corroborating evidence the design is sound, not proof it should be the
   default. *Touch*: a new kwarg, e.g.
   =food_expenditures(basis='total', valuation='median_price')=, threading
   through =median_price_valuation= with =geo_levels= from =cluster_features=
   (=v=, =Region=/=District=) the way GAP 7 already does for crop_production.
   *Risk*: LOW as an opt-in (never the default, never stored -- consistent
   with "core never aggregates silently"); MEDIUM if anyone is tempted to
   make it the =basis='total'= default, because =SYNTHESIS.org='s own Uganda
   evidence (next item) says the analyst-facing "right" valuation is contested
   even among defensible choices.

2. *Do NOT import the "at or near the market/purchase price" convention as if
   it settles the farmgate-vs-market question -- our own price-sources study
   already measured the size of the resulting bias and it's large.* The
   Uganda UNPS interviewer manual explicitly instructs farmgate valuation for
   own-consumption (=SYNTHESIS.org:739-742=); the corpus shows households
   actually answer at-or-near the PURCHASE price (median gap 0.0000, ratio
   1.000, on 1,666 pooled Uganda+GhanaLSS cells, =SYNTHESIS.org:666-676=), and
   the three-way Uganda comparison puts own-consumption valuation ~23% above
   the crop sale price (=SYNTHESIS.org:723-728=, "own-consumption is valued
   about 23% above what a sale actually fetched"). EPAR's ladder is built
   ENTIRELY from =food_purch_value/food_purch_qty= (the purchase side) --
   it never uses a =crop_sale_price= or =community_price= source as an
   alternative valuation, so EPAR's own-production values are, by
   construction, on the market/purchase side of the farmgate-market gap our
   study quantified. *Touch*: if we build learning #1, document explicitly
   which side of that gap the median-ladder valuation sits on, citing this
   evidence, rather than presenting it as neutral. *Risk*: reputational/
   analytical, not code -- the risk is a future user citing our
   =valuation='median_price'= output as "the" consumption value without
   knowing it embeds a market-side markup our own corpus shows is real and
   sizable (Deaton & Zaidi 2002 is cited on both sides, =SYNTHESIS.org:738-746=).

3. *The =crop_category1=/=crop_category2= hand-built crosswalk (identical
   across Uganda and Nigeria, and reused verbatim for the EHCVM countries'
   own item lists) is a candidate reference for extending
   =Aggregate Label= coverage to the EHCVM countries (GH #438-adjacent),
   NOT a template to copy uncritically.* We already know our own
   =Aggregate Label= folds sometimes cross a processing boundary that
   shouldn't be crossed (GH #833: "harmonize_* folds raw and processed into
   one =j= in 5 countries: Malawi groundnut 5.0x, Uganda maize 3.33x are
   milling"). EPAR's crosswalk has the identical shape of risk: e.g.
   Uganda's =crop_category1= folds "Rice" (=item_code==110=, presumably
   husked/milled) with no raw-paddy distinction, and folds all Oils/Fats
   (=item_code== 127-129=) into one bucket regardless of processing.
   *Touch*: when someone works GH #438 for EHCVM =Aggregate Label= columns,
   diff EPAR's ~80-category scheme against ours item-by-item as one input,
   but re-run the GH #833 processing-boundary check on every fold before
   accepting it -- don't inherit the same defect from a second source.
   *Risk*: LOW to consult, MEDIUM if adopted without the #833 check.

4. *EPAR's food-away-from-home fold is coarser than ours -- confirms our
   design is already ahead here, not a learning to take, but worth recording
   so nobody "fixes" our item-level away/home handling into EPAR's shape.*
   See KNOWN ISSUES #6. Uganda's =GSEC15B= =h15bq6=/=h15bq7= are already
   folded into =s='purchased'= per-item in
   =lsms_library/countries/Uganda/_/uganda.py:391,452-506=, keeping the item
   identity that EPAR discards. *Touch*: none needed; note this so a future
   "align with EPAR" refactor doesn't regress it. *Risk*: N/A (defensive
   documentation only).

5. *EPAR's same-household-same-item rung (KNOWN ISSUES #3) is a real, cheap,
   zero-imputation-risk idea worth separating from the ladder proper: value
   a household's own production/gift of item X at THAT SAME HOUSEHOLD's own
   reported purchase price for X, when it purchased X in the same unit that
   same recall period, before reaching for any spatial median.* This is
   arguably less objectionable than the EA/parish/... ladder (learning #2's
   concern) because it uses the respondent's own revealed price rather than
   a neighbourhood's, and EPAR's own README never discloses it as a distinct
   step -- we would be doing this MORE transparently than EPAR does.
   *Touch*: a narrower, separately-flagged sub-case of learning #1's
   =valuation=' kwarg, e.g. =valuation='own_price'= as a first-choice fill
   before =valuation='median_price'= falls back. *Risk*: LOW -- it's a
   same-household join, no cross-household information leakage, and
   degrades to "no fill" cleanly wherever the household bought nothing in
   that unit.
