** 1d CORRECTED -- Uganda: a plot-season with both ids blank does not reach the fallback

The findings say the head-sex fallback fires when "no id matched, *or
both ids blank*".  Both-blank rows are deleted at =:421= (=drop if
pid==. | plot_id==.=) *before* the collapse, so no group for that
plot-season ever exists in the collapse output; there is nothing for
=:438-439= to act on.

What the fallback actually covers, precisely:

1. Plot-seasons where every named id survived =:421= but failed the
   roster merge at =:423= (=keep(1 3)= retains the master row with
   =female==.=; Stata's =collapse (mean)= then yields =.= for the
   group).  EPAR's own comment counts 993 such unmatched rows.
2. A *degenerate second case the findings miss*: the merge at =:434=
   (=merge m:1 hhid using ..._hhsize.dta, nogen=) has *no =keep()=
   option*, so every household in =hhsize.dta= with zero surviving
   decision-maker rows is *added* as a row with missing =parcel_id=,
   =plot_id= and =season=.  Those rows then take the head's sex at
   =:438-439=.  They are household-grain rows with a null plot key
   sitting inside a plot-grain file.

Tanzania and Ethiopia do not have case 2: their equivalent merges are
=keep(3)= (=:270=) and =keep(1 3)= (=:528=), neither of which adds
using-only rows.  So the three countries' "missing" handling is *not*
uniform, and the findings present it as if it were.

The findings' third sub-claim -- "a named id that fails to match the
roster is silently absorbed into the missing-then-head-fallback path
(no separate non-household-person flag)" -- is CONFIRMED for all three
(=:423= Uganda, =:258= Tanzania, =:511= Ethiopia all use =keep(1 3)= or
its equivalent and no downstream flag is created).

* 2. CORRECTED -- the 358 and 335 tie rules are provably the SAME partition

Both cites are accurate:
- 358: =358_Estimating-Crop-Yields/EPAR_UW_358_Tanzania TNPS 2012-13 LSMS-ISA_W3.do:133-135=
  -- three merges (=:103-104=, =:114-115=, =:123-124=) producing
  =dm1_female/dm2_female/dm3_female=, then an explicit categorical
  =gen/replace= triple.
- 335: =EPAR_UW_Tanzania_NPS_W3.do:263-265=,
  =EPAR_UW_Uganda_UNPS_W5.do:433-439=,
  =EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W4.do:520-530= -- the mean-of-female rule.

But the findings' *reason* is wrong.  They assert the two "partition the
same raw ids differently whenever a plot has more than 2 named
decision-makers with an uneven split", while conceding "this pass did
not construct a minimal disagreeing example".  There is none.
Enumerating all 27 combinations of {male, female, missing} over three
slots:

- 358: category 1 iff every non-missing slot is 0 and at least one is
  non-missing; 2 iff every non-missing slot is 1 and at least one is
  non-missing; 3 otherwise-with-at-least-one-non-missing; missing iff
  all three missing.
- 335: =mean(female over non-missing)= = 0 iff all non-missing are 0;
  = 1 iff all non-missing are 1; fractional otherwise; missing iff none
  non-missing.

These are the same predicate written twice.  A 2-male/1-female plot gives
mean 1/3 -> mixed under 335, and fails both all-male and all-female
tests -> mixed under 358.  Exhaustive check over the 27 combinations:
*zero disagreements*.

*The real divergences, which the findings do not name:*

1. *Missing handling.*  335 substitutes the household head's sex when
   no id resolves (=W3.do:272-273=).  358 has *no fallback at all* --
   =decision_maker_gender= stays missing, and =:142-144= even guards the
   dummies with =if decision_maker_gender!=.=.  This is the substantive
   methodological disagreement between EPAR's two products, and it is
   the one that changes numbers.
2. *Season coverage.*  335 appends =AG_SEC_3B= and carries =ag3b_08_*=
   fallbacks (=W3.do:239, :249, :251, :253=); 358 reads =ag3a_*= only
   (long rainy season), so its plot universe is narrower.
3. *Output grain.*  335 collapses by =(hhid, plot_id, season,
   cultivated)= (=:263=); 358 keeps =(y3_hhid, plotnum)= with
   =field_cultivated= as a column (=:140=).

So the findings' Learning #4 ("pick and document ours, don't inherit
either silently") *survives*, but the justification must be replaced:
the two products disagree on *what to do when nobody is named*, not on
how to break a mixed tie.  As written, learning #4 would send an
implementer hunting for a tie rule that is not the difference.
