* 2. The API contract -- PASS (two notes)

** Scalar =median_price= does NOT run =own_price= first -- CONFIRMED

Synthetic frame: household =A= holds a produced =X/kg= row and one purchase of
=X/kg= at unit price 10; eleven other households buy the same =(t,j,u)= at 20,
so the pooled median is 20 and A's own price is 10.  Measured:

| =valuation=                   | price served | =ValuationSource= |
|-------------------------------+--------------+-------------------|
| =('own_price','median_price')= | 10.0        | own_price         |
| =('median_price','own_price')= | 20.0        | median_price      |
| ='median_price'=               | 20.0        | median_price      |
| ='own_price'=                  | 10.0        | own_price         |

So the tuple order *is* the precedence, and a scalar runs exactly the rung
named.  Row 2 vs. row 1 is the discriminating pair: the two orders give
different fills, and the first rung wins.

*Is the docstring unmistakable?*  Yes, in both places a caller would look, and
in imperative voice: =food_acquired_valued= -- "A scalar means **exactly that
rung** -- ='median_price'= runs the spatial ladder alone, it does NOT quietly
run ='own_price'= first"; and =food_expenditures_from_acquired= repeats it
verbatim in its own =valuation= entry.  Both then name
=('own_price','median_price')= as EPAR's consumption-repo ordering.  This is
the deliberate departure from the issue brief's plain reading; the ledger §6
already flags it for @ligon and I have not relitigated it.  The docstring
does what a red-team can ask of it: the departure cannot be discovered by
surprise.

* 3. =own_price= scoping -- PASS (one CONCERN)

** No leakage -- CONFIRMED on a constructed frame

Six rows, =valuation='own_price'= only:

| row                                           | expected  | measured                     |
|-----------------------------------------------+-----------+------------------------------|
| A purchased Y/kg, 2 @ 100 total (p=50)        | reported  | reported                     |
| A produced Y/kg                               | 50        | own_price, price 50.0        |
| *B* produced Y/kg (B never bought Y)          | *nothing* | none, price NaN              |
| A produced Y/*piece* (different =u=)          | nothing   | none, price NaN              |
| A produced Y/kg in *2021* (different =t=)     | nothing   | none, price NaN              |
| A produced *Z*/kg (different =j=)             | nothing   | none, price NaN              |

So the rung is keyed on exactly =(t, i, j, u)= and consults no other
household, unit, wave or item.  The code confirms it: =_own_price_rung= groups
=work[pool]= on =['_t','_i','_j','_u']= and reindexes onto the same four keys.

** Repeat purchases at different prices -- quantity-weighted, and documented so

Household A buys =X/kg= twice: 1 unit for 10 (p=10) and 3 units for 90 (p=30).

- sum/sum = (10+90)/(1+3) = *25*
- mean of the two prices = 20
- unweighted median of the two prices = 20

*Measured own price = 25.0.*  The docstring says "the sum of that household's
purchased =value_col= over the sum of its purchased =quantity_col=" and
"a household that bought an item twice contributes one quantity-weighted price
rather than two" -- which is what it does.  =tests/test_own_production_-
valuation.py::test_own_price_pools_a_households_repeat_purchases= pins it.
Statement and behaviour agree; no drift.

** CONCERN 3a -- =i= as a COLUMN silently disables the rung

=_own_price_rung= resolves the household axis with
=_level_or_column(pd.DataFrame(index=index), 'i')= -- it is handed =df.index=,
not =df=, so it can only ever see =i= as an *index level*.  If =i= is a column
it returns =None= and the rung returns an all-NaN offer:

: rows where i is an index level -> own_price serves 1 row
: same frame with i reset to a column -> own_price serves 0 rows, NO warning

This is inconsistent with the function's own treatment of the other keys:
missing =u= raises ("the purchase price the valuation uses is per unit"),
missing =s= raises, and missing =j= or =t= raises (unhelpfully, with pandas'
"All arrays must be of the same length").  Only =i= degrades silently, and the
in-function comment ("No household axis: there is no 'same household' to look
up") reads as if the frame genuinely lacked households.

*No live impact*: =food_acquired= carries =i= in the index for both audited
countries (=['i','t','v','j','u','s']=), and the =Country= path always hands
the transform a finalized frame.  But =food_acquired_valued= is exposed as a
public analyst-callable transform whose docstring invites row-by-row auditing,
and a user who did =fa.reset_index('i')= first would get =own_price: 0= with
no signal.  Suggested minimal fix: pass =df= rather than =df.index= to
=_own_price_rung= (=_level_or_column= then finds the column), or warn.

** Is +51.8% plausible?  Three independent checks say yes, and one says it is
   if anything CONSERVATIVE.

*Check A -- the own-production share it implies, against the price study.*
+51.751% uplift means non-purchased food is 34.10% of the *valued* total
(produced 23.79 + in-kind 10.31).  Reference points:

- =slurm_logs/price_sources/home_production_shares.csv=, *Uganda* (where the
  survey reports its own values, so nothing is imputed): =agg_produced=
  0.296-0.661 plus =agg_inkind= 0.034-0.086 across eight waves.  Measured
  independently here on the same corpus: Uganda's non-purchased share of
  valued expenditure is *42.36%* (produced 34.34 + in-kind 8.02).
- Same file, *GhanaLSS*: =agg_produced= 0.135-0.306 across seven rounds.

Malawi's imputed 34.10% therefore sits *inside* the observed cross-country
band and *below* Uganda's directly-reported 42.36% -- for a country that is,
if anything, more subsistence-agricultural than Uganda.  Note the file has *no
Malawi row*, and that is not an omission: Malawi reports no own-production
value at all, which is the whole reason this kwarg exists.  So the check is a
peer comparison, not a self-comparison, and it is the strongest one available.

The wave profile is also the right shape: the uplift falls monotonically
2004-05 -> 2019-20 (+101.6 -> +47.4), i.e. the own-production share shrinks as
the country urbanises and monetises.  A unit or key defect would not be
monotone in time.

*Check B -- is a native-=u= basis inflating it?  No: measured factor 1.0198.*
For every imputed row I recomputed the value it would have taken at its own
=(t, j, u)= cell's *mean* purchase unit value (=Sigma purchased Expenditure /
Sigma purchased Quantity= in that exact cell), which is unit-consistent by
construction:

: rows with a same-cell purchase pool : 283,556 / 283,556 (100.00%)
: Sigma imputed delta                 : 145,469,000
: Sigma at the cell MEAN unit value   : 142,648,000
: ratio                               : 1.0198

So the ladder's median-of-medians choice puts Malawi's own-production 2.0%
above what the *same physical quantities* would cost at the *same cells'* own
average purchase unit value.  There is no unit mismatch: =u= is an item key,
so a produced "Pail (Large)" is priced at the purchase price of a "Pail
(Large)".  The distribution of =imputed_price / cell_mean_unit_value= is
centred on 1 (median 0.982; p25 0.804, p75 1.203).

*Check B, per wave -- because Check B is corpus-wide and 2004-05 is only 4.4%
of the delta, a wave-specific inflation could hide inside a 1.0198.*  It does
not:

| wave    | Sigma imputed / Sigma at cell mean unit value | median(imputed qty / cell median purchased qty) |
|---------+-----------------------------------------------+-------------------------------------------------|
| 2004-05 |                                        1.0331 |                                           1.222 |
| 2010-11 |                                        1.2259 |                                           1.000 |
| 2013-14 |                                        1.0511 |                                           1.000 |
| 2016-17 |                                        1.0041 |                                           1.000 |
| 2019-20 |                                        0.9873 |                                           1.000 |

100% of imputed rows have a same-cell purchase pool in every wave.  *2004-05 --
the +101.6% wave -- prices at 1.033x its own cells' mean purchase unit value*,
so its uplift is a quantity/composition fact about that survey, not a price or
unit artefact; its imputed quantities run 22% above the median purchase in the
same cell, which is what own production of a staple looks like.  The one wave
worth a second glance is *2010-11 at 1.2259* -- 23% above the mean-unit-value
basis, the largest gap in the corpus and driven by right-skewed purchase price
distributions where the ladder's median sits above a thin cell's mean.  It is
still far from the sort of factor a unit mismatch produces (10x, 1000x), and
2010-11's uplift (+63.3%) is mid-range rather than extreme, so it does not
change the verdict.

*Check C -- a physical (kg) cross-check, independent of price entirely.*
Malawi's =food_acquired= ships =Quantity_kg= for 43.2% of rows.  On those:

| share of ...        | non-purchased |
|---------------------+---------------|
| physical kilograms  |        43.66% |
| valued expenditure  |        35.57% |
| ratio               |         0.815 |

Own-production is a *larger* share of the physical food than of the money --
which is what you expect (own production is maize flour and beans; purchases
skew to processed and animal foods), and which means the valuation is
*conservative* relative to a naive kg-weighted imputation, not inflated.

Two caveats on Check C, stated because they look like contradictions and are
not.  (i) The per-wave line for 2004-05 reads "kg 50.5% / value 81.8%",
apparently the opposite sign -- but 2004-05's =Quantity_kg= coverage is
*0.0-0.2%* of rows, so that line is computed on a few hundred rows and means
nothing.  The waves with real kg coverage (2013-14 25.6/16.7, 2016-17
48.0/38.1, 2019-20 37.2/32.8, 2010-11 48.6/45.2) all show the conservative
direction.  (ii) A naive corpus-wide =Sigma Quantity= comparison gives
=q_imp/q_pur = 0.217= against =v_imp/v_pur = 0.529=, an apparent 2.4x gap --
that statistic is meaningless, because it adds Grams to Kilogrammes to Pieces
across cells.  Check B is the same comparison done *within* cells, and it is

** CONCERN 6a -- the unconditional national rung prices a long thin tail

=median_price_valuation='s national rung is an *unconditional* fallback, so
=threshold=10= gates the geographic cells but gates *nothing* at the top of
the ladder.  For each =median_price= row I counted the purchase observations
in its own =(t, j, u)= *nationally* -- i.e. the deepest pool the ladder could
possibly have drawn on:

| national pool at the row's (t,j,u) | rows              | share of Malawi's total delta |
|------------------------------------+-------------------+-------------------------------|
| = 0                                | 0                 |                         0.00% |
| = 1 (a single transaction)         | 1,932 (0.69%)     |                     *2.85%*   |
| < 3                                | 4,079 (1.45%)     |                         4.18% |
| < 10 (below the stated gate)       | 11,652 (4.15%)    |                     *6.48%*   |

Median pool size is 533, so the bulk is well supported -- but 6.5% of the
imputed money is priced off fewer than ten transactions nationwide, and 2.9%
off exactly one.  The concrete cases are the ones you would expect:

- 2016-17 "Small Animal - Rabbit, Mice, Etc." / =u='Whole'=: a *single*
  purchase sets the price at 100,000 MWK, which is then applied to 195 rows
  and contributes *2.12% of the entire Malawi delta* -- including one in-kind
  row of 14 units valued at 1,400,000 MWK, the largest single imputation in
  the corpus.
- 2016-17 "Groundnut (Shelled)" / =u='Grams'=: one observation gives 800 MWK
  *per gram* (800,000/kg); a 500-gram produced row is valued at 400,000 MWK.
- 2019-20 "Bottled/Canned Beer" / =u='Kilogramme'=: pool of 2, 900 units,
  795,000 MWK.

This is the Malawi twin of Uganda's =u='---'= "Restaurant (soda)" row that the
ledger names -- and unlike Uganda's, it is *systematic* rather than a single
outlier, because a thin =(t,j,u)= cell is thin for every household in it.  I
agree with "count, never clip", so I am *not* asking for a magic outlier
screen.

*In fairness to the implementer, the row-share version of this is ALREADY in
the docstring, and it is exactly right.*  "national-rung coverage at
=threshold=10= is 94.2% (Malawi) / 76.5% (Uganda) on the native key" -- I
reproduced Malawi's 94.2% to the decimal on its stated denominator (share of
*candidate* rows whose =(t,j,u)= has >= 10 national purchase observations:
measured 94.2%).  My 6.48% is not a missing measurement, it is the
*value-weighted complement* of that same fact: the 5.8% of candidates below
the gate are not a random 5.8% of the money, they are 6.5% of it, and 2.9% of
it rests on single transactions.  The ask is therefore narrow -- add the
value-weighted figure beside the coverage figure -- because the docstring
presents 94.2% as evidence that the native-=u= basis "buys nothing
measurable" (a *coverage* argument, which it supports), and a reader can
easily carry that reassurance over to *accuracy*, where the thin tail is
slightly worse than the row share suggests.  Everything needed is already
returned: =valuation_price= per row and the =attrs= tallies.  Documentation
ask, not a code one.
