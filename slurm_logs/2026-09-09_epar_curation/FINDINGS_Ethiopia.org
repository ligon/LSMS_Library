  even after the #628 fix; W1 373/3,969 rows destroyed, W2 454/5,262, W3 732/4,954, W5

  (=LAT_DD_MOD= / =lat_dd_mod= / =lat_mod=), and W5 drops the EA key entirely
  (CONTENTS.org:956-1031).

  (ESPS-5) additionally excludes Tigray from the *represented population*, not just from

  =household_id2= W2/W3), and =_/food_acquired.py= lacked the =id_walk= that
  =_/crop_production.py= has until GH #819 landed on =development= 2026-09-08 --

  (CONTENTS.org:1367-1404).
- Panel-key question: our =panel_ids=/=id_walk= chains =household_id2= (W2/W3) back to
  the W1 baseline =household_id=; whether EPAR's own =hhid_panel_key.dta= is the same
  crosswalk is checked below.
- Livestock =SalesValue= is a sales-transaction total, NOT a herd value and NOT a
  per-animal price -- ESS asks no herd-value question in any wave (CONTENTS.org:1277-1302).
- The ESS instrument asks NO value of own-consumption anywhere (household module Q4 is
  purchase-only; agriculture §11/§12 Q10a ask quantity, not value) -- confirmed

the comment "this file does not exist in the WB W1 download. EPAR created it to track
panel households in the first three waves." So the file's OWN purpose is to attach
W1 rows with a forward-looking W2 id for users who want to link W1 outputs into
Panel I -- it does not implement W2->W3 or W1->W3 chaining anywhere in the do-files
(the other four waves' =hhid_panel= is just =gen hhid_panel = hhid=, i.e. a same-wave
alias, not a cross-wave key: W1 lines 6006/6120/6359, W2 6244/6341/6574, W3 5705/5810/
6049, W4 5578/5675/5907, W5 6028/6124/6358).

This is structurally the SAME crosswalk our =panel_ids=/=id_walk= chain relies on
(=household_id2= (W2/W3) -> W1 baseline =household_id=, CONTENTS.org:391-401,
926-941). EPAR's key is a strict subset of what we need (W1<->W2 only; we additionally
carry W3's =household_id2= reuse of the W2 scheme and reconstruct the W4/W5 discontinuity
as a genuinely new panel). Not a discrepancy -- corroboration that the id discontinuities
we document are real and EPAR hit the same wall (hence shipping a bespoke key rather than
trusting the WB files alone).

* Construction decisions by feature area (tagged reported/imputed/aggregated)

** 1. ALL PLOTS / crop values -- price-per-kg methodology, and the §11/§12 split (#820)

*Harvest quantity* (=quant_harv_kg=, feeds =all_fields.dta=): built from §9 post-harvest
(=sect9_ph_w{N}=, "PLOT AREAS"/"Plot variables" sub-section) ONLY, in every EPAR wave --
=EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W3.do:1073-1078= ("* GENERATE QUANT_HARV_KG, VALUE_HARVEST").
**Tagged: reported** quantity (=qty_harv = ph_s9q04_a=), with a farmer-estimate
fallback (=kg_est = ph_s9q05=, line 1078) when the primary field is blank -- **imputed**
in the narrow sense of substituting one reported field for another.
=lsms_library/countries/Ethiopia/2015-16/_/crop_production.py:20-38= (this repo) shows the
identical restriction: only =sect9_ph_w3.dta= is read for harvest; §12 is never touched.
**So on the HARVEST QUANTITY axis, EPAR has exactly our #820 limitation** -- §12 tree/
fruit/root crops never enter =all_plots.dta='s =value_harvest= via a harvest quantity at
all.

*Sale value / crop-value MEDIAN PRICE LADDER* (the "Crop Values" sub-section under ALL
PLOTS, used to impute =value_harvest= where a household reports no sale):
=EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W3.do:997-1067=. Here EPAR DOES append §12
(=sect12_ph_w3.dta=, tagged =permcrop=1=) to §11 (=sect11_ph_w3.dta=, =permcrop=0=)
before computing price medians (lines 999-1023). **Tagged: imputed** -- a ladder of
medians, EA -> kebele -> woreda -> zone -> region -> country (the =foreach i in region
zone woreda kebele ea hhid= loop, lines 1039-1046, and the country fallback, lines
1048-1052), each gated at >9 observations at the *unit* level first (=price_unit=) and
then again after currency-normalizing to per-kg (=price_kg=, lines 1055-1073); the
household's own price is preferred over the ladder (Cell 2d above). This ladder
methodology is architecturally identical to our =median_price_valuation=
(=transformations.py:2847=), independently converging on the same >=10-observation
threshold and finest-cell-first cascade -- see Ranked Learning #2/#3.

** 2. GROSS CROP REVENUE -- the W3->W4 regression (Cell 3a, detailed)

** 7. Population re-weighting -- a third weighting philosophy

Every EPAR readme's "Globals" section documents rescaling survey weights to TRUE
national rural/urban population totals (=Ethiopia_ESS_W{N}_pop_tot/pop_rur/pop_urb=,
World Bank WDI, e.g. =EthiopiaW5_Food Consumption by source.do:65-68=), producing
=weight_pop_tot= / =weight_pop_rururb= used as the analytic weight throughout (e.g. the
crop-price ladder's =[aw=weight_pop_rururb]=, =EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W3.do:1040=).
**Tagged: imputed** (a rescaling, not a survey-reported value). This is a THIRD weighting
convention distinct from both the raw survey weight and our own within-wave-mean-1
normalisation (=_normalise_sample_weights=, library design doc). Not reconciled here --
noted as a difference in what "weight" means when comparing any statistic EPAR publishes
against ours.

* Ranked learnings (what we could take, why, what it would touch, and the risk)

1. **Apply =median_price_valuation= to Ethiopia =food_acquired= for produced/gift rows,
   as an opt-in derived call -- do not store it.** EPAR's own-consumption valuation
   ladder (Construction #3) is architecturally identical to the WB ladder our
   =median_price_valuation= (=transformations.py:2847=) already implements for crops.
   Since the ESS instrument asks no own-consumption value (confirmed, CONTENTS.org:1311-
   1339), any such number is unavoidably imputed -- but a user who wants a WB-style
   food-consumption-value comparable to EPAR's own output currently has no path to one
   at all (=food_prices(units='unitprice')= returns empty by design). *What it would
   touch*: nothing in the data layer -- a new call site in =transformations.py= wiring
   =food_acquired='s purchased-row implied unit price as the price basis, with
   =geo_levels= from =cluster_features=. *Risk*: our =cluster_features.Region=/
   =District= are documented as broken/unharmonized for Ethiopia (CONTENTS.org:1418-
   1430) -- EPAR's ladder uses kebele/woreda/zone/subcity, finer levels we do not
   reliably expose. The transform would degrade to a coarser (region/country) ladder
   than EPAR's, and that degradation should be stated up front, not discovered later.

2. **Wire =Crop_CF_Wave{2,3,4,5}.dta= into =harvest_kg= as an additional layer, ahead of
   or alongside the survey-median inference.** We hold the DVC sidecar for four of five
   waves and read none of them (Cell 2c). EPAR's file is crop x unit x region -- exactly
   the granularity our own median-based inference tries to approximate FROM THE SURVEY
   DATA ITSELF (same country-wave). A WB-shipped, externally-sourced conversion factor
   is a genuinely different (and plausibly better-grounded) source than an in-sample
   median, and =harvest_kg='s layered design (reported -> survey-median -> library-
   inferred -> none) already has a slot shape for one more layer. *What it would touch*:
   =transformations.py:harvest_kg= layer order; needs a decision on where the new layer
   ranks relative to "reported" and "survey_median." *Risk*: W5's own do-file records a
   DUPLICATE-observation defect in =Crop_CF_Wave5.dta= itself (crop_code=74, unit_cd=62,
   two conflicting factors 4.34 and 6.125, EPAR picked 4.34 "for continuity" with W3,
   =EPAR_UW_Ethiopia_ESS_W5.do:349=) -- any wiring must de-duplicate deliberately, not by
   an unexamined =.median()= (which would average the two into a fabricated ~5.23,
   different from EITHER historical value).

3. **The finest-cell-first, obs>=10-gated median ladder is validated by independent
   convergence, worth citing as prior art in our own docstring.** Three separate EPAR
   constructions (crop sale price, ##1; livestock sale price, #4; food-consumption
   price, #3) and our own =median_price_valuation= all independently arrived at the
   same ladder shape and the same ">=10 observations" threshold. That is evidence the
   threshold is not an arbitrary choice on either side. *What it would touch*: a
   one-line addition to the =median_price_valuation= docstring noting the convergence
   (do not import EPAR's household-own-price-preference variant, #2d -- that is a
   documented, deliberate divergence, not something either side got wrong).

4. **Investigate the ESS4->ESS5 weight discontinuity EPAR flagged (readme.md:47,
   Cell 2f) against our own open question (CONTENTS.org:865-883).** Neither side has
   opened the actual =.dta= release to check whether a genuine longitudinal weight
   column exists in the W5 (2021-22) release under a name our config does not read.
   *What it would touch*: =Ethiopia/2021-22/_/data_info.yml= (the =sample= wave config),
   if a real =panel_weight= column exists. *Risk*: low to check (a metadata-only read of
   =sect_cover_hh_w5.dta=), but changing =panel_weight= for a live wave changes every
   downstream weighted statistic for 2021-22 -- needs a full before/after comparison, not
   a silent swap.

5. **Do NOT adopt EPAR's winsorization or population-rescaled weighting.** Both are
   real, defensible EPAR choices (Construction ##6-7) but contradict explicit library
   design decisions (no winsorization anywhere; weights normalised to within-wave mean 1,
   not to external population totals). Recorded here only so a future reader does not
   rediscover the difference and mistake it for an oversight.
